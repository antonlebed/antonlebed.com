Polynomial maps

The delay of a polynomial map of digit streams read through a signed digit set, of any smooth map of one stream, the maps with a pole included, what a chain of two readers pays against one, and what a comparator costs inside a unit.

A stream is a numeral in radix b over a digit set D = {−a−..a+}, read from its most significant digit: the first t digits name a cell of values, an interval, and the cells refine toward a point as t grows. A map of streams is read at lookahead c when its output's first t digits are a function of the inputs' first t + c; the least such c is the map's delay, and a map with a finite delay is local at this end. The set's slack ρ = a− + a+ + 1 − b counts the digits it holds beyond the radix; at slack 1 or more a value has several writings and the cells overlap, which is what lets a product or a sum be local at all. Its reach is W = a− + a+, the width of what the unseen digits can still contribute. An operation injects a spread, the width of what its unseen digits can still move the output by; with c digits of lookahead an overlapping cover swallows whole any spread up to bcρ, and the Lebesgue margin is the least c at which that reaches the spread injected, a size-only count. The laws below read a map's exact delay against that margin; the slope and addition laws over the same sets, and the leading end's own vocabulary, are on Redundant arithmetic. A census below counts digit systems, one per (radix, digit set), written radix b over {−a−..a+}, and a table's system is one of them.

Polynomial maps

The polynomial delay criterion

Let f be a polynomial in streams over a contiguous set {−a−..a+} at radix b whose terms have disjoint variable sets, the output aligned with the inputs and led by o positions when its range needs it (a sum's range doubles the window, so a map with an additive part runs led by one), and let Lip₁ be the largest L¹ norm of its gradient over the window, in window units, which disjoint terms attain at the corner of largest magnitude: dM̂d − 1 for a monomial of degree d, 2M̂ + 1 for the multiply–add xy + z and for x² + y, k for a sum of k streams. With L the lookahead plus the lead, the margin law — the Lebesgue margin with the injected spread read as Lip₁W —

bLρ  ≥  Lip1 (a−+a+)b^{L}\rho \;\ge\; \mathrm{Lip}_{1}\,(a^{-}+a^{+})

suffices for every continuous map and is the floor wherever a term has degree 2 or more, the Lipschitz constant its only parameter: the square shares the product's delay, the three degree-3 shapes share one, the multiply–add and x² + y share theirs, one lookahead above the product's at radix 2 over {−1..1} (3 against 2). An affine map's floor sits on a lattice instead. In units of b−(t+1) at the emitted level, with M± = a±/(b − 1) and ω = M− + M+ the reach at that scale, a sum of k streams has its image endpoints on the lattice (s − kM−)/bL, s an integer, so it is dead at L iff an integer lies in

((bL−k) M+,    bL−(bL−k) M−),\bigl((b^{L}-k)\,M^{+},\;\; b^{L}-(b^{L}-k)\,M^{-}\bigr),

the lookahead criterion at k = 2, the identity's survival at lookahead 0 at k = 1 (the interval is (0, 1)), and the three-sum's floor at k = 3. The interval's length is the margin's excess kω − bL(ω − 1), so the margin is exact off the wedge — the systems where that length is positive and the interval holds no integer — and one digit high on it: the two-sum's wedge is addition's, the identity's is every system, the three-sum's is empty through radix 4 and first appears at radix 5 over {−4..4}. A sum of streams is a lattice map with a wedge, never a map that reads below the margin outright; a non-affine map's reachable endpoints fill the dead arc at the emitted scale — proved for one stream by the one-stream theorem, certified at every non-affine map on this page — so its wedge is empty. The reader has no strategy. The output cells are [p − M−, p + M+] and the image of the input boxes is an interval inside the parent cell; a digit is legal iff its cell holds the image, so the reader is dead iff the image strictly contains an overlap zone [m + 1 − M−, m + M+] of two consecutive cells — a condition on the streams alone. The adversary fixes the streams in advance and any legal digit is as good as any other, so the greedy reader, any legal digit and no backtracking, survives wherever any reader does. Sufficiency: every prefix box lies in [−M−bn, M+bn], so by the mean value theorem every image is at most Lip₁ωb−L long, and the law says that is at most the zone's length ω − 1; with disjoint terms the corner width approaches that bound from below at every prefix length, or equals it when every term is affine, so the bound is the sup. Necessity is the excess placed on a zone: when the law fails by an integer excess E ≥ 1 the corner image exceeds the zone by a gap proportional to E/bL over a range of top prefixes, and the map's reachable endpoints sweep it. For the product, hold the streams' sum at 2U − 2I, U the prefix of n largest digits, and slide the split (U − I + i, U − I − i): the image keeps its length, its lower end falls by (2i − 1)/K a step with K = bn+c, and at I = ⌈√K⌉ the dead arcs of consecutive splits overlap and sweep a full unit. The square has no split and is the one-stream theorem's, a progression along which its phase is linear its first proof. The multiply–add is the first map where a lattice part and a dense part meet — z's endpoint on a lattice of step b−L, xy's sliding along the split line — and the union sweeps. On the diagonal the product row's right side is 4a² and its inequality is the delay condition of the on-line multiplier, the field's most-significant-first multiplier (Trivedi and Ercegovac, 1977, as restated in Frougny, Pavelka, Pelantová and Svobodová, DMTCS 2019, Corollary 2.2, an upper bound with optimality left open), read here as the floor for every reader and carried off the diagonal, up the degrees and into the multiply–add; that multiplier's rounding select is empty at five of the seven asymmetric systems of radices 2..5 and overpays at radix 5 over {−3..4}, reading 2 against the floor 1. The product's law is addition's bcρ ≥ 2W times M̂, the spread a bilinear map injects being the sum's scaled by the largest operand, and the digit is the second stream's: at radix 2 over {−1, 0, 1} the worst constant slope of magnitude at most 1 reads at 1 while two streams need 2, and at every system the worst slope reads one below the product's floor unless the floor is already 1. Two digits suffice at degree 2 (b² ≥ 4(b − 1)), and the degree-3 census reads 2 with 3 at radix 2 over {−1..1} alone. Maps of one stream

The one-stream theorem theorem

A polynomial's derivative is bounded on the window; a map of one stream may be any C² function of it, a map with a pole included, a point where the derivative is unbounded, read as x ↦ f(s + x) with s = M− + P and the pole distance P > 0 the argument's lower end over the window. For one stream the gradient's L¹ norm is |f′|, and with Λ = max|f′| over the window the reader, at the lead o its range sets (the output's first digit sitting o positions above the input's), is alive at every L = c + o with

bLρ  ≥  Λ (a−+a+)b^{L}\rho \;\ge\; \Lambda\,(a^{-}+a^{+})

and, unless f is affine on the window, dead at every L below, whatever it emitted before: the delay is exactly the margin law's L*, and nothing is asked of where |f′| peaks, of monotonicity on the window or of the curvature.

Sufficiency is the law's, by the mean value theorem. Necessity is a count over a region, not a descent in depth. Death is a property of the image alone: in units of the output cell, a prefix box whose image has lower end y and width w strictly contains the overlap zone between two cells iff its excess g′ = w − (W − 1) is positive and its phase {y + M−} lies in the dead arc (1 − g′, 1). Below L* the law's own excess g = ΛW/bL − (W − 1) is positive; let Λh be the midpoint of Λ and the law's threshold and C the component of {|f′| > Λh} holding the peak. The dichotomy. If f″ vanishes on C then f′ is ±Λ there, an endpoint of C interior to the window would read |f′| = Λh, so C is the window and f is affine. Otherwise |f″| ≥ κ > 0 with one sign on a curved subinterval I of C, where every box has excess at least g/2, f is strictly monotone, and the phase of the box at prefix v, bn−L times f at the box's lower end plus M−, has second derivative f″/bn+L in v: van der Corput's inequality in its explicit form (Kuipers and Niederreiter, Uniform Distribution of Sequences, 1974, Theorem 2.7) bounds the h-th harmonic of the phases by (hΔ + 2)(4 b(n+L)/2/√(hκ) + 3), Δ = g/(2W) the range of |f′|/bL over I, and Erdős–Turán (Theorem 2.5) turns the harmonics into a bound on the discrepancy in the count N alone once bn is read as under (N + W + 1)/|I|. So there is an N₀, a function of (b, L, W, g, |I|, κ) and never of the depth, past which some phase sits in the arc and the reader is dead at output depth n − c. Every interior peak of |f′| is a zero of f″, so an inflection at the peak is the curved case, not an exclusion. The affine remainder splits by slope: a rational slope reads at the rational-slope delay criterion at the symmetric sets it covers and at the lattice clause at slope 1, and an irrational slope (x/√2) is dead below L* at all 20 systems of radices 2..5 with no lattice clause and no region theorem, an observation. The full excess: at L = 0 a map with Λ = 1 has g = 1 and the arc is the circle less a point, so the identity survives at lookahead 0 only by its lattice sitting on the excluded point, and x − εx³ dies at output depth 1 or 2 at ε = 1/3, 1/30 and 1/300 alike while N₀ rises tenfold per decade: the kill depth is not a function of the curvature.

Two specimens with a pole. The square root x ↦ √(s + x), Λ = 1/(2√P): the law reads 4b2Lρ²P ≥ (a− + a+)², L* rising by one per two digits of pole depth; at radix 3 over {−1..2}, m = 1..5 reads 2, 2, 3, 3, 4. The reciprocal x ↦ 1/(s + x), decreasing and convex, its range's top 1/P at the pole side, Λ = 1/P²: the law reads bLρP² ≥ a− + a+, L* rising by two per digit; at radix 2 over {−1..1}, m = 1..5 reads 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. A decreasing map's box has its image read from the box's other end, and its lead is the least o with 1/P ≤ M+bo, zero or negative at 73 of its 120 sweep pairs. The output leads at both. The lookahead c* = L* − o is negative — the reader emits before it reads — at 65 of the square root's 120 sweep pairs, seven digits ahead at radix 2 over {−1..1} at P = 128, and at 40 of the reciprocal's; the law covers a negative lookahead as written, the zone at an output position before the first input digit being no narrower than the zone at L. The certificate is a scan. For one stream the adversary's certificate is the least output position at which some prefix's image strictly contains a zone, found by scanning the excess region, the prefixes whose box has an image wider than the zone, and the exhaustive game tree matches it at every pair its depth reaches. The pole arm is a rescaling. A map homogeneous of degree d in s + x has, at pole depth m, the map at depth 0 scaled by b−dm with the input read m digits deeper, so L* rises by m(1 − d): two per digit for the reciprocal, d = −1, one per two for the root, d = 1/2, the reciprocal's kill moving whole. The proof's constant against its check: N₀ runs from 4.4 · 10⁴ to 5.1 · 10¹⁰ over the certified pairs, while the curved subinterval at the depth before the kill never holds more than 35 prefixes, and at every specimen but the poles and the monomials none, so the proof's depth is never approached and the kills the scan finds are the curvature's own; the two inequalities, read at 402 depths of 10⁴ to 5 · 10⁴ phases, hold with the harmonics at most 0.11 of van der Corput's bound and the discrepancy at most 0.019.

Chained units

The chained delay criterion

A chain is a product reader that sees x and y and emits a stream P = xy over the same digit set at lookahead L₁, feeding a sum reader that sees P and z, aligned with P, and emits xy + z at lookahead L₂; it reads the inputs at L₁ + L₂, and the fused unit is the multiply–add above, one reader seeing all three streams. The product reader picks its digit by any rule wherever two are legal, and the sum reader may know the rule. What it then knows of xy is the fibre of the prefix it has seen, the union of the product images of every (x, y) prefix the rule sends there, and a cell holds the fibre plus z's box iff it holds the fibre's hull, an interval inside P's own cell, inset by α and β from the cell's ends in cell units. So the sum reader is dead at L₂ iff an integer lies in the two-sum's lattice interval with its ends drawn in, ((bL₂ − 2)M+ + β, bL₂ − (bL₂ − 2)M− − α), and its escape cost is the least α + β that empties the interval. The product reader pays for insets out of its slack: every image at level n + L₁ must sit in some hull, and consecutive hulls, at unit spacing, must overlap by the image width, so α + β ≤ (ω − 1) − 2M̂ω/bL₁, its slack budget, nonnegative exactly from the product's own floor on; a reader into cells inset by (α, β) reads at the product's law with the slack ρ − (b − 1)(α + β). The escape law: the chain survives at (L₁, L₂) iff the escape cost fits the budget. At every system of radices 2..5 it fits only where the interval is already empty, so the chain's floor is L₁* + L₂*, the two units' own floors, whatever the multiplier does with its digits. The composition rule is the hardware field's own — "for a series of online operations, the overall latency is the sum of online delays" (Usman, Ercegovac and Lee, 2023) — and it is here a floor: no selection rule for the multiplier lowers the aligned sum. The fused unit sits below the chain wherever the product's margin absorbs the addition outright, its floor the product's own, and at radix 2 over {−1..1}, where it pays one for z and the adder alone pays two; the two are level where the product's floor is 1 and both pay one for z:

Comparators

The comparator delay theorem

The margin law never grants lookahead 0 to a map whose Lipschitz constant is 1 or more, and the field's table gives max and min on-line delay 0 (Ercegovac and Lang, Digital Arithmetic, 2003, Table 9.1); the reason is the lattice. Every cell of depth n, the cell of an n-digit prefix, has its lower end on (Z − M−)/bn and width ω/bn, the output cells included, and a cell of depth n meets a cell of depth n − 1 inside one of the latter's children, the depth-n cells whose lower ends run through the parent's lattice points from its own lower end up, the last ending at the parent's top (the cell lemma). So a cell-valued map, one whose image of every box of prefixes lies inside some cell of the same depth, reads at lookahead 0 at every system: the committed cell, the one the reader's emitted digits name, holds the image, the image lies in a cell, the cell meets the committed one inside a child, and that child is a legal digit. max and min of any number of streams and their compositions are cell-valued, the image of a box under max lying in the input cell with the larger top: the clamp min(max(x, y), z), the rectifier max(x, 0), whose image is a point inside the committed cell when the cell sits below 0, and the identity as the case of one stream. |x| is cell-valued iff its mirror, the cell reflected through 0, is a cell, (b − 1) | (a+ − a−), which within the radix means a symmetric set; at every asymmetric system tried the reader dies at 0 in one round, and every system survives at 1, the margin's grant. The comparator is free. A reader's death is a property of the image alone: an image inside the committed cell fails every child iff it strictly contains an overlap zone, and a cell strictly contains no zone. A fused unit G(w, max(y, z)), one reader seeing every stream, has each box image inside G's image of a box of its own inputs at the same depth, the cell of y or of z with the larger top standing for the comparator, so a fused death is a death of G; and on the diagonal y = z the adversary hands the reader G's own game, so the fused unit reads no earlier. For every map G of streams the delay of G(w, max(y, z)) equals the delay of G(w, y), min likewise. A comparator on an output, max(G(x), z) with z a stream the reader sees beside G's inputs, costs exactly G's delay too. At most, since its image lies inside G's or inside z's cell. At least, because the constant stream of the lowest digit −a− spells the window's bottom −M− exactly, and fed as z it makes max(G(x), z) equal to G(x) wherever G's range lies inside z's window [−M−, M+], so a reader of the comparator is a reader of G at the same lookahead: every map at a representable system read aligned with its inputs, and any map whose partner is read at the output's own scale; min is the mirror case, the constant top stream. The sum led by one position with its partner at the input scale is the case left, z sitting inside the sum's range and the comparator fed the bottom a clamp of the sum rather than the sum. There a death at a box is a property of the sum of the two prefixes as integers alone, and adding bc+1 to that sum, legal while the prefixes stay in range, carries the death one overlap zone up, so every death has a translate whose zone lies above −M− at every system with slack 1 or more (the translation lemma), and there the clamp's image is the sum's and the clamp dies with it. max(x, y) + z reads at the lookahead criterion's floor for x + z and x·max(y, z) at the product's. A datapath's delay table is its arithmetic skeleton's with the comparators deleted: a digit-serial network's rectifier and pooling cost no lookahead, fused into the multiply–add or not, on an output as inside a unit.
