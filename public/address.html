The flip's address

Where the two images part when a window's reader tears, read off the cuts alone at every irrational window.

A window is a nested grid of cells refining toward a point, and it reads a map at lookahead c when the output cell at depth t is a function of the input cell at depth t + c; cmin(t) is the least lookahead that works at depth t, its values over t a map's column, and a map is gated when no bounded lookahead works at all (Reading). A map tears when pairs of inputs agreeing to ever greater depth have images that agree only to a bounded depth: the flip is the lowest position at which two such images differ, least over every such pair, and its address is that position.

For irrational α = [0; a1, a2, …] with convergents pk/qk (q0 = 1, q1 = a1, qk = ak qk−1 + qk−2), the Ostrowski digits of n write n = Σ bk qk greedily. Legality — what makes the writing unique — caps b0 at a1 − 1 and bk at ak+1 for k ≥ 1, and forces a zero below any digit sitting at its cap; the lowest admissible digit is position 0 when a1 ≥ 2 and position 1 when a1 = 1, the only position legality caps at 0; a column is unbounded from that digit when it is infinite from depth z + 1 on, z its position and z + 1 the first depth that reads it. The window is the trailing one: the depth-t cell of n is the set of nonnegative integers agreeing with n on their t low-order digits, cells partitioning at each depth and nesting across depths. A string is read through its star Σ bk θk, where θk = qkα − pk is the convergent remainder, |θ| falling geometrically with alternating signs. The star is nα minus an integer, so it is the circle point nα mod 1, and a string's low digits read off that point. The family has a member for every irrational, and where α is quadratic its own arithmetic adds more (Quadratic windows).

What the nested cells define in the limit is the window's completion, and every Ostrowski completion is an odometer, the adding machine on the legal digit strings: its points code circle points, the cells being the arcs into which those points cut the circle, and the odometer's cut points — the circle points with two codings — are −jα for a cut index j ≥ 1. A tear is the failure of a continuous extension to the completion. A scan cell is one (window, map) pair, and where α's continued fraction is eventually periodic a carry automaton settles a whole column outright — a relation between an integer and its image leaves a bounded carry read digit by digit, so the relation is a finite automaton (The lookahead limit) — which is the check every reading here is run against wherever a period exists. The address needs no period at all.

The principle and its witness

The depth-t cells are the arcs the circle is cut into at −jα for 1 ≤ j ≤ qt (one cell, the circle cut at −α alone, while qt = 1), so a map tears at any input off the cut orbit whose image lands on it, and whether some input does is a question about where the map sends the cuts.

The containment principle theorem

The two codings of the cut −jα part at zj = min{z : qz+1 ≥ max(j, 2)}, and −α's at the lowest admissible digit. A shift-free relation mo·w = mi·n + c between an integer n and its image w, in lowest terms, gcd(mo, mi) = 1, sends the input's circle point x to (mix + cα + δ)/mo with the offset δ = miP mod mo, P the integer part of nα. Every offset occurs inside any deep cell: consecutive convergents are unimodular — qkpk+1 − qk+1pk = ±1, so (qk, pk) and (qk+1, pk+1) span (Z/m)² at every k and every m — and the unimodular family below turns that spanning into an actual input inside a given deep cell. The lookahead at depth t is then the least ĉ with every output cut's preimages among the depth-(t + ĉ) input cuts, infinite exactly when some preimage is not a cut: a non-cut point is interior to a cell at every depth and the lattice of integer points accumulates at it from both sides. Member by member: at mo = 1 the preimages of −jα are the mi points (−(j + c)α + ν)/mi for ν = 0..mi − 1, cuts only at ν = 0 with mi | j + c, so ×m + c, m ≥ 2, is unbounded from the lowest admissible digit for every c; n + c at c ≥ 0 has every preimage a cut and is finite with the exact column min{ĉ : qt+ĉ ≥ qt + c} (0 while qt = 1), which at c = 1 is the constant 1 from the lowest admissible digit's depth on — the successor's lookahead 1, a theorem at every irrational window; and the decrement n − c at c ≥ 1 sends −α back to the lattice point (c − 1)α, never a cut, so the decrement borrows where the increment does not. At mo ≥ 2 the images of one converging input accumulate at mo points 1/mo apart, a jump that the sweep of the offset — δ run through its values, the image point stepping with it — carries across some cut, so every such relation — the floors ⌊n/m⌋ included, as the union over c — is unbounded from the lowest admissible digit. Every shift-free relation is unbounded from the lowest admissible digit except n + c at c ≥ 0, whose column is exact.

Scope. Theorem at every irrational window, periodicity used nowhere. Checked against the carry automaton at 189 periodic scan cells over nine windows — n + c for c = 2..5 equal to the formula entry for entry, the decrement, ×m + c and six mo ≥ 2 relations unbounded from exactly the depth that first reads the lowest admissible digit — and in exact integers at e − 2, ∛2 − 1 and five windows designed so that qK ≡ 1 mod m never occurs, 12,150 readings and the decrement's 1,350, 0 off.

The unimodular family theorem

The witness the principle writes down, at −α for ×m: YK = m·qK + λ·qK+4 + μ·qK+5 with (λ, μ) ∈ [0, m)² the one solution of λ·(q, p)K+4 + μ·(q, p)K+5 ≡ (1, 0) mod m, and nK = (YK − 1)/m. The images mnK sit beside −α on alternating sides within (2m − 1)|θK|, parting at exactly z1, while the inputs converge to −α/m, never a cut. The top-raised family — (qK − 1)/m against its top raised by one cap, under the crossing condition |θK+2/θK| < (m − 1)/m that puts the two images on opposite sides — is the special case whose witness is qK alone, with none of the two unimodular convergents the family adds, which needs qK ≡ 1 mod m, and needs that residue to recur where two unimodular convergents need nothing. At the golden window's obstructed scan cells (m = 3, 5, 7, where neither witness a quadratic window builds out of its own arithmetic exists, Quadratic windows) the automaton's own witness is of the top-raised kind, its images the two codings of −2α parting at b1 for ×3 and ×5 and of −α for ×7.

Scope. Theorem at every irrational window; read at e − 2 and ∛2 − 1 for m = 2..7 and at five designed tails on which qK ≡ 1 mod m never occurs, every K = 8..56, parting exactly z1 at all 833 readings; the top-raised case at all 54 (window, m) scan cells the automaton reads.

The drop

The drop's column theorem

A relation read after the input string is moved r positions has for its image point the pseudo-star Σ bkθk+r, a function of the string and of no circle point; which shifts read — a shift moving every digit up by a fixed stride — is the question of Digit shifts. The drop by g — the string moved down g positions — is no shift-free relation, so the principle hands it no address, and its column is read off two facts instead. The depth-t cells are the arcs into which the points −jα, 1 ≤ j ≤ qt, cut the circle: the cell of a head — the digits below position t — is its star plus the closure of the tails, an arc from −θt to −θt−1 when the head's top digit is 0 and to −θt−1 − θt otherwise, the cap barring the tail's first digit at its top value, the lengths summing to qt|θt−1| + qt−1|θt| = 1. And the first tail digit bs ≤ as+1 moves the drop by bs·θs−1, a sweep of as+1 steps from the drop of the head below position s. Where a legal head of drop value h — the integer Σk≥1 bkqk−1 its digits make, which is where the drop by 1 sends it — and a cut j have ⟨(h + j)α⟩ — writing ⟨x⟩ for the signed distance from x to the nearest integer — equal to −uθs−1 with 0 < u < as+1 non-integral, the digits ⌊u⌋ and ⌊u⌋ + 1 land in the two cells adjacent to −jα at every depth t ≤ s − 1 with two cells and that cut, while the inputs agree to depth exactly s: the pair straddles the cut, and the column is infinite exactly when straddles recur at infinitely many s. The single-digit inputs j·qD and (j + 1)·qD drop to points stepping by θD−g, the sweep of the empty head — no digits at all below position D; where aD+1 ≥ qD−g+1 + qD−g it wraps the circle and a consecutive pair straddles −α, so a window with that inequality at infinitely many D — a designed tail of fast-growing quotients — has the drop by g unbounded from the lowest admissible digit. The golden 1/φ, silver √2 − 1, bronze [0; 3, 3, …] and √3 − 1 windows read the drop by their period l finite with peak l + 1; the drop by 1 is infinite from depth 2 at √3 − 1 by the carry automaton, and at the golden, silver and bronze windows the straddles sit at s ≤ t + 1 alone, the columns 2, 1, 2 at depths 2, 3, 4. At e − 2 = [0; 1, 2, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 6, …] the big digit as+1 = 2i sits at s = 3i − 2, and the head hi = Σk=2i−1 2k·q3k−3 + 1 — one digit at each earlier big quotient — has ⟨(hi + 1)α⟩ = −θs−1(2i − 1 + i|θs|/|θs−1|), so the digits 2i − 1 and 2i land on the two sides of −α at distances i|θs| and i|θs| + |θs+1|: the drop by 1's column at e − 2 is infinite at every depth t ≥ 2. At ∛2 − 1 the landings sit at five of the eight big quotients from position 2 to 16 and the criterion is silent at the other three, so the depth-2 lookahead is at least 11 at scanned scope. A window with bounded, non-periodic quotients can read the drop by 1 infinitely, by The parity chain.

Scope. Theorem for the cells' cuts, the straddle criterion, the fast-growing end above (the straddling pair found within 2 of its predicted crossing at every D = 4, 8, …, 24) and e − 2 (the head family exact in integers at i = 2..12, the pairs to 1.9·1016); a rule at the four periodic scan cells of the finite, periodic end, the automaton deciding each column; an observation at ∛2 − 1, the criterion sufficient only and the head search capped at 3·106 heads through position 17.

The parity chain theorem

Write R(K) for “qK − 1 is a drop value on positions 1..K”, the digits bk running over a legal input string. Since qK − 1 = aKqK−1 + qK−2 − 1 and the digit aK is legal at position K when aK ≤ aK+1 (at the cap it forces position K − 1 to zero, which the remainder qK−2 − 1 does not reach), R(K − 2) implies R(K), with R(0) trivial and R(1) iff a1 ≤ a2 + 1. The parity chain below s is the run ai ≤ ai+1 at i = s − 2, s − 4, …, down to that base. At every ascent as+1 > as whose chain is non-descending, the head qs−2 − 1 is a drop value with bs−1 = 0, the point of qs−2·α is θs−2 = −uθs−1 with u = as + |θs|/|θs−1|, never an integer, and the inputs with first tail digit as and as + 1 agree to depth exactly s while their drops sit at |θs| and |θs−1| − |θs| from −α on either side, inside the two adjacent cells at every depth 2 ≤ t ≤ s − 1. Such s recurring, the column is infinite at every t ≥ 2: [0; 1, x1, 1, x2, …] with xm ∈ {1, 2} aperiodic and x = 2 recurring is a bounded non-periodic window reading the drop by 1 infinitely. The converse fails: whether a cut is straddled at s is a question of reach, which integers are drop values of strings on positions 1..s − 1, a set the quotients’ descents cut with gaps (at the window whose quotients are the Fibonacci word over {1, 2}, the digit at position 7 sitting at its cap forbids position 6, so no drop value on positions 1..8 lies in (46, 68) and the ascent at 9 lands nothing). The periodic windows [0; (1, 1, 2)∞] and [0; (2, 1, 1)∞] read finite columns, 4, 3, 2 and 2, 1, 3 at depths 2, 3, 4, by the carry automaton; the Fibonacci-word windows over {1, 2} and {1, 3} read 5, 4, 3 and the Thue–Morse-word windows over {1, 2} and {2, 3} read 3, 2, 4, with no straddle past the last one to s = 18; an eventually constant window with last ascent slast reads slast − t + 1 at t ≤ slast − 2. A descent can be straddled too, by a two-step sweep ([0; (2, 3)∞] at every s). Which bounded windows read a finite column, beyond the periodic case the automaton decides, is open.

Scope. Theorem for the chain lemma and the straddle, read at three parity-chain windows to s = 18, every ascent straddling at depths 2, 3, 4; the finite columns a rule at [0; (1, 1, 2)∞], [0; (2, 1, 1)∞] (the automaton, exact) and at three designed eventually constant windows, an observation at the Fibonacci-word and Thue–Morse-word windows (no straddle past the last one to s = 18); the exact straddle test agrees with the automaton at all thirteen periodic windows and with enumeration to s = 8 at all 23 windows read.

verifiers: explore_drop_ascent.py, explore_limit_maps.py

At every irrational window the containment principle decides every shift-free relation's column, and the unimodular family writes down the ×m witness wherever that column is infinite. The map the principle does not reach is the drop, which is no shift-free relation. Its column is read off the straddles instead: infinite at a designed fast-growing tail, at e − 2 and at the ascending windows the parity chain describes; the drop by the period is finite at the four periodic windows the carry automaton decides, and which bounded windows read a finite column beyond the periodic ones is open.
