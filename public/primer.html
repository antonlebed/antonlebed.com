Primer

Take any number. Divide it by 2, by 3, by 5, by 7, by 11, by 13, by 17. Keep only the remainders. Those seven remainders are completely independent of each other — and together they reconstruct the original number exactly. This gives you free error correction, parallel computation, and a tower of nested rings, all from pure arithmetic.

CRT in 30 seconds

The Chinese Remainder Theorem says: if you have coprime moduli (they share no common factors), then knowing all the remainders uniquely determines the number. It's a bijection — no information lost, no ambiguity.

For our ring, N = 510,510 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17. Every number from 0 to N−1 maps to a unique 7-tuple of remainders. Every valid 7-tuple maps back to exactly one number. Type one and see:

Independence: the key insight

Here's what most explanations miss. The CRT doesn't just give you a way to represent numbers — it gives you independence. Knowing a number's remainder mod 2 tells you nothing about its remainder mod 3, or mod 5, or any other channel. Zero mutual information. This is a theorem, not an approximation.

Watch independence in action

Click +1 repeatedly. Channels highlight when they wrap (roll from max back to 0). Mod 2 wraps every 2 steps, mod 3 every 3 — they wrap at different times. No channel's wrap affects any other.

This means: adding 1 in the full ring is just adding 1 in each channel independently. Multiplying two numbers is just multiplying channel by channel. The ring's arithmetic decomposes into 7 tiny, parallel, non-interfering computations.

Instead of one problem with 510,510 possibilities, you have 7 problems with at most 17 possibilities each. Total positions to search: 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 + 13 + 17 = 58. That's an 8,802× reduction.

Why these seven primes

Start with seeds {1, 2} and apply one map: c(n) = 2n+1. Two Cunningham chains emerge, each terminating when the map hits a composite: 1 → 3 → 7 → 15 (stop: 15 = 3×5) and 2 → 5 → 11 → 23 → 47 → 95 (stop: 95 = 5×19). The first five axiom primes: {2, 3, 5, 7, 11}. Two extension primes complete the set: 13 = 2²+3² and 17 = 2+3+5+7. These give 7 channels: 4 data, 3 parity.

The tower

Each prime adds one channel. Every channel is a prime field — no zero divisors, every nonzero element invertible. The tower builds from the data ring to the full 7-channel ring:

λ is the Carmichael function — the universal period of the power map. Adding prime 11 jumps λ from 12 to 60. Adding 17 jumps it to 240. The data channels carry information (2 × 3 × 5 × 7 = 210 states); the parity channels provide protection (11 × 13 × 17 = 2,431).

What this gives you

Seven independent channels, by construction, yield three things for free:

Error correction. The 3 parity channels are algebraically determined by the 4 data channels. Corrupt any single channel and the parity detects, locates, and corrects it. The code is MDS (distance 4) — no [7,4] code can do better.

Parallel computation. Any operation on the full ring decomposes into 7 independent operations on tiny channels. Matrix multiplications become block-diagonal. Gradients don't cross channels.

A tower of rings. Use fewer primes for a coarser ring (4 primes gives Z/210), all seven for full resolution (Z/510,510). Each level nests inside the next. Raising prime exponents beyond 1 gives more resolution per channel at the cost of field structure.
