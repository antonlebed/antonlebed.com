Primer

Take any number. Divide it by 8, by 9, by 25, by 49, by 11, by 13, by 17. Keep only the remainders. Those seven remainders are completely independent of each other — and together they reconstruct the original number exactly. This gives you free error correction, parallel computation, and a resolution hierarchy, all from pure arithmetic.

CRT in 30 seconds

The Chinese Remainder Theorem says: if you have coprime moduli (they share no common factors), then knowing all the remainders uniquely determines the number. It's a bijection — no information lost, no ambiguity.

For our ring, N = 214,414,200 = 8 × 9 × 25 × 49 × 11 × 13 × 17. Every number from 0 to N−1 maps to a unique 7-tuple of remainders. Every valid 7-tuple maps back to exactly one number. Type one and see:

Independence: the key insight

Here's what most explanations miss. The CRT doesn't just give you a way to represent numbers — it gives you independence. Knowing a number's remainder mod 8 tells you nothing about its remainder mod 9, or mod 25, or any other channel. Zero mutual information. This is a theorem, not an approximation.

Watch independence in action

Click +1 repeatedly. Channels highlight when they wrap (roll from max back to 0). Mod 8 wraps every 8 steps, mod 9 every 9 — they wrap at different times. No channel's wrap affects any other.

This means: adding 1 in the full ring is just adding 1 in each channel independently. Multiplying two numbers is just multiplying channel by channel. The ring's arithmetic decomposes into 7 tiny, parallel, non-interfering computations.

Instead of one problem with 214 million possibilities, you have 7 problems with at most 49 possibilities each. Total positions to search: 8 + 9 + 25 + 49 + 11 + 13 + 17 = 132. That's a 1.6 million× reduction.

Why these seven primes

Start with seeds {1, 2} and apply one map: c(n) = 2n+1. Two Cunningham chains emerge: 1 → 3 → 7 → 15 (stop, composite) and 2 → 5 → 11 → 23 (stop). The primes harvested: {2, 3, 5, 7, 11}. Both chains self-terminate when the map produces a composite. Two extension primes complete the set: 13 = 2²+3² and 17 = 2+3+5+7. These give 7 channels: 4 data, 3 parity for free error correction.

Why the exponents

The ring uses exponents {3, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1} — giving moduli 8, 9, 25, 49, 11, 13, 17. These are Pareto-optimal: each is the largest exponent whose channel period divides the ring's Carmichael function (λ = 1680 at TRANS level). Raising any exponent further would force a larger period that breaks the lattice. The result: maximum resolution per channel with perfect synchronization.

What this gives you

Seven independent channels, by construction, yield three things for free:

Error correction. The 3 parity channels are algebraically determined by the 4 data channels. Corrupt any single channel and the parity detects, locates, and corrects it. Rate 4/7 — the same as Hamming(7,4), but embedded in the ring's own arithmetic.

Parallel computation. Any operation on the full ring decomposes into 7 independent operations on tiny channels. Matrix multiplications become block-diagonal. Gradients don't cross channels.

A resolution ladder. Use fewer channels for a coarser ring (5 channels gives Z/2310, or fattened to Z/970200), all seven for full resolution (TRANS = Z/214414200). Each level nests inside the next.
