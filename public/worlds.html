Words and curves

What two leaves can measure about each other, and which loci hold torsion at all.

A rung of the tower is Z/N with N the product of the first k primes, one channel per prime (The Object). Every channel is a field, so its nonzero residues form a cyclic group of order p − 1, and a unit's discrete log is its exponent to a fixed generator of that group. A measurement program is a word — a formula over the leaves, the input variables, in MUL/INV/NOT, where INV is the meadow inverse (channel-wise inverse on the support, 0 elsewhere, so division is total) and NOT is 1 − a; dropping NOT leaves the monomial alphabet, whose words are the aibj with exponents in Z, negative ones reached through that same inverse. Words are read by the gates gatem(w) = [wm = 1], which decide order divisibility channel by channel, and what a program is asked to decide is a shadow: an exact Boolean condition on the ring, [ab = 1] or [f = 0]. One leaf, read by every gate there is, measures exactly its order profile — ord a and ord(1−a) — and stops there (the ladder's wall). Two leaves measure their relative position. Throughout, a conjugacy orbit is the set a pair sweeps under the power automorphisms (a, b) → (au, bu), u a unit mod p − 1.

Words: the two-leaf world

The word-profile theorem rule

The word family — gates on aibj, exponents in Z via the meadow inverse — measures exactly the leaf discrete logs up to one shared unit: profiles coincide iff (a′, b′) = (au, bu) for a single automorphism x → xu (a cyclic-group fact at any letter count: the two pencils ord(aib), ord(abj) already generate the profile, the three words ord a, ord b, ord ab do not). A single leaf is the one-letter case — an order is a discrete log up to its own unit — so the genuinely two-leaf content is the relative position: where a is a primitive root the profile pins dloga b exactly, and [ab = 1], which walls the one-leaf ladder, is one gate. A two-variable shadow is therefore word-decidable iff constant on conjugacy orbits: every multiplicative coset [aibj = 1] is decided, and the additive world is blind except its crystallographic skeleton (below).

Scope. Proved; the profile partition checked exhaustively against the orbit partition in eight cyclic groups and at the seven primes 5 ≤ p ≤ 23.

The equality grading rule

The two-leaf read gatem(ab⁻¹) is [dlog a ≡ dlog b mod (p−1)/gcd(m, p−1)]: a divisor-indexed grading of equality on the units — reflexive, symmetric, AND-transitive, unit-translation-invariant — the congruence filtration of the diagonal, which is the profinite metric on the index ring one level down (the coupling order). It is not an inner product: every word's discrete log is a linear form in the leaf discrete logs, and the bilinear candidate gcd(dlog a · dlog b, p−1) factors through the two single-leaf orders. One ring gate reads all k channels' gradings at once, and at Z/510510 the 20 gradings, one per divisor of λ = 240, are distinct as relations.

Scope. Proved; exhaustive over all unit pairs and divisors at seven primes, the factorization over every pair in eight cyclic groups.

The crystallographic skeleton rule

A point (a, b) of a locus is stable under the automorphism x → xu when its conjugate (au, bu) lies on the locus too. On a line a + b = c with c ≠ 0, the inversion u = −1 forces stable points onto ab = 1 and stability under every u forces every order-d element to one trace, φ(d) ≤ 2 — only torsion of order 1, 2, 3, 6 survives (order 4 lands on c = 0 = [ab⁻¹ = −1], wholly decided): c = ±2 keep (±1, ±1), c = 1 keeps the Φ6 pair (iff p ≡ 1 mod 6 — the word-visible part of the dependent shadow a AND NOT a), c = −1 the Φ3 pair (iff p ≡ 1 mod 3), and every other line is word-invisible. Deciding channels are exactly p ≤ 3 — the trivial Galois group, and the same arithmetic one level up as the p = 3 rigidity.

Scope. Proved, plus census over all p ≤ 100.

The alphabet ladder rule

Reach is alphabet-graded. Mixed words on one leaf's letters {a, 1−a} already refine the order profile — first at p = 11, where the residues 9 and 3 share the profile (ord a, ord(1−a)) = (5, 5) and the word a²(1−a) separates them: the wall witness was itself a word. The four letters {a, b, 1−a, 1−b} add the six ±1-ratio affine lines (a + b ∈ {0, 1, 2}, a − b ∈ {0, 1, −1}, each one or two gates), while [a + b = 3] stays blind, witnessed at the Φ6 points of p = 7, which share the full four-letter profile and split the shadow.

Scope. Rule for the swept ranges (the one-leaf refinement exhaustive p ≤ 23, the six lines exhaustive p ≤ 19), observation beyond.

The meadow wall-breaker rule

The full meadow closure — NOT and the inverse nested freely — is complete: NOT(a⁻¹)·(NOT a)⁻¹·a = −1 on units a ≠ 1, so 2 = NOT(−1) and every constant is a word in one graded leaf, and [x = c] = gate1(xc⁻¹) pins every residue (0 and 1 need no word: [x = 1] is □x and [x = 0] is NOT ◇x, the two bits of the pair). The meadow inverse is the wall-breaker: every measurement wall in the hierarchy, the single-leaf wall included, is a fact about inverse-free alphabets, and total division buys omniscience — at a price charged in word operations, the height floor.

Scope. Proved; the identity and the closure from every graded start exhaustive at p ≤ 31.

Curves: the cut side

Past degree 1 the question inverts: not what a program reads, but which loci hold torsion — which order-d unit pairs are orbit-stable for a shadow [f = 0] — stable under every u, as on the lines above: the pair and all its conjugates (au, bu) lie on the locus together. The grading meets coding theory: a torsion order becomes a cyclic code, and what a shadow can hold becomes that code's minimum distance.

The fiber criterion criterion

A unit pair of torsion order d = lcm(ord a, ord b) is orbit-stable for a shadow [f = 0] iff its collected monomial fiber Σ cmwmt (wm = aimbjm, a character sum on Z/d) either vanishes identically — f contains the whole cyclic group ⟨(a, b)⟩, the finite shadow of Lang's torsion-coset theorem — or is a codeword of the cyclotomic cyclic code (Φd) ⊆ Fp[T]/(Td − 1). Those two cases are the two species: the whole-group species, and the codeword species.

Scope. Proved, both directions; checked against brute orbit stability on 67,704 curve-point tests at four primes.

verifier: explore_curve_skeleton.py

The torsion-menu law rule

That code's minimum distance is exactly qmin(d), the least prime factor of d: the longest run of consecutive units mod d is qmin − 1 (CRT), no nonzero M-term character sum vanishes at M consecutive points (Vandermonde), and the q-gon (Td − 1)/(Td/q − 1) realizes weight q. So a shadow with M monomials admits codeword-species torsion of order d only if qmin(d) ≤ M — the crystallographic restriction generalized (the line family is the M = 3 row, its unit coefficients and constant term cutting that menu down to φ(d) ≤ 2) — and a new weight class arrives at every prime. Weight 2 is the antipodal class (even torsion unbounded); weight 1 is empty, and odd d admits no weight 2. The run bound is field-free, so characteristic p adds no sporadic minima (in characteristic 0 the same minimum follows from Mann's vanishing-sums theorem).

Scope. Proved field-free; the minimum weight censused exhaustively for twelve odd d ≤ 35.

The pentagon witness rule

The first species past the lines arrives at M = 5, inside the conic budget of six monomials: the pentagon conic y² + xy + x + y + 1 = 0 carries the fiber 1 + T + T² + T³ + T⁴ at (θ, θ²), θ of order 5, and holds exactly one stable order-5 orbit, (θ, θ²) under conjugacy, at every p ≡ 1 mod 5 and none elsewhere — degree-2 shadows see torsion no line can, and five monomials are the least that can hold it.

Scope. Proved (the orbit count by exponent bookkeeping mod 5); present at p = 11, 31, 41, 61 and absent at p = 7 by census.

The exponent reduction criterion

Bounded degree changes realizability, not the menu. Writing a pair of torsion order d as (θA, θB) with gcd(A, B, d) = 1, the monomial xiyj contributes the exponent Ai + Bj mod d, so a curve of degree D can only reach the triangle exponent set {Ai + Bj mod d : i + j ≤ D} — and degree D reaches order d through the codeword species iff some such pair puts a nonzero (Φd)-codeword inside its triangle. Coefficients are free per monomial, so collection is surjective, and joint unit scaling of (A, B) with the x ↔ y swap preserves everything: the question is a census over scaling classes, and every exclusion at degree D closes every degree below it.

Scope. Proved; every realization below rebuilt as an actual curve vanishing on an actual orbit, and brute-forced over all lines and pairs at two (d, p) pairs.

The realizability law rule

The minimum degree at which a shadow holds order-d torsion through the codeword species is 1 for every even d (the antipodal word 1 + Td/2, which is x = −1 in curve clothing); the triangle cover number c(q) = Θ(√q) for odd prime d = q (below); and qmin(d) − 1 for every other odd d, realized by the q-gon. The last price is proved piece by piece: prime powers by the line pigeonhole (every codeword's support is a union of full cosets of the order-q subgroup, whose exponents sit on one mod-q line, and such a line meets the degree triangle in at most D + 1 points); composites with qmin ≤ 7, with qmin² | d, or whose second prime exceeds 3(qmin − 1)/2 by the slice forcing (a codeword's mod-q slices are pairwise congruent mod the inner code, and the menu law with line counts forces a pure product of full q-gons); every two-prime d by the rook reduction and the staircase lemma; and everything with three or more prime factors, counted with multiplicity, by the gon theorem and the coset collapse (all below). The composite tax prices primality itself: at equal least prime, prime orders cost √-scale degree where composite orders pay their least prime linearly (5 against 25 and 35: degree 2 against 4; 13 against 143: 5 against 10) — degree reads compositeness where monomial count cannot, and 25-torsion enters exactly at quartics.

Scope. The even and other-odd prices are proved rules, field-free; the prime row is proved to be the cover number, whose values are a census (below). Verified end-to-end at six prime powers and thirteen composites, and reproduced at second primes.

The triangle cover number rule

For an odd prime d = q the code has dimension one, every nonzero codeword is a multiple of the full q-gon, so the triangle must cover all of Z/q: a modular postage-stamp problem, and its answer c(q) is bracketed between √(2q) − 3/2 and 2⌈√q⌉ − 1 (the count floor and an explicit construction), with c(q) ≤ q − 2 at every odd q. c(q) is exactly the minimal diameter of a double-loop network on Z/q — the least D such that some step pair reaches every residue as Ai + Bj with i + j ≤ D — whose literature (Wong–Coppersmith 1974; Fiol et al.) proves the sharper floor ⌈√(3q)⌉ − 2, and the census collapses onto it: of the 34 odd primes ≤ 149, 28 sit on that floor exactly (c(5) = 2, the pentagon's tightness, among them) and six sit one above. The excess over the count floor grows (+3 at q = 53, +4 at 149) and c is not monotone (c(107) = 16 < 17 = c(103)).

Scope. The reduction, the bracket and the double-loop identification proved; the ⌈√(3q)⌉ − 2 floor cited from the literature; the values an exhaustive census for q ≤ 149.

The rook reduction criterion

For a two-prime d = q·q′, an evader — a nonzero codeword inside a triangle exponent set at degree D ≤ q − 2 — has a rigid shape: with q′ prime the codeword's slices differ by constants, so the evader is a tensor-sum, a row term plus a column term on the q × q′ CRT grid, and one exists iff A, B are both units and the triangle's complement in the grid is rook-disconnected — two complement cells adjacent when they share a row or a column, the components grouping into matched row/column partitions with every off-diagonal block inside the triangle. Counting the blocks alone re-proves the slice-forcing boundary q′ > (3q − 4)/2, no prime living between it and 3(q − 1)/2, so the open zone is the pairs of close primes below it.

Scope. Proved; verified per scaling class against the kernel census at d = 143, 221 and 323.

The two-fat-slice exclusion rule

In the open zone the question is one lattice statement. Lay the intervals [−Bu, −Bu + u] for u = 0..D on Z/m, B a unit, and count each cell's covering multiplicity: at most s + 1 cells are covered D + 1 − s times or more, for every s < (D + 1)/2 — the lemma holds at every prime m, every D and every B, and fails past half-range. The weak form (at most 2s + 1 such cells) is pure counting and closes 46 of the 98 close pairs to q ≤ 97 on its own; the strong form reduces to two caps on one staircase's maximum and is proved by lattice counting — a shortest vector of the lattice {v ≡ Bu mod m}, Minkowski's bound and Brunn concavity for the generic direction, three bespoke families for the short ones — for m ≥ 213, and exhaustively below. Consequence: no covering system with two fat slices evades at any two-prime d, any D ≤ q − 2, so the composite row's price holds at every odd prime pair; the swept complements, rook-connected at every close pair to q ≤ 97 and every unit B, stand as verification.

Scope. Rule with complete coverage — analytic at m ≥ 213, exhaustive at every prime m ≤ 211, nothing conjectured; the word theorem is withheld only because the finite leg is computed.

verifiers: explore_staircase_reduction.py, explore_cover_exclusion.py

The gon theorem rule

For every odd d, a nonzero (Φd)-codeword of weight below 2qmin(d) is a scalar gon: one constant on one coset of a prime-order subgroup of Z/d. The proof is an induction over the largest prime's slices — a codeword's restrictions to the residue classes mod one prime q | d, each a vector of length d/q — with the code decomposing as a sum of translation-invariant cylinders, one per prime; a congruence among the slices within one coset carries the non-squarefree case. The classification is sharp: weight exactly 2qmin is achievable, and below it only the single-column and single-row profiles occur.

Scope. Proved at every odd modulus, field-free; checked exhaustively by value profile at two prime pairs and by kernel at a prime power and a mixed modulus.

The coset collapse rule

Everything past two prime factors closes by codeword geometry alone. An evader's qmin-slices differ pairwise by codewords of weight at most 2D + 2 < 2qmin, so by the gon theorem by scalar gons — and by multiples of one common gon, two different gons overrunning the weight budget — so its support collapses into a coset of a two-prime-order subgroup, which holds at most D + 1 < qmin triangle points, below the menu law's own minimum distance: the evader is zero. The collapse covers every odd d with three or more prime factors counted with multiplicity — 11·13² is inside — and qmin² ∤ d, at every D ≤ qmin − 2; at two primes the coset is everything, exactly where the rook machinery was needed. With it the composite row is closed: Dmin(d) = qmin(d) − 1 at every odd composite non-prime-power d, the q-gon entering at D = qmin − 1 exactly.

Scope. Proved, field-free; the kernel census empty at every scaling class of the six smallest moduli with a close second prime and a 300-class four-prime sample — 7,828 checks, no evader — with the gon entering one degree above by census.
