CASCADE — whether the greedy walk over a number ring must lock

The object: the greedy walk of GROWTH over the ideals of a ring of integers, where a trajectory locks iff its costs are bounded infinitely often. This page asks what a trajectory whose costs diverge would need, at each rank-1 characteristic p of the ring, where the need fails, and what a proof that it always fails must answer. Its verifier is cascade.py, with module_law.py for the unit groups at one place and growth.py for the moves over Z.

A place over p, of ramification index e and residue degree f, is rank-1 when e = f = 1, so its norm is p, its completion is Z_p and its 1-unit module has Z_p-rank ef = 1 (GROWTH: The module law); p is then a rank-1 characteristic of the ring. The level of a state at p is V = v_p(L), L the exponent of the unit group. A carrier at level V is a place H not over p with p^(V+1) dividing N(H) − 1, so that opening H lifts the level. The level's candidates are the numbers m·p^(V+1) + 1 with 1 ≤ m ≤ p − 1 at odd p, and 2^(V+1) + 1, 2^(V+2) + 1 and 3·2^(V+1) + 1 at p = 2: a carrier the walk opens has a candidate for its norm (The escape is a conjunction). A level is a dead level when no candidate is a prime power. D(p) is the first dead level a walk from level 1 meets. The carrier ladder at p is the sequence of levels a walk climbs at p; it dies at each dead level it meets, which only a pick at a place over p, a greedy move opening or deepening that place, crosses. The answer, in one line, for the ideal world: a trajectory that never locks must climb a carrier ladder forever at every rank-1 characteristic at once, meeting only finitely many dead levels at each (theorem); every ladder tried dies (rule, at 2 and every odd p < 1000), eleven die infinitely often (rule, proved by covering); and whether every ladder does is one question about primes in a progression, and sifting by the primes below 10⁴ empties the first dead level at no p from 100 to 1000 (observation).

The escape is a conjunction

Tier: theorem; observation (the mean τ_K over 305 fields, and the counts of possible norms, an estimate).
Verifier: proof; cascade.py::section_c, cascade.py::section_r, module_law.py.

Let P be rank-1 over p, at depth a in the state. Since λ(P^b), the exponent of the unit group of O/P^b (GROWTH's λ), is (p − 1)p^(b−1) at odd p, the move P^(V+2−a) raises L whatever else the state holds, at norm at most p^(V+2); at p = 2 the bound is 2^(V+3). The greedy move costs no more, so if costs diverge, the level diverges, and it does so at every rank-1 characteristic of the ring at once: each bounds the cost on its own at every step. One ladder dying infinitely often closes the ring's ideal world: it forces every trajectory there to lock.

The rises run through carriers. A rise through a place P′ over p, past the pump's threshold (GROWTH: The module law), leaves P′ at the least depth whose column count (CLOCK: A ring's door is one valuation and its clock a count) reaches v_p(L), so its next door is exactly e deep, at the constant p^(ef) of GROWTH's module law, and the next move costs at most that; infinitely many such rises would bound the costs. So along a divergent trajectory all but finitely many rises open a carrier, a rise through a place not over p being an opening, since deepening a place adds only its own characteristic to L, and a carrier opening the walk makes is a least move, so its norm N(H) is below p^(V+2) (2^(V+3) at p = 2) and, a power of another prime, never equal to it. At odd p the norm of a carrier the walk opens is therefore one of exactly p − 1 numbers, m·p^(V+1) + 1 with 1 ≤ m ≤ p − 1, the same candidates for every ring; which of the prime powers among them are norms of places depends on the ring, so no ring has more carrier norms than these. Such a carrier rises one level at odd p, so a dead level is crossed only by a pick at a place over p, which past the threshold makes the next move cost at most p^(ef); below it each place has finitely many picks, and a divergent trajectory has finitely many moves that cheap, so it crosses finitely many dead levels. At p = 2 a carrier can also rise two levels, from V to V + 2, but only through 2^(V+2) + 1, the first candidate of level V + 1 itself, so none jumps a dead level. Infinitely many dead levels at one rank-1 characteristic therefore lock every ideal-world trajectory of the ring; finitely many give this argument nothing.

Every seated characteristic climbs too. Let W = v_q(L) be the level at q. A seated place Y over q at exponent h has door μ_Y(W+1) − h, priced N(Y)^(μ_Y(W+1)−h), μ_Y(j) the least depth at which Y's column count reaches j (CLOCK: A ring's door is one valuation and its clock a count), and that price stands while v_q(L) = W stands, so on a trajectory that never locks, where the costs diverge, W diverges at q. An opening over a prime q not yet dividing L is that ladder at level 0, pending its own norm at once. A clock move at q past its place's tail leaves it pending its tail gap's price q^(ef) (CLOCK: The two numbers), so, as at a rank-1 characteristic, all but finitely many rises at q open a carrier for q, a place H not over q with q^(W+1) dividing N(H) − 1: the escape of CLOCK: A lidded block locks the walk for good, openings forever and each block clocked finitely often, is this conjunction and nothing more, the carrier ladder restated. At a characteristic with a rank-1 place the ceiling p^(V+2) (2^(V+3) at 2) binds; elsewhere the seated place's ceiling grows like q^(ef·W) past its tail, ef ≥ 2, so about q^((ef − 1)W) carrier norms are possible below it, against the rank-1 ladder's p − 1 candidates (3 at p = 2): the rank-1 ladders are the tight ones.

The element world, states and moves principal, keeps the ladder at a principal rank-1 place: P = (π), at depth a with V = v_p(L) the level at p, makes π^(V+2−a) an element (π^(V+3−a) at p = 2); a deepening of P′ padded by an ideal in the inverse class still costs a bounded amount; and a raising element below p^(V+2) (2^(V+3) at 2) holds one carrier at most, since each carrier's norm exceeds p^(V+1), so two cost more than p^(2V+2), at least that ceiling except at p = 2, V = 0, where two odd norms cost at least 9 > 8. An element walk whose costs diverge climbs the ideal world's ladder at every principal rank-1 characteristic; one whose costs are bounded infinitely often reaches a lidded block and makes finitely many openings (ELEMENT: The element lid). At a non-principal rank-1 place the door's price is padded by the least norm in a class, and the largest of those over the classes, τ_K, is an invariant of the field K: over an imaginary quadratic field it is the largest leading coefficient of a reduced form, at most the square root of a third of the field's absolute discriminant, and 1 exactly at class number 1. Q(√−23) has τ_K = 2 against a Minkowski bound of 3.05. Over the 305 fields of discriminant down to −1000 the mean τ_K is 9.58; τ_K is unbounded over the fields, since class numbers grow without bound while a discriminant has at most A² + 2A reduced forms of leading coefficient at most A, the first two coefficients fixing the third, so the loosened ceiling grows with the field and no sweep of fields bounds it; the ideal world needs only a rank-1 characteristic, the element world one whose place is principal.

The budget inequality

Tier: theorem (the rise through a carrier the walk opens is one level at odd p and at most two at 2); rule (the walks at 3 and 5, exhaustive to their dead levels, and at 2 the two recorded walks, through 9 or through 5 first, to its dead level).
Verifier: proof; cascade.py::section_b, cascade.py::section_c.

At odd p the norm of a carrier the walk opens, less one, is below p^(V+2) and divisible by p^(V+1), so every rise through such a carrier is exactly one level: the ceiling is pinned and no carrier the walk opens buys a second level. At p = 2 the extra Z/2 of the units of Z₂ widens the ceiling to 2^(V+3); the candidates are 2^(V+1) + 1, 2^(V+2) + 1 and 3·2^(V+1) + 1, and the middle one rises two levels. A prime power 2^k + 1 is a Fermat prime or 9 (Mihăilescu's theorem), so the double rise is rationed by the Fermat primes: for k ≤ 300 the prime powers of that form are exactly 3, 5, 9, 17, 257 and 65537. A norm is a prime power, so 9 counts in a ring with a place of degree 2 over 3. Walking each level to the prime-power candidate that climbs furthest, 2 climbs through 9, 17, 97, 193 and 257 to level 8 and finds nothing: 513, 1025 and 1537 are composite. Taking the least norm instead, it climbs through 5, 9, 17, 97, 193 and 257 to the same level, since no carrier steps over a dead level. 3 dies at level 2 and 5 at 3.

The characteristic sweep

Tier: rule (verified every odd p < 1000).
Verifier: cascade.py::section_s.

Each odd characteristic is an O(p) computation per level, and all 167 odd primes below 1000 reach a dead level, certified by Miller–Rabin at the primes to 41. Its composite verdicts are proofs, so every level it calls dead is dead; its prime verdicts are proofs only below 3.3·10²⁴, and past that a false one could only lengthen a ladder, so each D(p) printed is proved an upper bound. The dead level is erratic: D(19) = D(23) = 1, D(17) = 2 beside D(29) = 8, and D(751) ≤ 33 beside the largest printed, D(719) ≤ 62, bounds whose ladders pass 3.3·10²⁴. An odd multiplier makes the candidate even, so it could carry only as a power of 2, and none does: the working set is the ⌊(p − 1)/2⌋ even multipliers. A single dead level can be crossed, so the sweep is evidence about the ladders and closes no ring; the rings closed here are closed by the recurrences below.

Dead levels recur at eleven characteristics

Tier: rule (proved by covering at 2 and at ten odd p; the walks verified from every start to 300 at 2, 3 and 5).
Verifier: cascade.py::section_k.

A trajectory crosses finitely many dead levels, so what closes a ring is a rank-1 characteristic with infinitely many. A prime q other than p divides m·p^(V+1) + 1 for one class of m, periodic in V with period the order of p mod q. So a set of primes whose classes cover every even multiplier at one level covers them every lcm of their orders, and from level p − 2 on, where the exponent identity (The shape of a dead level) rules out proper powers, each such level is dead. At 3 the prime 7 kills every level with V + 1 ≡ 1 mod 6; at 37 nine primes up to 67 kill one level in 1,680. At 2, 3 kills 2^(V+1) + 1, 17 kills 2^(V+2) + 1 and 5 kills 3·2^(V+1) + 1 whenever V + 1 ≡ 3 mod 8, from V + 1 = 11 on, and no carrier jumps it (The escape is a conjunction). None of the three is a proper power from V + 1 = 11 on: 2^k + 1 is one only at 9 (Mihăilescu's theorem), and 3·2^k + 1 = 5^s forces s = 2, k = 3, since at odd s, 5^s − 1 ≡ 4 mod 8 forces k = 2 and 13 is no power of 5, and at s = 2t the factor 5^t + 1, twice an odd number, must be 6. A walk at odd p rises one level at a time, so none steps over a dead level. Coverings exist at 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 31 and 37 and at no other odd p below 1000 within the search (primes below 2,000 whose order of p divides 5,040), and all 109 levels a covering kills that were checked, to level 200 at odd p and 400 at 2, are dead; at 13, 17, 31 and 37 the first such level past p − 2 lies beyond 200, so there the covering is the proof alone. A ring with a rank-1 characteristic among those eleven has its ideal world lock from every seed; from every start to 300 the walks at 2, 3 and 5 span at most 8, 4 and 3 levels, the start's included. A ring is closed this way only if one of its rank-1 characteristics has a known covering: they are read prime by prime off how each prime splits, but Q(√(2·3···37)) has none below 53, and no covering was found at any odd p from 41 to 997, so no claim on this page closes that ring.

The residual is inhabited at every bound

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; cascade.py::section_q prints every odd prime y ≤ 97.

The residual at a bound y is the rings none of whose rank-1 characteristics lies at or below y, the rings a sweep of the characteristics up to y cannot close. For every prime y, the field Q(√(2·3·5···y)) has every prime up to y in its discriminant, so every one ramifies and none is rank-1: its least rank-1 characteristic exceeds y (103 at y = 97). No sweep of characteristics empties the residual; raising the sweep raises the bar.

What suffices is weaker than every ladder dying infinitely often. A ring is closed as soon as one rank-1 characteristic is, and the rank-1 characteristics of a degree-n field have density at least 1/n, by Chebotarev's density theorem: a Frobenius fixes one of the n roots with probability at least 1/n, the stabilizer of a root having index n. So if the odd primes with finitely many dead levels have density zero, every ring's ideal world locks from every seed, a conditional proved from Chebotarev's theorem. A density cannot bound where the first rank-1 characteristic sits: a density ignores every finite set of primes, and the primorial field's first rank-1 characteristic lies past all the primes it ramifies.

The shape of a dead level

Tier: theorem (the identity and the one-class count, for every odd p and level); rule (the enumeration of proper power candidates, verified every level of every odd p < 1000); observation (the sieve at 10⁴ for every p from 100 to 1000, and the pooled densities).
Verifier: cascade.py::section_x.

The exponent identity. A proper power candidate r^s at level V ≥ 1 of an odd p has s equal to the order of r mod p^(V+1), s dividing p − 1, r < p and s ≥ V + 2. For if the order d were a proper divisor of s, r^s would exceed p^(2V+2); if p divided s, r^(s/p) ≡ 1 mod p^V would put r^s above p^(V+2); and p^(V+1) dividing (r^s − 1)/(r − 1), with p ∤ r − 1 since s | p − 1 and s ≥ 2, forces r < p + 1. So no proper power is a candidate at V ≥ p − 2, and below that they are enumerable: over every level of every odd p < 1000 the one is 3⁵ = 2·11² + 1 at p = 11, V = 1. A dead level is a finite check plus one question: is there a prime ≡ 1 mod p^(V+1) below p^(V+2)?

The sieve's expected count does not see the level. A prime q other than p kills one class of m at every level, so the multipliers surviving a sieve below z number about ((p − 1)/2)∏(1 − 1/q) over the odd primes q < z other than p, with no V in it, while in the prime number theorem's model primes in the range thin like 1/(V + 2). Pooled over the 144 primes with D(p) ≥ 10, the density of prime-power candidates falls to 0.245 times its level-1 value by level 10, against the model's 1/4. The pool is chosen for a carrier at every level from 1 to 9, so the ratio's denominator, the level-1 count, is selected on and its level-10 count is not; and its prime verdicts on candidates past 3.3·10²⁴, met at every pooled p from 113 on, are probable, not proved. Sifted density changes by a factor 1.025 and sits at Mertens's value 2·exp(−γ)/ln z = 0.1219 at z = 10⁴ (γ Euler's constant). Covering congruences act through the same classes: sifting below 10⁴ empties the first dead level D(p) at no p from 100 to 1000; a covering that closed a level would be an alignment of its classes against one interval, and averaging over levels cannot help a bound with no V in it. What would decide the question is the count of primes ≡ 1 mod p^(V+1) below p^(V+2), a range linear in the modulus. The model expects about p/((V + 2) ln p) of them, tending to 0, so it predicts, as a heuristic carrying no tier, that every ladder dies infinitely often; but even the generalized Riemann hypothesis places a prime in the progression only below about the modulus squared.

The full-price opening at odd p is a dead level over Z

Tier: criterion (proved both ways); rule (the agreement, compared at every level holding a proper power candidate, at every odd p < 1000, the only levels where the two can differ; every D(p) a full-price opening).
Verifier: cascade.py::section_w, growth.py.

Over Z a least move at an odd p can be an opening at full price: p prime to the state's modulus M, p dividing L and p − 1 dividing L, at p^(V+2) (GROWTH: The lock-prime law). It is the least move from some state iff no prime ≡ 1 mod p^(V+1) lies below p^(V+2). Only if: such a prime r either divides M, lifting v_p(L) past V, or opens at its own door 1, price r, since r − 1 does not divide L. If: let B be the lcm of u − 1 over the primes u below p^(V+2) other than p, of p − 1, and of each prime s below p^(V+2) other than p to its least power s^c with s^(c+2) above p^(V+2) (2^(c+3) at s = 2), so that its own least move costs more than p^(V+2), with its p-part set to exactly p^V, which the hypothesis allows since every such u − 1 has v_p at most V; a prime S ≡ 1 mod B with v_p(S − 1) = V exactly exists by Dirichlet's theorem, in any class 1 + kB mod pB with p ∤ k, and from the seed S every cheaper move is shut. S itself costs more, since B exceeds p^(V+2): 2^c, above p^(V+2)/8, and p^V divide it, which settles every level with p^V ≥ 8, and the levels (3, 1), (5, 1) and (7, 1) hold the primes 19, 101 and 197 ≡ 1 mod p^(V+1), outside the hypothesis. Every prime above p^(V+2) costs more as well. So the least move opens p at full price. At p = 3, V = 2 the least such S is 961440481, the seed GROWTH's lock-prime law shows opening 3 at 81.

So the full-price opening over Z at level V and V being dead read one predicate at odd p, save at a level holding a proper power candidate and no prime one; the exponent identity rules such a level out at V ≥ p − 2, and over every level of every odd p < 1000 there is none: the one proper power candidate, 3⁵ at (11, 1), shares its level with the prime 727. Every dead level D(p) at odd p below 1000 is a full-price opening over Z, 3's dead level 2 being the opening at 81 above: at odd p the full-price opening, the fourth kind of least move over Z, is the cascade's dead level read in Z, compared at every level of every odd p < 1000 where the two can differ.
