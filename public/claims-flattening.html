Flattening

  • In the charted corner the minimum is a pure-product height, on both dials rule — At all 63 cells with M = 4 to 12 the least height h(M, J) equals the least height of an admissible pure product, and the height-1 row of the degree dial is the classical table, every value attained by a pure product with distinct parts. — explore_flatten_height.py, explore_flatten_offchart.py
  • Product extremality at a second height rule — The least degree of a height-2 polynomial flattened to depth J runs 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16, 22, 26 at J = 1 to 8, each the least degree of a pure product of height at most 2, the extremal unique up to sign at J = 2 to 7. — explore_flatten_d2.py
  • Two floors beneath both dials, at no compute property — A cyclotomic floor on dk and a pigeonhole floor giving h(M, J) = 1 whenever 2M exceeds the product of C(M, j+1) + 1 over j < J are both proved and both weak, the second growing as √(2M/log₂ M). — explore_flatten_d2.py, explore_flatten_height.py
  • Off the corner the law fails, and the instrument that shows it is the lattice rule — Over the 695 cells with M = 4 to 40 and J = 2 to 30, h is a pure-product height at 612 and strictly below one at 83, the failures 0.8% of cells at J ≤ 12, 9.0% at J = 13 to 20 and 43.4% at J ≥ 21. — explore_flatten_lattice.py, explore_flatten_select.py, explore_flatten_height.py
  • At rank 1 the two bounds coincide by construction property — At rank 1 the lattice is generated by (x−1)M−1 alone, so h(M, M−1) = C(M−1, ⌊(M−1)/2⌋) and the only admissible pure product is the same polynomial; a rank-1 cell cannot fail. — explore_flatten_lattice.py

Where the products lose

  • The rank window is the chart's corner crossing a per-rank depth threshold rule — Every one of the 83 failures has rank 5 to 18, and those are exactly the ranks whose first failing depth Jlo(r) is at most min(30, 40 − r), so the window is the chart's rectangle crossing a per-rank threshold. — explore_flatten_select.py, explore_flatten_band.py
  • The low wall is a real absence, far wider than the chart rule — Ranks 1 to 4 fail at no depth from 2 to 45, ranks 2 to 4 at none to depth 140, and rank 2 at no depth at all by theorem. — explore_flatten_band.py
  • The high wall is not a wall rule — Ranks 19 to 29 all fail, first at depths 26, 23, 25, 30, 22, 29, 31, 27, 34, 31 and 25, just outside the chart's corner, and the first failing depth is not monotone in the rank. — explore_flatten_band.py, explore_flatten_class.py, explore_flatten_gen.py
  • The band is a containment, ragged at both edges, and its far end is where the champion takes over rule — At ranks 5 to 8 the failing depths lie inside [Jlo, Jhi] without filling it, and the depth from which the champion's product attains h is Jhi + 1 exactly at all four ranks. — explore_flatten_band.py, explore_flatten_deep.py
  • Inside the window the depth drives the rate, and nothing else selects rule — Of the 370 window cells 83 fail, 1.9%, 15.2% and 60.6% by depth band; of eight cheap cell quantities scored as one-sided cuts, seven predict that nobody fails and the eighth buys 7 of the 83. — explore_flatten_select.py
  • The lattice's own geometry does not know which cells fail observation — The ratio λ2/λ1 of the lattice's first two successive minima separates failing from clean cells at only two of the fourteen failing ranks, its values 1.00 to 1.71 with no trend in the rank. — explore_flatten_near.py
  • The mechanism is the vanishing order at −1, and it names a closed form rule — Where the vanishing order at −1 binds, h(M, J) is the height of (1+x)r−1(x−1)J with no search, attained from J = 7 and 13 through 140 at ranks 3 and 4 and past the far end at ranks 5 to 8. — explore_flatten_band.py, explore_flatten_theorem.py
  • At rank 2 the minimum is a difference of binomial coefficients theorem — For every J ≥ 2, h(J+2, J) is the largest difference of adjacent entries of the binomial row C(J, k), the minimiser ±(1+x)(x−1)J alone, and the second minimum is C(J, ⌊J/2⌋). — explore_flatten_theorem.py
  • At low rank the champion's threshold is decided inside the pure family, and the winner below it has the wrong order rule — At ranks 3 and 4 the champion is the least pure cofactor at every depth from J = 7 and 13 on, the thresholds the lattice scans measured, and not at 6 and 12, where 1 + x and (1 + x)(1 + x + x²) win with the wrong vanishing order. — explore_flatten_theorem.py, explore_flatten_band.py
  • At a rank that fails, the champion is not what the near end is about observation — Of the 82 window failures at J ≥ r − 1 only 16 have the champion in the pure argmin set, and the 119 cells with J < r − 1, where it is not a pure product, carry exactly one failure. — explore_flatten_near.py

The multipliers

  • The residual is the failure flag, and a class of five residuals beats the products on the chart rule — Dividing each of the 695 exhibited minimisers by its cyclotomic factors leaves five residuals, 1 at 612 cells and A, B, A·B and C at 51, 29, 2 and 1, so the four non-trivial residuals cover exactly the 83 failing cells. — explore_flatten_select.py
  • The swap is a construction, and it builds the exhibited minimiser itself rule — The swap family, every admissible pure cofactor with a sub-multiset of its cyclotomic factors traded for members of equal degree, has least height equal to h at all 83 failing cells with no lattice reduction in it. — explore_flatten_near.py, explore_flatten_swap.py
  • Past the corner the class grows, one member at a time, and the count is seven observation — Walking each rank's column past its first failing cell mints four members off the chart, D, E, F and G, one at a time, the count seven, each admission dropping hs to h exactly. — explore_flatten_swap.py, explore_flatten_class.py, explore_flatten_gen.py
  • The members sit in the unit-circle class, which Kronecker makes a class, and E leaves it property — Every chart residual is reciprocal with all roots on the unit circle, non-monic as Kronecker forces; the class holds the infinite family Wc = c + (2c−1)x + cx², and E has four of its eight roots off the circle. — explore_flatten_deep.py, explore_flatten_gen.py
  • Every minimiser's residual is 1 at −1 from height 4 up, and not below rule — At every one of the 462 chart cells with h ≥ 4 every minimiser's residual takes the value 1 at −1; sixteen cells at h = 1, 2 or 3 carry a residual with another value. — explore_flatten_endvalue.py, explore_flatten_recur.py
  • Both end-value patterns died at the seventh member, and the value at 1 could never have been a law observation — A(1) to F(1) are prime and A(−1) to F(−1) are 1, and G breaks both with G(1) = 26 and G(−1) = 2; the rule at −1 is false at h ≤ 3 and off the chart, so no hypothesis in the height alone states it. — explore_flatten_deep.py, explore_flatten_gen.py
  • The law is about attainment, and the collected set adds one polynomial observation — h is attained inside the class at all 695 cells, but over the 442 cells at h ≥ 5 the whole minimiser sets exhibit six residual classes, the sixth S = 1 + 2x + 4x² + 5x³ + 4x⁴ + 2x⁵ + x⁶, monic and irreducible, at one cell. — explore_flatten_family.py, explore_flatten_endvalue.py, explore_flatten_recur.py
