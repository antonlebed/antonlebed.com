The blind spot

The probe a grown world holds over its own period is coarse: what telling two merged states apart costs, which primes it cannot see arrive, and the largest blind class its census finds — counted, characterized, proved infinite, and proved a fixed share of the primes.

The worlds here are grown by a growth law: extend the modulus N by the least m ≥ 2 meeting a structural demand, and read each state N through its period λ(N), the exponent of the unit group of Z/N. The wall W(L) is the largest modulus whose period divides L: 2 when L is odd, 2v₂(L)+2 when L is even, times pvp(L)+1 over the odd primes p with (p−1) | L, the doors of L. A probe that reads a state only through whether an offered move leaves its period fixed sees one number, the transparency headroom V(N) = W(λ(N))/N, since the moves that do — the transparent ones — are exactly the divisors of V(N) (the headroom ledger). Two states with one headroom are a blind pair, and the states sharing one value form a blind class. The probe's other percept is the premium G′ of a move, the integer factor by which the headroom rises after the move's burn — equivalently the wall gain W(λ(Nm))/W(λ(N)) over the unpaid part m/gcd(m, V).

The price of telling two states apart

The resolution floor theorem

A transparent move keeps a blind pair blind with certainty — the wall gain is 1 exactly when the multiplier divides V — so the transparent moves are at once the wall-freezing and the λ-freezing ones, and resolution is confined to the non-divisors. Blindness is entirely a cross-λ phenomenon: λ and V determine N, so the probe is injective on each λ-fibre, and a multiplier d tells a blind pair apart iff the two premiums differ, for every d, with no coprimality hypothesis. Since every such resolver is a non-divisor of V,

least resolver  ≥  floor⁡(V)  :=  min⁡q primeq vq(V)+1,\text{least resolver} \;\ge\; \operatorname{floor}(V) \;:=\; \min_{q\ \text{prime}} q^{\,v_q(V)+1} ,

the least non-divisor of V — always a prime power, since a least non-divisor pam with m > 1 coprime to p has both parts smaller, so both divide V, so their coprime product does too. The bound is a function of the reading alone: a probe that cannot tell which state it is in computes a sharp lower bound on its own cheapest way of finding out, knowing neither state. V = 24 gives 5, and the pair 10, 11 resolves at exactly 5. The floor is usually the whole price — 90 of the 96 blind pairs below 1500 attain it — and the residue is what the reading cannot supply, namely which blind partner is on the other side: 34 of the 52 classes with three or more members carry more than one least resolver across their pairs.

Scope. The criterion and the floor are proved and checked on 5664 (pair, move) and 23482 (state, move) cases; the attainment rate is measured over the pairs below 1500 with multipliers under 60, nothing censored under a search to 200, the six misses landing on 3, 4 or 7. The floor is not a benchmark ceiling: a ceiling is an expectation over an ensemble computed by a designer outside the ignorance priced, where this is a per-instance bound computed by the ignorant party from a strictly poorer percept.

The Proth window observation

The floor's prime-power shape does not transfer to the price. The pair 211, 212, blind at V = 65535, survives every multiplier up to 27 and splits first at 28 = 2²·7, where 12289 = 3·212 + 1 becomes a door at the deeper state only. On adjacent powers of two the whole question is primality at exact depths: a move 2x·m resolves the pair at depths a, a+1 exactly when some u·2a−1+x + 1 is prime with u dividing the odd part of λ(m) — Fermat primes when the move is a pure power of two, Proth primes otherwise — so the least resolver runs 2, 7, 4, 2, 31, 28, 14, 7, 8, 4, 2 across the pairs a = 4 to 16, composite at a = 11 and 12, and its growth along the family reduces to Proth-prime gaps.

Scope. The witness is an exact finite check (every multiplier to 200) and the window table exact within the same cap; the mechanism is a derivation confirmed cell by cell, its reduction to Proth gaps a reading rather than a claim.

The threshold is unbounded theorem

floor(V) grows with the divisibility of V, and the walls are the supply of divisible readings, so unboundedness reduces to occupancy: do arbitrarily divisible readings host blind pairs? The silent-set theorem below answers it — at every even L infinitely many primes read W(L) — and along L = lcm(1, …, n) every prime up to n+1 is a door of L whose own term in the floor exceeds n times the door, so floor(W(L)) > n+1. The classes enumerate exactly — V(N) = V₀ says N = W(λ(N))/V₀ — and every member of the class of reading W(L₀) has λ divisible by an explicit base, so a sweep over base multiples reaches periods in the billions: readings designed to carry floors 47 and 81 both host blind pairs, on states of up to 453 digits, resolving at exactly their floor.

Scope. The unboundedness is proved on the silent-set theorem, so on Bombieri–Vinogradov and nothing conjectural; the ladder base is a theorem; occupancy above 80% among base multiples and the floor's attainment at all eleven populated targets are measured, through floor 81.

The watcher's position theorem

No pair is blind forever: a blind pair's walls differ, and a prime q ≡ 1 mod lcm(λ₁, λ₂) drives both states to period q−1, making the walls agree while the two readings cannot — so the resolving set is never empty, on Dirichlet and nothing stronger. That buys certainty, not economy: the constructed q runs to 111827 over the pairs below 1500 while every pair is in fact resolved by some multiplier under 60. Which is why the harder position is the watcher's, who takes the move it is given and cannot go looking, against the chooser, who picks. A watcher wins on m iff some divisor of m resolves, so every blind pair survives at least τ(V) − 1 moves with certainty — one per divisor of the reading above 1, τ the divisor count — and a chooser never picks one; over the pool a watcher is told the pair apart on 75.1% of the moves to 60 where a chooser wins on every pair. Reading inside a move is the same faculty: of the (blind pair, splittable move) cases that stay blind across a whole move, 5.7% are split by an intermediate reading, which is exactly the failure of the resolving set to be an up-set — a resolver d whose multiple dk is not one, the pair 9, 513 under 57 the first witness of both. Choosing when to read and choosing what to play are two faculties the word observer bundles into one.

Scope. Non-permanence is proved, 96 of 96 pairs below 1500; the certain survival count is proved; the watcher's rate, the slow-motion rate and the up-set failures (138 of 5964 tests) are measured over the pool. The resolving set is a union of λ-fibres (theorem, 1533 of 1533) and nothing tidier.

The primes the probe cannot see arrive

Silence is blindness theorem

A fresh prime q with premium 1 is not transparent in two ways. Behind an open door it is trivial: λ is frozen and V(Nq) = V(N)/q. Behind a shut door a premium of 1 says the wall gain is exactly q, which holds iff the primes newly admitted to the wall are {q} alone with no door already open bumping its exponent — the silent import, which shifts λ and leaves V untouched (λ = 2 under q = 11: wall 24 → 264), invisible to the probe without being transparent. Silence says W(lcm(λ, q−1)) = q·W(λ), which forces every door of q−1 but q to be a door of g = gcd(q−1, λ) at g's own exponent: every prime silent at λ is a member of the blind class V = W(g) of the part of λ it shares, and of the class W(λ) exactly when λ | q−1. The converse fails — 11 reads 24 = W(gcd(10, 8)) and is not silent at 8, since 8·5 + 1 = 41 is prime. At λ = 2 the silent set is the class V = 24; at every other λ the silent primes off the class are the majority, 55% at λ = 6 to 98% at λ = 60, most of them members of V = 24 carrying the extra demands the even divisors of λ add. At an odd λ nothing is silent, a fresh odd prime lifting the wall's 2-part from 2 to at least 8.

Scope. The silent import's criterion is proved and checked on 1103 open-door and 436 shut-door imports; the blindness of silent primes is proved and checked over 25 even periods to 60 against every fresh prime below 10⁵, 0 exceptions.

The silent-set theorem theorem

The silent set is infinite at every even λ: the primes q ≡ 1 (mod λ) with V(q) = W(λ) — the blind class of λ's own wall — number ≫ x/log²x below x, the constant depending on λ, and those with (q−1)/λ composite are ≫ x/log²x by themselves. The route is the blind-spot sieve below run on the cofactor m = (q−1)/λ, now asked to be odd, prime to every door of λ, and to have e·f + 1 composite for every even divisor e of λ and every divisor f > 1 of m short of the pair (λ, m) — an odd e costs nothing, ef + 1 being even. Each odd door of λ costs the constant a factor and each even divisor of λ is one more demand on a composite cofactor: at 10⁷ the composite-cofactor share reads 0.571 at λ = 2 and 0.000 at λ = 48 and 60, so at the multiples of 12 the class is almost all prime cofactors.

Scope. Theorem at every even λ, on Bombieri–Vinogradov; the census by λ below 10⁷ checks the proved statement and reads the constants, over 25 even periods to 60.

How coarse the probe is

The blindness census observation

The fraction of states below a cap that share their headroom with another state falls at every cap tried — 39.2% below 1500, 26.8% below 192000 — while the probe's resolution, distinct readings as a fraction of states, improves only from 67.2% to 75.3%. The shape is close to c/log x and not quite it: the fraction times ln(cap) creeps 2.87 → 3.26, so the decay runs a shade slower than 1/log; that form vanishes, a slow approach to a small positive limit fits the same points, and nothing here decides between them. The largest blind class below 6000 is V = 24, holding 178 states, 101 of them prime, and its prime part is characterized exactly: a prime q > 5 lies in it iff no prime outside {2, 3, q} has its predecessor dividing q−1 and 3 does not divide q−1 — equivalently W(q−1) = 24q. The safe primes q = 2r+1 with r prime ≥ 5 are a proper sub-family: 81 of the 101 are safe and 20 are not, and the two smallest safe primes, 5 and 7, fall outside the class.

Scope. The census is measured over states below six caps; the characterization of the prime part is proved, and the class's 77 composite members lie outside it.

verifiers: explore_headroom.py, explore_premium.py

The blind-spot theorem theorem

The class V = 24 is infinite, and no conjecture enters: the primes q with V(q) = 24 number ≫ x/log²x below x, and the non-safe members — 239, 443, 647 leading, (q−1)/2 composite — are ≫ x/log²x by themselves. The route is the characterization in divisor terms: with q−1 = 2m, the primes q > 5 with m odd and prime to 3 are the eligible primes, and the class is those with 2e+1 composite for every divisor e of m with 1 < e < m, a divisor with 2e+1 prime being a bad divisor — an odd cofactor with a finite list of compositeness demands, which is what a sieve counts. The linear sieve's lower bound at the level the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem supplies — primes in arithmetic progressions equidistributed on average to modulus x1/2 less logarithms — gives ≫ x/log²x primes q ≤ x with (q−1)/2 free of prime factors below x1/10; the ones among them that some prime 2e+1 cuts out number O(x log log x/log³x), by an upper-bound sieve on e, 2e+1 and q at once; and prescribing m a prime factor in [x1/10, x1/4] leaves m composite but for O(x1/4) of them. The safe primes were one route to the class and never the class itself: the Sophie Germain question is untouched. Below 10⁷ the class holds 71,419 primes, 30,655 safe and 40,764 not, the non-safe share rising 0.29, 0.41, 0.51, 0.57 across the decades from 10⁴; and the fraction the proof sends to zero reads 0.57 at sifting range 5 and 0.19 at 47 there, so that limit is asymptotic.

Scope. Theorem, resting on the census's proved characterization; the counts below 10⁷ check proved statements rather than supporting them.

The blind-share theorem theorem

The bound is a floor and not the order: at λ = 2 and at every even λ the class numbers ≫ x/(log x·log log x) below x, the constant depending on λ, so N·log²x/x grows without bound. It is the same sieve with its sifting range lowered from x1/10 to z = (log x)C, which leaves ≫ x/(log x·log z) cofactors free of prime factors below z; the members some prime 2e+1 cuts out are bounded by a union over the divisor e, and that union is where the one factor log log x is paid — it sums the mean number of bad divisors over such a cofactor, which grows like log log x/log z where the typical number is bounded, so z must be a power of log x for the mean to stay small. The census reads N·log²x/x as 1.35, 1.50, 1.67, 1.85 across the decades to 10⁷ and N/(x/(log x·log log x)) flat at 0.325, 0.319, 0.318, 0.320.

Scope. Theorem; the flat census fits the proved bound as an order over three decades, and the order is settled one level up by the density theorem.

The blind-density theorem theorem

The order is x/log x: the class is a fixed share of the primes, N ~ δ·π(x) with δ > 0, π(x) counting the primes below x, at λ = 2 and at every even λ. The route runs through the Bernoulli numbers: the denominator of B2k is the product of the primes p with (p−1) | 2k (von Staudt–Clausen), so for q ≥ 11 the class is exactly the primes with denom(Bq−1) = 6q — 5 and 7 alone have the denominator without the headroom, 4 | q−1 lifting the wall's 2-part to 16 at q = 5 and 3 | q−1 its 3-part to 9 at q = 7, prime powers a denominator made of primes never sees. Erdős and Wagstaff proved the integers m with denom(B2m) = 6 to have positive density, the integers with a divisor p−1 > T being a share at most ε(T) → 0 of all; the transfer to the primes q = 2m+1 runs their argument with every sieve step supplied by Pollack's Shiu bound over a sifted set, so the primes q with (p−1) | (q−1) for some prime p > T, p ≠ q, are in the limit a share at most ε(T) ≪ (log T)−c of the primes; and positivity is Harris's inequality — the exponent of each prime in q−1 is independent across primes by Dirichlet, avoiding a divisor p−1 is a decreasing event in those exponents, so the finitely many conditions below T keep a positive density and the tail costs at most the factor 1 − ε(T). At general λ the class conditions read, within q ≡ 1 (mod λ), as bounded exponents on λ's own doors and (p−1) ∤ (q−1) off them, decreasing events both. The theorem withholds δ: every truncation at a finite T is a ceiling on it — 0.109 at T = 10⁶ from the primes to 10⁸ — and the class's share of the eligible primes still falls, 0.49 → 0.42 across the decades to 10⁸, the theorem's c being 0.0066, a rate no census sees. The product ∏(1 − 1/φ(p−1)) an independence bound would give crosses below the class share at T = 23: independence was never the mechanism; the gap between the divergent mean and the bounded typical count was.

Scope. Theorem; the exact truncated densities agree with the empirical shares to 5·10−5 at every T ≤ 199 on the primes to 10⁸; the density δ is proved positive and not estimated, and the integer share reads 0.149 at 5·10⁷ and falling, Erdős–Wagstaff's "near 1/6" being a reading at 5·10⁴.

The divisor model observation

The bad-divisor heuristic written as a model — the bad-divisor events independent across the divisors of m, each priced at the sieve's chance that 2e+1 is prime, of order 1/log e — predicts the census within 0.3% at every decade to 10⁷. Read beyond the census on random integers m drawn with their factorizations, weighted and sieved to the residue law of a shifted prime — an instrument good to 3% — it has the class's share of the eligible primes falling 0.42 → 0.31 from 10⁷ to 10⁵⁰ while N/(x/(log x·log log x)) rises only 0.29 → 0.37: the flat census is the model's own prediction through forty more decades. The model reads the approach to δ and not δ; the slow decay like (log x)−0.16 its sample could not separate from a positive proportion is excluded by the density theorem.

Scope. The match to the census is an observation on the actual primes; the reading past 10⁷ is a sampled model's and no measurement.

The tombstones are a blind class theorem

A second infinite blind spot needs no sieve. V = 1 is the class of closed states, those with no transparent move left, where the mortal fate — the growth fate that halts — dies; W(L) is closed for every L, and W(2k) ≥ 2k+2 is unbounded. Every wall reads exactly like every other wall, and there are infinitely many walls.

Scope. Proved from the wall's definition.
