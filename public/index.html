The Primorial Tower

The algebra of the Eratosthenes sieve

The sieve of Eratosthenes finds primes by removing multiples: cross out multiples of 2, then 3, then 5, and so on. Each step is a projection onto a prime field. The sieve has an algebraic form — the primorial tower — and that form gives you structure for free.

The tower

Multiply the first k primes. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the resulting ring decomposes into k independent channels, each a prime field. Every rung adds one prime, one channel, one sieve step.

k = 7 is the demo rung throughout this site. Every rung: all channels are fields, arithmetic is bijective, meadow structure holds.

What the tower gives you

Independent channels. Knowing a number's remainder mod 2 tells you nothing about its remainder mod 3, or any other prime. Zero mutual information — a theorem, not an approximation. One problem with 510,510 possibilities becomes seven problems with at most 17 each.

Built-in error correction. From k = 7 onward, any (k−3) of k channels reconstruct the original element. Maximum distance separable — no code with those parameters can do better. No separate encoding layer. The ring is the code.

Prime prediction. Build a sub-ring from a few primes. Its Euler characteristic names primes the sub-ring has never met. At k = 7, fourteen of fifteen primes after the 7th are predicted. The tower sees further as it climbs.

Explore

Everything on this site is computed live in your browser. Type a number and watch it decompose into 7 channels. Corrupt a channel and watch the ring heal it. Trace power-map orbits. Pick primes and see what they predict.
