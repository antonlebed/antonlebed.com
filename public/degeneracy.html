The degenerate regime

At some cubic fields every totally split prime carries one ideal class three times over; at the rest the three classes range freely. What the regime IS, is the three classes agreeing modulo the cubes, a subgroup whose index is 3 to the 3-rank, so that above 3-rank 1 one word covers two regimes; what sorts a field into it is the conductor of the field over its quadratic resolvent, read as a congruence on the discriminant, and the index of the realized subgroup is 3 to the number of lines that conductor carries; and what it does to the counts is a shortfall that deepens with the class number, which one term of the explicit formula puts back flat.

The objects. A cubic field K has a finite class group Cl(K) of order h, the class number: its ideals taken modulo those generated by a single element, the principal ones, the class of an ideal being its ideal class. A rational prime is totally split in K when it factors into three places whose residue fields are the prime field, partially split when it carries one such place and one of residue degree 2, and inert when it carries none; a place with the prime field as residue field is a degree-1 place. The three places over a totally split prime carry classes summing to the class of the prime itself, which is trivial, so write M for the group of triples in Cl(K)³ summing to zero, of order h², and R for the subgroup of M the realized triples fill — a subgroup, because the triple is a Frobenius, the Galois-group element a prime determines up to conjugacy, and on the primes in question the map to the three classes is a homomorphism (The split triple). The nominal share of an event is what a uniform triple on M gives it. A class number is read here as a relation class number, the order of the group the relations found generate, which the true one divides. The 3-rank r of the class group is the number of its cyclic factors whose order 3 divides, equivalently the dimension of Cl/3Cl over the field of three elements, 3Cl being the subgroup of cubes. Every field is complex — one real embedding — unless the signature is named.

At class number 3 the realized set is M or the diagonal D = {(t, t, t)}, and both occur: 38 of the 83 complex fields of relation class number 3 to |dK| ≤ 6000 carry one class at all three places over every split prime they have, the other 45 range freely, and no field lies between (the two-populations block). The first population is the degenerate regime, the second the uniform one. A complex cubic field's discriminant factors as dK = f²d0 with d0 the discriminant of a quadratic field: that field k = Q(√d0), the quadratic field inside the Galois closure of K, is the resolvent, and the factor f is the conductor, which as an ideal of k is (f). Write σ for conjugation, the nontrivial automorphism of k. A line is a subgroup of order 3.

What the regime is

The degeneracy condition is a subgroup one, of index 3 to the 3-rank; its scalar form is a form only where the 3-part is cyclic property

Write Δ for the triples in M whose three classes agree modulo the cubes, a − b and b − c both in 3Cl. At h = 3 the cubes are trivial and Δ is D exactly; in general Δ asks for less than equality. The map (a, b, c) ↦ (a − b, b − c) into (Cl/3Cl)² has kernel Δ, lands in the diagonal because a + b + c = 0, and hits all of it, so [M : Δ] = 3r. Computably the condition is a lattice membership — with Cl = Zk/L, 3Cl is spanned by L's rows and 3e1 … 3ek — and needs no scalar. Two scalar tests have stood in for it. Multiplying the differences by h stripped of all its factors of 3 agrees with the subgroup condition exactly when the 3-part of h is 3, and parts at 9 | h, where it demands the three classes be EQUAL and no field obliges. The right scalar is h/3: writing Cl = A ⊕ T with T the 3-part, multiplication by h/3 kills A and acts on T as 3e−1 times a unit, so with T cyclic its kernel is A ⊕ 3T = 3Cl; above 3-rank 1 the form INVERTS, 3e−1 annihilating T entirely, and the scalar test reads degenerate at every field whose 3-part is non-cyclic whatever its triples do.

Scope. Property, derived; the scalar agreement is verified at zero disagreements over the 1365 fields with 3 | h, 3-rank at most 1 and ten mapped split primes to |d| ≤ 50,000.

An event's model share is its size over the order of the image, one formula for both events property

The image has order h² or h²/3r; the all-principal event has size 1 and the all-equal event |Cl[3]| = 3r, counted inside the image, Cl[3] lying in Δ and so in every image containing Δ. Off 3 the two events coincide field by field; where 3 | h the all-equal level reads 0.934 (z = −3.5, the same cell the all-principal event departs at), 0.993 and 1.059 at h = 6 on the two images, 1.101 and 0.674 at h = 9 — inside 2σ of 1 at every row but that one. At h = 9 the pooled 1.200 and 1.359 are misclassifications and not levels: nine of the stratum's thirty fields generate only an index-3 subgroup and were read against a share three times too small; split, they read 0.805 and 0.674 on their own 1/27 against the twenty-one's 0.727 and 1.101.

Scope. Property, derived; it reproduces every triple-event share over the 1283 fields with h > 1 to |d| ≤ 24,000. Measured throughout is the subgroup the observed triples GENERATE, a lower bound on the image; a full image putting a field's ten or more triples inside one proper subgroup has probability under 4·3⁻¹⁰, so the expected number misread that way is under 0.09 fields. Of the 1283, 1128 generate all of M and 155 a subgroup of index exactly 3, at h = 3, 6, 9 and 12.

Above 3-rank 1 a proper image need not be Δ observation

Both fields of 3-rank 2 in the box to |d| ≤ 50,000 have Cl = Z/3 × Z/3, h = 9. At |d| = 24843, over 20 mapped split primes, the generated subgroup has order 9 and index 9: it IS Δ. At |d| = 47628, over 31, it has order 27 and index 3, and Δ is measured to lie inside it. So a field generating a proper subgroup need not generate Δ, the scalar test, which calls both degenerate, is wrong at 47628 by a share of 3, and the lattice form is the instrument above 3-rank 1.

Scope. Observation on both such fields; no other hides, a relation class number being a multiple of the true one and a quotient's 3-rank never exceeding its group's. The box's 3-part census over the 3133 complex fields with h > 1: 1766 trivial, 1277 at Z/3, 88 at Z/9, 2 at Z/3 × Z/3, where the 1/|Aut| weighting expects 10 of the 90 (P ≤ 0.0019), the fraction a floor. A discriminant does not name a field: |d| = 47628 carries four, the one meant having h = 9.

What sorts a field

The genus index law: [M : R] = 3t for t the lines the conductor carries, and R is the triples agreeing modulo the principal genus rule

t is the number of rational primes q ≡ 1 (mod 3) dividing f, plus one when 9 | f and 3 splits in k. FORCED: any S3-stable subgroup of M projecting onto Cl contains M ∩ (3Cl)³ — the differences of (a, b, c) with its three transpositions sum to (3a, 3b, 3c) — so it is the preimage of a stable subspace of (Cl/3Cl)³ projecting onto Cl/3Cl, and those are indexed by the subspaces W of Cl/3Cl, of codimension r − dim W. So off 3 the image is full at every h, at 3-rank 1 it is M or Δ, and the class-number-3 trichotomy is the smallest case of a statement at every class number. BELOW: each line names a cyclic cubic field F over Q, of conductor q or 9, with KF/K unramified, so K with its t fields F is an unramified (Z/3)t-extension — the genus field — and the principal genus, the subgroup of Cl(K) it answers to under class field theory, has index 3t; F being Galois over Q, a Frobenius restricts to it the same way from all three copies, so R lies in the triples agreeing modulo the principal genus and the index is at least 3t, which is why t ≤ r at every field. ABOVE: the largest subfield abelian over k of the compositum of the three copies of the 3-elementary class field — the subfield of the Hilbert class field whose group over K is Cl/3Cl — carries a space of conjugation-FIXED cubic characters of dimension exactly r − dim W, each a ray class character mod f — a character of the group of ideals of k prime to f modulo those generated by elements ≡ 1 mod f, the group whose class field is the ray class field mod f — injecting into the fixed lines at the split primes of the conductor, of which there are t. So r − dim W = t.

Scope. Rule, proved; exact at all 1367 fields with 3 | h and ten mapped split primes to |dK| ≤ 50,000 — the generated order times 3t equal to h² at every one — both 3-rank 2 fields among them, and t ≤ r at all 3133 fields with h > 1. The two 3-rank 2 fields are two evaluations of one line, f = 91 = 7·13 at t = 2 and f = 126 = 2·3²·7 at t = 1, the 9 buying nothing over a resolvent in which 3 ramifies; "degenerate" at 3-rank r covers the r values t ≥ 1, and how often t = 1 at 3-rank 2 is a census question. The argument never consults the signature; the totally real side is unread. The enumeration control at thirteen small groups: one such subgroup off 3, two at 3-rank 1, six at Z/3 × Z/3.

What sorts a field into its regime is whether the conductor carries a split prime the character can absorb criterion

The field is degenerate exactly when some prime of k dividing (f) is SPLIT over Q and carries a nontrivial local 3-part — the 3-part of the units modulo that prime's full power in (f); as a congruence on f alone, when a rational prime q ≡ 1 (mod 3) divides f, or 9 divides f and 3 splits in k. The two forms are one condition because the conductor's primes are not free data: an anti-invariant cubic character ramifies at q ≡ 1 (mod 3) only where q splits in k and at q ≡ 2 (mod 3) only where q is inert, while v3(f) ≤ 2 with v3(f) = 1 only where 3 | d0. Neither form sorts by the integer f: 18 carries the degenerate regime at d0 = −11 and the uniform one at d0 = −19. The regime is a fact about the discriminant — of 19 discriminants carrying more than one readable field of class number 3, none is mixed — and not a 3-rank phenomenon of the resolvent, 35 of the first 38 degenerate fields having a resolvent whose class number 3 does not divide.

Scope. Criterion at 3-rank at most 1, the uniform direction proved by the mechanism below and the degenerate one by the genus index law; exact at every h = 3 field of every band to 50,000 — 489 uniform, 137 degenerate — and at every 3-rank 1 field of h = 6, 9, 12, 15 and 18; the conductor rule verified on all 193 conductors to 13,000. In general the realized subgroup is proper exactly when t ≥ 1 and is Δ exactly when t = r. The regime label at class number 3 is the two-populations block's measurement and inherits its reading of the class map.

The mechanism is the triple's own derivation read at k, and the second line must be fixed, which carries the whole distance rule

The degenerate regime asks for an abelian E of degree 9 over k inside the ray class field mod f, stable under conjugation, containing the Galois closure N of K and unramified over it; N supplies one line, inverted by σ, so E needs a second. E is the compositum of N with one copy of the Hilbert class field H, so restriction identifies Gal(E/N) with Gal(H/K); the transposition fixing that K pointwise induces σ on k and lies IN the abelian group Gal(H/K), so it acts trivially on it. Hence Gal(E/N) is a FIXED line and Gal(N/k) an inverted one; 2 being invertible on a group of order 9 splits the module, and an order count makes Gal(E/k) = (Z/3)². E unramified over N means a second character ψ has ψ|UQ in ⟨χ|UQ⟩ at every prime Q of the conductor: where σ FIXES Q the local χ is anti-invariant and its powers hold no nontrivial fixed character, so ψ is unramified there, and every fixed character is ramified somewhere over f, the fixed part of the ray class group being purely local (an imaginary quadratic field's class group is all inverted); where σ SWAPS two primes the condition is automatic. So a usable fixed line is exactly a split prime of the conductor with a nontrivial local 3-part, and the uniform half of the law is unconditional. Drop the fixedness and only the EXISTENCE of a conjugation-stable E unramified over N remains, which holds at every field of the population — all 123 uniform fields carry a usable inverted second line, as do 15 of the 70 degenerate ones — and so sorts nothing.

Scope. Rule, proved. Existence of a fixed line is the wrong condition too, visible at exactly two fields: at an inert 3 with 9 | f the one-units mod 9 are F9 with conjugation acting as Frobenius, which fixes the prime field, so a fixed line exists — both fields carrying that configuration, at dK = −6156, are uniform. The joint table over 193 fields has three cells: 121 uniform with no fixed line, 2 uniform with one that cannot be used, 70 degenerate with one that can. The corner no census reaches — 9 | f with 3 ramified in k, one readable field below 13,000 — is decided by the same argument: the prime above 3 is fixed by conjugation, its line unusable, and the field is uniform unless some q ≡ 1 (mod 3) divides f (dK = −11907 at f = 63 is degenerate by its 7).

At conductor 1 the resolvent's 3-rank is at least 2, and the family count is the resolvent correspondence rule

A field of fundamental discriminant has no conductor prime to carry a fixed line, so it is uniform; H̃/k is then unramified, Cl(k) surjects onto the abelianization of Gal(H̃/k), which is non-cyclic at either regime, so its 3-rank is at least 2 — never the 1 that 603 of the 607 fundamental discriminants carrying a complex cubic field below 6000 have. The cubic fields of one fundamental discriminant correspond to the index-3 subgroups of the 3-class group of k, (3r − 1)/2 of them, and every one of the 607 carries exactly the predicted number, 603 at family size 1 and four at size 4; at f > 1 a ray class group mod f counts the family instead, which is why sizes 2 and 3 occur there — −2891 = 7²·(−59) is a family of three at conductor 7.

Scope. Rule, proved, 39 of 39 at the rank statement; the count verified over the whole complex population to |dK| ≤ 6000.

What it costs the counts

The all-equal shortfall deepens with h where the algebra forbids degeneracy, and what deepens is the explicit formula's cube term pattern

Where 3 ∤ h no field is degenerate (largest equal fraction 0.375), and the all-equal count sits below the uniform model by a factor that GROWS with the class number: 498 against 580.0 at h = 2 (z = −3.4), 12 against 27.5 at h = 4, 6 against 19.2 at h = 5 (0.86, 0.44, 0.31), and h = 3's generic half 245 against 377.0 (z = −6.8). A tilt of uniformity by λ(principal places), fitted to the measured share, leaves 0.663, 0.463, 0.351 on the all-principal count, the one event every stratum shares: the deepening was never the share deficit's. What deepens: the all-principal triple is the IDENTITY of Gal(M̃/Q), M̃ the Galois closure over Q of the Hilbert class field, the group M ⋊ S3 of order 6h² wherever the image fills M, and Chebotarev's explicit formula, which counts prime powers at weight 1/k at the k-th power, shorts a count of primes with Frobenius the identity by 1/k per prime q with qk in the window and Frobqk = e: a split prime's square when its classes are 2-torsion, a partial prime's square when its degree-1 place is principal, and EVERY inert prime's cube, since (v, r)³ = (s, s, s) with s the sum of v's coordinates, zero by the norm relation. The square's group count is 3h + |Cl[2]|², the cube's 2h² + |Cl[3]|² — quadratic in h where the share deficit is flat. Scope. The cube's landing on the identity is a property of the sum-zero relation; the corrected level's flatness is a pattern across seven strata of the 1367 fields with h > 1 to |d| ≤ 24,000, at a spread Poisson on each stratum's expected corrected count; the residuals are an observation.
