The degenerate regime

At some cubic fields every totally split prime carries one ideal class three times over; at the rest, up to 3-rank 1, the three classes range freely, and above 3-rank 1 there is room between. What the regime IS, is the realized triples falling short of everything the sum-zero relation allows; its strictest form is the three classes agreeing modulo the cubes, a subgroup whose index is 3 to the 3-rank, and above 3-rank 1 one word covers as many regimes as that rank; what sorts a field into it is the conductor of the field over its quadratic resolvent, read as a congruence on the discriminant, and the index of the realized subgroup is 3 to the number of lines that conductor carries.

The objects. A cubic field K has discriminant d, also written dK, and a finite class group Cl(K) of order h, the class number: its ideals taken modulo those generated by a single element, the principal ones, the class of an ideal being its ideal class. The all-principal event is the triple whose three classes are all trivial and the all-equal one the triple whose three classes agree. A rational prime is totally split in K when it factors into three places whose residue fields are the prime field, and inert when it carries none. The three places over a totally split prime carry classes summing to the class of the prime itself, which is trivial, so write M for the group of triples in Cl(K)³ summing to zero, of order h², and R for the subgroup of M the realized triples fill, the image — a subgroup, because the triple is a Frobenius, the Galois-group element a prime determines up to conjugacy, and on the primes in question the map to the three classes is a homomorphism, onto Cl in each coordinate (The split triple). An event's level is its observed count over the count a stated share predicts, so that 1 is agreement; a bar is one standard deviation of the figure it follows. A class number is read here as a relation class number, the order of the group the relations found generate, which the true one divides. The 3-rank r of the class group is the number of its cyclic factors whose order 3 divides, equivalently the dimension of Cl/3Cl over the field of three elements, 3Cl being the subgroup of cubes and Cl[3] the subgroup killed by 3. Every field is complex — one real embedding — unless the signature is named.

At class number 3 the realized set is M or the diagonal D = {(t, t, t)}, and both occur: 38 of the 83 complex fields of relation class number 3 to |dK| ≤ 6000 carry one class at all three places over every split prime they have, the other 45 range freely, and no field lies between (the two-populations block). The first population is the degenerate regime, the second the uniform one. A complex cubic field's discriminant factors as dK = f²d0 with d0 the discriminant of a quadratic field: that field k = Q(√d0), the quadratic field inside the Galois closure of K, is the resolvent, and the factor f is the conductor, which as an ideal of k is (f). Write σ for conjugation, the nontrivial automorphism of k. A line is a subgroup of order 3.

What the regime is

The degeneracy condition is a subgroup one, of index 3 to the 3-rank; its scalar form is a form only where the 3-part is cyclic property

Write Δ for the triples in M whose three classes agree modulo the cubes, a − b and b − c both in 3Cl. At h = 3 the cubes are trivial and Δ is D exactly; in general Δ asks for less than equality. The map (a, b, c) ↦ (a − b, b − c) into (Cl/3Cl)² has kernel Δ, lands in the diagonal because a + b + c = 0, and hits all of it, so [M : Δ] = 3r. Computably the condition is a lattice membership — with Cl = Zn/L for a relation lattice L, 3Cl is spanned by L's rows and 3e1 … 3en — and needs no scalar. Two scalar tests have stood in for it. Multiplying the differences by h stripped of all its factors of 3 agrees with the subgroup condition exactly when the 3-part of h is 3, and parts at 9 | h, where that test demands the three classes be EQUAL and no field obliges. The right scalar is h/3: writing Cl = A ⊕ T with T the 3-part, of order 3e, multiplication by h/3 kills A and acts on T as 3e−1 times a unit, so with T cyclic its kernel is A ⊕ 3T = 3Cl; above 3-rank 1 the form GOES VACUOUS, 3e−1 annihilating T entirely, and the scalar test reads degenerate at every field whose 3-part is non-cyclic whatever its triples do.

Scope. Property, derived; the scalar agreement is verified at zero disagreements over the 1365 fields with 3 | h, 3-rank at most 1 and ten MAPPED split primes — ones whose three places' classes the run computed — to |d| ≤ 50,000.

An event's model share is its size over the order of the image, one formula for both events property

The image has order h² or h²/3r; the all-principal event has size 1 and the all-equal event |Cl[3]| = 3r, counted inside the image, Cl[3] lying in Δ and so in every image containing Δ. Off 3 the two events coincide field by field; where 3 | h the all-equal level reads 0.934, 3.5 bars low and the same cell the all-principal event departs at, 0.993 and 1.059 at h = 6 on the two images, 1.101 and 0.674 at h = 9 — inside 2 bars of 1 at every row but the 0.934. At h = 9 the pooled 1.200 and 1.359 are misclassifications and not levels: nine of the thirty fields at h = 9 generate only an index-3 subgroup and were read against a share three times too small; split, they read 0.805 and 0.674 on their own 1/27 against the twenty-one's 0.727 and 1.101.

Scope. Property, derived; it reproduces every triple-event share over the 1283 fields with h > 1 and ten or more mapped split primes to |d| ≤ 24,000 — a box with no non-cyclic 3-part in it at all, so its 3-rank is at most 1 everywhere and those two orders are the whole menu; in general the order is h²/3t, by the genus index law. Measured throughout is the subgroup the observed triples GENERATE, a lower bound on the image; a full image putting a field's ten or more triples inside one proper subgroup has probability under 4·3⁻¹⁰, so the expected number misread that way is under 0.09 fields. Of the 1283, 1128 generate all of M and 155 a subgroup of index exactly 3, at h = 3, 6, 9 and 12.

Above 3-rank 1 a proper image need not be Δ observation

Both fields of 3-rank 2 in the box to |d| ≤ 50,000 have Cl = Z/3 × Z/3, h = 9. At |d| = 24843, over 20 mapped split primes, the generated subgroup has order 9 and index 9: it IS Δ. At |d| = 47628, over 31, it has order 27 and index 3, and Δ is measured to lie inside it. So a field generating a proper subgroup need not generate Δ; the scalar test, which calls both degenerate, is wrong at 47628 by a share of 3, and the lattice form is the instrument above 3-rank 1.

Scope. Observation on both such fields; no other hides, a relation class number being a multiple of the true one and a quotient's 3-rank never exceeding its group's. The box's 3-part census over the 3133 complex fields with h > 1: 1766 trivial, 1277 at Z/3, 88 at Z/9, 2 at Z/3 × Z/3, where the 1/|Aut| weighting over the 3-part groups expects 10 of the 90 (P ≤ 0.0019), the fraction a floor. A discriminant does not name a field: |d| = 47628 carries four, the one meant having h = 9.

What sorts a field

The genus index law: [M : R] = 3t for t the lines the conductor carries, and R is the triples agreeing modulo the principal genus rule

t is the number of rational primes q ≡ 1 (mod 3) dividing f, plus one when 9 | f and 3 splits in k. FORCED: any S3-stable subgroup of M projecting onto Cl contains M ∩ (3Cl)³ — for (a, b, c) in it the transposition differences (a − b, b − a, 0) and, permuted, (a − c, c − a, 0) lie in it and sum to (3a, −3a, 0), a ranging over Cl — so it is the preimage of a stable subspace of (Cl/3Cl)³ projecting onto Cl/3Cl, and those are indexed by the subspaces W of Cl/3Cl, of codimension r − dim W. So off 3 the image is full at every h, at 3-rank 1 it is M or Δ, and the class-number-3 case above is the smallest case of a statement at every class number. BELOW: each line names a cyclic cubic field F over Q, of conductor q or 9, with KF/K unramified, so K with its t fields F is an unramified (Z/3)t-extension — the genus field — and the principal genus, the subgroup of Cl(K) it answers to under class field theory, has index 3t; F being Galois over Q, a Frobenius restricts to it the same way from all three copies, so R lies in the triples agreeing modulo the principal genus and the index is at least 3t, which is why t ≤ r at every field. ABOVE: the largest subfield abelian over k of the compositum of the three copies of the 3-elementary class field — the subfield of the Hilbert class field whose group over K is Cl/3Cl — carries a space of conjugation-FIXED cubic characters of dimension exactly r − dim W, each a ray class character mod f — a character of the group of ideals of k prime to f modulo those generated by elements ≡ 1 mod f, the group whose class field is the ray class field mod f — injecting into the fixed lines at the split primes of the conductor, of which there are t. So r − dim W = t.

Scope. Rule, proved; exact at all 1367 fields with 3 | h and ten mapped split primes to |dK| ≤ 50,000 — the generated order times 3t equal to h² at every one — both 3-rank 2 fields among them, and t ≤ r at all 3133 fields with h > 1. The two 3-rank 2 fields are two evaluations of one law, f = 91 = 7·13 at t = 2 and f = 126 = 2·3²·7 at t = 1, the 9 buying nothing over a resolvent in which 3 ramifies; "degenerate" at 3-rank r covers the r values t ≥ 1, and how often t = 1 at 3-rank 2 is a census question. The argument never consults the signature; the totally real side is unread. The enumeration control at thirteen small groups: one such subgroup off 3, two at 3-rank 1, six at Z/3 × Z/3.

What sorts a field into its regime is whether the conductor carries a split prime the character can absorb criterion

The field is degenerate exactly when some prime of k dividing (f) is SPLIT over Q and carries a nontrivial local 3-part — the 3-part of the units modulo that prime's full power in (f); as a congruence on f alone, when a rational prime q ≡ 1 (mod 3) divides f, or 9 divides f and 3 splits in k. The two forms are one condition because the conductor's primes are not free data: an anti-invariant — σ-inverted — cubic character ramifies at q ≡ 1 (mod 3) only where q splits in k and at q ≡ 2 (mod 3) only where q is inert, while v3(f) ≤ 2 with v3(f) = 1 only where 3 | d0. Neither form sorts by the integer f: 18 carries the degenerate regime at d0 = −11 and the uniform one at d0 = −19. The regime is a fact about the discriminant — of 19 discriminants carrying more than one field of class number 3 the run reads, none is mixed — and not a 3-rank phenomenon of the resolvent, 35 of the first 38 degenerate fields having a resolvent whose class number 3 does not divide.

Scope. Criterion at 3-rank at most 1, the uniform direction proved by the mechanism below and the degenerate one by the genus index law; exact at every h = 3 field of every band to 50,000 — 489 uniform, 137 degenerate — and at every 3-rank 1 field of h = 6, 9, 12, 15 and 18; the conductor rule verified on all 193 fields' conductors to 13,000. In general the realized subgroup is proper exactly when t ≥ 1 and is Δ exactly when t = r. The regime label at class number 3 is the two-populations block's measurement and inherits that block's reading of the class map.

The mechanism is the triple's own derivation read at k, and the second line must be fixed, which carries the whole distance rule

The degenerate regime asks for an abelian E of degree 9 over k inside the ray class field mod f, stable under conjugation, containing the Galois closure N of K and unramified over it; N supplies one line, inverted by σ, so E needs a second. E is the compositum of N with one copy of the Hilbert class field H, so restriction identifies Gal(E/N) with Gal(H/K); the transposition fixing that copy of K pointwise induces σ on k and lies IN the abelian group Gal(H/K), so conjugation by it is trivial there. Hence Gal(E/N) is a FIXED line and Gal(N/k) an inverted one; 2 being invertible on a group of order 9 splits the module, and an order count makes Gal(E/k) = (Z/3)². Write χ for the anti-invariant cubic ray class character mod f that N itself answers to, and UQ for the local units at a prime Q. E unramified over N means a second character ψ has ψ|UQ in ⟨χ|UQ⟩ at every prime Q of the conductor: where σ FIXES Q the local χ is anti-invariant and its powers hold no nontrivial fixed character, so ψ is unramified there, and every fixed character is ramified somewhere over f, the fixed part of the ray class group being purely local (an imaginary quadratic field's class group is all inverted); where σ SWAPS two primes the condition is automatic. So a usable fixed line is exactly a split prime of the conductor with a nontrivial local 3-part, and the uniform half of the law is unconditional. Drop the fixedness and only the EXISTENCE of a conjugation-stable E unramified over N remains, which holds at every field of the population — all 123 uniform fields carry a usable inverted second line, as do 15 of the 70 degenerate ones, the other 55 carrying their second line fixed — and so sorts nothing. The absence of an INVERTED line does sort, one way: every one of the 55 fields without one is degenerate.

Scope. Rule, proved. Existence of a fixed line is the wrong condition too, visible at exactly two fields: at an inert 3 with 9 | f the one-units mod 9 are F9 with conjugation acting as x ↦ x³, which fixes the prime field, so a fixed line exists — both fields carrying that configuration, at dK = −6156, are uniform. The joint table over 193 fields has three cells: 121 uniform with no fixed line, 2 uniform with one that cannot be used, 70 degenerate with one that can. The corner no census reaches — 9 | f with 3 ramified in k, one readable field below 13,000 — is decided by the same argument: the prime above 3 is fixed by conjugation, its line unusable, and the field is uniform unless some q ≡ 1 (mod 3) divides f (dK = −11907 at f = 63 is degenerate by its 7).

At conductor 1 a field of class number 3 forces its resolvent's 3-rank to at least 2, and the family count is the resolvent correspondence rule

A field of class number 3 and fundamental discriminant has no conductor prime to carry a fixed line, so it is uniform; the Galois closure H̃ of the Hilbert class field over Q is then unramified over k, Cl(k) surjects onto the abelianization of Gal(H̃/k), non-cyclic whichever of Δ and M the realized set is, so its 3-rank is at least 2 — never the 1 that 603 of the 607 fundamental discriminants carrying a complex cubic field below 6000 have. The cubic fields of one fundamental discriminant correspond to the index-3 subgroups of the 3-class group of k, (3r − 1)/2 of them, and every one of the 607 carries exactly the predicted number, 603 at family size 1 and four at size 4; at f > 1 a ray class group mod f counts the family instead, which is why sizes 2 and 3 occur there — −2891 = 7²·(−59) is a family of three at conductor 7.

Scope. Rule, proved, and read at the 39 conductor-1 fields of the 193 of class number 3 to 13,000, all 39 at 3-rank 2 or more; the count verified over the whole complex population to |dK| ≤ 6000.

What it costs the counts

What the regime costs the prime counts is now The forcing, and with it the anchors of its three claims. What they establish: where the algebra FORBIDS the regime, at every class number prime to 3, the count of primes whose three places are all principal sits below its nominal count, deepest at the large class numbers, and what deepens is the explicit formula's prime-power terms, ramified included — the inert primes' cubes the sum-zero relation puts on the identity at every h. Put back at the nominal share, what a uniform triple gives an event, those terms overshoot, the level passing 2 by h = 10; read at the share its own image gives each term, every readable stratum sits 5 bars or more below 1, and the nominal level's rise with h is a fixed fraction near 0.4 of what the algebra forces, the part the raw primes leave unpaid — a fraction the same statistic on a quadratic field's principal class reads at 0.02. The control for all three is the split count itself, whose own deficit at a small prime is the cubic family's and not the field's.
