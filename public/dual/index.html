DUAL — the leading end of base b, read against the trailing end

The object: base b read top first. A positive integer n has e + 1 digits, e = e(n) = ⌊log_b n⌋, and its depth-t leading tile is the set of integers with the same digit count and the same t leading digits. Like a window of SIZE, this is a reading at a set of places, here the archimedean place alone: size, read to t digits. So a floating-point number names a leading tile. The trailing end reads the same integer bottom first, and its depth-t tile is n mod b^t. A map f of the positive integers is read at lookahead c when at every depth t the depth-t tile of f(n) is a function of the depth-(t + c) tile of n, over the n with f(n) ≥ 1, since zero has no leading tile; a map of two integers reads the pair of their tiles, and a map valued in {0, 1} or {−1, 0, 1}, as [x = y] or the sign of x − y, is read at lookahead c when at every depth t its value is a function of the inputs' depth-(t + c) tiles. This page shows that the two ends of base b exchange what they read: the places p | b that the trailing end reads exactly, the leading end hides exactly; the difference map (x, y) ↦ x − y, which the trailing end reads digit by digit, is read at no finite lookahead at the leading end; multiplication is free at the trailing end and division at the leading end. It decides the affine maps the leading end reads and bounds their least lookahead, exactly in the cases the least-lookahead law names, and shows that it reads maps outside that class. Then it gives the leading end redundant signed digits {−a, …, a}, 2a + 1 > b: the overlapping cover they buy reads every Lipschitz map of their range, or its powers, into that range, and the purchase costs order, a lower bound on size before a division, and, unless p ∣ 2a + 1, the exact hiding of the places p ∣ b, degraded to a bias. Last come the leading end's densities, whose own measure is log-uniform, the Haar measure of the scale circle.

Which end pays for what. Each end reads freely the operation whose carries run away from it. A product's low digits depend only on the factors' low digits, so the trailing end multiplies at lookahead 0 and divides only where the divisor's primes divide b. Long division emits the quotient from the top with a bounded remainder, so the leading end divides by anything and multiplies only where the multiplier's primes divide b. Where READING reads a leading numeration on R, a scaling y ↦ uy with u > 0 reads iff 1/u ∈ Z[1/b]. On the integers a map is a floor, and a floor adds one condition, that its jumps fall on tile boundaries. Redundant digits blur size inside the archimedean window itself: the integers sharing t leading digits no longer make disjoint tiles, and every wall of a Lipschitz map from the redundant strings' range, or its powers, into that range dissolves. Order stays walled, and wider.

The leading-tile lemma

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_t.

If e + 1 ≤ t the depth-t leading tile of n is {n}. Otherwise it is [P b^j, (P + 1) b^j) with j = e + 1 − t and P the t-digit prefix: b^j consecutive integers, sign-definite. Its span is j, the digits it hides, and it is deep when j ≥ 1. A tile of span j is therefore a complete residue system mod b^j, and for m ≥ 2, n mod m is constant on no tile of length at least 2. The predicate [m ∣ n] is weaker and is answered by length, not depth: a tile shorter than m that holds no multiple of m decides it false. At base 2 the depth-2 tile of 4 is [4, 6), deep, and decides [7 ∣ n].

Proof. The integers with e + 1 digits and prefix P are exactly those with ⌊n/b^j⌋ = P.

The script groups every n below 2^12, 3^7 and 10^4 by its tile at depths 1 to 4, and every group is the stated interval.

The two-ends law

Tier: criterion.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_h.

For m ≥ 2 and j ≥ 0, n mod m is a function of the last j digits iff m ∣ b^j, and every residue mod m has the same count on every leading tile of span j iff m ∣ b^j. So one condition decides both ends: depth j of the trailing end reads m exactly where span j at the leading end hides it exactly. A prime p is read by some trailing depth and hidden on every deep leading tile iff p ∣ b, and for p ∤ b, n mod p is a function of no trailing depth and of no deep leading tile.

Proof. The first half is the suffix read (COORDINATES: The suffix and floor reads). For the second, a span-j tile is b^j consecutive integers. Each residue appears b^j/m times when m ∣ b^j, and otherwise ⌊b^j/m⌋ or ⌊b^j/m⌋ + 1 times, both counts occurring, on every such tile.

When rad(m) ∣ rad(b), m is hidden from span max_p ⌈v_p(m)/v_p(b)⌉ on, v_p the p-adic valuation, the depth at which the trailing end first reads it. The finite mirror is SIZE: The hiding lemma: a window of a residue ring hides the sign bit exactly iff the ring's size over the window's is even, as a leading tile hides m exactly iff m ∣ b^j. The script reads all 1595 triples b = 2..12, m = 2..30, j = 0..4, the leading half on every tile with a one- or two-digit prefix and the trailing half on the n < min(b^j, 60) against n + k b^j, k ≤ 3.

The crown exchange

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_z.

The leading end reads sign and exponent natively and a product's exponent to one bit, e(xy) − e(x) − e(y) ∈ {0, 1}. It reads neither equality nor the order of nearby values at any depth: two points of one deep tile of span j differ by any integer in (−b^j, b^j), so on that tile paired with itself [x = y] and the sign of x − y take every value and e(|x − y|) takes 0, …, j − 1, at every depth. The trailing end reads x − y at lookahead 0 but x = y only modulo b^t, and a residue ring reads x − y = 0 exactly, channel by channel.

Proof. b^(e(x)+e(y)) ≤ xy < b^(e(x)+e(y)+2). The difference set of an interval of b^j integers is (−b^j, b^j).

The exact zero-test a residue ring is built on and the leading end's blind spot are one point, 0, where a logarithm is singular. On a squarefree modulus the ring's support log reaches 0 by passing to a smaller support (COORDINATES: The support log), so a squarefree residue ring meets that point and reads through it. The leading read is a chart of the real logarithm that omits 0, and a difference is the operation that reaches 0, so the leading end meets the same point and is walled by it. The script finds e(xy) − e(x) − e(y) taking exactly 0 and 1 over all x, y below 2^10, 3^7 and 500 at bases 2, 3 and 10, and the tiles [64, 128) at base 2 and [1000, 2000) at base 10 realizing every difference exponent below their spans, 6 and 3, with both signs and 0.

The coset rigidity

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_c.

Let a digit set D write every integer as a finite trailing string, as {−a, …, a} does whenever 2a + 1 ≥ b and a ≥ 1. The completions of a trailing prefix of value V and length t are then exactly the coset V + b^t Z. Two such tiles are equal or disjoint, so the trailing tiles are the b-adic balls however redundant D is. READING's Lipschitz and division criteria hold unchanged, and no such digit set buys the trailing end an overlap: redundancy is a purchase for the leading end only.

Proof. The extension's digits write any integer z, and the completion is V + b^t z. For {−a, …, a}: take d ≡ n mod b in D and n′ = (n − d)/b. Then |n′| ≤ (|n| + a)/b < |n| while |n| > a/(b − 1), and every |n| ≤ a is a digit.

The script writes and reads back every |n| ≤ 10^4 at (b, a) = (2, 1), (3, 1), (4, 2), (10, 5) and (10, 6). At each, five extension digits reach every z with |z| ≤ b^3, so every prefix of length up to 3 fills its coset within b^(t+3) of V.

The affine class theorem

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_a, dual.py::section_0.

Let f be nondecreasing with |f(n) − λn| ≤ C, λ > 0. f is read at some lookahead iff - Slope: λ = b^r/h for integers r ≥ 0 and h ≥ 1, the reduced numerator of λ dividing a power of b, r taken least; and - Alignment: for some K ≥ 0 every positive multiple N of h b^K has f(N) ≥ λN > f(N − 1).

Three families follow. The numerator criterion: ⌊(u/v)n⌋ with gcd(u, v) = 1 is read iff rad(u) ∣ rad(b), so the denominator is free and every floor division reads. The phase law: ⌊(n + s)/v⌋ with 0 ≤ s < v is read iff s = 0, and n + ω, ω an integer, iff ω = 0. The sum criterion: f = Σ w_i ⌊n/v_i⌋ with w_i ≥ 1, ℓ = lcm(v_i) and U = Σ w_i ℓ/v_i is read iff rad(U) ∣ rad(b), U the numerator over the lcm, unreduced. So g = ⌊n/3⌋ + ⌊n/7⌋ is read at base 10, U = 10, and is no ⌊(10n + s)/21⌋ with 0 ≤ s < 21, since those stay within 1 of 10n/21 and g(20) − 200/21 = −32/21. The arithmetic maps the leading end reads are wider than the numerator family and its phases.

Proof. Write Δ = ⌈C/λ⌉ + 1, and n*(B) = min{n : f(n) ≥ B} for the crossing of B, so |n*(B) − B/λ| ≤ Δ. The depth-t output boundaries are the integers M b^J with 1 ≤ M < b^t. A monotone f sends a depth-(t + c) input tile into one output tile iff no crossing sits strictly inside it. So f is read at lookahead c iff every crossing n* with f(n* − 1) ≥ 1 lying in a deep tile, of span j = digits(n*) − t − c, is that tile's left end, b^j ∣ n*: call such a crossing aligned.

Slope. n*(b^J) has at least J − G digits for a constant G, so at depth 1 and lookahead c being aligned makes b^(J−G−1−c) divide it. The integer n*(b^J)/b^(J−G−1−c) lies within Δ b^(G+1+c−J) of the fixed real b^(G+1+c)/λ, which is therefore an integer: λ is a power of b over an integer, which is b^r/h once r is taken least.

Alignment, necessity. For J ≥ r and M with exactly t digits, N₀ = M b^J/λ = h M b^(J−r) is an integer. The crossing lies within Δ of it and has at least t + J − r − 1 digits, so being aligned gives b^(J−r−1−c) ∣ n*. N₀ is a multiple of the same power, so once b^(J−r−1−c) > Δ the two are equal. That threshold J₀ depends on neither M nor t, and every multiple of h b^(J₀−r) is h M b^(J−r) with b ∤ M, t = digits(M) and J ≥ J₀, so it is its own crossing.

Alignment, sufficiency. For J ≥ r + K the crossing of M b^J is N = h M b^(J−r), of at most digits(h) + t + J − r digits, so at c ≥ digits(h) its tile has span at most J − r and N is aligned. For J < r + K the crossing is below h b^(t+K−1) + Δ + 1, in no deep tile at c ≥ digits(h) + K + digits(Δ).

The families. ⌊(u/v)n⌋: the slope needs u ∣ b^r, and then every multiple N of h = v b^r/u has f(N) = λN > f(N − 1), K = 0. ⌊(n + s)/v⌋ with 0 < s < v has f(N − 1) = N/v at every multiple of v, so it fails at every K, and n + ω needs ω ≥ 0 for f(N) ≥ N and ω < 1 for f(N − 1) < N. For the sums, f(n) ≤ λn with equality iff ℓ ∣ n, and f(N − 1) = λN − Σ w_i when ℓ ∣ N, so alignment holds at K iff ℓ ∣ h b^K, iff U ∣ b^(r+K).

The slope half is READING: The wall criterion on the integers; the alignment half is what floors add. It is not implied by the slope: ⌊n/3⌋ + ⌊n/6⌋ has λ = 1/2 and U = 3, so it fails at every base prime to 3. The proof constructs a misaligned crossing wherever a condition fails, and the families' verdicts are also checked by computation. At 171 sums, the 55 pairs 2 ≤ v₁ < v₂ ≤ 12 at bases 2, 6 and 10 and six more sums at base 10, three of them weighted, a crossing reader (depths 1 to 3) and the alignment condition read directly each agree with rad(U) ∣ rad(b) at all 171, 42 of them read. The slope holds with rad(U) ∤ rad(b) at 2 sums at base 2, 3 at base 10 and none at base 6. The numerator criterion holds at all 273 cases, the 91 coprime maps ⌊(u/v)n⌋ with u, v ≤ 12 at bases 2, 6 and 10, and the phase law at v = 3, 5, 7 and |ω| ≤ 3 at bases 2 and 10. An exhaustive reader grouping every n below 10^6 finds g at base 10 with 1552 and 1103 input tiles whose image meets two output tiles, summed over depths 1 to 3, at c = 0 and 1 and none at 2. Over n below 2^13, 6^5 and 10^4 it agrees with the crossing reader at all 1368 (sum, depth, c) it decides.

The least-lookahead law

Tier: theorem; pattern (the scanned lookahead under the sharper bound of the last paragraph at the 42 read sums, unproved as a bound on c_min).
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_k.

In the affine class theorem's notation, with Δ = ⌈C/λ⌉ + 1 as in its proof and c_min the least lookahead at which f is read, take K least, δ = digits_b(h), μ = v_b(h), the exponent of the largest power of b dividing h, and h′ = h/b^μ. Then c_min ≥ c_deep = digits_b(h′) − [h′ = 1], and c_min ≤ max(c_deep, δ + K) whenever Δ ≤ (b − 1) b^(δ+K). So when K = 0, h ≥ 2, b ∤ h and Δ ≤ (b − 1) b^δ,

c_min = digits_b(h),

the lookahead priced exactly: ⌊n/3⌋ reads at 1 at base 10, and g at 2.

Proof. The deep bound. For J ≥ r + K the crossing of M b^J is N = h M b^(J−r), its depth-(t + c) tile has span digits(hM) + J − r − t − c, and N is aligned iff c ≥ digits(hM) − t − v_b(hM). For M prime to b, v_b(hM) = μ; M is taken so because v_b is not additive at a composite b (2 · 5 at base 10). The bound is then δ − μ when the product overflows into δ + t digits and δ − μ − 1 otherwise, and every M gives at most δ − μ. The overflow interval [b^(δ+t−1)/h, b^t) is nonempty iff h > b^(δ−1), iff h′ ≥ 2. Its length grows with t, and any b consecutive integers hold one ≡ 1 mod b. So at every large J a deep crossing needs c_deep. For J < r + K the crossing is below b^(δ+t+K−1) + Δ, hence below b^(δ+t+K) when Δ ≤ (b − 1) b^(δ+K), and its tile has span at most δ + K − c.

The script reads c_scan, the least lookahead that suffices at every crossing it scans (output depths up to the first overflow and at least to 3, exponents to r + K + 6), so c_scan ≤ c_min, at the 42 read sums. c_scan ≥ c_deep at all 42, with the deep crossing exhibited. All 42 meet Δ ≤ (b − 1) b^(δ+K) and sit under the bound, and c_scan = digits_b(h) at all 30 sums the law covers; since c_scan ≤ c_min, these are consistency checks, and only the proof carries the upper bound. ⌊n/3⌋ + ⌊n/9⌋ at base 2 has h = 9 and c_scan = 4, decided first at depth 4 by the crossing 135 of 15 · 2². A reader stopping at depth 3 reads 3, as the script prints. c_scan sits under the sharper bound max(c_deep, δ + K − 1) at all 42 sums, with equality at 41: a pattern, unproved as a bound on c_min.

The two-ends exchange

Tier: criterion.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_a, reading.py::section_r.

Let u, v ≥ 1 be coprime. At the trailing end n ↦ un is read at lookahead 0 and ⌊n/v⌋ is read iff rad(v) ∣ rad(b). At the leading end ⌊n/v⌋ is read and ⌊(u/v)n⌋ is read iff rad(u) ∣ rad(b). One radical condition falls on opposite parts of the fraction.

Proof. The trailing half is READING: The division criterion at the chain b^t, with multiplication 1-Lipschitz. The leading half is the numerator criterion.

The structural split

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_s.

A map is read at lookahead 0 iff it sends every tile into a tile of the same depth, and that asks for no arithmetic. At base 2, the flip n ↦ n XOR 2^(e−1) changes the second digit, on n ≥ 2. At base 10, π(n) sends the second digit d to 9 − d, on n ≥ 10. The power n ↦ b^e is nondecreasing with b^e/n running over (1/b, 1], so no line lies within a bounded distance of it. All three are read at lookahead 0, and the flip and π are not monotone. On a finite product of finite fields a map is channel-local iff it is a polynomial (SIZE: The locality criterion): on a squarefree residue ring locality and algebra coincide, and at the leading end they part. The trailing end of Z is no such ring past depth 1: over Z/8 the maps read at lookahead 0 number 2^(2+4+8) = 2^14, the value mod 2^t chosen one binary digit at a time, while the polynomial functions number ∏_(i<4) 8/gcd(8, i!) = 8 · 8 · 4 · 4 = 2^10 (Kempner); both counts are by hand.

Proof. Each fixes the digit count and rewrites the leading t digits by a map that reads only them, so a depth-t tile maps into one depth-t tile.

The script finds every tile sent into one tile at depths 1 to 4 for the flip below 2^12 and for π and the power below 10^5. The flip sends 2 to 3 and 3 to 2, π(10) = 19 > π(11) = 18, and the power's b^e/n is 0.1010 at n = 99.

The redundant reader

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_r.

Give the leading end the digits {−a, …, a} with 2a + 1 > b, and read 0.d₁d₂… as y = Σ d_i b^(−i) in [−ρ, ρ], ρ = a/(b − 1) > 1/2. The completions of a depth-t prefix of value V are exactly the interval [V − ρb^(−t), V + ρb^(−t)], and adjacent children of a tile overlap by (2ρ − 1)b^(−t−1) > 0. A map is read at lookahead c here when a reader seeing the inputs c digits ahead can always commit a child tile that holds every image still possible. Every map F: [−ρ, ρ]^k → [−ρ, ρ] of k ≥ 1 inputs that is L-Lipschitz in the sup metric is read at lookahead

max(0, ⌈log_b L + log_b(2a/(2a − b + 1))⌉).

So every scaling y ↦ (u/v)y with |u/v| ≤ 1 is read whatever the radical of u, and so are the mean (x + y)/2 and the product xy/ρ: redundancy dissolves every wall of a Lipschitz map of [−ρ, ρ]^k into [−ρ, ρ].

Proof. The j-digit strings write every integer in [−aR_j, aR_j], R_j = (b^j − 1)/(b − 1), by induction, since the intervals [bz − a, bz + a] over consecutive z abut when 2a + 1 ≥ b. A limit of finite completions is a completion, which gives the interval. A tile's children are centred at V + d b^(−t−1) with radius ρb^(−t−1); the extreme ones reach V ± (a + ρ)b^(−t−1) = V ± ρb^(−t), and adjacent ones overlap by 2ρb^(−t−1) − b^(−t−1). So every subinterval of a tile of length at most (2ρ − 1)b^(−t−1) lies in one child. At b^c ≥ 2ρL/(2ρ − 1), if the committed depth-t tile holds F of the inputs' depth-(t + c) tiles, as [−ρ, ρ] does at t = 0, then F of the depth-(t + 1 + c) tiles lies inside it with diameter at most 2ρL b^(−t−1−c) ≤ (2ρ − 1)b^(−t−1). It fits a child, and committing that child keeps the hypothesis.

At (2, 1) the lookahead is READING's max(0, ⌈log₂ L⌉ + 1). The bound is a sufficient lookahead, and for a C² map F of one input, not affine on [−ρ, ρ], L the sup of |F′|, it is the least: with ρ = a/(b − 1) it is the inequality b^c(2a + 1 − b) ≥ 2aL of REDUNDANT: The many-stream theorem at one stream and lead 0, the lead at which that page's output root tile is [−ρ, ρ] itself. In that page's letters, its gradient sup Λ is this page's L, its delay (lead plus lookahead) is c here, its slack is 2a + 1 − b, and its digit-interval length |I| is 2a. At the continued-fraction numeration the same purchase cures walls and not the lag (RATES: The ladder law), since there a tile's scale is not a function of its depth. With 2a + 1 = b the overlap is 0, and balanced ternary reads y/2 at the stream 1 0 0 … at no finite lookahead, every input tile's image holding the boundary 1/6 in its interior. At lookahead 3 the script's balanced-ternary reader commits no digit of it in 12 steps, while at signed binary a reader at lookahead 0 commits all 12. At (2, 1), (3, 2), (4, 2), (10, 6) and (10, 9) it never sticks at the stated lookahead on random streams to depth 30, for y/3, 2y/5, −y/7, the mean and xy/ρ.

The hedge

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_e.

If moreover a ≤ b − 1, then y ↦ |y| is read at lookahead 0, while on the all-zero stream the sign of y is read at no depth. The reader copies the prefix, negated when its first nonzero digit is negative, and writes zeros while the prefix is zero. It never learns the sign; it hedges with the same all-zero prefix that makes the sign unreadable. Continuity, not information, is what a reader needs.

Proof. With first nonzero digit d_i, |V| − ρb^(−t) ≥ b^(−i)(1 − ρ) ≥ 0, so every completion is 0 or has the sign of d_i, and the absolute values of the tile form the tile of the prefix the reader writes. The all-zero tile holds both signs at every depth.

At a > b − 1 the first nonzero digit no longer fixes the sign: at (2, 2) the prefix 1 has the tile [−1/2, 3/2]. The script checks every prefix to depth 5 at (2, 1) and (3, 1) and to depth 3 at (10, 6) and (10, 9); balanced ternary (3, 1) has no overlap, and the proof, which needs only ρ ≤ 1, holds there too.

What the purchase spends

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_p.

  • - The order blur. Two depth-t tiles of values V < V′ have completions in both orders iff V′ − V < 2ρb^(−t). A tile clear of the ends then has its order open against 2⌈2a/(b − 1)⌉ − 1 tiles, itself included: three at (2, 1) and (10, 6), where the standard digits leave one. The band of open order, one tile wide under standard digits, widens to 2⌈2a/(b − 1)⌉ − 1 tiles.
  • - No normalization. The tile of the prefix 1 0 … 0 is [1/b − ρb^(−t), 1/b + ρb^(−t)] and holds 1/b in its interior at every depth, so neither the exponent of y nor its standard string is read on that stream.
  • - Division's bit. y ↦ x/y is read at no depth on a y-prefix of zeros, whose tile holds values of both signs arbitrarily near 0. Given a certificate y ≥ η > 0, η x/y is (1 + ρ/η)-Lipschitz on [−ρ, ρ] × [η, ρ], and the redundant reader reads it with each y-tile clipped to [η, ρ]. A lower bound on |y| is archimedean data the cover cannot supply.

Proof. The tiles are intervals of radius ρb^(−t) about values spaced b^(−t), and the rest is interval arithmetic.

The sign's non-locality is not on this list: a balanced non-redundant end has it already. The script counts open-order sets of exactly three at (2, 1), (10, 6) and (3, 2), sees the tile of 1 0 … 0 hold 1/b in its interior at depths 1 to 10, and reads η x/y at η = 1/2 at (b, a) = (2, 1) at lookahead 3 on 100 random streams.

The redundancy leak

Tier: criterion.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_p.

Read an integer with t + j signed digits {−a, …, a}, 2a + 1 > b and j ≥ 1. For a prime p ∣ b, the residues mod p are uniform over a prefix's completions, counted once per value or once per string, iff p ∣ 2a + 1. Counted per value the bias, the largest gap between a residue's share of the completions and 1/p, is below 1/(2aR_j + 1), R_j = (b^j − 1)/(b − 1), and vanishes with j. Counted per string it does not depend on j.

Proof. With V the integer a prefix's t digits write, its completions are V b^j + z with z ∈ [−aR_j, aR_j], once each as values. Since p ∣ b, R_j ≡ 1 and z ≡ d₀ mod p, d₀ the last digit. The first count is uniform iff p ∣ 2aR_j + 1 ≡ 2a + 1, and a string's residue is its last digit's, uniform over {−a, …, a} iff p ∣ 2a + 1.

The standard digits hide every p ∣ b exactly (the two-ends law), so redundancy degrades that hiding to a bias. This purchase spends a finite place's hiding, not archimedean data. Every even base leaks parity, and an odd radical can restore exact hiding: b = 9, a = 7 hides 3. The script reads all 132 (b, a, p, j) with b ≤ 10, a ≤ b − 1 and 1 ≤ j ≤ 4.

The period law

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_m.

Standard digits, n ↦ un with u ≥ 2, κ = ⌊log_b u⌋, input depth t + c and output depth t, with lookahead c ≥ 1. A deep input tile [P b^j, (P + 1)b^j), j ≥ 1, is an exception when its image meets two output tiles. With θ = 1 when uP has t + c + κ + 1 digits and 0 otherwise, and τ = c + κ + θ, every b^τ consecutive P of one θ hold exactly

γ ⌊u(b^j − 1)/(b^j γ)⌋    exceptions,  γ = gcd(u, b^τ),

u − γ when b^j γ > u. The count is 0 iff u ∣ b^τ, that is iff rad(u) ∣ rad(b) and τ ≥ max_p ⌈v_p(u)/v_p(b)⌉: the numerator criterion's multiplier half, at its depth threshold, is the zero locus of one density. For gcd(u, b) = 1, on tiles longer than u (b^j > u), over the (t + c)-digit P as t grows, the density of exceptions tends to

(1 − 1/u)/b^c      counted per tile, and
(1 − 1/u) H(ξ)/b^c with each tile P weighted log_b((P + 1)/P),

ξ = {log_b u}, H(ξ) = b^(ξ−1)(1 + (b − 1)(1 − ξ)). Under counting, the split between the classes θ = 0 and θ = 1, which the multiplier's mantissa sets, cancels, leaving the factor 1 − 1/u. The log weights keep the mantissa's share, in H(ξ), and H > 1 on 0 < ξ < 1, so they see more exceptions at every base.

Proof. The image's output tiles have length b^J, J = j + τ, and the tile is an exception iff a multiple of b^J lies in (uPb^j, uPb^j + u(b^j − 1)], iff 1 ≤ σ ≤ u(b^j − 1)/b^j with σ = (−uP) mod b^τ; σ = 0 fails since b^τ > u. Over b^τ consecutive P, σ runs over the multiples of γ, each γ times, which gives the count. It is 0 iff γ > u(1 − b^(−j)), and as γ ∣ u and 1 − b^(−j) ≥ 1/2, iff γ = u. For the densities the count is u − 1 per b^τ, and the class θ = 0 holds the fraction w₀ = (b^(κ+1) − u)/(u(b − 1)) of the P, so the density tends to (u − 1)b^(−c−κ)(w₀ + (1 − w₀)/b) = (1 − 1/u)/b^c. Under the log weights the classes hold 1 − ξ and ξ, which gives H. ln H is concave and 0 at ξ = 0 and 1, so it is positive between.

The script counts each of 1257 cases (base, multiplier, lookahead, depth, class, phase) at bases 2, 6 and 10, multipliers 2 to 12 and 25, each exactly on the formula, with every zero where u ∣ b^τ. At (b, u, c, j) = (10, 3, 1, 2) the densities are 0.066667 = 1/15 and 0.114118, and at (2, 3, 1, 2) they are 0.333336 and 0.353762 against 0.353759.

The dual Haar measure

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_m.

The mantissas form the compact group R_>0/b^Z, the scale circle, whose Haar measure is log-uniform. The mantissas of the depth-t leading tiles with prefix P lie in the arc [P, P + 1)·b^Z, of Haar measure log_b((P + 1)/P): Benford's law is the Haar measure of these arcs. The arcs have unequal Haar measure, which is why a density at the leading end must name its measure. A trailing tile is a coset of b^t Z, the integers in a coset of b^t Z_b in the b-adic integers Z_b, and those cosets have equal Haar measure b^(−t), which is why a trailing density of a union of residue classes needs no named measure.

Proof. log_b carries the scale circle onto R/Z and Haar onto Lebesgue measure. Haar measure is invariant under multiplication by every q > 0 because log is a homomorphism, so a fixed arc keeps its mass when the measure is pushed forward.

Log-uniformity as the scale-invariant law is known (Pinkham, Ann. Math. Statist. 32, 1961; Hill, Statist. Sci. 10, 1995), and the period law's factor H > 1 is what it changes. The script's positive control: leading digit 1 carries 0.301030 at base 10 and keeps it under multiplication by 3 to 1e-5, while the counting share of that tile moves from 0.1111 to 0.3704.

Each end prices in its own metric

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; dual.py::section_k, dual.py::section_0, reading.py::section_r.

One map, ⌊n/m⌋ with m ≥ 2 and b ∤ m, has an exact price at both ends. The trailing end reads it at exactly max_(p∣m) ⌈v_p(m)/v_p(b)⌉ digits of lookahead, a maximum over the places, infinite where some p ∤ b. The leading end reads it at exactly digits_b(m) = ⌊Σ_p v_p(m) log_b p⌋ + 1, a sum over the places, each weighted by its size. At base 10, ⌊n/4⌋ costs 2 at the trailing end and 1 at the leading end, and ⌊n/3⌋ is read at no finite lookahead at the trailing end and at 1 at the leading end. By analogy, the ultrametric triangle inequality prices by the maximum and the archimedean one by the sum: the two kinds of absolute value Ostrowski's theorem allows on Q.

Proof. The trailing price is the least c with m ∣ b^c, by READING: The division criterion on the chain b^t. The leading price is the least-lookahead law at h = m, K = 0 and Δ = m ≤ (b − 1)b^(digits_b(m)).
