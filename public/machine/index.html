MACHINE — a state that only multiplies, read as a machine

The object: a state M that only ever multiplies, M ↦ Mm, driven by a program that may read the state before each move. Read as a counter machine, the depths v_p(M) only rise, so the bare machine has no decrement. The question is whether that settles it: which reads leave a program's halting decidable, and which make it universal. Universality is Minsky's: two counters with increment and a decrement fused to a zero-test (Minsky 1967). The verifiers are machine.py, borrow.py and comparison.py.

  1. The depth machine decides its halting theorem
  2. Clearing a place decides and dividing once does not theorem
  3. One form decides and two can run Minsky theorem
  4. Two places at the ratios 1 and α/β run FRACTRAN theorem
  5. The headroom is a bank of Minsky counters theorem
  6. A blind read decides and a sighted read is universal theorem

λ is the Carmichael function, W(L) the largest modulus whose λ divides L, and ξ(M) = W(λ(M))/M the headroom (HEADROOM: The headroom): a move m is transparent at M, λ(Mm) = λ(M), exactly when m divides ξ(M). A push is a move by one prime p, M ↦ Mp. An odd prime l is seated in M when l | M, and at a seated l, v_l(ξ) = v_l(λ) + 1 − v_l(M).

The answer, in one line: the depths are counters with no decrement and decide, but the headroom's exponents at the seated primes are counters that pushes move both ways, so a program that reads λ is universal while one that reads only support and squarefreeness, with moves that read λ only to choose a fresh prime, each multiplying by it alone or times a fixed one, is not. Neither the move nor the read settles it alone. The number of classes of the coarsest equivalence that refines the reads and that every move carries into itself is finite for pushes under presence reads and for a move that clears a place, and finite classes decide; it is one per value for a single division or a read of λ, and there the machine is universal. Past finite the count grades nothing: tests comparing depths through one linear form keep one class per value and decide, and two forms, with as many classes, admit a universal program, as two constructions show.

The depth machine decides its halting

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; machine.py::section_decide.

The depth machine takes counters c_i = v_(p_i)(M), the increment the push of p_i and the zero-test the presence read p_i | M. A counter once nonzero stays nonzero, and a program over increments and zero-tests passes from (instruction, zero-pattern) to the next such pair by a map reading nothing else. So its run projects to a deterministic run on the finite set I × {0, +}^η, I the instructions and η the counters, which halts or revisits a pair within |I|·2^η steps, and that decides halting. The quotient agrees with a direct run on all 258,352 cases of every such program of up to three instructions over two counters, from the starts (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0) and (2, 3).

The decrement breaks it. The program "at q₀: if zero halt, else decrement and go to q₁; at q₁: if zero loop, else decrement and go to q₀" halts from 2 and loops from 3, and both runs start at the pair (q₀, +): the magnitude the quotient discards decides whether the run halts.

Clearing a place decides and dividing once does not

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; borrow.py::section_clear; borrow.py::section_classes; borrow.py::section_divide.

Give the depth machine a move that lowers a depth. CLEAR p sends M to M/p^(v_p(M)), dropping the place whole; DIVIDE p sends M to M/p when p divides M, dropping one. Let the tests ask whether p^t divides M for t ≤ T. Under pushes and clears, v ↦ min(v, T) respects every move and every test: min(v + 1, T) is a function of min(v, T), a clear sends the cleared place's depth to 0, and v ≥ t iff min(v, T) ≥ t. So (instruction, min(v_p, T) at each place) is a bisimulation onto |I|·(T + 1)^η states, and halting decides; the depth machine is the clear-free case T = 1. A hand making finitely many pushes between consecutive steps of the program, choosing its primes by any rule, uncomputable or not, moves that quotient by the same law, so program and hand play a finite game, and whether some hand makes the program halt, or every hand does, decides there. Division has no such quotient. DIVIDE^v followed by the presence test separates the depth v from every larger one, so the coarsest equivalence that refines the tests and that every move carries into itself is equality, and two places with pushes, division and the presence test run Minsky's machine. The program "if 2 does not divide M halt, else divide by 2; then if 2 does not divide M loop, else divide by 2 and repeat" halts from 4 and loops from 8, and both runs start at the presence pair (q₀, +).

So what separates the two moves is that equivalence's number of classes and not their direction: a clear takes a depth all the way to zero and leaves T + 1 classes, while division leaves one per depth. A tested depth falling back to zero is not the mark of the danger, as push-and-clear flips its test from yes to no 100 times in 400 steps and still decides. The quotient decider matches a direct run on all 1,016,280 (program, start) cases over pushes, clears and tests at the places 2 and 3, from every start in {0, 1, 2}², with tests to T = 2 in programs of up to two instructions and to T = 1 in programs of three, and on the halve-twice program it gives the starts 4 and 8 one verdict where one halts and the other loops. Counting classes through words of length up to 4, 6 and 8 over the depths 0 to 30 prints T + 1 for push and clear at T = 1, 2 and 3 at each of the three lengths, and two more than the length for push and divide, rising with it. Whether a move erases is a different axis: over the depths 0 to 30, push and division on positive depths merge nothing, clear merges all 31 into one and truncated division, which leaves the depth 0 at 0, merges 0 with 1, so a move that forgets can decide or not, and so can one that forgets nothing (borrow.py::section_classes); reversible machines are universal (Bennett 1973). The count is sufficient and not necessary: the one-form machine below keeps one class per value and decides. In a nondeterministic system a decrement blocks at zero, a one-sided test, and vector addition systems, with no other test, keep reachability decidable (Mayr 1981; Kosaraju 1982) though their counters keep one class per value, while adding a reset loses it (Dufourd, Finkel and Schnoebelen 1998). Past finite, the count stops grading (One form decides and two can run Minsky).

One form decides and two can run Minsky

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; comparison.py::section_one; comparison.py::section_minsky.

Let the tests compare depths: the three-way sign of f − γ for a form f = α·v_p − β·v_q, α, β ≥ 1, and finitely many offsets γ, beside presence reads, the moves still pushes. A comparison flips back and forth as the two depths leapfrog, so the depth machine's argument fails; when every comparison reads one form the machine decides all the same. Its state is the instruction, the presence bits and f. Outside the range of the offsets every comparison answers alike, so the instruction and the bits trace a path that halts or cycles, and the cycle moves f by a fixed drift: a drift of zero or away from the range never returns, and one toward it returns at a point computed with whole cycles skipped, no farther from the range than one move can change f. The states a run can hold at a return or inside the range are finitely many, so it halts or repeats one. Two forms can run Minsky's machine. Read X = v_2 − v_3 and Y = v_3 − v_5 by equality: the push 2 raises X, the pushes 3 then 5 lower it, the pushes 3 then 2 raise Y and the push 5 lowers it, each leaving the other counter where it was. A difference of two depths that only rise is a counter one push lowers.

Both machines keep one class per value in the coarsest equivalence the moves and reads respect, since pushes move f both ways and the offsets tell every value apart by the pushes it takes to cross one. Once the classes are infinite their count, countable either way, grades nothing; what separates the two machines here is how many independent forms the tests read: one form keeps every program decidable, a one-counter machine, and two admit a universal program, shown for differences at three places and for 1:1 beside α:β at two (Two places at the ratios 1 and α/β run FRACTRAN). Whether every pair of independent forms does is not settled here. Over every program of two instructions reading one form, each a push at 2, 3 or 5, a comparison at an offset γ ∈ {−1, 0, 1} or a presence read at 2, 3 or 5, its targets the two instructions and a halt, at the ratio 1:1 or 3:2, from six starts, 164,268 cases taking 1,452 cycle skips, the decider's halts match a direct run and its loops run 200 steps without halting, while the finite quotient of the depth machine, applied as if the signs were presence bits, disagrees with it on 98. Three compiled two-counter programs match a reference interpreter at all 421 trace states of 21 runs, the looping ones cut at 80 steps.

Two places at the ratios 1 and α/β run FRACTRAN

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; comparison.py::section_read; comparison.py::section_block; comparison.py::section_fractran.

At exactly two places the counters above have no linear encoding: the moves add (1, 0) and (0, 1) to (v_p, v_q), so under any two independent forms a program's displacements lie in the cone of two independent vectors, which never holds a vector and its negative, and so cannot move one counter both ways while the other stands still. A cycle does it instead. Read G = c − d and F = α·c − β·d at the two depths c and d, α > β ≥ 1 coprime, and call c = d = u home, where F reads (α − β)u. Walk a word of c-steps and d-steps cyclically until F reads a chosen offset below that, then take c-steps until G reads 0; they do reach it, since F = αG + (α − β)d, so G is at most 0 when F reads the offset with d ≥ u. A cycle of a c-steps and b d-steps drops F by D = bβ − aα, so F first hits the offset at a substep j fixed by u's class mod D, and the new home is ρu + s_j with ρ = 1 + b(α − β)/D and s_j a shift fixed by the substep j and the offset.

The word (c^(β−1) d^(α−1))^k has ρ = α. Its d-steps visit α − 1 consecutive multiples of α modulo α − β, which is prime to α, so they cover every residue there, and the k repetitions cover every class mod D: the word fires at every u, and the substep that fires reads u mod k. The word c^a d^b with a = Pβ − Qα and b = α(P − Q) has ρ = P/Q, and with Q prime to β its d-steps cover every class once b ≥ D, that is once P/Q ≥ α − β + 1. The control zeroes s_j: it fires at a larger offset, a multiple of D, then walks a number of cycles and d-steps a finite table assigns to j, counting them in its states. So on Q | u the home moves to (P/Q)u exactly.

That is FRACTRAN (Conway 1987). Over primes prime to αβ, each fraction multiplied by a power of a prime J dividing α until its value is at least α − β + 1, a step reads u mod each denominator in turn, each read multiplying u by α, and multiplies by the first padded fraction whose denominator divides u; with the primes of α stripped, u is the FRACTRAN state. Each padded fraction is at least α − β + 1 and each read multiplies u by α, so u never falls, and a start holding enough powers of J keeps every home above the offsets the reads fire at. At 3:2 with J = 3, a two-number multiplication program relabelled onto 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19 takes 5²·7³ to 11⁶ in 25 steps and 5³·7² to 11⁶ in 26, step for step with a reference, the home value ending at 63 and 64 digits. The read word fires at five ratios, k to 12 and u to 300, the closed form agreeing with a literal walk that reads only signs on all 18,000 walks; eight blocks at 3:2 land exactly on 320 multiples; and below the threshold ×11/7 never fires on u ≡ 14 mod 21.

The headroom is a bank of Minsky counters

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; machine.py::section_control; machine.py::section_counters; machine.py::section_minsky.

Fix odd primes l₁, …, l_η seated in M and read h_i = v_(l_i)(ξ(M)). Two moves and one read run Minsky's instructions on these counters.

  • - Decrement: push l = l_i. With a = v_l(M) before the push, λ(l^(a+1)) = l^a·(l − 1), and since l − 1 already divides λ, only v_l(λ) can move, to max(v_l(λ), a); with v_l(M) up by one, h_i becomes max(0, h_i − 1), and no sibling moves. This is HEADROOM: The ledger's truncated subtraction at a prime move.
  • - Zero-test: h_i = 0 iff l does not divide ξ(M), iff the push l is not transparent. The test is read off λ before the push, never after.
  • - Increment: push a prime q not dividing M with v_l(q − 1) = v_l(λ) + 1 and q ≢ 1 mod every sibling l_j. Then v_l(λ) rises by one and no sibling's v(λ) or v(M) moves. The conditions are congruences to the coprime moduli l^(v_l(λ)+2) and l_j, so Dirichlet's theorem provides q at every state for any number of counters. The increment is a selector, a choice of a move's fresh prime made from the state, here the least such q read off v_l(λ), and not one fixed move.

So pushes alone, the increment's prime chosen by a selector that reads v_l(λ), run any Minsky program, and a multiply-only world is universal. As with division, h_i decrements followed by the zero-test separate the value h_i from every larger one, so the coarsest equivalence refining the read that every move carries into itself keeps one class per counter value. The three laws hold with no exception and no sibling moved at 2,816 decrement cells over odd M ≤ 3000 and 25,546 increment cells over odd M ≤ 1200 and primes q < 3000, against W computed from its closed form, while a prime 1 mod a sibling (31 from 15) moves that sibling too. From the seed 15, counters at 3 and 5, halts-iff-even, a one-way transfer and a transfer there and back match a reference interpreter step for step from the first counter's values 0 to 6, the second at 0, the counter read off λ and M at every step; over one run at each of two to five counters, three rounds of increments and then one decrement per counter, 56 moves in all, no move shifts a sibling.

A blind read decides and a sighted read is universal

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; machine.py::section_square.

Of the demands GROWTH: The three fates asks of a move, the rate aside, which chooses among moves rather than testing one, two are blind to the headroom, and so is a third test, semisimplicity: independence (gcd(m, M) = 1), new idempotents (m brings a prime M lacks) and Mm squarefree answer, for a fixed m, as functions of M's support on the primes of m and of whether M is squarefree. Give a program the moves above, fixed pushes, the increment selector and the padded decrement below, each selector reading λ, and the support to pass over primes M holds, only to choose its fresh prime, chosen off the fixed moves' primes, which Dirichlet's theorem still allows. An import, the fresh prime a selector pushes, is a prime M lacks, and a selector's move is its import times a fixed prime or none, so on it the three answers are functions of the support on the fixed primes and the squarefree bit, and what they can see is the instruction beside the support on the fixed moves' primes and that bit, both write-once; the depth machine's argument decides it. On the moves these programs offer, the bare push of a seated l, the increment's fresh import and the padded decrement, every blind answer is fixed by the move's kind and that bit, by construction; over the runs at the end of this section, where every move a program makes raises λ, the zero-test takes both values at three of the four (counter, bit) keys. Only the read differs. The other three are sighted: dynamics (λ rises), new orders (the same predicate) and transparency (its complement) answer the zero-test exactly, since a one-bit probe separates what its negation separates. 855 and 2565 share the support {3, 5, 19}, neither squarefree, and read 1 and 0 at the counter prime 3: the blind demands answer alike on both at every 2 ≤ m ≤ 400, and dynamics answers the push 3 differently.

The price is a read and not a move. v_l(ξ) compares λ's depth at l, the most any place's unit group carries there, against M's depth at l, depth across places, and no blind demand reads it. With the move held fixed, the read sets the class count: the push 3 from 21, 57 and 489 passes 1, 2 and 4 times before λ rises, so dynamics tells the three apart, while the blind demands answer the push 3 alike at every state of the three orbits until λ rises, as they must, 3 dividing every state (borrow.py::section_read).

A program may also be held to moving only where its own demand admits. Under dynamics the bare decrement is transparent and so forbidden, but the padded decrement l·q′ is admitted: q′ a fresh prime with q′ ≢ 1 mod every counter prime and q′ − 1 not dividing λ raises λ without touching a counter, and the push of l decrements. The increment raises λ by construction. So a dynamics-autonomous program is universal, spending its probe on the bare push it never makes; the three programs run that way match the reference with every one of 210 moves raising λ. Under transparency an autonomous program's states are divisors of W(λ(seed)) (GROWTH: The three fates), finitely many, and it decides.

Reading and hosting are independent axes. A demand hosts an instruction when it admits some move doing it: independence and semisimplicity host no decrement, which pushes a seated prime; transparency hosts no increment, which moves λ; new idempotents and dynamics host both, the padded decrement bringing a new prime, and so does new orders, whose predicate is dynamics'. With these moves new idempotents cannot read the zero-test; transparency reads it and hosts only the decrement. So among these demands only dynamics and new orders both read and host, and with these moves an autonomous program is universal there alone.
