Numbers read one place at a time

Digit readers: the real place

A reader that sees a number through its digits, a few at a time, and must act before the digits end. How many digits it reads beyond the ones it writes is its lookahead. These pages cover numerations read digit by digit: positional bases read from either end; continued fractions; the Ostrowski numeration, which writes each integer as a sum of multiples of the denominators of an irrational's continued-fraction convergents, read on the circle, and the maps linear in its digits; redundant outputs; and signed-digit streams. Each page says how much lookahead its maps cost (on continued fractions, a lag in quotients and a deficit in scale), or that no lookahead reads them, and the first page asks which place pays for a Lipschitz map that no lookahead reads.
