Redundant arithmetic

What a signed digit set buys a most-significant-first reader, and what it costs, in exact form.

A positional numeration writes a number in radix b over a digit set D = {−a−..a+}. A numeral has two ends, its two poles; the leading end — the most significant digits, the end a floating-point number keeps — sees a value through a window: the first t digits name a cell of values, and the cells refine toward a point as t grows. A function is read at lookahead c when its output's first t digits are a function of the input's first t + c; the least such c is the delay, a function with a finite delay is local at that end, and one with none is not. The set is redundant when its slack ρ = a− + a+ + 1 − b, the count of digits it holds beyond the radix, is at least 1, so that a value has several writings and the cells overlap; balanced, at slack 0, is the non-redundant control. Over the ordinary digits the leading end is local only under an alignment condition, a divisibility tie between the map and the radix's primes; a redundant set dissolves it — every rational slope reads at bounded delay, and addition itself goes local. The purchase in the floating-point pole's own vocabulary is on The dual pole; the laws are here, read against one heuristic. An operation injects a spread, the width of what the unseen digits can still contribute — 2W for addition, W = a− + a+ the set's reach, and 2a for scaling over a symmetric set. With c digits of lookahead an overlapping cover swallows whole any spread up to bcρ, its Lebesgue number, and the Lebesgue margin is the least c at which that reaches the spread injected: a size-only count, reading the slack and nothing of the set's shape or the operation's. Each law below replaces it with a closed form, and the gap between the two is where the shape lives. A digit system is written (b, a) where its set is the symmetric {−a..a}, and below, a table's cell is one (radix, digit set, slope, phase, lookahead).

Scaling by a rational slope

The rational-slope delay criterion criterion

Reading f(n) = ⌊(u/v)n + w/z⌋ from the digits of n, over a symmetric set {−a..a}, is a safety game on one integer state. With L = lcm(v, z) the state scale, N = Lbc the emission step and q = Lu/v the injection step, the residual m — what the reader still owes — moves as m′ = bm + qx − eN under the opponent's digit x and the reader's digit e, and the emitted prefix stays valid iff ⌊m/L⌋ lies in the flush window [−arc, arc], rc the c-digit repunit, overhanging to the right by L − 1, the floor's own truncation. f is local at lookahead c iff the start state, the phase φ = Lw/z, lies in the game's greatest invariant set, the winning set W.

Write E = ⌊aL(vbc − u)/(v(b − 1))⌋ for the endpoint bound, what injecting the extreme digit forever forces on a surviving residual; I for the derived interval, the endpoint bound and the flush window intersected about φ; g = gcd(q, N); and call a class t ∈ ℤ/g saturated when each of its N/g residues mod N has a representative in I. The game is feasible at lookahead c iff the class of φ mod g is saturated, and the delay is the least such c: the class map t ↦ bt − (b − 1)φ (mod g) fixes the start class, so no orbit and no residual enters. At g = 1 the test is a length test — feasible iff I is at least N long, and W = I then. At every g the length test is sufficient, and the only cells where it declines a delay the criterion grants have I shorter than N by less than g. Three consequences: a phase translates the flush window, so it never raises the delay and lowers it by one exactly where the endpoint bound binds on the right while the window binds on the left; the Lebesgue bound max(0, ⌈logb(2au/(v(2a − b + 1)))⌉) is the criterion with its floors dropped, so it misses by at most one digit and in both directions; and b-power slopes bk split at zero — k ≥ 0 reads k exactly, k < 0 reads 0 iff a ≥ b − 1 and 1 otherwise at every depth. Dividing by the radix is a free shift only for digit sets at least {−(b − 1)..b − 1}, which at every radix above 2 is strictly more than redundancy; division by 3 at redundant binary runs at delay zero.

Scope. Criterion, proved for every radix, every symmetric redundant set, every rational slope and every phase. Checks on the derivation: the 54 cells where the length test declines all have I short of N by less than g; of 336 phased cells the two where the phase lowers the delay from 1 to 0 are (2, 1) at slope 3/5 and (10, 6) at slope 1/3, the bound binding on the right at both; of 169 cells the Lebesgue bound is one above the delay at 18 and one below at 14; and the delay tables measured on the dual pole's page are reproduced.

The winning set is a tree-avoidance condition theorem

Modulo N the reader's play is invisible, so the residues reachable from m in j steps are the opponent's alone: Tj(m) = bjm − (b − 1)φrj + q·[−arj, arj], an interval because redundancy makes the j-fold digit sums an interval — the one place the digit set's shape enters. A state wins iff Tj(m) misses the complement of I at every depth: necessity because the winning region sits inside I, sufficiency because a residue of I has a unique representative in I and the endpoint bound hands over the emission. Where I is at least N long the complement is empty, every state of I wins, and the winning set is I, as the length test says: the two proofs agree wherever both apply. From the covering depth J = min{j : 2arj + 1 ≥ N/g}, at which the digit sums cover the subgroup, the reachable set is a full coset and the condition is saturation of the class the orbit sits in; below J it depends on the state's residue mod N and not on its class, so W is not in general a union of classes mod g. A state dodges when it wins though its class is unsaturated. Over the table of every system to radix 12, digit sets to a = 8, slopes to 12/12 and lookahead to 5, 55 cells admit a dodge at all, and 21 carry one — the smallest at (2, 1) with slope 12/5 at lookahead 2, 13 winning states against the 9 the class reading allows, the widest gap 46,771 against 9,371 — all 21 at the redundancy floor 2a + 1 ≤ b + 1, every extra state dodging at depth 1. Where the steps share a factor the winning set is a union of classes only from the covering depth on: at (3, 2) with slope 1/3 under phase 1/8 the survivors are {0, 2, 3} mod 8, no progression. The feasibility verdict is untouched, the start class being the fixed point.

Scope. Theorem at the criterion's scope. The agreement of the two proofs where both apply is checked at 674 of 674 cells of one grid and 1733 of 1733 of a second, with no digit system in common.

The closed count below the covering depth property

Write a state as its class t mod g and its position w in the class, the index of its residue among the class's n = N/g residues, with σ = q/g a unit of ℤ/n and Mj = arj. A class loses depth j when its reachable set lands in an unsaturated class there, and level j is the set of positions failing that depth: one run condition bjw + cj ∈ Dj (mod n), a displacement and a run of positions fixed by the depth, level 0 being "the state lies in I". The complement of I meets a class in one block of λ consecutive positions, λ = 0 exactly at a saturated class and λ ≤ 1 wherever a dodge is possible at all, since an I short of N by g or more leaves no saturated class. The survivors of level j are one interval per gap of the point set σ·[−Mj, Mj] longer than λ, of length gap − λ; the gaps take three lengths from the continued fraction of σ/n, so a class's survivor count is n − Σ min(gapi, λ), and a class can dodge iff its widest gap exceeds λ. The point set only subdivides with a and with j, which is why every dodge sits at the narrowest digit set and at depth 1. Assembling the levels a class loses: every inclusion–exclusion term containing level 0 is a count over the block's λ positions, the one remaining term is a pairwise intersection of two runs, and reducing it by a gcd turns it into two floor-sums whether or not b is a unit mod n. A level contained in a deeper one leaves the union, so the count closes whenever the width — the number of lost levels contained in no other — is at most 2. Two lost levels are ordinary, and one route to them is a rigidity: wherever 2a/(b − 1) is an integer exactly one class is saturated. Width 3 is inhabited, and the closed form survives it at every instance measured because the triple term is empty there: the two shallowest of the three levels never meet, which the next block derives below a threshold on the pair and leaves open above it.

Scope. The run condition, the block, the survivor count and the assembly are properties; the three-distance recursion behind the gap lengths is verified exhaustively at n < 60 and a rule there. The count is exact at 2346 of 2346 class tests below N = 3000. Two lost levels: 262 witnesses below N = 6000, the smallest radix 2, digits {−1, 0, 1}, slope 40/9, lookahead 3, with 37 winning states against 17. The rigidity is a rule at 931 of 931 cells over the 29 digit systems where 2a/(b − 1) is an integer; over the 34 where it is not, 368 of 886 cells have exactly one saturated class too, and no system contradicts its prediction. Width 3: 4844 classes over 29,233 cells at N ≤ 150,000, the smallest at N = 11,664 under radix 6, digits {−4..4}, slope 4384/9; that the shallow pair never meets is a rule at that scope, phase 0. What would break the assembly is a width-3 class with all three pairs non-empty, and none is measured.

The empty-window law: containment and meeting at two radii rule

For levels i < j with Δ = j − i, the pair has one coordinate, the offset y between the deeper run's centre and the image of the shallower one's. Containment holds whenever y ≤ 2MΔ, a radius set by the level difference (sufficient, not necessary); meeting requires y ≤ 2Mj or its mirror arc, a radius set by the deeper level alone; the pair's meeting measure is the share of the circle an offset must land in for a meeting to be possible, and where a class has a third lost level below the two, that third level caps the shallow pair's measure at 2/bd, d the depth from the second level to the third. Between the two radii lie two arcs, the lower arc 2MΔ < y ≤ 2Mj and the upper arc at the top of the circle, where the levels meet without containment; an arc is empty when no offset the pair can attain lies in it. At phase 0 every offset's carry — the value the pair holds mapping to it — has the closed form C(y) = ((Nτ(y) + yκ)/(2ag)) mod n, with κ = 2aq − (2a − b + 1)N, h = gcd(2a, b − 1) and τ(y) = (−y(b − 1)) mod 2a; the containing carries are the progression of step s = κ/(hg), capped at (b − 1)/h. Call a cell rigid when I falls short of N by exactly g − 1 and the endpoint bound's floor is exact: there the step meets its cap. Both arcs read off that step, at every pair measured. The lower arc is empty iff (hrΔ + 1)s ≥ bΔ, which at a rigid cell holds for every b, h and Δ; the upper arc is empty iff nh ≥ 2abΔ + hsymax, ymax the largest offset in the arc's range attained by the class τ = h, whose carries are least, total over every disjoint-arc pair by a landing argument — where the carry wraps it deposits a value in [0, s) at an attainable offset. The dichotomy has instances only where the cap exceeds 1: wherever h = b − 1 the step is 1 and every pair contains; over the table the radices carrying a non-contained pair are 4, 6 and 10. Over every non-contained pair, of any width, none below meeting measure 0.3 meets, the least measure at which any meets being 0.3602 and the first non-empty upper arc sitting at 0.4597.

Scope. The two radii and the cap are properties; the containment radius is sufficient, its converse failing at 284 pairs at N ≤ 60,000 and 1436 at N ≤ 150,000, always by declining a containment that holds; the cap's measured maximum over width-3 shallow pairs is 0.4118. The carry form is a property, 1,868,112 coordinates at 0 misses. The lower-arc law is a property at every rigid cell and a rule at N ≤ 60,000 over the 3516 pairs below meeting measure 0.4; the upper-arc law a rule at N ≤ 60,000, phase 0, 6856 of 6856. That n is odd at every even-radix cell is a property, and no cell at odd radix has an even n to test. The law's edge is soft, a pattern: 0 meetings of 20,128 non-contained pairs below measure 0.3 at N ≤ 150,000, the least meeting measure being 0.4733 at N ≤ 60,000 and 0.3602 at N ≤ 150,000. Open: the phase axis, every arc law above being derived at phase 0 only, and a derivation of the empty shallow pair above measure 0.4, at least 48 shallow pairs sitting there and empty anyway.

Addition

The lookahead criterion for addition criterion

Over a contiguous digit set {−a−..a+} at radix b, the sum of two streams is local at lookahead c = 1 iff ρ ≥ ⌈a−/(b − 1)⌉ + ⌈a+/(b − 1)⌉, the winning set then being exactly [−(a− − ⌈a−/(b − 1)⌉), a+ − ⌈a+/(b − 1)⌉]; c = 2 always suffices and c = 0 never. Necessity is the endpoint bounds plus a residue count — the injected digit stamps the successor's class mod b, so a nonempty winning set spans b consecutive residuals, which the interval fits exactly when the inequality holds; sufficiency is that interval's invariance. Unpacked, the inequality reads ρ ≥ 2 and b ≥ 3 and D is not a slack-2 set with an endpoint at magnitude 1: an endpoint at magnitude x spends ⌈x/(b − 1)⌉ units of slack, an endpoint at 1 a full unit, an endpoint at 0 none. The clause form is the hardware field's own — the generalized signed-digit framework (Parhami, IEEE Transactions on Computers, 1990, Theorem 1) proves its carry-free addition applicable iff ρ ≥ 3 when an endpoint has magnitude 1 and ρ ≥ 2 otherwise, at radix ≥ 3, its limited-carry algorithm (Theorem 5) covering every remaining set. The statement here is locality of any window reader rather than one algorithm's applicability, so its necessity side bars every design and not one recipe.

Scope. Criterion, proved for every radix b ≥ 2 and every contiguous digit set with ρ ≥ 1; the proof checked against the game solved at 354 cells.

The margin's wedge theorem

The Lebesgue heuristic bcρ ≥ 2W never underpredicts addition's lookahead, and overpredicts by exactly one digit precisely on the wedge (b ≥ 3, ρ = 2, least endpoint ≠ 1) ∪ (b = 3, ρ = 3) — equivalently ρ ≥ ⌈a−/(b − 1)⌉ + ⌈a+/(b − 1)⌉ with ρ(b − 2) < 2(b − 1): substituting W = ρ + b − 1 shows the margin's grant reads only radix and slack, never the set's shape. Over symmetric sets alone the wedge is invisible, their slack 2a + 1 − b having parity opposite the radix's, so that "ρ = 2" is all of it that shows; the asymmetric sets fill the other half, and the slack-only sentence breaks at radix 2, where a− = a+ = 2 with ρ = 3 reads 2. One margin serves two operations: addition's endpoint a − ⌈a/(b − 1)⌉ is the slope criterion's E at u/v = 2 and c = 1 verbatim, the Lebesgue count is that shape-aware object with the floors dropped, and the Lebesgue bound for scaling by an integer walks the same wedge, one digit high at ρ = 2 and at the radix-3 ρ = 3 cells.

Scope. Theorem at the criterion's scope. The endpoint identity checked at 760 of 760 cells over radices 3..40; the scaling bound's walk a rule at scanned scope, multipliers 2, 3, 5 and 7 over six symmetric systems and eight asymmetric cells.
