Constants

Named analytic constants — Golomb–Dickman, a Poisson–Dirichlet spectrum, Artin — as the solvency thresholds of designed growth economies.

The primorial schedule seats the primes in order: the k-th rung is pk# = 2·3·5⋯pk. Its unit count φ(pk#) = ∏i≤k(pi − 1) is the rung's capacity, and its period λ(pk#) = lcm(pi − 1), the exponent of the unit group, its dynamical complexity. A step is transparent when the new prime adds no complexity, (pk−1) | λ(pk−1#); the transparent steps have density 1 (the density theorem), and the complexity has an exact ledger in the shifted primes p − 1 (the complexity ledger). What happens when a growth fate is designed to have a numerical threshold? Overlay on the schedule a reserve that earns ρ log p at each prime and pays a chosen cost w(p) ≥ 0:

R(k) = ρ·θ(pk) − Σi≤k w(pi),  θ(x) = Σp≤x log p.

The reserve thrives when R → +∞ and sinks otherwise, and which happens is decided by a single number. The numbers that appear are the analytic-prime-constant zoo. Every in-range value below is at k = 104, the first 104 primes, p ≤ 104,729.

The realizer

The realizer identity rule

R(k) = θ(pk)·[ρ − S(k)/θ(pk)] with S = Σ w, so the reserve thrives iff ρ exceeds ρc(w) = lim Σ w(pi)/θ(pk), the weight's Cesàro log-average, when it converges. Calibration weights land exactly: w = log p realizes ρc = 1.000000, sinking at ρ = 0.9 and thriving at 1.1, and w = ½ log p realizes 0.500000. Every nonnegative value is therefore trivially reachable — w = c·log p gives ρc = c — and the realizer re-expresses a prime log-average, never derives it. The content is which structured weights land on which named constants: the two families below.

Scope. Elementary, from the reserve's per-step drift; the calibrations are exact in range.

The rank family

The rank family rule

Weight wj(p) = log Pj(p−1), the j-th largest prime factor of p − 1 with multiplicity, P+ denoting P1, the largest. The factors partition log(p−1) exactly — Σj log Pj(p−1) = log(p−1) at every prime — so the thresholds sum to 1 in the limit, Σj ρc(j) = log φ/θ → 1, in range 0.999971. The spectrum is ordered, positive, decreasing: ρc(1..3) = 0.5791, 0.1931, 0.0980, the tail over ranks ≥ 4 0.1298. In the limit, under Elliott–Halberstam, it is the Poisson–Dirichlet(1) spectrum of the Dickman ordered factorization (0.6243, 0.2096, 0.0883, …), the first entry the Golomb–Dickman constant; the identification is open unconditionally. The integer positive control — the same machinery on unshifted n ≤ x — gives [0.6523, 0.2006, 0.0789], inside the Poisson–Dirichlet bands and above the shifted value at j = 1: the two straddle the limit, and the finite range confirms the ordered spectrum and the conservation, not the limit values.

Scope. The partition and the per-k conservation are exact, Σ → 1 elementary; the in-range values are observations. The Poisson–Dirichlet identification is a theorem under Elliott–Halberstam — level of distribution 1 gives the full joint law (Bharadwaj–Rodgers, arXiv:2402.11884; the j = 1 Dickman case is Pomerance's conjecture, proved under EH by Granville–Wang) — and open unconditionally: shifted primes hold level ½ (Bombieri–Vinogradov), buying restricted-support correlations only.

The density family

The density family rule

Weight w(p) = log p · 1[E(p)], the indicator of an event E of prime density δ, realizes ρc = δ: the threshold is the density itself, the log-weighted density equalling the natural one. E = “p ≡ 1 mod 4” gives 0.4987 → ½ (Dirichlet). E = “2 is a primitive root mod p” gives 0.3749 — Artin's constant 0.3740 under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (Hooley), an infinite intersection of Kummer conditions rather than a single Chebotarev class. A density constant is a different kind from the rank family's Dickman size-averages; the one lever w reads both kinds.

Scope. Threshold = density is a rule; the in-range values are observations. The Artin value is Hooley's theorem under GRH; the ½ anchor is Dirichlet's theorem.

The hinge

The count reserve is settled rule

A count-weighted sibling of the size weight — earn ρ per step, pay 1 per raise of λ, the complexity ledger's jumps — has R(k) = R₀ + ρk − Nnt(k), Nnt counting the non-transparent steps, and thrives iff ρ exceeds the limsup non-transparent density, exactly. Since the transparency density → 1 is a theorem, that threshold is 0: the count reserve thrives for every positive reward, a binary fate that once depended on an estimate and is now cashed.

Scope. The threshold identity is elementary and the walked reserve matches its closed form in range; the vanishing is the density theorem's.

The collision hinge property

One distinction explains why the count reserve collapses to 0 while the size reserve, rank 1 above, holds a positive constant. log φ counts every hit of every prime power in the shifted primes with multiplicity; log λ counts each hit power once. Their gap is the collision mass — prime powers dividing several shifted primes — and α = log λ/log φ = 1 − collision/log φ, exactly. In range the collision mass is 0.8349 of log φ (α = 0.1651), and nearly all of log λ is distinct large primes: the mass from primes above √pk is 0.9749, while the Linnik range — prime powers up to pk1/5, every one of which divides some shifted prime in range — carries 4.5·10−4. α → 0 is equivalent to transparency density → 1 (the collision equivalence), and both hold, since log lcm{p−1 : p ≤ x} = o(x) is proved unconditionally. The hinge: the count reserve (lcm, distinct) loses the collision mass and its threshold is exactly 0; the size reserve (product, multiplicity) keeps every occurrence and its threshold is positive and stable in range. The collapse's rate constant and the size limit's identification with λGD both stay open unconditionally, in the same shifted-prime circle.

Scope. The identity is exact, three computations agreeing to 10−6 at k ≤ 104; the mass locus is an observation; the equivalence and the vanishing are theorems; the rate's constant is the open residual.

The settled end

The abscissa reserve observation

A genuinely different solvency mechanism — the least s at which D(s) = Σp 1/P+(p−1)s converges, the Dirichlet abscissa σc, rather than an average — realizes no constant, and the reason is an inversion. Split the shifted primes at the square root, rough where P+(p−1) > √(p−1) and smooth otherwise: the Cesàro log-weight puts 0.7456 of its mass on the rough set, the reciprocal weight 1/P+2 puts 0.0461 there, and the band P+ ≤ 11 alone carries 0.7638 of D(2). That tail is carried by smooth shifted primes — P+ = 2 exactly the Fermat primes, frozen at 5 in range; P+ = 3 the Pierpont primes — and Dk(2) climbs 2.25 → 12.66 with a non-vanishing tail, the last decade carrying half the sum. Divergence is a theorem at every computed exponent: friable-shifted-prime counts force D(s) = +∞ for every s < 1/0.2844 ≈ 3.52, so σc > 3.51 and only the endpoint σc = +∞ stays open — where Pierpont infinitude is a sufficient certificate, not an equivalent. The integer control isolates the shift at that endpoint: unshifted smooth counts are unconditionally infinite (3-smooth counts in range: 100 unshifted against 31 shifted), so the integer endpoint is provable while the shifted one waits.

Scope. The inversion and the climb are observations, k ≤ 104. The fixed-s divergence is Lichtman's theorem (arXiv:2211.09641, Thm 1.1, won by extending the Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec mean-value range; Baker–Harman 1998 before it); the endpoint is open.

verifier: explore_abscissa_reserve.py

One circle governs the whole page: the shifted-prime level of distribution — Bombieri–Vinogradov at ½, Elliott–Halberstam at 1, the Fouvry and Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec range extensions between. The Cesàro average reads the bulk of P+(p−1), where the constants are open — the Poisson–Dirichlet spectrum under EH, Artin under GRH. The abscissa reads the smooth tail, where the same circle's theorems already force degeneracy, leaving existence only the endpoint. The two ways to ask whether a designed economy is solvent read the two ends of one distribution, and the tail end, where existence lives, is the one the theorems already own.
