The widened output

What a redundant Ostrowski alphabet buys the arithmetic: let the reader write more than legality allows, and ×m acquires a bounded reader while ⌊n/m⌋ does not — and finishing on an integer costs that reader a floor of its own.

An Ostrowski window at an irrational α = [0; a1, a2, …] writes n = Σ dk qk over the denominators qk of α's continued-fraction convergents pk/qk, the ak being its partial quotients; the golden, silver and bronze windows have every quotient 1, 2 and 3. Legality — the greedy writing, the unique one — caps d0 at a1 − 1 and dk at ak+1, and forces a zero below any digit sitting at its cap; the below-a-cap rule is that second clause. The depth-t cell of a string is the set of strings agreeing with it on their t low-order digits. A window reads a map at lookahead c when the output's depth-t cell is a function of the input's depth-(t + c) cell, and a map is gated when no finite lookahead serves every t; the mechanism of a gate is a tear, two inputs agreeing ever deeper whose images part at one fixed position, that position the tear's address. Put θk = qkα − pk, the signed error of the k-th convergent, its absolute value falling geometrically; a string's star Σ dkθk read modulo 1 is its value on the circle, and the completion is the map on infinite strings, whose value is a circle point. Legally, ×m and ⌊n/m⌋ are gated at every irrational window while n + 1 reads at lookahead 1 — the arithmetic gate, on Quadratic windows. Here the output alphabet is widened: the reader may emit digits up to ak+1 + s, and up to a1 − 1 + s0 at position 0, with the below-a-cap rule dropped, while the input stays legal. The pair (s, s0) is the slack; the output strings are a redundant writing, several per integer. Read an output string's real star Σ ekθk as a real number rather than a circle point — two writings of one integer differ by an integer there — and the real stars of all admissible strings fill an interval, the range, of length 1 + E, the excess E = s0α + s Σk≥1|θk|; the range from position L is the same interval for the strings supported on positions L and above.

The two tears

The tear survives at zero excess theorem

Drop the below-a-cap rule and keep every cap, so E = 0. The range is then exactly [−α, 1 − α], and ×m's tear keeps the address it had. The pairs that prove the gate agree ever deeper while their images sit on opposite sides of one circle point, so their images' real stars close on the range's two endpoints, one image to each; and a string whose real star is within δ of an endpoint agrees with the extremal string (0, a2, 0, a4, …) or (a1 − 1, 0, a3, 0, …) at every position with |θk| > δ, the difference being a sum of non-negative terms. Whatever the reader chooses, the two images part where those two strings part, at the lowest position where they differ. Dropping the rule alone buys ×m nothing.

Scope. Theorem at every irrational window; periodicity used nowhere.

verifier: explore_redundant_ostrowski.py

The completion's reader exists at any positive excess theorem

Raise any cap, so E > 0. Every circle point then has a real lift interior to the range, and a writing interior to its cell at every depth: the ranges of two consecutive members — the real stars of the strings continuing a fixed prefix through position L with digit eL, one member per value of that digit — overlap by |θL+1| + s Σk>L|θk|. The overlap lemma — an interval shorter than the overlap sits inside one member — hands ×m a bottom-up reader: at lookahead c what the reader does not yet know of the image is an interval under 2m|θL+c|, so a digit keeping the real star in range exists at every position once 2m|θk+c−1| ≤ (1 + s)|θk| at every k ≥ 1 and 2m|θc| ≤ E. That is the overlap bound: bounded, c = O(log(2m/(1 + s))) at every irrational window, and raising position 0's cap by one is enough. So ×m's tear was the writing's — z ↦ mz is continuous on the circle, and it was legality's uniqueness that forced the images apart.

Scope. Theorem at every irrational window and every slack with E > 0. The reader it hands over is the completion's, the map on infinite strings; what it costs to land on an integer is the rest of this page.

verifier: explore_redundant_ostrowski.py

The floors stay torn at every slack theorem

⌊n/m⌋ does not follow. Two tails of different residue mod m send one converging input point to images a fixed nonzero rotation apart, and the cells a common output prefix pins those images to shrink as the prefix grows, so no reader at any lookahead can hand them one prefix. The floors' tear is the map's: x ↦ x/m is m-valued on the circle and the branch is n mod m, a function of every digit, which no alphabet widens away. The two tears are therefore different objects. The successor sits with the multiplications: n + 1 reads at lookahead 0 the moment the below-a-cap rule is dropped, at zero slack, so its legal lookahead 1 was that rule's and never the carry's — the overflow of a digit into the position above.

Scope. Theorem at every irrational window and every slack.

Landing on an integer

That reader is the completion's, not the integers' property

The residual at a position is the integer the reader still owes there, the input's image less what it has written below. A real star inside the range from position L does not make the residual writable from position L up, every value writable from there being 0 or at least qL, which the star cannot see: at the golden window with s = s0 = 1 a residual of 3 at position 3 is below q4 = 5 and so unwritable from position 4, yet its star stays in the range from position 4 under two of the three digits the reader could write at position 3, and the one digit that does write it is not the one a residual of 6 would want, so no rule on the star alone chooses right. An integer input must also flush, reaching residual 0 under zero input, so the integer reader carries the whole set of residuals still possible — the alive offsets — in the residual game on the finite automaton that decides a linear relation between digit strings at a periodic window (The lookahead limit): the reader chooses its output digit before the next input digit shows, holds a set of alive offsets per state, and must flush. At a periodic window the game is finite and exact, and its least winning lookahead is the integer lookahead cint.

Scope. Property, the golden exhibit exact. The integer lookahead is read exactly over the grid: six periodic windows — golden, silver, bronze, √3 − 1 and two of period 4 — at ×2..×5 over six slack pairs, 144 cells, of which 120 read at some lookahead; every winning strategy checked on 1,500 inputs, the legal alphabet through the same game reproducing the gate's verdicts as the control. Every reading cell reads at or under the overlap bound; position 0 raised alone reads every ×m at 2 to 6; lookahead 0 exactly where the digitwise writing ek = m dk fits at every position past the first; and no cell at lookahead 1.

The universal reader over bounded quotients rule

Write a residual in the frame of two consecutive convergents, x qt + y qt−1: the pair (x, y) is a bounded integer pair whose step under a digit reads the local quotient at+1 and nothing older. So the game at every window with partial quotients at most A is one finite game, the opponent choosing each quotient as it reveals each digit, and its winning strategy is a universal reader, seeing the digits through dt+c and the quotients through at+c+1 and nothing else of the window. ×2 at s = s0 = 1 reads at lookahead 2 at every irrational window with quotients at most 3, and ×3 at s = s0 = 1 at exactly 3 over quotients at most 2 — one above every periodic window of that class, all 2,538 periodic quotient patterns of period at most 14 that are not repetitions of a shorter one having a reader of their own at 2. Not knowing the next quotient costs the one unit: a reader at lookahead c can be run at c + 1 by ignoring the extra digit, so a loss at 2 is a loss at 1 and 0 too. The game is solved inside a box, |x| ≤ X and |y| ≤ Y, and a win found in any box is a win, while a loss is a loss only in a box containing every pair a flushing play can occupy. That set is bounded — the star by the future, through Σk≥t ak+1|θk| = |θt−1| + |θt|, and the residual by the past, at qt+1 — by Y = 2(1 + s) + 2m + max(m, 1 + s) and X the same with that maximum multiplied by A + 1, no lookahead term anywhere in it; at the ×3 cell that box is (X, Y) = (19, 13). A single window whose quotients are 1 repeated T times and then A, periodically, has a reader of its own for ×2 at s = s0 = 1 at lookahead 2 for every T ≤ 6 at A = 2, 3, 4 and every T ≤ 4 at A = 6: the run of ones costs nothing.

Scope. Rule: the strategies extracted and run on the integers below 1000 at twelve random windows of sixty quotients over each alphabet and at its periodic windows of period at most 2, value, caps and flush checked, the one-letter alphabets reproducing the grid's cells as the control. The "exactly" is the losing half, an exhaustive solve at lookahead 2 at three boxes each containing (19, 13). The box is derived and then read back off the trajectories that flush, at six winning cells each solved in a box several times it. At unbounded partial quotients the integer reader stays open, the game's state being finite only under a bound.

The price of the flush

The flush floor: lookahead 0 or at least 2, never 1 rule

Let amax be the window's largest partial quotient. cint is 0 when s ≥ (m − 1)amax, whatever s0, and at least 2 otherwise. Call s = (m − 1)amax the digitwise line: at or above it the digitwise writing fits at every position past the first, and position 0 needs no slack of its own, its overflow spilling up into q1 = a1. What forbids 1 is that the flushed state is the exposed one. A reader holding residual 0 from some position on must, after a late digit at its cap ak+1 qk over a zero, write m ak+1 qk from position k − c with nothing below it. From position k − 1 that integer has one writing within the caps, ek = m ak+1 — the qk−1-coefficient of any string from there is a multiple of qk, hence 0 once qk−1 > m ak+1 — and it breaks the cap ak+1 + s exactly when s < (m − 1)ak+1. An overflow at position k spills into the two positions below it, qk = ak qk−1 + qk−2, and a reader flushed at lookahead 0 or 1 has already written both. In the game's own terms the flush condition does not trim the winning set at lookahead 1, it empties it.

Scope. Rule, proved at every periodic window and every m ≥ 2; the emptying read at all 115 cells below the digitwise line among the grid's 120 reading cells and the ten band cells solved beside the grid. Past periodicity the two halves carry on their own hypotheses — the zero half wherever s ≥ (m − 1)ak+1 at every k, the floor wherever (m − 1)ak+1 > s at infinitely many k with qk−1 > m ak+1, which bounded quotients supply.

The price of the flush, and the law it reads observation

Two readers bound the integer reader from below. The released reader is the same game with the flush condition dropped — it must keep some offset alive forever and need never land on the value — with least winning lookahead csaf. And L* is the lookahead of a reader shown the whole input from a flushed state: for each legal tail τ that can arrive after the reader has flushed at position k, take the least number of positions below k from which m·val(τ) has a writing within the caps, and let L* be the largest of those over all such tails — an exact object, the residuals any output can reach forming a finite automaton on the tail's digits. The price of the flush is cint − csaf. It is 0 at 91 grid cells, 1 at 28 and 2 at one; where it is nonzero the reader lands on 2, except at two cells landing on 4 — the period-4 window (1, 1, 1, 2) at ×5 — where L* is set by a late tail rather than a late digit. And at every cell decided, cint = max(csaf, L*). The floor's sharpest exhibit is the band 2m − 1 ≤ s < (m − 1)amax, where the overlap bound grants the completion's reader lookahead 1 and the digitwise writing does not fit: it opens at amax > 2 + 1/(m − 1), no cell of the grid lies in it, and at every band cell read the integer reader reads 2 — the residual deliverable one digit ahead and the flush refusing it anyway.

Scope. Both bounds are proved. The equality cint = max(csaf, L*) is an observation at 153 cells — the grid's 120 reading cells and 33 band cells, each decided exactly by the automaton — with no sufficiency proof: what a proof must rule out is a commitment, two continuations of the seen digits demanding two different pending writings of one residual that the box does not tell apart. The band's price is 1 at 29 cells and 2 at four. The band holds 42 cells under s ≤ 12: ten solved beside the grid, 23 more since, and the nine undecided — the window with every quotient 5 at ×4 and ×5 from s = 9, and (2, 1, 3, 1) at ×5 from s = 10 — have games too large to solve.

The flush is the whole price theorem

The completion's reader — which tracks no offsets and asks only that the real star stay in range — has a least lookahead ccomp of its own, and it equals csaf. The released reader's state is a finite set of alive offsets, one per integer lift of the target circle point, each as a frame pair stepping to one successor and dropped once it leaves a box scaled to its depth; a winning play keeps the set non-empty forever, so by König's lemma one offset survives forever, and the box forces that offset's residual to 0 — the box holds a star under a fixed multiple of |θt| at depth t, and its width never enters — making the written string a writing of the target lifted by that offset's lift. Wherever the completion's reader is constrained at all, the continuations of one seen prefix have residuals filling an interval inside a disjoint union of closed members, so the lifts agree across the prefix and the released reader's strategy wins the completion's game at the same lookahead. Conversely a winning completion strategy's true-lift offset has residual at most (1 + s)(|θt−1| + |θt|) + m(|θt| + |θt+1|), by the tail lemma Σj≥t|θj| ≤ |θt−1| + |θt|, an equality at golden — inside the reader's own box with a unit of θ to spare. So the offset tracking, the box and the frame cost nothing in lookahead anywhere, and the whole distance from the integer reader down to the completion's is the flush's own price. No reading of a state's present offsets against the members is the criterion: at √3 − 1 at ×3 and slack (1, 0) a winning released play reaches a state none of whose alive offsets sits, by its present star, inside a member at its depth, and that state completes all the same. The overlap bound computed with the members' true widths lies under the bound stated above at 89 of 120 cells and is still not the minimum — where the digitwise writing fits it overstates by up to two, the winning placements being isolated points no condition quantified over all placements can see.

Scope. Theorem at every purely periodic window, every m ≥ 1 and every slack pair with s0 ≤ s + 1, the one place the box's derivation enters. Verified at the grid's 120 reading cells and the ten band cells solved beside the grid: at ten of the 120 the completion's game played on the real star as one interval does not decide, and they are decided by playing it on the circle instead, each input taking its own lift, which costs a finite prefix game at the depths whose range is a full turn or more; and the converse's containment is printed at all 154 cells, the grid's 144 and those ten. Not claimed: any window off periodicity, where there is no frame and no box; and nothing about the finite game's size.

What a proof of the price law must supply observation

Call a state safe when the released reader can go on from it forever, and flushable when some safe play from it reaches residual 0 under zero input. The winning set at the winning lookahead is exactly the flushable states, at every grid cell, and at every losing lookahead from csaf up one round of removing states with no safe move annihilates that set rather than trimming it. The lemma a proof must supply is that at c ≥ max(csaf, L*) every flushable state has a digit keeping every reply flushable. Its invariant is not "safe and the residual writable" — five cells hold such a state that every winning strategy avoids — and no digit rule uniform over the states serves. Sort each loss by whether it is met after the reader has flushed or while it is still mid-flush: at 21 of the 41 losing (cell, lookahead) pairs losses met after flushing drag down mid-flush states, losses met mid-flush never drag down a flushed one, and at no cell are all the losses met on one side; and the states that seed the collapse recur verbatim among the winning lookahead's flushable states, so having a serving digit is a function of the state and the lookahead, and no lookahead-free invariant of the state can carry the proof. A flushable interior deep below the flush needs its own argument at exactly two pairs, both at the (1, 1, 1, 2) window at ×5, where a late tail sets L*.

Scope. Observation at the 130 cells — the grid's 120 reading cells and the ten band cells solved beside the grid — and their 41 losing pairs.
