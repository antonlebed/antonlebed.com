The order wall

Sign computed from digits is finite-state and not finite-window: what the wall is made of, counted exactly for every finite digit set.

A numeration reads a value through its leading digits to some precision, and reads an operation at lookahead c when the output's digits to precision t are a function of the input's to precision t + c (The dual pole, where the reading criterion lives). The digit set decides what can be read. Digits {−a..a} in radix b with 2a + 1 = b are balanced, one spelling per value, non-redundant; with 2a + 1 > b they are redundant, a value having many spellings, and the surplus ρ = 2a − b + 1 is the set's slack. Reading through a redundant set is the redundant purchase: every rational slope becomes readable at bounded delay and addition goes local (Redundant arithmetic), paid for with the archimedean data the window still had. Sign, folded into the digits instead of stored as its own coordinate, is non-local at every lookahead on either side of the purchase (the exchange of crown and blind spot). That non-locality is the order wall, and it states what the wall forbids rather than what it is made of. Throughout, a width-n numeral is read most-significant-first, and a state is what a most-significant-first scan must remember to finish; the minimal number of states is the machine's size.

The leading-nonzero law rule

Where a ≤ b − 1 the sign of a numeral is the sign of its first nonzero digit, and a numeral is zero iff every digit is 0: a zero-test at lookahead 0 on a per-digit predicate. The tail below a position is bounded by a(bj − 1)/(b − 1) < bj, so it cannot overturn a unit there. Above the line the law fails abundantly, and the smallest witness is at radix 2 with digits {−2..2}: the string (1, −2) has value zero behind a nonzero lead.

Scope. Derived; exhaustive at the swept widths over nine symmetric digit sets.

The minimal count is the ceiling of the normalized reach theorem

Run s ← bs + d on the digits d. Once |s| ≥ a/(b − 1) no continuation can outweigh what is accumulated, and the run clamps to one of two absorbing verdicts. Call a, how far the digit set reaches on one side of zero, that side's reach, and a/(b − 1) its normalized reach, the radix steps of headroom the side buys. A tail of m digits takes every value in its range, because 2a + 1 ≥ b makes the tail bridge consecutive multiples of b, so a live state is distinguishable from an absorbing one exactly when some tail drives it to zero, which happens iff its value is strictly below the normalized reach; every remaining pair is separated by the tail that zeroes one of them, and every such state is reached by the single digit of its own value. The minimal machine therefore has 2⌈a/(b − 1)⌉ + 1 states, and the rounding is the ceiling, the reach ceiling: the two states sitting at the normalized reach, which exist only where b − 1 divides a, are never driven to zero and merge away. A machine clamped at the floor instead carries a surplus pair wherever that divisibility holds and reproduces sign exactly all the same, so agreement with brute force cannot detect a surplus state. The count is 3 exactly at a ≤ b − 1, which is the leading-nonzero law's own machine, seen only zeros, negative, positive: below the line the law is not a shortcut past the machine but is the minimal object.

Over a signed asymmetric set {−a−..a+}, both reaches at least 1, the count is ⌈a−/(b − 1)⌉ + ⌈a+/(b − 1)⌉ + 1, and the two ceilings attach to the opposite sides from the obvious reading: a positive prefix can be overturned only by a negative tail, so it stays live while it is below a−/(b − 1). At a reach of 0 the numeration is unsigned and the machine has two states whatever the other reach is, a degenerate regime and not a case of the formula. One function, two currencies: addition spends ⌈x/(b − 1)⌉ slack units on a side of reach x (the lookahead law) and sign spends ⌈x/(b − 1)⌉ states per side, plus the one state that is the all-zeros class; the slack ρ carries neither cost. Comparison rides sign: it is the sign of a digitwise difference into the set of reach a− + a+ on both sides, redundant at the same radix, so it costs 2⌈(a− + a+)/(b − 1)⌉ + 1 states, reads the reaches only through their sum and never fewer than sign's; equality is that difference against zero.

Scope. Theorem for symmetric contiguous covering sets, verified exhaustively at 107 systems to radix 12; the asymmetric count a theorem by the same tail argument with reachability by descent, verified at 456 signed systems to radix 9. Comparison's count is a property following from sign's; comparison and equality are otherwise reasoned from sign rather than swept.

Off contiguous sets the count has three factors theorem

Let D be any finite set of integers with a negative and a positive member, its reaches a− = −min D and a+ = max D. A tail's contribution to the value is a point of the attractor, the closed set of tail values Σ tib−i, the fixed set of the maps x ↦ (x + d)/b over d in D, whose two extremes the all-extreme tails supply. So the absorbing thresholds are the extreme digits' alone whatever the interior, and the live states are integers of the open crossed interval (−a+/(b − 1), a−/(b − 1)). Two things then vary. An integer is reached when some prefix has that value, a cycle question on the backward map n ↦ (n − d)/b that a complete residue system does not settle: at radix 2 with {−1, 2, 3} the integer −2 is its own backward image under the only digit of its residue and is never reached, 4 states against the contiguous 5. And two reached neighbours v < w are one state iff the reflected interval (−w, −v) misses the attractor, the finite tail values being dense in it, so the classes are runs of consecutive reached integers, broken exactly at a gap of the attractor. The minimal count, for every such D, is 2 + (reached integers of the crossed interval) − (consecutive reached pairs across a gap of the attractor). With Δ the largest gap between consecutive digits and L = (a− + a+)/(b − 1) the length of the hull, the interval between the attractor's extremes, Δ ≤ L makes the attractor an interval and nothing merges, which for contiguous sets is the covering condition; a merge needs Δ − L ≥ b, so none exists at radix 2, and the least positive reach that merges is 7, at radix 3 with {−2, −1, 7}. The contiguous count is therefore the maximum over every digit set with those reaches, lost only to a cycle or a gap, and its own reachability is a descent: an integer of the interval beyond the digits, stepped by the largest digit of its residue, stays on its side and strictly decreases into the digits.

Scope. Theorem, its three factors derived above; the count checked against an independent minimizer at 19,450 systems over two sweeps, every interior at radix 2 to 6 with reaches to 5, and three- and four-digit sets at radix 2 to 5 with the negative reach to 3 and the positive to 15, 236 of them merging.

The merge criterion reads one level criterion

Let K be the least exponent with bK > L, the length at which a tail's undecided span first falls under a unit, and c−, c+ the two ceilings ⌈a−/(b − 1)⌉, ⌈a+/(b − 1)⌉. Consecutive reached v < w are one state iff no digit string of length K, read as the integer Σ ujbK−j, has a value in [bK(−w) − c+ + 1, bK(−v) + c− − 1]: the attractor's level-K cover has pieces shorter than 1 and so meets an interval of length at least 1 exactly where the attractor does. That is where the loose condition's order of magnitude goes. At level 1 that interval is a hole in D of length b + c+ + c− − 1 that must sit where a unit interval of the hull's interior reflects, and the two integers it would merge must both be reached: of the 2,532 systems of the sweep with Δ − L ≥ b, alignment refuses 1,429 and reach 867, leaving the 236 that merge. The level is not always 1 and the merged pair not always adjacent. At radix 3 with {−2, −1, 13}, −3 has the residue no digit has and the reached neighbours −4 and −2 are one state across it; at radix 3 with {−1, 1, 30}, past the sweep, the pair −13, −12 is one state decided at level 2 and at no lower, the gap it sits across being the image of a level-1 gap under one more digit. And reach does not close to the digits: of the 867 systems that lose a pair to reach, 301 lose it with both residues held, to the backward graph; 75 merges have no monotone descent to the merged integer and 49 need a chain longer than K. The criterion is a statement about D through the finite graph it induces on the crossed interval, and no shorter reading is it.

Scope. Criterion, proved both ways, and agreeing with the minimizer at all 9,840 three- and four-digit systems of the second sweep; the reach readings are a rule over that sweep, the step law behind them derived.

The syntactic monoid is the state count plus a residue rule

Each digit is a map on the finite state set, and a numeral's sign is the composite of its digits' maps, a product in the finite transition monoid those maps generate under composition. For the minimal machine that monoid is the syntactic monoid of sign, the canonical object, and it is exactly the set of clamped affine maps v ↦ clamp(bmv + t). Its size is n2 + n + 1 + Σm Em, where n is the number of live states, the machine less its two absorbing verdicts, and Em counts the values a length-m tail can take whose map leaves two or more states live. The leading term is fixed by the state count and therefore by the reach ceiling; the correction is where the reach runs out, and it does run out. Radix 3 with {−7..7} and with {−8..8} share the reach ceiling, the nine-state machine and the leading 57, and their monoids have 73 and 75 elements. The collapse onto one parameter that governs the state cost does not extend to the algebra the bracketing evaluates: what the wall costs answers to the reach, and what the wall is made of does not. Below the leading-nonzero line the monoid is 3 at every system, so that law's machine is the whole algebra there.

Scope. Rule, exhaustive over the 107 symmetric and 456 signed systems, with the monoid rebuilt from a machine clamped three states wider on each side and minimized, so the count is a fact about sign and not about where a builder clamped; at four systems the closure is reproduced by enumerating every word to a bounded length.

The correction is closed rule

A level contributes exactly when bm ≤ n − 1: the live states are a run of n consecutive integers, and such a run holds two integers bm apart exactly then, so which terms exist is a fact about the state count alone and everything that escapes the reach sits in their sizes. Drop the clamp, and the tail values a level admits are n − 1 blocks of n − bm integers spaced bm apart; they merge into one interval when 2bm ≤ n and separate when they do not, with no third case. Em is that set met with the exact value range of a length-m tail. Each side has a ceiling defect ε = ⌈a/(b − 1)⌉ − a/(b − 1), the fraction of a state the rounding throws away there. Where the blocks merge the tail range lies inside them outright, the containment rearranging on each side to 2bm − n ≤ ε(bm − 1), whose left side is at most 0 and whose right side at least 0, so the machine drops out of the count and Em is the whole range, (a− + a+)(bm − 1)/(b − 1) + 1: every length-m tail leaves two or more states live. That reads the summed reach at full resolution instead of through the ceiling, which is how {−7..7} and {−8..8} come apart, their corrections 16 and 18 differing because 14 and 16 do. The lattice structure survives only above the merge, and there the defect decides which constraint binds, the tail range doing so iff max(ε−, ε+)(bm − 1) > 2bm − n. Where every level merges, exactly when twice the largest contributing bm is at most n, the correction is one geometric sum and the monoid is a single expression in (b, a−, a+); otherwise it is at most ⌊logb(n − 1)⌋ + 1 closed terms.

Scope. Rule over all 7,524 contributing levels of the 3,579 distinct systems the two grids and an extended sweep make up, 6,076 of the levels merged and 1,448 split, the defect binding at 178 of the split ones; tied at eleven systems to the monoid rebuilt by closure, and at one to a replay of every digit string on the machine. The merged case is a theorem, proved by the containment above, with the merged levels a control on the proof and not its evidence.

Above the ideal, a nilpotent algebra blind to the split theorem

The maps the leading term counts, those leaving at most one state live, absorb whatever they meet on either side, so they form a two-sided ideal; collapse the whole of it to a single zero and what stands above it is exactly what the correction counts. Each of those elements is a tail remembered by its length m and its value t and by nothing else, and two compose as (m1, t1)(m2, t2) = (m1 + m2, bm2t1 + t2), a law with no machine in it. The quotient is that length-graded value semigroup with everything the machine kills sent to zero, a Rees quotient: everything past the last contributing level, and at a split level part of that level too, so it is a truncation and nothing else exactly where every level merges. It is therefore nilpotent, of degree exactly the number of contributing levels, ⌊logb(n − 1)⌋ + 1, the correction's own term count read again. Word length adds under composition and only the identity has length 0, so mutual divisibility forces equality, so no two distinct elements divide each other on either side, the Green relations are trivial, and the only idempotents are the identity and the zero. At radix 3 with {−7..7} the contributing levels are 0 and 1: fifteen values one digit takes and an identity are the 16 above the ideal, and every product of two of the fifteen is zero.

Substituting u = t + a−(bm − 1)/(b − 1) telescopes every a− out of the composition law, leaving u = bm2u1 + u2, and turns a merged level into 0 ≤ u ≤ (a− + a+)(bm − 1)/(b − 1). Where every level merges the algebra therefore sees the radix, the summed reach and the number of levels, and is blind to how the digit set is split between the two sides: radix 3 with {−7..7} and with {−6..8} have 7 and 6 live states and syntactic monoids of 73 and 59, and one and the same 16 elements above the ideal. A split level breaks that blindness, and what it reads is the ordered pair of ceiling defects, its blocks beginning at 1 − (a− + a+)/(b − 1) − ε+ − bmε− + 2bm, which weights the two differently where the state count reads only their sum. At radix 2 both defects are 0 and the blindness is total. So the residue above the automaton is an arithmetic object plus a boundary datum, and neither is a lattice.

Scope. The composition law, the truncation, the nilpotency and the triviality of the Green relations are derived; the blindness is a theorem at a fixed level count. At seven named systems the (m, t) description is checked against the monoid rebuilt by closure as a bijection respecting products, and the nilpotency degree over a radix 2 to 7 sweep of 773 systems. The readings over that sweep are rules: 80 groups of two or more all-merged systems sharing the radix, the summed reach and the level count, all 80 carrying one algebra; 717 pairs of split-level systems with no crossings, the 116 sharing the defect sum while differing in its order never sharing a quotient. That the full monoid is aperiodic is an observation at the seven systems.

The order wall's shape rule

Sign is a per-digit map at lookahead 0 followed by the syntactic monoid's product, and the wall lies entirely in the product: the minimal number of leading digits determining the sign is the full width at every system and every width run, never n − 1, and so nothing bounded as n grows. Yet composition is associative, and evaluating the product by a balanced-tree bracketing instead of a left-to-right scan agrees with brute force everywhere, at depth log n with no data-dependent chain. A carry-propagation measure charges the reduction nothing and the window measure charges it everything; the two coincide on digit-output maps such as addition, where a bounded carry chain and a bounded lookahead are one condition, and part on reductions. Sign separates finite-state from finite-window, and that is the wall: not a hardness claim, and it holds at fixed width, where the argument that proves it on unbounded numerals, an all-zero prefix of any depth, is unavailable.

The full width is not redundancy's price. At slack zero, balanced ternary and radix 5 with digits {−2..2}, non-redundant and the read otherwise identical, the determining prefix is the full width just the same: sign is comparison against zero, zero sits on a cell boundary, and the non-redundant window's one-cell ambiguity already puts it out of reach. Neither does the state count mark the regime. It cuts across the redundancy boundary rather than tracking it, 3 states at both non-redundant controls and at radix 5 with {−3..3}, which is redundant, and it is not a function of the slack either: at ρ = 3, radix 4 with {−3..3} carries 3 states while radix 2 with {−2..2} carries 5. The window margin a redundant cover buys, logb(2a/ρ) (the reading criterion), needs the digit bound too; what settles both the state cost and addition's is the reach, and the slack, the dial the word "redundant" coarsens, carries neither.

Scope. Rules, exhaustive at the swept widths over nine symmetric digit sets, seven redundant and two balanced non-redundant, the latter the control that places the full width. The full-width and factorization claims are not theorems: the widths are bounded by enumeration.
