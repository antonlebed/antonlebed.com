The seed-flower

What a rung names: the integer a sub-ring carries, the congruence deciding which absent primes divide it, where the supply of new namings comes from, the gate that closes on it, how far the naming reaches, why some primes are missed, and the map that iterates the naming.

The tower is the chain of rings Z/6 ⊂ Z/30 ⊂ Z/210 ⊂ ⋯, one rung per prime: rung k is Z/pk# (the primorial, the product of the first k primes), the product of the first k prime fields, and the tower primes at rung k are those first k primes. A sub-ring is the same product over any m of them {p1, …, pm}, N = ∏pi, and it carries an integer of its own, its Euler characteristic χ = N(1 − m + Σ 1/pi) — the Euler characteristic of a complex the sub-ring carries, stated with the geometry. For m ≥ 2, −χ = N(m−1) − Σ N/pi is a positive integer whose prime factors routinely include tower primes the sub-ring does not contain: {3, 5} gives −χ = (3−1)(5−1) − 1 = 7 — the next prime, never met. The sub-ring is the seed, the primes dividing −χ the flower: those primes are what the sub-ring names. Above pk a named prime is a prediction, a prime the tower has never met. One congruence governs every naming.

The reciprocal naming criterion criterion

An absent prime s divides −χ({p1, …, pm}) iff

p1−1+⋯+pm−1  ≡  m−1(mods),p_1^{-1} + \cdots + p_m^{-1} \;\equiv\; m-1 \pmod{s},

the inverses taken mod s: since s is coprime to N, −χ can be divided by N mod s. Two exact properties pre-sieve −χ. It is coprime to the sub-ring's own primes, −χ ≡ −N/p (mod p) for every member p, so members never divide and the criterion governs absent primes only; and it is always odd, so 2 is never named — by coprimality when 2 is a member and, when it is absent, because every odd prime is its own inverse mod 2 and the sum reads m ≡ m − 1 never.

Scope. Proved, and checked at 960/960 (sub-ring, absent prime) pairs; the pre-sieving is proved.

verifiers: explore_tower_naming.py, explore_chi_primality.py

The weight law criterion

Give each prime the weight ws(p) = 1 − p−1 in Z/s. Then −χ(S) = N·(Σp∈S ws(p) − 1), so s names S iff the weights of S sum to 1 (mod s): the criterion with the set size removed, the weights adding over disjoint unions — one linear equation over Z/s on indicator vectors, the named sub-rings its solution set. Three readings follow. The census: a weight depends only on p mod s, so naming is a function of the residues of the sub-ring's primes mod s and of no tower quantity — not the sub-ring's size, not λ, not whether any prime was transparent, with which it shows zero correlation. Invisibility: the weight is 0 exactly at p ≡ 1 (mod s), so such a prime can be adjoined or deleted free without changing any naming; at s = 2 every odd prime is invisible, which is 2 never being named seen again. 3-dominance: mod 3 the weights are 0 and 2, so 3 names S iff the count of members ≡ 2 (mod 3) is ≡ 2 (mod 3); on the 35 three-prime sub-rings of rung 8 that omit 3, that is 18 of them, 51% against the 1/3 baseline below — the Chebyshev bias toward 2 mod 3 read through the criterion, decaying to 1/3 by Dirichlet.

Scope. Proved; checked at 21,202 (sub-ring, absent prime) pairs over the first nine primes and every absent prime below 200, the census at five moduli over an eleven-prime pool with 0 violations, invisibility at 3,392 adjunctions with 0 changed namings, and the independence from transparency at k = 3..50.

verifiers: explore_naming_complex.py, explore_tower_naming.py

The naming baseline rule

Over a pool P of primes, the number of sub-rings s names differs from 2|P|/s by at most ((s − 1)/s)·2|P| times the largest over nonzero characters of ∏p |cos(πj·ws(p)/s)|, by summing the weights against the characters of Z/s. Every factor with a nonzero weight is at most cos(π/s) < 1, so the naming fraction tends to 1/s exponentially in the number of pool primes not ≡ 1 (mod s).

Scope. Proved; the measured deviation sits under the bound in all ten cells run, s ∈ {3, 5, 7, 11, 13} and pools of 6 and 9 primes.

verifier: explore_naming_complex.py

The threshold ladder rule

Over the integers the same weights sum to g(S) = Σp∈S(1 − 1/p), and −χ = N(g − 1). Several conditions recorded separately are levels of this one functional: g is the mean Hamming distance from any fixed point of the sub-ring (the shell distribution); s names S iff g ≡ 1 (mod s); −χ > 0 iff g > 1; −χ < N iff g < 2; and the exact residue comparator on the walls page beats reconstruction iff g > m − 1, which is Σ 1/p < 1. Over the integers every summand lies in (0, 1), so no prime is invisible to g and every adjunction raises it: the integer rows are monotone classes, while the naming row, a congruence, has no closure.

Scope. Proved; checked on the 511 sub-rings over the first nine primes in exact arithmetic.

verifier: explore_weight_thresholds.py

The supply of predictions

The factorization rule rule

Count the pairs {pi, pj} of tower primes whose −χ = (pi−1)(pj−1) − 1 is itself one of the first k primes, each stamped at the rung that completes it. New pairs at rung k come from exactly two sources: the twin term {2, pk} → pk − 2, firing when pk − 2 is prime, plus one pair per factorization pk + 1 = ab with a + 1 and b + 1 smaller tower primes. A new pair must involve pk: as a pair element with an odd partner its target is at least 2pk − 3 and escapes the set, so only the partner 2 works; as the target it forces the factorization, each such pair having named pk one rung before it arrived — {3, 7} named 11 at k = 4. A rung admitting a factorization is rich, typically a highly composite pk + 1 (pk = 71: 72 = 2³·3², three new pairs), and richness requires pk ≡ 3 (mod 4), both factors of pk + 1 being even.

Scope. Proved and verified k = 3..24; sub-rings on three or more primes name primes beyond this count, governed by the criterion above.

verifiers: explore_seed_flower_k8.py, explore_tower_naming.py

The rich-rung supply rule

Censused to 5·10⁷: 599,875 of 3,001,134 rungs are rich (20.0%; 40.0% of the 3-mod-4 class), the decade density decaying only logarithmically, and rich rungs outnumber twin rungs 2.51× with the ratio growing — the factorization term, not the twin term, dominates the long-run supply. Behind it is a law: a factorization pk + 1 = ab with q = a + 1 and r = b + 1 prime puts pk = (q − 1)r − q, a prime pair on a line, so the supply up to x is a Hardy–Littlewood sum — the twin-prime heuristic's count of the prime pairs (r, (q − 1)r − q), summed over primes q ≤ √x — which tracks the census at ratio 0.994–0.999 in every decade from 10⁴. Its shape is 2C2A·x lnln x/(ln x)² with A = ∏ℓ odd(1 + 1/((ℓ − 1)(ℓ − 2))) = 1.743, so the limiting constant is 2.301, the census's own 2.53–2.58 being that law's reading at finite x, falling toward the limit. "Infinitely many rich rungs" is therefore a conjecture at the twin prime conjecture's tier, implied by Dickson through the q = 3 family alone.

Scope. The census is exhaustive to p ≤ 5·10⁷ and the sum tracks it in range; the supply law is the Hardy–Littlewood heuristic, its constant a derivation within it and not a proof.

verifiers: explore_rich_rungs.py, explore_rich_rung_constants.py

The primality gate rule

A sub-ring names −χ itself, and nothing else, exactly when −χ is prime — the gate on every naming — and −χ arrives pre-sieved, odd and coprime to every member. The gate still closes. Over the sub-rings of rung k on two or more primes, the fraction with −χ prime falls 82% (k = 4) → 45% (k = 8) → 12.9% (k = 20, all 1,048,555 of them) → 7.3% (k = 32) → 3.3% (k = 64) → 1.5% (k = 128), with no floor, while the count of such sub-rings still multiplies about 1.9× per rung and the fraction at k = 20 is nearly flat across sub-ring sizes 8 to 18 — a larger −χ is thinner in primes but has fewer small primes left free to divide it. A Cramér model conditioned on the pre-sieving — divisibility by every prime ≤ 100 checked exactly per sub-ring, survivors weighted 1/(ln(−χ) ∏(1 − 1/s)) — tracks the fraction at 0.97–1.07 from k = 8 on, and its mean is a product: every odd prime p ≤ pk contributes 1 + 1/(2(p − 1)), a member's boost at probability ½ or a non-member's survival, −χ mod p being equidistributed off members, and 2 contributes exactly 2. So the fraction ≈ 2∏(1 + 1/(2(p − 1))) · E[1/ln(−χ)] with E[1/ln(−χ)] ≈ 2/θ(pk), θ(x) = Σp ≤ x ln p, within 10% of the model from k = 10, 3% from k = 20 and 0.1% at k = 128. By Mertens' theorem the product grows like √(ln pk), so the fraction goes to zero at rate C√(ln pk)/θ(pk) with C = 2K√(2eγ) = 3.904, K = ∏p odd(1 + 1/(2(p − 1)))√(1 − 1/p) = 1.0342; the measured fraction times θ(pk)/√(ln pk) sits between 3.7 and 4.2 from k = 8 to 128, within 2.7% of C from k = 10 and descending toward it.

Scope. The census is a rule to k = 18 (a deterministic Miller–Rabin base set), a 25-base test at k = 19 and 20, a sampled pattern from k = 24 to 128; the equidistribution is measured to 0.2% at every odd p ≤ 71 but 59 at k = 20; the model is a heuristic, and the rate and the constant are derivations within it, not proofs; the product form is a rule in range against the model.

verifiers: explore_chi_primality.py, explore_chi_decay_constant.py

The gate says how often a sub-ring names; the other question is how far. The horizon H(k) is the largest prime any sub-ring of rung k names.

The prediction horizon rule

The horizon is the primorial up to a small drop. Every proper sub-ring's −χ is below the full ring's, −χ(rung k) = pk# · (k − 1 − Σ 1/pi), by the product of the tower primes it drops, so the horizon sits at a sub-ring dropping little, and H(k) = −χ(rung k)/Dk defines the drop Dk ≥ 1. Certified to k = 60 by walking the sub-rings in increasing dropped product and testing each −χ's cofactor after the primes ≤ 10⁵ are stripped, until the bound falls below the horizon found: the full ring attains at 8 of the 58 rungs, the last at k = 28; a single dropped prime at 35; Dk under 76 at every rung but three and under 209 at all. So the per-rung rate is, exactly, log2(H/pk)/k = [θ(pk) + ln(k − 1 − Σ 1/pi) − ln pk − ln Dk]/(k ln 2), rising like log2 k: 3.24 bits at k = 11, 4.29 at 20, 5.45 at 40, 6.27 at 60. The horizon is pk#1 + o(1) — super-exponential in k — as long as ln Dk = o(k). The drop is modelled by the first prime −χ over sub-rings ordered by dropped product d, each prime with chance (eγ ln pk/ln(−χ)) · φ(d′)/d′, d′ the odd part of d, together with the composite route — −χ = c · prime, c an odd product of the dropped primes, naming −χ/c at drop dc. That route attains the horizon at 2 of the 58 rungs against 1.6 modelled, and the mean of ln Dk over k = 13..60 is 2.87 measured against 2.66 modelled, 1.2 standard deviations of the model's own spread. The rate is not monotone, and the dips are the drop's noise, not a change of law.

Scope. The horizons, the drops and the route's count are a rule to k = 60 by certificate (a deterministic Miller–Rabin base set below 3.3 · 1024, a 25-base test above it); the rate identity is a property; the super-exponential limit is a heuristic law resting on ln Dk = o(k), which the model predicts and the range shows; the drop model is a heuristic and its fit a pattern.

verifiers: explore_horizon_rate.py, explore_horizon_route.py

Why the naming misses

A machine that names primes it has never met invites the opposite question: which ones does it miss, and why those? At rung 7 the first miss is 41, and the reason it escapes is the reason the whole question is a counting one.

One target, not many rule

Read the criterion as a search: s is named by a sub-ring on m primes exactly when m of the inverses pi−1 mod s sum to m − 1, a subset-sum question in Z/s whose target moves with the subset's size. Subtracting 1 from each term collapses the family to one question: with bi = pi−1 − 1 (mod s), s is named iff some non-empty subset of the bi sums to −1. A miss is a prime whose fixed target no subset reaches. At rung 7 the seven inverses mod 41 are {21, 14, 33, 6, 15, 19, 29}; their subset sums cover 37 of the 41 residues, yet no subset of any size m sums to m − 1, so 41 is named by no sub-ring of that rung. The next prime breaks the deadlock at once: −χ({17, 19}) = 16·18 − 1 = 287 = 7·41.

Scope. The collapse is proved, the criterion restated; checked on 286,107 (rung, prime, sub-ring) triples, k = 2..12, s < 200. The rung-7 figures are exhaustive.

verifiers: explore_subset_sum_weld.py, explore_missed_primes.py

Misses are coverage accidents pattern

Take the inverses to be pseudo-random — spread through Z/s as uniform independent values would be, carrying nothing of the tower's own arithmetic — and the 2k − 1 non-empty sub-rings give a miss probability (1 − 1/s)2k−1, which the observed rates match within sampling noise at every measured rung, and to under a point at the two quoted: over the primes from pk+1 up to 10pk, 22.6% predicted against 21.9% observed at rung 7, and 9.0% against 8.8% at rung 8. The missed residues are structureless in position too, a cyclic ambient having no ends for a shortfall to sit at. Naming is monotone in k, so a miss persists only until its absorption rung: the rung-7 window's last absorptions are 83, 163 and 167 at k = 9.

One apparent structure was tested at scale and did not survive. The primes {41, 83, 167} form a Cunningham chain — each the double of the one before, plus one — and all three were missed whole at rung 7, a 0.44% coincidence under the model applied to each independently, which suggested that chained primes miss together. Over every prime s ≤ 20,000 the model instead tracks the rung at which s is first named (residual mean +0.12, sd 1.74, 2,258 primes), and the 187 Sophie Germain pairs — s and 2s + 1 both prime, the first link of such a chain — show no residual correlation at all (−0.002, permutation p = 0.98). Of the 26 chain triples in range, {41, 83, 167} carries the largest residual sum: the fluke itself, not a mechanism.

Scope. The model is a pattern, measured at k = 4..12 (worst z = +1.5); the positional test is an observation at k = 6..9 over s in [50, 400]; the chain refutation is an observation over primes s ≤ 20,000, its pair tests run on detrended residuals.

verifiers: explore_missed_primes.py, explore_cunningham_bias.py

The coverage threshold pattern

Full subset-sum coverage of Z/s — every residue reached — needs about 1.36 log₂ s tower primes, and the coupon collector's form log₂(s·H(s)), H the harmonic sum 1 + 1/2 + ⋯ + 1/s, predicts the measured mean to 0.8%; that form is the Erdős–Rényi threshold for k random elements of an abelian group to cover it by subset sums, the measured 1.36 being the small-s read of its asymptotic 1. Naming is cheaper than coverage, needing one target hit rather than every residue reached: 7.2 primes on average against 9.6, the order reversing rarely — 83 is named only at rung 9 while its residues are fully covered at rung 8.

That threshold is where the classical bounds part company with this object. The Davenport constant D(G) of a finite abelian group is the least length at which a sequence of its elements must contain a non-empty subsequence summing to the identity; for a cyclic group it is the group's own order, s. Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv asks for a zero-sum subsequence of exactly s terms and answers 2s − 1. The tower reaches full coverage with about a fourteenth of the first and a twenty-eighth of the second, because a Davenport constant answers for an adversarial sequence and these values are pseudo-random: the effective constant here is O(log s). (The same invariant is read of a class group under Growth, where it budgets what element-coprimality misses; the notion is one and the group is what changes.) Nor is naming a primes-in-progression question in disguise: where the named prime is one the tower already holds, the members do reduce mod s, but with far more than log₂ s letters the subset sums cover whatever the residues are; where the letters are sparse enough to bite, s is far past pk, nothing reduces, and the letters are exact inverses.

Scope. A pattern over 26 primes s = 73..199 (ratio range [1.25, 1.50]). The two-regime argument is a framing and not a measurement: it argues from the regimes already stated and computes nothing new.

verifier: explore_davenport_egz.py

The naming map iterated

Any squarefree set of primes is a tower ring, so sending a set to the prime support of its −χ is a self-map on designed towers, and the orbit lives on the lattice of prime sets rather than inside any one rung.

The seed-flower operator rule

Write F(S) for the prime support of |−χ(S)|. Three laws fall out of the criterion. F is fixed-point-free: no member prime divides −χ, so F(S) is disjoint from S and any cycle needs period at least 2. 2 is never named, −χ being always odd, so after one step every orbit lives on odd primes — and since |−χ| = 1 only at singletons and at {2, 3}, which is therefore unreachable, every death except a seed placed at {2, 3} itself runs through a singleton, the support of the predecessor's prime-power −χ. And F shrinks on a class named exactly: −χ(S) < N(S) iff ∑p∈S 1/p > m − 2, which holds on every support of size 1 or 2, on {2, 3, 5}, and on nothing else — at three primes the largest reciprocal sum is 31/30 and the next 41/42, at four it is 247/210 against a bar of 2, and each further prime moves the slack by 1/p − 1 < 0. Since N(F(S)) is a radical of |−χ|, no set in that class can grow; outside it growth is permitted, not forced.

Scope. The first two laws are properties and the third is proved by case analysis; all three checked at every computed step.

verifiers: explore_seed_flower_operator.py (13 checks), explore_weight_thresholds.py

Mortality observation

Death — reaching the empty set — is the near-universal fate. All 127 sub-rings of the k = 7 rung die, median 2 steps and maximum 15, the slowest being {2, 5, 7, 11, 13}, which wanders up to 90143 first; 2044 of the 2047 subsets of the first eleven primes die; and no cycle of any period occurs anywhere in 12,111 computed steps. The flowers wilt fast: {2, 3, 5} → {29} → ∅. Restricted to the k = 7 primes, where 14 of the 127 sub-rings have their image, the graph is a forest, and the longest life staying inside them is four sets: {2, 3, 13} → {5, 17} → {3, 7} → {11} → ∅. Support-2 steps never grow; steps from supports 3 to 5 grow about 80% of the time, so an orbit is a race between compounding growth and the prime-power gate.

Scope. The skeleton inside the rung's own primes is exhaustive at k = 7; the fates are one sweep at the stated ranges. An orbit is followed until a value exceeds 1018, past which it is not factored and the support is unknown: five orbits across the sweeps escape that way, each one that strung together long runs at support 3 or more. Whether any escape grows forever is aliquot-shaped, and untestable past the bound.

verifier: explore_seed_flower_operator.py

The orbit also has a geometric reading. −χ is an invariant of a complex each rung carries, so the operator maps that shape alone — the descent to homotopy types, the collision census that makes it non-vacuous, and why no covering or ring map realizes it are stated with the geometry.
