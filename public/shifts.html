Digit shifts

Moving every digit of a string up by a fixed stride, and which of those moves a bounded reader can follow.

A window is a nested grid of cells refining toward a point, and it reads a map at lookahead c when the output cell at depth t is a function of the input cell at depth t + c. The windows here are the Ostrowski ones: for irrational α = [0; a1, a2, …] the digits of n write n = Σ bk qk over the denominators of α's continued-fraction convergents (q0 = 1, q1 = a1, qk = ak qk−1 + qk−2), and the depth-t cell of n is the set of nonnegative integers agreeing with n on their t low-order digits — cells partition at each depth and nest across depths. Legality — what makes the writing unique — caps b0 at a1 − 1 and bk at ak+1, and forces a zero below any digit sitting at its cap. What this family is, and which arithmetic maps its windows read — the arithmetic gate, where multiplication and division are held to no bounded lookahead while the unit action is not — are on Quadratic windows.

Where α is a quadratic irrational its quotient sequence is eventually periodic, and a window fixes more than one repeating length: each block below says which one it reads against. And the agreement depth of a pair of inputs is the number of low-order digits they share, which is what a scan reports when it asks how deep two inputs can agree while their images differ.

The maps read here are the shifts: Lr sends n = Σ bk qk to Σ bk qk+r, moving every digit up by a stride r. Shifting by one period of the quotient sequence multiplies asymptotically by the fundamental unit ε of Z[α]; a stride that is not a multiple of the period is nobody's unit action, so the shifts are a family of questions and not one.

A shifted string need not be legal. Digit bk arrives at position k + r, whose cap is ak+r+1, where legality promised only bk ≤ ak+1. Where the quotient sequence is non-decreasing along the stride nothing can overflow, the shift is the bare coordinate map, and it reads at lookahead 0. Otherwise the illegal string is renormalized — the repair — and the converse fails: a shift whose repair fires on almost every input can still read at bounded lookahead. So there are three verdicts, and which (window, stride) pair gets which is the question: lookahead 0, bounded, and gated — no one lookahead serving every depth, the deepest witness growing with the range scanned.

The stride law rule

On the designed family [0; (1, …, 1, a)∞] — one large quotient a per period of length P, every other quotient 1 — the verdict is a function of r mod P alone, and carries no dependence on a whatever: r ≡ 0 is the period shift and reads at lookahead 0; r mod P even and nonzero reads at bounded lookahead; r mod P odd gates. Unanimous, no exceptions. Read off P = 2, 3, 4, 5, it then calls all 34 (window, stride) pairs at P = 6 and 7 — periods no fitted window carried, and where r mod P = 6 is the first even residue above 4 anywhere in the family. And it is not a fit: e − 2 = [0; 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 6, …] — outside the family, and its continued fraction never eventually periodic — is the P = 3 pattern with a growing large quotient, and obeys the P = 3 row at every stride to 8. Two rivals die on the way: parity of r itself fails at e − 2's r = 4, even and gated, and the criterion "gated iff gcd(r, P) = 1" fits every pair through P = 4 and is parted at P = 5, whose residues 2 and 4 are coprime to 5 and bounded.

The interpretation that fits — an interpretation, not a derivation — is the reach of the window's own borrow. At a position whose quotient is a the denominator recurrence reads a·qk = qk+1 − qk−1, a rewrite spanning two positions, so a repair walks the digit lattice in steps of two, and what decides the pair is whether it can return to the residue class carrying the large quotients — the same step-of-two recursion the greedy string of qK − 1 runs on (the down-borrow, the maximal string). The naive interpretation — the cap above the landing site where an up-carry would need room — is refuted at 29 pairs, five at the cubic window ∛2 − 1 and twenty-four across the designed family. One thing the law settles behind it: the repair's reach, the lowest position any repair changes, does not decide the verdict but is exactly the lookahead-0 floor — every gated column's first nonzero entry sits one position above it, at all 32 pairs.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope: the designed family at P = 2, 3, 4, 5 and a = 2, 3, 5 — twelve windows, every stride to 2P, two ranges — plus the 34 out-of-sample pairs at P = 6, 7 with a = 2, 4, and e − 2 to stride 8. P = 2 has no even nonzero residue and so tests two of the three arms; the third rests on the rest of the table. The non-decreasing sufficient half is settled separately. The step-of-two mechanism is an interpretation and is not proved.

The value law at the half-period stride rule

Give the period two different large quotients and the law breaks at exactly one stride. The graded window [0; (1, 1, A, 1, 1, B)∞] is the designed family at P = 3 with the large quotients of consecutive periods graded apart: they still sit three positions apart, so the stride law still reads at P = 3, while the window's own period is 6. Its stride-3 shift — the half-period one — carries each large quotient onto the other. Where A = B that is literally the designed family and there is no repair at all; where they differ, one of the two directions has a large quotient overflowing onto a position whose own quotient is large — a drop whose landing site can itself absorb.

The positions do not decide it. (4, 8) and (8, 4) are the same window up to which value sits on which class member — the large quotients on identical positions, position for position — and print different verdicts at r = 3, gated against bounded; three of eleven swapped pairs differ there, where one would have done. The verdict reads the values, and at r = 3 the pair is bounded exactly when

B < A ≤ 2B + 1   and   A·B ≥ 30,

and gated at every other unequal pair. The three clauses — the floor B < A, the roof A ≤ 2B + 1, and the product — are separately witnessed and are not equally strong. The roof is the sharp one: every row carrying a bounded run turns gated at exactly A = 2B + 2 and stays gated — first gated A = 10, 12, 14 at B = 4, 5, 6, three rows of three, where the rival ratio criterion A < 8B/3 predicts 11, 14, 16 and misses all three. The B = 3 row has no bounded run and so no roof datum. The product clause is the weak one: its threshold sits in (28, 30], pinned by the extremes rather than by neighbours — (7, 4) at 28 is the largest product among gated pairs meeting floor and roof, (6, 5) at exactly 30 the smallest among bounded — and it bites only at B ≤ 4, since at B ≥ 5 the floor pair already clears it; any monotone reparametrization of the product fits identically. Order is dead against the table, (7, 4) gating where (6, 5) does not with the order unchanged.

And 2B ≥ A − 1 is a condition the same recurrence writes elsewhere. Where a large quotient a sits with two unit quotients above it, qK+2 = 2qK + qK−1, so a coefficient c standing on qK−1 absorbs a cost a·qK in place — nothing below it moving — exactly when 2c = a − 1. That is the equality the arithmetic gate's combs turn on (the absorption lemma), read here as an inequality with the absorbing quotient B where it has c and the overflowing quotient A where it has a. That is a coincidence of the boundary at three rows and a mechanism interpretation, not a derivation, and it is loosest exactly where the two are identified: there c is a top charge where B is a digit cap, and its equality governs a comb extension where this inequality governs a shift's drop. Elsewhere the parity law stands, read at P = 3 — r = 6 is 0 mod 3 and is also the window's own period, and reads at lookahead 0; r = 1 and 4 are both odd there and gate at every pair measured — and it stands with no exception at all: r = 3 is the only stride that departs from it, at every one of the 25 pairs swept at those strides.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope over the graded window above: the 8×8 grid over A, B = 2..9 and the rows B = 3..6 with A run to 16, zero misses. The classifier behind those verdicts compares one range's agreement depth against another's, which makes a verdict partly a fact about the range set used; so the law was re-read under a second classifier that has no range set in it at all — one that reads a single table at the data's own agreement depth, which over these pairs runs 12 to 20 rather than stopping at the shared cap of 10, and asks whether the excess-lookahead column's longest run outlasts the window's period. All 92 pairs reproduce, at zero misses and with nothing refused for want of depth, both sharp clauses intact, and the parity law elsewhere reproduces with it. The law is therefore not an artifact of how it was measured. The roof is still a mechanism interpretation of the absorption lemma and never a derivation; the product clause is still an observation pinned by two pairs.

The run-length rule rule

The value law above was first read by a classifier that compares the agreement depth one range reached against another's. That makes a verdict partly a fact about the range set a sweep happened to use: adding a shallower range can move a verdict with no new information about that pair in it. A verdict can instead be read off a single table, and the thing that decides it is a length.

At a window whose quotient sequence has period P, let the excess-lookahead column record, at each depth t, how much further than t a reader must see before it can commit t digits of the image. Cut that column into maximal runs of nonzero entries. An obstruction that is local — generated by a bounded configuration of digits — recurs wherever that configuration recurs, so its run cannot reach length P: consecutive copies would touch and it would not be local at all. So a run of length P or more is not a local feature of the period and the stride is gated, while runs shorter than P recurring at the period read at bounded delay. The tell is not the decline of exactly one per depth — a periodic bump declines that way too — but whether the decline outlasts the period.

Because the rule takes no range set, it has the fixed point the comparison lacked: adding a range cannot move a verdict already read.

And the period it reads against is the quotient sequence's, which is not a free choice. Two lengths are fixed by a window's own data — the length at which the quotient sequence repeats, and the length at which its pattern of large quotients repeats — and at the graded window they differ, 6 against 3. The choice is not cosmetic: across the 210-pair region named in the scope below it moves 175. Since the cap values decide and not their positions, the configuration generating an obstruction carries the values and the scale is the sequence period — but the argument is not what settles it. The value law does: the rival length misses that law at every bounded pair among those swept against it, each of them carrying a run that falls between the two lengths.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope, periodic windows. It reproduces the value law's 92 pairs at zero misses. It also decides, with nothing refused, all 84 pairs of a two-class family — eight windows whose period carries two large quotients each — running over four periods, P = 4..7, a family its threshold was not read from — and there it confirms a negative result rather than a law: the criterion that predicts a verdict from the large quotients' positions alone still fails, at 16 of the 84 where the range comparison left 17. And the 210-pair region — the cells above the value law's grid, B = 3..12 over A = 4..24, read at r = 2 — is bounded throughout under it; the second surface once read there was the range comparison's, not the window's. The threshold itself was read off the nine designed windows, so those are a consistency check and not an independent control of it. It is bounded to periodic windows by derivation rather than by omission — a window whose quotient sequence never repeats has no length the rule is entitled to, the only other length its own data fixes being the one the value law refutes — so at ∛2 − 1 it refuses 7 of the 8 strides scanned and at e − 2 four of 8, reaching the rest only where the column runs to the scan's own lookahead cap, or where the shift needs no repair at all and reads at lookahead 0 for that reason.

The lookahead cap is calibration pattern

Every clause of that rule follows from something — the run against the period from what makes an obstruction local, the refusal from the room a signature needs — except one. A column whose peak — the largest entry it reaches — climbs to a fixed ceiling is read as saturated, and that ceiling is a bare number, 10: a threshold on the excess itself, not the table-depth cap of the same size above. It is also the only clause left where there is no period, once the strides needing no repair at all are set aside, so every other verdict at ∛2 − 1 and e − 2 rests on it.

Nothing in a column can replace it. One column the range comparison gated has four exact twins among pairs the value law calls bounded — same table depth, same lengths and peaks — differing only in where the run sits, which is the axis the rival readings already died on. And above that the specimens run out, since every bounded verdict lives at a window of period 6 or less, where a run reaching the period is gated by construction. So the number is read off the corpus instead: across 732 bounded columns at three ranges the largest excess is 5, and the honest constant is one past it, 6 — which at the deepest range issues exactly the verdicts the range comparison issued at both windows with no period, moving no periodic pair across the divide.

That margin is measured at the deepest range rather than inferred there. A verdict caps a bounded column's run at the period, and with it the peak of any run that returns to zero; a run still open at the foot of the table is capped only by the saturation number itself, so a bounded column landing high enough would have made the constant a choice — high enough being 7 and not 6, since the constant is one past the ceiling and the shallowest peak the split needs saturated is 7. There is none: the bounded columns top out at 5 and the still-open ones at 3, two values of margin rather than one. What depth moves is the other side — the bounded spectrum unchanged between the two deepest ranges while the gated peaks climb, 13 to 15 over the value law's grid and 18 to 19 over the designed windows. The gap the constant sits in widens as the reading deepens.

Scope. Pattern at scanned scope, and it stays pattern: what would promote it is a derivation, and above run 5 the corpus is silent by its own periods. The bounded population is 732 columns over three ranges to N = 300000 — the 210-pair region at r = 2, the value law's grid at r = 3, and the designed windows at the deepest two — each labelled by the law that owns it and never by this reading.

Neither end of a drop decides observation

Every clause of the value law is a criterion on one drop, which a periodic window has and repeats forever. A window with no period carries a whole multiset of quotient pairs per stride, so any lift of the law reads a verdict per site and the quantifier over them is the question. There is none, and the witness needs neither quantifier nor count. At e − 2 the quotient pattern 1, 1, 2n puts a 1 at every landing site, so its strides pair up carrying one identical multiset of drop-site quotient pairs — the even quotients dropping onto 1 — at identical drop positions j = 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, differing only in where the drop lands, at j + r. And the pairs read gated, bounded, gated, bounded, gated, bounded. Any criterion that is a function of a drop's source gives both members of a pair one verdict, so it must be wrong at one member of each — at least three of the six scored strides — and three is exactly what every form scored: the roof under either quantifier, and the product at its threshold. A miss, not a silence.

With the swap above that empties both ends. The swap kills the positions, two windows carrying one set of large positions and different verdicts; this kills the values, two strides of one window carrying one quotient-pair multiset and different verdicts. Where e − 2's verdict does live is the absorbing end: its large quotients sit on one residue class mod 3, which is why it is the P = 3 pattern the stride law already calls. The cubic ∛2 − 1 has no such class — its quotients above 1 sit at positions 0, 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, 13, 15, …, with gaps of 1, 2 and 3 from the start — and there the roof scores independently and misses too, 2 of 7 strides existentially and 4 of 7 universally, with no count or fraction threshold separating. So what plays the period at a window that has none is open.

Scope. Observation at the two windows with no period, e − 2 and ∛2 − 1, every scored stride read at three ranges. Three forms are scored — roof existential, roof universal, product at its threshold — each under both bounds on which drop sites count, and each missing three at e − 2; at the cubic the roof misses 2 of 7 strides existentially and 4 of 7 universally; that no statistic over the pairs can do better is the argument above and not a further measurement. What is refuted is the lift of the value law off the period; the law itself, at the graded family, is untouched.

The shifts sit beside the arithmetic gate rather than under it. Where the continued fraction is periodic the shift by one period and multiplication by ε are the same map, so no window in the family can say which of the two its reading was ever about — and off that stride the shift is nobody's unit action and reads at bounded lookahead anyway, at every nonzero even residue mod the period. What decides a shift is therefore the stride read against a length the window itself fixes — and where the window fixes no such length, what plays that part is open.
