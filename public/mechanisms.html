Collision mechanisms

What the second factorization at a collision is. Where the two factorizations share a factor, a criterion derives the descent dimension from the polygons of three factors — a parallelism that fails at the one full-dimension witness, and an accident that fails it a second way; where they share none, a mixing pair at twelve terms is one explicit family or one-variable, a theorem resting on a criterion that decides a binomial times a block against a binomial times a block at any size; and at a two-factor seed one sign scan decides whether there is a second factorization at all.

A menu is a finite set of integers at least 2 — in the grown worlds of the amnesia certificate, the moves a state admits, each move multiplying the state by one member. Read at a temperature β, with one variable xp standing for p−β at each prime p, a menu is a polynomial with every coefficient 0 or 1, one term per member, and its core is that polynomial with its largest single-term factor divided out. Two pairs of menus whose products agree as polynomials collide, and the collisions that are more than bookkeeping are the products with two different factorizations in the semiring of such polynomials — groupings of the integer factors into blocks, each a product with no negative coefficient that splits into no two such, an atomic block — which happens only where some factor irreducible over the integers carries a negative coefficient, so that the nonnegative factors can pair around it two ways. A menu whose core has such a factor is a seed; the smallest is {2, 16}, with core 1 + x3 = (1 + x)(1 − x + x2), and the smallest genuine collision is the six-term identity (1 + x3)(1 + x + x2) = (1 + x)(1 + x2 + x4). Menu collisions grades such a product by its faces. The exponent vectors of its terms span a hull — a segment when they are collinear, on one line, its polygon when the product dimension is 2; a direction picks out a face of that hull, the terms extreme that way, and keeps from each factor its initial form, that factor's own extreme terms, initial forms multiplying as the factors do. The descent dimension δ is the smallest dimension of a face on which the two factorizations already differ, each initial form stripped of its single-term content first: δ = 1 says the mechanism is inherited from an edge, and δ = 2 on a two-dimensional product says it is inherited from no face at all, which is the full-dimension witness, the one such object in the widest box walked. The boxes are the corpus's own menus: the (2, 6) box, sizes 2 and 6 with members in {2..32}, and the (3, 4) box, size 3 in {2..32} against size 4 in {2..24}; a product is in frame when every coefficient is 0 or 1 — twelve terms at either pairing — and the boxes' counts are read over in-frame products, at the core, a menu and its multiple by a fixed integer being one object.

Where the factorizations share a factor

The face law criterion

Where the two factorizations share a factor the collision reads {p·n, q} against {p, n·q}: one block of each side carrying the shared p, the negative factor n moving between them, and any blocks that agree on both sides riding along as spectators. Spectators contribute the same stripped form to both sides and cancel, so along a direction the two sides read the multisets {P·N, Q} and {P, N·Q} of stripped initial forms, equal exactly when N = 1 or P = Q, by cancellation in the polynomial ring: a face separates the two factorizations if and only if n's initial form has more than one term and p and q do not read the same stripped initial form there. The necessary half bounds δ from BELOW, the direction a sampled reading never reaches. A factor's initial form has more than one term exactly when the direction is normal to an edge of that factor's own polygon, so at product dimension 2 the law reads: δ = 1 exactly when the polygon of n has an edge parallel to an edge of the polygon of p or of q along which p and q read different stripped forms. At the 18 objects of the (2, 6) box the criterion is stated at, the shared-form clause never fires, for a reason: at 17 of them q is a collinear trinomial — x² + x + 1 at 15, its homogenization x² + xy + y² at the two seeded by {8, 27} — whose only many-term initial form is itself, three terms against p's two; at the witness p = x + 1 and q = x + y are distinct binomials. Every δ = 1 object reached is the six-term identity in different clothes, n a three-term factor on p's own line with a binomial spectator; the witness is the only object with no spectator, and its n is five terms spanning two dimensions whose polygon's edges miss the directions of both binomials present, (1, 0) and (1, −1). The dimension of n's polygon agrees with δ at every row too and is a CORRELATE only, agreeing because every collinear n reached happens to lie on p's line.

Scope. Proved in both directions, checked at 157 faces over the 18 shared-factor objects of the (2, 6) box, 39 of them separating, with no disagreement either way; the parallel-edge form is the criterion restated at product dimension 2. At the box's four mixing objects the criterion is UNSTATED rather than false — their blocks share no factor across the two factorizations, so there is no p, n or q to read a face against — and the mixing theorem stands there.

The accident is real, and carries a second full-dimension mechanism property

The shared-form clause bites. With p = 1 + t, n = 1 − t + t² and q = (1 + t) + y(1 + t + t²) + y²(1 + t), the product (1 + t³)·q is a 14-term 0/1 collision with exactly two atomic factorizations — in menu clothes {2, 16}·{2, 4, 6, 12, 18, 24, 36} = {2, 4}·{2, 6, 16, 18, 24, 96, 144}, size pair (2, 7), outside every box walked — whose bottom and top edges are both parallel to n's segment and both read q's initial form as 1 + t, which is p: no proper face separates, and δ = 2 where the edge form without its clause reads 1. So an edge of n's polygon fails to separate in two ways, which an object may mix: no parallel edge on p or q, the witness at every edge, or a shared form, this object at every edge. With n and p on one line the accident has a closed reading — n has many-term initial forms only straight up and straight down in y, where p reads itself — so δ = 2 exactly when both outermost y-layers of q are p up to a single term. Over p = 1 + t at degree at most 6 in three layers it is not rare: 628 collisions, 138 at δ = 2 (287 and 42 up to the box's reflections), every δ = 2 object misread by the clause-free form and none by the criterion.

Scope. The 14-term object is exact, its two factorizations counted and every proper face read; the closed reading is derived and holds at all 628 objects of the one box named, whose counts are exhaustive there. Toy scale.

Twelve is empty in the shared shape property

Why the wide box's lone full-dimension object stays alone at its own term count has a derivation. In the shape above — F = B·q with B a one-variable 0/1 polynomial divisible by 1 + t whose quotient n carries a negative coefficient, and q three y-layers, the outermost two being 1 + t up to a single term at δ = 2 — a 0/1 polynomial's term count is its value at 1, so |F| = |B|·(4 + |q₁|) with q₁ the middle layer, and B(−1) = 0 makes |B| EVEN. The one cell reading 12 is a binomial B over a binomial middle layer, which nonnegativity forces to be 1 + t times a single term, making every layer of q a multiple of 1 + t and q a multiple of p — excluded. So TWELVE IS EMPTY, at every degree bound, the argument never consulting the degree. The δ = 2 objects sit at 14 and 18 through degree 8; a four-term B enters at degree 9 (200 objects at term count 28) and a six-term middle layer at degree 8 (term count 20), both box facts; 16 is empty through degree 9 and underived.

Scope. The emptiness of 12 is proved; the spectrum beyond it is exhaustive over one-variable supports to degree 9 and claimed no further. Toy scale.

Where they share none

A mixing pair at twelve terms is one family, or one-variable theorem

Call two atomic factorizations of one product mixing when, the blocks common to both set aside as spectators, no block of either divides a block of the other, and shared otherwise. At twelve terms the block sizes on a side are {2, 6}, {3, 4} or {2, 2, 3}, and a mixing pair carries no spectator: one would leave a six-term residue, whose only collision is the six-term identity — shared, 1 + v dividing 1 + v³ — or a four-term one, which factors uniquely. So a three-term block in it is REDUCIBLE, an irreducible one having to divide a block across, hence a collinear trinomial 1 + va + vb (the trinomial lemma). THE CYCLE LEMMA does the rest: a six-term product tiled two ways as a three-term block times a binomial, with the binomials distinct, is the six-term identity in some power vs — each binomial pairs the six exponents as a perfect matching, the two matchings' union is one alternating hexagon, and a hexagon of steps s and k closes only at k = 3s. Slicing every block along the cosets of v leaves two outcomes: every block a polynomial in v, product dimension 1, or, with z off the line, THE FAMILY {1 + v2e + v4e, (1 + v3e) + z(1 + ve)} against {1 + v3e, (1 + v2e + v4e) + z(1 + ve + v2e)}, e ≥ 1, mixing at every e: both blocks atomic, and 1 + ve and 1 + ve + v2e sharing no cyclotomic factor. The one twelve-term shape the slicing never meets, no three-term block on either side, is shared always by the binomial-pair criterion, so the dichotomy is the whole of twelve terms. Every mixing object the sweeps met is the family at e = 1: the four of the (2, 6) box are {2, 16} against {2, 8, 32} ∪ m·{1, 2, 4}, and the (3, 4) box's 71 in-frame collisions read 55 shared, every one the six-term identity beside a spectator binomial, and 16 mixing, every one {2, 8, 32} against c′·{1, 8} ∪ m·{1, 2} with c′ ∈ {2, 3} — object for object the 16 pairs the confinement leaves where a collinear seed's partner is itself a seed. On the line to exponent 24 it is all there is: 124 mixing pairs among 201 one-variable collisions, every one the family's (3, 4) side at e = 1..6, at two of which the other side's six-term block splits and reads three blocks.

Scope. The dichotomy is a theorem at twelve terms in frame, in any number of variables, on the cycle lemma, the trinomial lemma and the binomial-pair criterion; the family's mixing at every e is proved. The cycle lemma is checked beside its proof at 11,760 six-term products to exponent 30, all 17 second decompositions the identity; the (3, 4) reading is exact over its box and the one-variable search exact to exponent 24. Open: one-variable mixing with a three-term block past exponent 24.

Between two binomials, mixing is a cycle with a nonzero closing sum criterion

A pair of binomials (1 + u)·H = (1 + u′)·H′, u ≠ u′ single terms and H, H′ 0/1 blocks of any size, is two perfect matchings on the product's support, its set of exponent vectors — a term h of H paired with h + u — whose union is a disjoint union of even alternating cycles. Off one line, u and u′ powers of two distinct primitive monomials — monomials that are no proper power — the binomials' irreducible factors, each cyclotomic, vanishing at roots of unity, are coprime, and 1 + u divides H′ outright: shared for free. On a line, u = vs and u′ = vk with g = gcd(s, k), split the support along the cosets of vg: each slice Pc is a polynomial in w = vg alone, and 1 + u divides H′ exactly when it divides every slice. With s/g and k/g both odd the two binomials share the one factor 1 + w, each exactly once, and every other factor of 1 + u already divides every slice, so the question is whether (1 + w)² divides each slice — whether Pc′(−1) = 0 at every c, the same condition read from either side. The pair, read on its two blocks as they stand, mixes if and only if u and u′ lie on one line, s/g and k/g are coprime odd integers at least 3, and some slice has Pc′(−1) ≠ 0 — at any size, in any number of variables. The cycles compute that derivative: a cycle of 2m points closes when its signed steps cancel, a·s′ + b·k′ = 0 with a ≡ b ≡ m (mod 2), its closing sum, and the derivative is a signed sum of the a's over the cycles. A nonzero a is a multiple of k′ and its b of s′, so such a cycle has at least 2·max(s′, k′) ≥ 10 points; a 12-cycle is impossible, and the shortest with a nonzero closing sum is the 10-cycle at (3, 5). Twelve points seat no 10-cycle beside a 2-slice, so every twelve-term binomial pair is shared, in any number of variables. The emptiness is twelve's: at ten terms {0, 3} + {0, 2, 4, 6, 8} = {0, 5} + {0, 2, 3, 4, 6} — in menu clothes {2, 16}·{2, 8, 32, 128, 512} = {2, 64}·{2, 8, 16, 32, 128}, the product Φ₂Φ₅Φ₆Φ₁₀ of cyclotomic polynomials — mixes on a single 10-cycle at (3, 5) with P′(−1) = 15, the only such collision to exponent 30 beside its dilate, and at fourteen terms there are 24 to exponent 24.

Scope. Proved for a pair of binomials against blocks of any size in any number of variables, read on the two blocks as they stand — whether each is atomic is the pair's question and not the criterion's; the sharing at twelve terms is a theorem on it. The census is one-variable and exact in its boxes: 19 (2, 6)-against-(2, 6) pairs to exponent 30, every one shared; of 879,133 binomial × 6-set products to exponent 36 no (8, 4) cycle type occurs; the ten-term specimen's uniqueness to exponent 30 and the fourteen-term count to 24.

Whether there is a second factorization at all

At a two-factor seed the collision condition is one sign scan rule

Let a seed's core be exactly two integer-irreducibles n·p, n the one with a negative coefficient, and the partner's core a single irreducible q. A factorization in the semiring is a grouping of n, p, q into nonnegative blocks; the groupings are five, and weighing them with only n negative and n·p nonnegative: the product is non-unique exactly when n·q has no negative coefficient — unless q = p, where {n·p}, {q} and {n·q}, {p} are one factorization and the product is unique. The sibling from the same five groupings: a two-factor core with BOTH factors negative is unique against any single-factor partner. The shape is the box's and not one core's — both seed cores on the two-member side of {2..32} are of it, (1 + x)(1 − x + x²) and {8, 27}'s (x + y)(x² − xy + y²) — and 735,357 of the box's 736,281 six-member menus have a single-factor core, so the scan decides 99.87% of the (2, 6) box and the counter, the direct enumeration of a product's factorizations into blocks, is spent only on the rest. The full-dimension witness is the scan firing: its seed {3, 4, 8, 9, 18, 24} has a two-factor core, its partner {2, 3} the single factor x + y, and n·q there is the second factorization's own six-term factor, nonnegative as written.

Scope. Proved at every seed whose core is two irreducibles with exactly one negative, against every single-factor partner; 0 disagreements wherever the counter was run alongside — 114,449 pairs in {2..24} and 96,072 in {2..32}. The scan decides collision or not and never δ.

A box's collisions generate by exponent arithmetic observation

A product's hull is the Minkowski sum of its factors' hulls, so a binomial divisor of a core has its direction parallel to an edge of the core's hull and hence to a difference of two of its exponent vectors, and a box's collisions generate with no menu factored — cores from exponent arithmetic, divisors from the support's own differences, factorization spent only on confirmation — in under a seventeenth of the wide box's census time. Widening the divisor search to 0/1 trinomial divisors reaches every seed the box has, 199 to 203, and buys only the 4 out-of-frame objects; factoring all 203 cores outright and pairing every one of their 204 negative factors against all 465 partners returns 0 new collisions. One theorem bounds where a sign filter can be wrong: a pair whose six-member core carries no negative factor cannot collide unseen, the block holding the two-member core's negative factor forcing n times the other core to be a product of nonnegatives, which fires the test. So the missable class is seed against seed, 4 menus by 203, and walking it with no filter at all closes the count exactly at the census's 22 in frame and 4 out, 21 and 1 at δ — the census's own object list never having been recorded — the two runs together under a sixth of it. The four it adds all mix, one family: {2, 16} against {2, 8, 32} ∪ m·{1, 2, 4}, whose in-box members are m = 3, 5, 6, 7 and whose every swept member to m = 16 that forms a menu collides the same way, evens included — a family the element bound truncates, growing as the box widens.

Scope. Exact over the (2, 6) box; the missable-class bound is proved. Toy scale.
