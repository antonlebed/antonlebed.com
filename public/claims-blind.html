The blind spot

  • The resolution floor theorem — A multiplier tells a blind pair apart iff the two premiums differ, every resolver is a non-divisor of V, so the least resolver is at least the least non-divisor of V, a prime power the pairs below 1500 attain in 90 of 96 cases. — explore_resolver_threshold.py, explore_headroom.py
  • The Proth window observation — The pair 211, 212 at V = 65535 splits first at 28, and on adjacent powers of two a move resolves at depths a, a+1 exactly when some u·2a−1+x + 1 is prime, so the least resolver's growth reduces to Proth-prime gaps. — explore_proth_window.py
  • The threshold is unbounded theorem — The resolution floor is unbounded: along L = lcm(1, …, n) the floor of W(L) exceeds n+1, the silent-set theorem populates every such reading, and designed readings with floors 47 and 81 host blind pairs resolving exactly there. — explore_silent_set.py, explore_proth_window.py
  • The watcher's position theorem — No pair is blind forever, a Dirichlet prime resolving it, but a watcher who takes the move given survives at least τ(V) − 1 moves blind where a chooser never does, told apart on 75.1% of moves to 60 against the chooser's every pair. — explore_resolver_threshold.py, explore_composite_move.py
  • Silence is blindness theorem — A fresh prime with premium 1 behind a shut door is a silent import, shifting λ and leaving V untouched, and every prime silent at λ is a member of the blind class V = W(gcd(q−1, λ)); at an odd λ nothing is silent. — explore_premium.py, explore_silent_set.py
  • The silent-set theorem theorem — At every even λ the primes q ≡ 1 mod λ reading V(q) = W(λ) number ≫ x/log²x below x, those with composite cofactor (q−1)/λ by themselves, on Bombieri–Vinogradov. — explore_silent_set.py
  • The blindness census observation — The share of states below a cap sharing their headroom falls 39.2% to 26.8% from 1500 to 192000, a shade slower than 1/log; the largest class below 6000 is V = 24, its prime part exactly the primes q with W(q−1) = 24q. — explore_headroom.py, explore_premium.py
  • The blind-spot theorem theorem — The primes q with V(q) = 24 number ≫ x/log²x below x, the non-safe members by themselves, by the linear sieve at the Bombieri–Vinogradov level: the class is infinite and the Sophie Germain question untouched. — explore_blind_spot_infinite.py
  • The blind-share theorem theorem — At every even λ the blind class of λ's wall numbers ≫ x/(log x·log log x) below x, the sieve's sifting range lowered to a power of log x, and the census reads that ratio flat at 0.32 over three decades. — explore_blind_share.py
  • The blind-density theorem theorem — At every even λ the blind class is a fixed positive share of the primes, N ~ δ·π(x), by the Bernoulli route: for q ≥ 11 the class V = 24 is exactly the primes with denom(Bq−1) = 6q, Erdős–Wagstaff transferred to primes. — explore_blind_bernoulli.py
  • The loose share's floor theorem — The primes whose headroom their cohort does not fix are a share of the primes bounded below by the density of the blind class V = 480, every member of which is loose at 2, so no shape tending to zero is that share's limit. — explore_loose_floor.py, explore_blind_bernoulli.py
  • The divisor model observation — A model with the bad-divisor events independent, each priced at the chance 2e+1 is prime, predicts the census within 0.3% at every decade to 107 and reads the class share falling to 0.31 at 1050, the approach to δ and not δ. — explore_blind_model.py
  • The tombstones are a blind class theorem — V = 1 is the class of closed states, W(L) is closed for every L and W(2k) ≥ 2k+2 is unbounded, so the tombstones are a second infinite blind class, no sieve needed. — explore_headroom.py
