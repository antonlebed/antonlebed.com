Seed-Flower

Build a ring from just a few primes — a seed. Compute its Euler characteristic. The answer can name primes the seed has never seen.

Formula: −χ = N × (k−1 − Σ 1/pi). For two primes: (p−1)(q−1) − 1.

The Chain Predictions

Four pairs connected by Cunningham chains predict the next prime in the ring. Each is an algebraic identity, not a numerical coincidence. Writing D = 2 and K = 3 for the chain starting points:

Pair−χIdentityFactoring
{2, 5} → Z/10 3 (2D+1)(D−2) = 0 5 × (D−2)
{2, 7} → Z/14 5 (3D+1)(D−2) = 0 7 × (D−2)
{3, 5} → Z/15 7 (2K−1)(K−3) = 0 5 × (K−3)
{3, 7} → Z/21 11 2K(K−3) = 0 6 × (K−3)

Every polynomial factors as (ring element) × (D−2 or K−3). The roots D = 2 and K = 3 are forced — the unique starting points of the Cunningham chains among the seven primes. Discriminants are perfect squares: 5², 7², 5², 6².

2-invisibility. The prime 2 is never named by any sub-ring that doesn't contain it. For odd-prime sub-rings, −χ is always odd. 2 is the only one of the seven primes invisible to the topology.

Naming frequency across all 127 sub-rings: 3 is named 24 times, 5 by 12, 7 by 8, 11 by 6, 13 and 17 by 3 each, 2 by 0. Smaller primes are more visible.
