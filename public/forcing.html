The forcing

Where the algebra FORBIDS the degenerate regime, the count of primes whose three places are all principal sits below its nominal count, deepest at the large class numbers, and what deepens is no share deficit but the explicit formula's prime-power terms — the inert primes' cubes above all, which the sum-zero relation lands on the identity at every class number. Put back at the nominal share those terms overshoot; read instead at the share its own image gives each one, every stratum sits below 1, and the nominal level's rise with the class number is a fixed fraction of the FORCING — the excess those terms alone put on the level — that the raw primes leave unpaid, a fraction the same statistic on a quadratic field's principal class does not carry. The control is the split count itself, whose own deficit at a small prime is the cubic family's and not the field's.

The objects. A cubic field K of discriminant d has a finite class group Cl(K) of order h, the class number: its ideals taken modulo those generated by a single element, the principal ones, the class of an ideal being its ideal class. Cl[k] is the subgroup killed by k, and the 3-rank r is the number of cyclic factors of Cl whose order 3 divides. A rational prime is totally split in K when it factors into three places whose residue fields are the prime field, partially split when it carries one such place and one of residue degree 2, and inert when it carries none; a place with the prime field as residue field is a degree-1 place. The three places over a totally split prime carry classes summing to the class of the prime itself, which is trivial, so write M for the group of triples in Cl(K)³ summing to zero, of order h², and R for the subgroup of M the realized triples fill, the image — of order h²/3t for an exponent t the field's conductor fixes, never above r (the genus index law). A field is degenerate when its realized triples fall short of everything the sum-zero relation allows, R proper in M, and uniform when R is all of M; where 3 ∤ h none is degenerate (The degenerate regime). A Frobenius is the Galois-group element a prime determines up to conjugacy, read here in Gal(H̃/Q) for H̃ the Galois closure over Q of the Hilbert class field of K, the unramified abelian extension whose group over K is Cl(K). The nominal share of an event is what a uniform triple on M gives it, its level is its observed count over the count a stated share predicts, so that 1 is agreement, and a bar is one standard deviation of the figure it follows. Every field is complex — one real embedding — unless the signature is named.

The all-principal shortfall deepens with h where the algebra forbids degeneracy, and what deepens is the explicit formula's prime-power terms, ramified included, which put back at the nominal share overshoot observation

A tilt of uniformity weighting a triple by a fitted constant per principal place leaves 0.663, 0.463, 0.351 on the all-principal count, the one event every stratum shares: the deepening survives the tilt, so it was not the share deficit's. What deepens: the all-principal triple is the IDENTITY of Gal(H̃/Q), the group M ⋊ S3 of order 6h² wherever the image fills M, and Chebotarev's explicit formula, which counts prime powers at weight 1/k at the k-th power — each prime power qk in the window a term of that weight — shorts a count of primes with Frobenius the identity by 1/k per prime q with qk in the window and Frobqk = e: a split prime's square when its classes are 2-torsion, a partial prime's square when its degree-1 place is principal, and EVERY inert prime's cube, whose Frobenius (v, ρ) — a triple v and a 3-cycle ρ — cubes to (s, s, s) with s the sum of v's coordinates, zero by the sum-zero relation. The square's group count is 3h + |Cl[2]|², the cube's 2h² + |Cl[3]|² — quadratic in h where the share deficit is flat. Put back per field and prime over qk < 1000, the prime 2 included, each term at the nominal share 1/h², the corrected level to |d| ≤ 24,000 reads 1.058 ± 0.017 at h = 2 (raw 0.860) and 0.877 ± 0.033 on h = 3's full-image regime (raw 0.604), and over 24,000 < |d| ≤ 96,000, where the bars halve, it CLIMBS with h, level once and never falling — 1.19, 1.28, 1.60, 1.60, 2.12, 2.24 at h = 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, each 4 bars or more above 1 — because the inert cube lands on the identity with certainty at every h and is counted against 1/h², so what the algebra forces onto the identity grows as h² in the level.

The ramified primes' powers are in the formula too, read by their inertia cosets: H̃ is unramified over the S3 closure of K, so inertia at a ramified q injects into S3 and qk weighs 1/(k|I|) on the class its Frobenius coset lifts to — ([P], [P], [Q]) with 2[P] + [Q] = 0 at q = P²Q, ([P], [P], [P]) with 3[P] = 0 at q = P³, the relation holding at every one of the 3004 ramified primes mapped to 24,000. With them in, h = 3's uniform regime rises from 0.877 to 0.943 ± 0.032, 1.8 bars low, and its all-equal event to 1.025 ± 0.019, closed; h = 2 rises to 1.099 ± 0.016 to 24,000, and above it every readable stratum but one rises further, 1.31, 1.45, 1.76, 1.83, 2.28, 2.16 at h = 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, because at every class number prime to 3 the algebra puts every P³ place, and at h = 2 every Q place, on the identity. The one that does not is h = 11, FALLING from 2.24, where putting weight back on the identity's own count cannot lower its level: both figures are the same run's and the fall is left standing rather than smoothed. The h = 2 excess is not a term of the unramified S4 quartic over K, in which the all-principal event is the prime splitting completely: a secondary term of that count would fall with the discriminant box, and the level with both terms in is flat over six boxes to 96,000 — a free exponent of decay 0.09, from −0.02 to 0.18, where a term of the cubic count's size reads 0.26 on the same boxes — with its small-prime and large-prime bins moving in OPPOSITE directions, which no single family term does. What climbs is the ramified count: at h = 2 the P³ places and the Q of every P²Q pair are both principal by the algebra, [Q] = −2[P] vanishing in a group of order 2, the pairs per field rise 1.5 to 2.2 across the boxes, and their weights counted at the quarter add +0.03 to +0.05 of level in every box, the unramified level alone reading 1.009 ± 0.009 above 24,000. The excess is the forced landings counted at the nominal share, and what every term is worth at its own share is the fraction block. The degenerate regime enters exactly through the share the image gives an event, and on the cube term alone: at h = 6 it separates 15 fields whose triples form a group of order 12 from 50 whose triples fill M, and a stratum the first wide run read at 1.49 unsorted reads 1.117 ± 0.158 against 1/36 and 1.097 ± 0.171 against the degenerate share 1/12 to 24,000; the degenerate fields at h = 3 read 0.968 ± 0.029 against their share of one third, 0.971 with the ramified term in.

Scope. The cube's landing on the identity is a property of the sum-zero relation and the ramified weights a property of the inertia cosets; the levels are observations over the 16,313 complex cubic fields to |d| ≤ 96,000 in six discriminant boxes, 2,204 of them at h = 2, the reading to 24,000 over its 1367 fields with h > 1; a stratum is readable when its expected corrected count is at least 10, and the 3 | h strata are read against their own images (the image-share block). The control is the totally split count itself, which needs no class reading, and its deficit is not the field's but the family's (the family term); the control of the box statistic is that count's own deficit, a family term by the theorem, which the same fit reads as decaying and within its bar of the two terms. The closed form of the cube term, every base q ≤ 31 at its asymptotic share, overshoots the measured correction by a third to a half because those bases carry the small-prime deficit; the term is read per prime, never by its leading coefficient.
