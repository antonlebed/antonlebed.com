The substrate door

Growth in place of a fixed reader: the machinery that does the distinguishing grown on the data rather than chosen in advance — what it lands on when the data carries structure to exploit, what it lands on when it does not, and what natural language does at that door.

Descent in reader space varies what a reader is scored by and what it has to spend, while the machinery itself is chosen at the start and held. Three of its terms carry over, in these senses and no others. A reader there is a commitment policy on a fixed cover of intervals; its metabolism is the pair of resources it runs on, a rank budget per input step and a bank holding what a step does not spend; and destination universality is the finding that one policy is optimal on all of the training data at once, so the optimum there is a function of the metabolism and never of the data.

Growth needs a substrate with a growth rule, which reader space does not have, so the question moves to an arena that does: supervised separation of integers by a set of distinct primes, and any such set is a tower ring. A substrate is such a set S, drawn from a fixed pool of the first few primes; it resolves two inputs when some prime it holds gives them different remainders. A task is a supervised partition of the inputs — which pairs the supervision says must be told apart — and a pair the current substrate fails to resolve is a collision. The growth rule is the tower's own (least-new): on a collision, add the smallest pool prime not yet held that resolves something still unresolved. Learning is then growth, and the question is whether what it grows to is set by the data or by nothing but the resolution the data demands — whether the data can pick the machinery, the door of the title. Every verdict below is at pools of five to twelve primes, where the door is easiest: a larger pool only raises the scale at which a difference can need one prime alone.

What a grown substrate lands on

Growth is hitting-set cover rule

Whether S resolves a pair depends on nothing about the pair but the difference between its two inputs: S fails exactly when the product of its primes divides that difference. So a task's entire demand is one set of numbers, its difference set D — the differences of the pairs it requires told apart — and least-new growth is a hitting-set cover of D taken smallest-first, a prime p hitting a difference d precisely when p does not divide d. The same rule written from scratch as a cover algorithm — take the smallest prime that hits anything still uncovered — reproduces the grower step for step. The cover reading also prices the rule. Smallest-first is myopic, its grown set strictly above the minimum cover in 40 of 40 random small instances, because it takes 2 — odd differences are always present — where one larger prime would cover the whole demand at once, as the demand {1, 770} grows {2, 3} against a minimum cover of {3}; the minimum cover costs lookahead. The field's greedy ranks candidates by coverage and pays at most a harmonic factor over the optimum, a bound that is tight and, under standard assumptions, unbeatable in the worst case (Chvátal 1979; Slavík; Feige); least-new ranks by size, and the harmonic cap is not its theorem — a stricter myopia than the field's.

Scope. The difference-set reduction proved, its product form checked against the per-prime test on 4,000 sampled substrates and pairs; the from-scratch cover checked against the grower on a random task; the myopia rate an observation over 40 random instances on the first six primes. Toy scale.

The grown substrate is a counter rule

Demand every distinction there is — all of {0, …, M−1} pairwise separated — and the grown substrate is forced. Least-new takes 2, 3, 5, … in order and halts as soon as their product passes M−1, so what it grows is the primorial prefix, the first k primes with none skipped: depth 3, 4, 5 and 6 at M = 30, 210, 2310 and 30030. Nothing about the task enters but how fine a resolution it asks for. Unstructured data does the same: a prime resolves any difference it does not divide, so in a task with no structure every prime resolves something and smallest-first walks the prefix — 24 random labellings over one range grow one identical substrate. That is destination universality one level up. Where the reader adapts to its metabolism and not to its data, a substrate grown on data carrying no structure adapts to the resolution demanded and not to what is demanding it — a counter, whose depth the task sets and whose content it does not.

Scope. The all-distinct prefix law proved; the generic verdict an observation over 24 random labellings with a pool of twelve primes. Toy scale.

The flip condition observation

Structure in the data does move the substrate, and not far enough to pick a destination. A task living on the multiples of q has every difference divisible by q, so q resolves nothing and is never taken (proved): the multiples of 3, of 5 and of 7 grow {2, 5, 7, 11}, {2, 3, 7, 11} and {2, 3, 5, 11}, three different substrates. A prime skipped is what makes a designed tower rather than a prefix, and here the data does the designing, where the growth laws have it done by a capability demand. Yet those substrates transfer to each other near-intact: the one grown on the multiples of 3 leaves 0 of the multiples-of-5 task's 125 differences unresolved, and the reverse direction leaves 1 of 209, that lone residual being the single difference equal to the product of the resolving substrate's own primes. Redundant small primes still cover, so a different substrate is not a data-picked destination. What would be one is a unique-need difference, divisible by every pool prime but one — within the first five primes, 770 = 2·5·7·11 is resolvable only by 3 — together with a sibling, the second task of a transfer pair, that skips exactly that prime: the bare demand {770} grows {3}, and a sibling on the multiples of 3 never demands 3 and leaves that one distinction unresolved. The gap between the two is the count of differences uniquely needing the skipped prime. So structure alone does not open the door; what opened it, in the one construction built to open it, was that conjunction.

Scope. The skipped-prime law proved; the transfer figures and the flip condition observations on hand-designed tasks — the multiples-of-q families against a pool of twelve primes, and the unique-need pair on {0, …, 299} with a pool of five. Toy scale.

Language at the door

A conjunction met only by tasks built to meet it says nothing about data anyone has, so the question is whether real structure meets it. Natural language is the obvious place to look, and reaching it takes two maps rather than one. A surface form is a string of symbols — walk, walked, walks. Inflectional morphology arranges those forms in a paradigm: one row per lexeme, the word a dictionary lists, and one column per cell, the grammatical slot the form fills. Morphology is concatenative where a cell is a fixed affix glued to a shared stem and fusional where the cell shows as a change inside the stem, as foot/feet and go/went. The two maps are these. An embedding carries a surface form to a number the substrate can be asked about; an encoder then arranges those numbers so that moving between cells is one fixed transform applied to any lexeme. Neither map is the grammar, and that is what the three claims turn on: a substrate grown on language-shaped data can be tracking either.

The encoding is what the substrate tracks observation

Build the tasks in grammar's own terms — stems against affixes, a paradigm of lexemes against cells, a fusional table of feature values — embed each surface form by the obvious reading, its symbols as the digits of an integer in base B, and grow. What decides the answer is the base. Where the affix is a single symbol, a task whose labels are the cells never produces a difference divisible by B — a cross-cell difference is congruent to the difference of two distinct symbols, nonzero and smaller than the base — so for squarefree B the prime factors of B suffice for every distinction the task asks (proved). Sufficiency is not containment, and which primes least-new takes is where the factors sit in the pool: B = 30, whose factors are the pool's three smallest, grows exactly {2, 3, 5}; B = 35 grows {2, 3, 5, 7} and not {5, 7}, every pool prime below its larger factor and then that factor; the prime base 29 has no factor in the pool and grows the generic prefix, as a structureless task does. One set per base, the encoding setting the content. Compositionality — one fixed transform per cell, shared across every lexeme — is visible in the arithmetic and not in the substrate: the same-lexeme demand collapses onto the differences between the transforms alone, 431 differences against a matched non-compositional control's 644, and both grow the same generic prefix. Compression is not unique need. Natural grammar does carry unique-need differences, and even pool-unresolvable ones, and the door stays shut anyway: every sibling pair transfers near-intact, worst residual 0.625% of the demand and most exactly zero, the two leaks encoding-scale — one a difference no pool prime resolves at all, one from the sizes of the lexemes' codes. Grammatical distinctions are local, hence small differences, hence divisible by few primes and redundantly covered. The door opens only for a designed encoder whose transforms are chosen to be unique-need — the 770 gap against a multiples-of-3 sibling — which re-imports the structure the growth was meant to reveal.

Scope. Base sufficiency and compositional compression rules, mechanism-proved; which set least-new lands on read off three bases; the transfer verdicts and the difference-set counts observations on these grammar families and encodings with the first five primes as the pool, where a unique-need difference is cheapest to reach and so the fairest shot the door gets. Toy scale.

The encoder does not move the door rule

Both growth tests used additive encoders. Bind with the ring's own operation instead — the exact vector-symbolic encoder on Z/510510, the integers modulo the product of the first seven primes, the pool staying the first five: a form is a lexeme L times a cell value K, both units, residues coprime to the modulus and so invertible there, and unbinding is the ring's own inverse. The door verdict is unchanged, and the mechanism is proved: a same-lexeme difference across two cells is L·(K′ − K) modulo every pool prime, and a unit carries no pool factor, so a pool prime divides it exactly when it divides the cell difference — given the same cell values in both, the multiplicative and the additive encoder grow the identical substrate, siblings transfer near-intact at a residual of 0.134%, and only a cell gap chosen to be unique-need opens it. What the multiplication buys is capability rather than a destination. Fix one cell as the reference and one lexeme as the anchor: a cell's transform is the ratio of the anchor's two forms, the lexeme factor cancels out of that ratio, and the same transform applied to any other lexeme's reference form lands on that lexeme's form in that cell — every held-out analogy, 55 of 55, where a non-compositional control scores 0. That capability is priced in unit membership and degrades by degrees: an encoder whose forms are mostly non-units completes 3 of the 55 exactly, and the ring's pseudo-inverse still recovers every per-prime remainder on which the anchor is invertible, 277 of the held-out forms' 385 reads, a 0.719 fraction, garbling the rest; the structural cure is to encode into the unit group, capacity φ(N). The encoder is where compositional capability lives; it is not a lever on the door.

Scope. The encoder-invariance of same-lexeme resolvability a rule, mechanism-proved; the analogy lift, the per-window price and the transfer residual observations on these paradigm families over Z/510510. Toy scale.

The embedding factors through the abelianization rule

Both readings above took their embedding as given, symbols read as digits in the first and forms handed over as ring elements in the second. The natural candidate is the map that respects concatenation, the monoid homomorphism: give each alphabet symbol a unit and send a string to the product of its symbols'. For concatenative morphology that is free. The image of a stem followed by an affix is the product of their images, so the transform extracted for a cell is the affix's alone, and completion runs from raw strings at 55 of 55 against 0 of 55 for a control that hands each distinct string its own unrelated unit — a structural constraint and not a per-lexeme table. Its price is order, and the price is forced rather than chosen. Multiplication commutes, so the map cannot see the sequence of symbols, only which occur and how often — 13 forms collapse onto 3 values, and an affix at the front is indistinguishable from the same affix at the back. No choice of units repairs that: every homomorphism from strings under concatenation into a commutative ring factors through the abelianization, the quotient in which any two symbols may be exchanged freely, and all of the order lives in exactly what that quotient forgets. Tagging each symbol with its position separates the anagrams and breaks the shared transform, because the affix then sits at a place that depends on the stem's length: completion holds at 33 of 33 where the stems share a length and falls to 0 of 33 where they do not. The two natural embeddings pay opposite halves of one exchange, and the same trade sits under the tower itself, between an exact test for zero and a native reading of order (crown and blind spot) — an echo across two constructions, not a second impossibility. Fusional morphology settles on the same side: a cell whose change is idiosyncratic per lexeme shares no transform, so the homomorphism completes 0 of 33 forms it was not shown, a table read off the seen lexemes reads seen forms and no held-out one, and an embedding built to respect the relation restores 33 of 33 — building it from the surface asks which stems pattern together, a similarity grading. Where the classes are tree-shaped that embedding need not be learned, the ring's own agreement depth carrying it exactly (the similarity carrier), and what deepest-match retrieval can read exactly of any similarity grading is bounded by the readable hull.

Scope. The homomorphism identity and the position-length trade rules, mechanism-proved. The completion rates, the collision counts and the fusional verdict observations over Z/510510, a Latin alphabet, these paradigm families, and the fusional extreme taken as cells whose forms share no symbols at all. What is claimed of order is a statement about commutative targets, not about every embedding: nothing here rules out a native rule that holds both order and a shared transform. Toy scale.
