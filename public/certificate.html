The certificate

A seated place's door reads the whole state, and the schedule family has no such price. Run the family's four laws against a cost built to read the state, and three survive; what makes a lock permanent turns out to be a finite certificate, a monotone door, and one theorem saying the walk settles on a single vehicle.

Throughout, a state is a nonzero ring element, a move multiplies it, and λ is the exponent of the unit group of the quotient. A place is a prime of the ring with an integer degree; seating one is an opening, deepening a seated one a clock move, which advances the tick every price is read against. A place's column is λ read across its depths, its ladder the depths where the column jumps, the gap the least climb that raises λ, and the door of a place its gap at the depth it stands: a lone door is read against the place's own column, a populated door against the state's invariant L, the least common multiple of the seated places' λ's, and the populated one is what a ring charges. The surplus at a seated place over p is the power of p standing in L above what the place's own λ carries. The menu is the priced set of admissible moves; a walk locks when it stops opening for good, the move it repeats forever is its recurrent vehicle, and the budget is what that vehicle pays. A coordinate carried up without bound is a runaway, one left above exponent 1 by a move never affordable again a strand. The schedule family — items of integer degree under a global or per-item clock, a price read off degree and tick — is bound at The pricing schedule; its four laws here are the limit's shape, the budget, the degree ceiling (how deep a state's deepest degree can sit) and the strand sort, and what one place hands it is The clock.

A cost that reads the state

The four laws separate three ways under a state-read cost, and only the stop law dies rule

Give each item a TAG standing for the p-part of its own column, and let its door be the least climb whose column outruns the largest contribution of the other seated items. On the exact ladder — the one the local arithmetic generates — this reproduces the door identity, door = vp(L) − e + 2. The door inverts its ladder's valuation — linear in the surplus at a constant gap, exponential on a multiplicative one, pricing a function field's state-widened move out of every range — which is why the walks are the constant-gap ladders. What costs the family its shape is the tag's VARIANCE: under a uniform tag 0 of 121 (item, depth) pairs read two doors, under tags differing across items 120 of 121; read back at the ring, the excess is set by the spread of the seated residue cardinalities. THE STOP LAW DIES at 3 of 15 cells, every one the growing-tag regime, at gaps 1, 2 and 3 alike: the recurrent move reads 248, 494, 740 against a cheapest opening of 124, and by a mechanism the derivation did not offer — every opening raises the surplus before an item can take its first move, 122 items seated and none above exponent 1, an ever-widening flat support with no deep coordinate. THE BUDGET SURVIVES AND THE SORT DOES NOT: at every stopping cell both products predict the flat tail minimum, while at gap 3 under a sparse tag the naive product reads a strand at the budget where its door reads 18, priced out. A widened door changes what is seated at 8 of 12 cells. THE CEILING survives a door that reads the state and collapses to the least degree under a price reading the landing depth; that cost climbs at all 10 cells, the global-clock shape survives it — one coordinate above exponent 1 at all five ladders — and the per-item side breaks at 20 to 44. So a bounded gap stops a walk only where the recurrent price cannot read the state, which is the whole of what the stop law on The clock is scoped to.

Scope. A rule in range: two costs, 3 ladders × 5 tag regimes and 5 ladders × 2 clocks at 120 moves, 3,120 states each carrying a brute full-scan menu beside the early-stop one at 0 disagreements.

Why the populated readings are safe

A door never falls while its own exponent is untouched, and that is not a fact about the ring theorem

If L divides L′, every climb below the door at L keeps λ(Qe+r) dividing L′, so a place's cost never falls while its own exponent is untouched — a lemma consulting the transitivity of divisibility and nothing else, holding over worlds whose columns are independent random draws. A surplus can only price a place OUT. What is arithmetic is the hypothesis under it, that L grows at a move, which the column being a CHAIN supplies — the unit group of O/Pa+1 surjecting onto that of O/Pa — sufficient and not necessary: delete it and the invariant loses primes at 1,386 of 1,416 steps and doors fall at 311 pairs, a place priced back in. What is bounded outright is the SUPPORT: a place is seated only by winning the menu and a door is at least 1, so every norm a move seats is at most the largest cost the walk ever pays, tight at 10 of 12 walks over five rings and free in every world. That settles the residue route's race without running it: a surplus of k needs a seated norm ≥ pk + 1, so the walked cross-supply is at most log2 of the largest price paid. The one ring fact consumed is λ(Q) = N(Q) − 1, a first rung cheaper than its place; decoupling the two reads a supply of 7 against a ceiling of 1. The largest cross-supply measured, 3 at Z[i]'s inert place over 3, sits exactly at its ceiling ⌊log2 9⌋, attained precisely when N − 1 is a pure p-power.

Scope. A theorem for the lemma and the support bound; the standing hypothesis measured at 1,416 steps over 24 ring-free worlds.

What makes a lock permanent

A lock carries a finite certificate of permanence, and the surplus is frozen or diverges — greedy takes the first theorem

Simulate the vehicle-only future, take its largest cost C* and the cheapest rival m at that step: C* < m carries the lock to infinity by the monotone door. The certificate is sufficient, so its step is an upper bound on the lock step, and it fires only where the vehicle's door sequence repeats — a bounded gap, the stop law's own hypothesis: over columns whose gaps grow the doors climb without period and no step of a 500-move walk certifies. Its rival exceeds C* only because the places under a norm bound are finitely many; over 600 places at one floor norm the walk seats a fresh place every move and nothing certifies. Under a lock only the vehicle's prime moves in L, so every rival's door reaches a closed form, finite or infinite, and a co-characteristic place falls in the second class by being seated, its covered first rung the whole of its condition away from p. After a certified lock at P over p, a seated place over another prime holds its surplus forever, and a seated place over p itself has one growing with vp(L) — realized by no walk, 0 of 16, because a seated place's covered rung makes it cheaper than its unseated sibling and greedy takes it as the vehicle; only a seed holding every place over p forces the configuration, and then the surplus gains exactly what vp(L) gains, 440 over 440 moves. So "a walk's surplus stays small" is a fact about greedy, and past a certified lock 500 moves and infinity read the same number.

Scope. A theorem for the certificate and the dichotomy; 12 of 12 walks over five rings certified at 500 moves; the two deletions read at the ring-free worlds.

The walk settles on one vehicle: between the stop of openings and the certificate there is nothing theorem

A walk over a number ring's places that opens finitely many places locks on exactly one of them. Once seated, a place's door reads vp(L) against its own level and a move raises it by exactly one, so once every recurrent place has moved inside its arithmetic regime the mover drops to its steady cost, the norm to the ramification index, while no rival's door falls; the cost sequence never rises, and a non-rising sequence of positive integers is eventually constant — after which only places attaining vp(L) move, at most one per prime, and the fixed tie order picks one forever. The 140 seeds read as the proof says, zero rises past the regime. The third case is real where the regime is deleted: two thinning columns over distinct primes change vehicle 307 times in 400 moves, opening nothing past the third and certifying nowhere. The hypothesis left standing is the stop of openings itself, proved over Z and censused at the other rings (Growth); on the sprawl side the same cost sequence read for divergence gives the counterpart — a place picked infinitely often at bounded cost absorbs the tail, so a bounded-cost tail is equivalent to a lock and equal-characteristic costs diverge (Growth over other fields). The monotone is the number ring's, bought by the regime; equal characteristic has none to enter.

Scope. A theorem; 9,168 places over five rings to depth 80 read for the hypotheses, 140 seeds for the mechanism, the third case realized where the regime is deleted.

What the widening selects

The populated door is a different selection rule, and what it selects is an unseated place always, its widening necessary and not sufficient rule

Pricing every step by each place's LONE invariant names a different chosen place at 409 of 720 steps, in 7 of 12 walks across three rings. Every widened lone winner is an UNSEATED place, 410 of 410 — a seated re-pricing never selects, a strand being by construction not what a menu is about to buy. The covering splits by norm: at 293 the winner has norm above 2 with N − 1 dividing the invariant, the state having paid its first rung so the lone rule still sells it; the other 117 are norm-2 places widened off the second rung. Of the 410, 409 part, and the one that does not is widened from door 1 to 2 and still the cheapest under both rules: what parts a menu is the widening LOSING THE ARGMIN. The condition is no coarse invariant — the cheapest norm, a norm-2 place's presence and void-against-planted each disagree with the verdict somewhere — and the five agreeing walks agree for two reasons, four never carrying a covered lone winner and the fifth surviving its one.

Scope. A rule in range: twelve walks over five rings, 720 steps priced under both rules.
