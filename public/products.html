Where the products lose

At 83 of 695 charted cells the cheapest integer polynomial vanishing to a given order at 1 is not a product of factors xd − 1. The failures looked like a law about the rank of a lattice and are the chart's own corner crossing a threshold in the depth, a threshold each rank has its own value of; past it the minimum has a closed form, measured wherever a scan reached and proved at rank 2, where the same four-line case list names the lattice's second successive minimum. Inside the window the depth drives the failure rate and nothing else selects, the lattice's own geometry included.

A vector of integers c0, …, cM−1 is the polynomial P(x) = Σ cixi; its width is M, its height the largest |ci|, and its depth J the multiplicity of the root 1. Write h(M, J) for the least height of a nonzero vector of width M and depth at least J, computed exactly at each cell (M, J) of a chart of 695 cells with 4 ≤ M ≤ 40 and 2 ≤ J ≤ 30. The vectors of a cell form a lattice, the multiples of (x−1)J cut at degree below M, of rank r = M − J; every one is a cofactor q of degree below the rank times (x−1)J. A pure product is a product of factors xd − 1 with at least J of them and degree below M, its cofactor a pure cofactor; the least height over the pure products fitting a cell is the pure bound, and a cell fails when h sits strictly below it. A rank's column is its cells read upward in the depth; its first failing depth Jlo and last Jhi bound its band. The champion of a rank is the cofactor (1+x)r−1, of the largest vanishing order at −1 a cofactor of that degree can carry. How h is computed is on Flattening; what attains it where the products lose is on The multipliers.

The band

The rank window is the chart's corner crossing a per-rank depth threshold rule

Every one of the 83 failures has rank between 5 and 18, and the 325 cells outside that window fail zero times, 221 of them with h ≥ 2 and so able to fail; the window did not move when 145 cells at J = 21 to 30 were added, forty of them at rank at most 4 and one at rank 19. Scan each rank's own column past the corner and record Jlo: the ranks that can fail on a chart cut at M ≤ 40 and J ≤ 30 are exactly those with Jlo(r) ≤ min(30, 40 − r), and that set is {5, …, 18}, the census's failing ranks rank for rank. Inside the window the agreement restates a fact; the content is at the ranks the window excludes, and the two walls turn out to be different things. The scan reaches ranks 1 to 22, so ranks 23 to 38 keep their exclusion from the census's own rectangle rather than from a measurement.

Scope. Rule in range: the census exhaustive over the 695 cells, the column scans exhaustive over their stated depths, exact integer arithmetic throughout. Toy scale.

Inside the window the depth drives the rate, and nothing else selects rule

Of the 370 window cells 83 fail, 22.4% raw and 25.9% over the 321 that can fail, and by depth 3 of 154, 17 of 112 and 63 of 104: 1.9%, 15.2% and 60.6% raw, 2.9%, 15.2% and 60.6% conditioned on h ≥ 2. Eight cheap cell quantities were scored as one-sided cuts on the failure flag, the stall in the pure argmin, the divisor count of J, a ball-counting heuristic at two radii, two plateaus in the pure bound, the rank and the depth: seven have a degenerate best threshold predicting that nobody fails, and the eighth buys 7 of the 83 for one false positive. The rank scores degenerate there because a cut has one side and a window two, and no variable but the rank was tried as a window. Two nulls carry content. The pure bound's plateau, the cost of taking four atoms of width away, is large at the failures, a factor 4.9 against 2.0, so a failing cell is one where the pure family degrades fast as the width narrows, far from the true minimum, and not one where an enrichment is cheap; as excess over chance it carries 0.276 against the raw depth's 0.358 on the same cells, a repaired mechanism and not a second variable. And at some failing cells the Euclidean ball of radius (pure bound − 1)√M has volume below the covolume and contains a lattice point all the same, so the sup-norm question is not well approximated by the ball it is enumerated in.

Scope. Rule for the window's rates, exhaustive over its 370 cells; observation for the scoring, a best cut chosen per quantity by its two error counts, with a quantity that must separate perfectly and one that must not both behaving as they must before any verdict is read. Toy scale.

The champion

The mechanism is the vanishing order at −1, and it names a closed form rule

A pure product with c ≥ J parts is (x−1)c∏[di]x with [d]x = (xd − 1)/(x − 1), so its cofactor is (x−1)t∏d>1[d]x with t + Σ(d − 1) ≤ r − 1. The coefficients of (x−1)J are the alternating binomial row, a bell of width about √J/2, and each factor (1+x) of the cofactor differences that row, costing a factor of order 1/√J in the height, so to leading order in J only the vanishing order of the cofactor at −1 matters, and the largest a cofactor of degree below r can carry is r − 1, attained by the multiples of the champion alone. The champion's product is a pure product, (x²−1)r−1(x−1)J−r+1, exactly when J ≥ r − 1; below that it is a lattice vector the pure family does not contain, and the chart has such cells, rank 38 at M = 40, J = 2. Where the mechanism binds,

h(M,J)=height⁡((1+x) r−1(x−1)J),h(M, J) = \operatorname{height}\bigl((1+x)^{\,r-1}(x-1)^{J}\bigr),

with no search of any kind. Attained continuously from J = 7 and 13 through J = 140 at ranks 3 and 4, and at every depth past the far end that the scan reached at ranks 5 to 8. Above those depths the minimiser is unique up to sign: collecting the whole minimiser set at the fixed radius h√M, sixty-nine of the 86 closed cells at ranks 2, 3 and 4 carry exactly ±(1+x)r−1(x−1)J, every cell from J = 2 at rank 2, from 9 at rank 3, a stricter threshold than attainment's 7 since the champion ties without being alone at 7 and 8, and from 13 at rank 4, their vanishing orders 1, 2 and 3; below those depths the set is larger, up to eight minimisers carrying three vanishing orders at rank 3, J = 3.

Scope. The mechanism is a heuristic asymptotic in J and carries no tier; what it did was PREDICT the closed form. The attainment is a rule in range, read off column scans with stopping rules and never a proof that it never reverses, and a theorem at rank 2 below by a route that owes the mechanism nothing. The uniqueness counts are the collecting pass's, exhaustive at the 86 cells it closes. Toy scale.

At rank 2 the minimum is a difference of binomial coefficients theorem

The rank-2 lattice is {(a + bx)(x−1)J}, so with ck = C(J, k), zero outside 0 ≤ k ≤ J, the coefficient of xk is ±(a·ck − b·ck−1) and h is the least value of H(a, b) = maxk |a·ck − b·ck−1| over (a, b) ≠ (0, 0). For every J ≥ 2,

max⁡k∣a ck−b ck−1∣  ≥  max⁡k∣ck−ck−1∣,\max_k \lvert a\,c_k - b\,c_{k-1}\rvert \;\ge\; \max_k \lvert c_k - c_{k-1}\rvert,

with equality only at (a, b) = ±(1, 1), so h(J+2, J) is the largest difference of adjacent binomial coefficients and the minimiser is ±(1+x)(x−1)J. The proof turns on the palindrome: the row reads the same backwards, so reversal is a lattice symmetry swapping a and b, which collapses the positive quadrant to a ≥ b. At the peak index m = ⌊J/2⌋, the first index attaining the maximum N = cm, the row is still strictly rising, so a pair with a > b ≥ 1 already pays (a − b)N ≥ N where the target D is at most N − 1; a zero coordinate or opposite signs pay N on the same line; and a = b = t pays tD by homogeneity, D at t = 1 and at least 2D otherwise, which is what the uniqueness rests on. The hypothesis enters once, as N ≥ 2; at J = 1 the conclusion is false, (1, 0) tying with (1, 1). The same case list gives the second successive minimum, λ2 = C(J, ⌊J/2⌋), attained among vectors independent of the minimiser at ±(1, 0) and ±(0, 1) and nowhere else, the scope load-bearing since a multiple of the minimiser also reaches height N whenever h divides N, at J = 2, 4, 5 and 13 among the first thirteen depths, and the choice of ⌊J/2⌋ over ⌈J/2⌉ deciding the attaining set, the two central entries tying at odd J. So a rank-2 flattening lattice has no second short vector, its two minima separated by the whole differencing gain, λ2/λ1 ≈ (√e/2)√(J+1), 2.41% high at J = 10 and 0.01% at J = 2000. The bridge to the pure bound: the rank-2 pure family offers only the cofactors 1, x − 1 and 1 + x, and 1 gives height N while x − 1 gives C(J+1, ⌊(J+1)/2⌋) > N by Pascal's rule, so the pure bound equals h at rank 2 too, and that rank leaves the failing set for a reason.

Scope. Theorem, every J ≥ 2, no computation at any value. The instrument checks the proof: the lemmas at every depth 2 to 200, and at depths 2 to 14 a complete enumeration over the whole integer plane, made finite by the peak inequality cutting it to a strip of about two candidates per first coordinate, which re-derives both minima and both attaining sets; controls at J = 1, where the statement is false, and two mutated targets rejected at every depth. The ratio's asymptotic is measured and not proved, its residual oscillating because the argmax is an integer where the saddle point is not. The route does not carry to rank 3 on its own: a cofactor vanishing at −1 makes the rank-2 question over the first-difference row, which is antisymmetric rather than palindromic, and the height comparison the argument needs fails at every J from 2 to 8 and holds from 9 to 200, rank 3's own threshold being 7. Toy scale.

At low rank the champion's threshold is decided inside the pure family, and the winner below it has the wrong order rule

Ranks 3 and 4 fail at no depth swept and still have a threshold, J = 7 and 13, below which some other pure product wins a clean cell. The pure family there is 7 and 14 cofactors once the depth carries them all, and comparing their heights directly the champion fails to be the least exactly below the thresholds the lattice scans measured, the last non-attaining depth being 6 at rank 3 and 12 at rank 4. Who wins instead: 1 + x at rank 3, the champion of the rank below, alone at J = 6 and tying with 1 + x + x² at 2 and 4; and (1 + x)(1 + x + x²) alone at every depth 3 to 12 at rank 4, whose vanishing order at −1 is 1 and not 3. Below the threshold the order-counting argument points the wrong way. The non-attaining sets are ragged, {2, 4, 6} and {3, …, 10, 12}, the gaps exact ties, the same raggedness the failing band's low edge carries; rank 4's set starts at 3 because at J = 2 the champion is absent from the family, a pure product with |E| parts above 1 needing J + t − |E| parts equal to 1, a constraint the degree budget does not imply and which moves the pure minimum at none of the 496 answer-key cells of rank 2 to 20.

Scope. Rule in range: the comparison is of pure heights, so the statement is about the pure bound, and what makes it a statement about h at these two ranks is their agreement over the column scan's 139 depths each. It says nothing about where any rank first fails. Toy scale.
