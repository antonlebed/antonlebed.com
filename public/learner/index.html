LEARNER — a reader that learns by exact comparisons of its price

The object: a reader of a continued-fraction stream. At step n it knows the image J_n = f(I_n), I_n the input's tile of depth n + 1 (READING, RATES), so that step 0 already reads a₀, and f a monotone map, and it commits a member of the straddle cover holding J_n, never undoing a commitment. The cover's members are the Stern–Brocot tiles and the straddles S_k(v) of RATES: The ladder law, written out in The straddle cover and its readers. A member holds an interval when it contains the interval's closure. The deficit of a step is the image's scale less the committed interval's, scale being ln(1/length), the price RATES: The commitment bound bounds for a tile reader. Learning here is strict descent on a finite set of policies, every comparison an exact cross-multiplication of big integers. The question is what that descent finds and what the optimum it finds depends on; then the substrate itself grows, on the collisions a labelled set of integers presents.

The answer, as far as it is carried: with no resource limit, under every loss monotone in the committed intervals, the optimum at a step whose image is a bounded interval of positive length held by the root depends on nothing but the image. The members holding an image have a least one, its bottom (The bottom lemma), though they seldom form a chain, and every reader that commits soundly (Greedy patience dominates every sound reader) commits a member containing the bottom at every such step. So the best reader commits the bottom and reads nothing from the stream that J_n does not already say. No step that picks it reads a magnitude: the moves test containment, which compares endpoints in the order of the real line, and the deficit's lengths only price a choice that containment has already made. On the eight (map, stream) rows read (The straddle cover and its readers), descent finds that optimum from every start but the four policies that refuse every move, which never leave the root.

With an income of rank per step (rank as in The straddle cover and its readers) the bottom can fall out of reach, wherever the images deepen faster than the income, and the members holding an image fall into layers by rank, each layer an antichain. Every greedy reader that spends all it has sits at one rank fixed by the images and the resources alone, so two of them can differ only by committing incomparable members of one layer. That is where the policy enters. On the nine rows read, their best choices are not all the same, but at every income and bank tried some choice is best on every row at once, and which one depends on the income and the bank, no policy being best at all sixteen: a reader taking it learns its resources and not its data.

Scored on forecasting the stream, the reader's optimum does come to depend on it, by a route the cover fixes. A forecast read as a density at the outcome is a length loss and changes nothing. A forecast of which side of a cut the image point, f of the stream's point, falls on leaves the nine rows' best readers with no member in common, one scored step being enough. With no resource limit, at every step where a reader forecasts better than the greedy one, it commits an interval containing the greedy one's: the stream enters at a reversal, a nested pair the loss orders against containment, never inside a layer (The stream enters at a reversal). So for every reader without a limit and every greedy spend-all reader under an income, a choice escapes the image at two places only, a layer the income leaves open and a reversal the loss makes; readers patient under an income are not covered. The reversal is what breaks the common optimum, and the stream is not: a loss reading only the committed lengths breaks it as well.

Grown on a labelled set's collisions, a substrate of primes is the tower (TOWER) read by the data: the least-new grower (The collision grower) takes exactly the primes at which the chain of unresolved differences drops, which are the least primes not dividing the differences. The labelled set fixes how fine it is by the largest of them, and which primes it takes only by a skip, a prime below that no difference names; the 24 random labelled sets tried, 0 … 209 in eight classes, all have the primorials 1, 2, 6 and 30 among their differences and every difference below 210, which leaves no skip. Collisions only fall as a substrate grows, so no loss that counts them alone orders two nested substrates against inclusion: under such a loss two labelled sets' substrates part either by depth, one grower stopping where the other goes on, which the loss orders with inclusion and which reads the data itself as the image does, or inside a layer of the order by inclusion, the substrates of one size, the counterpart of the reader's layer and the only one of the two places where a choice escapes that it can reach. The prime a difference names is p_(α + 1), α the tower's agreement on the pair (The grown depth is the readable depth), so while the separated pairs agree below the pool's end the grown depth is the level at which the labels first read exactly off the tower's residue classes. Exact reading is tree-shaped in every world, the line's distance included: it is the readout, not the size the tower deletes, that forces the tree, and the tower is a world that loses nothing to it.

The straddle cover and its readers

Tier: property; observation (the 38 behaviour classes).
Verifier: proof; learner.py::section_cover, learner.py::section_landscape, forecast.py::section_controls.

Write a member as (l, r, k): (l, r) a unimodular pair of integer points, the tile with vertex v = l + r, and k ≥ 0, the member being the interval (l + kv, r + kv), so k = 0 is the tile and k ≥ 1 the straddle S_k(v). Its rank is v's tree depth plus k, the tree depth counting Stern–Brocot steps from the root tile (0/1, 1/0) at 0: a quotient a spans a tree steps, so the tree depth is not the continued-fraction depth that indexes I_n. Two moves leave a member: the tree move to the child tile (l + kv, v) or (v, r + kv) holding a reference, the image J_(n − p), p set by the policy below, and the chain move to the largest index whose straddle at the same tile holds a reference, taken when it exceeds k. Since det(l + kv, v) = det(l, r), every member a move reaches is again a tile with an index. S_(k+1)(v) lies inside S_k(v), and S_1(v) inside v's tile.

A policy has four coordinates: which move it prefers at a tile, which at a straddle, and two patiences p_tree and p_chain in 0, 1, 2, 3 and ∞, so 2·2·5·5 = 100 policies. A move of a kind with patience p must hold J_(n − p), and ∞ refuses the kind. At each step the reader moves, in its preferred order, until no move is legal. Two policies are neighbours when one flipped preference or one patience step separates them. The loss on a row, the deficit loss, is the product of the committed lengths over the counted steps, infinite when a committed interval is unbounded, and a set of rows' loss multiplies its rows'.

Two policies whose committed intervals agree at every counted step cannot be ordered by any loss read off those steps, so a tie between them is the design's and not the landscape's: the 100 policies fall into 38 such behaviour classes over the eight rows below, and the ninth row (Destination universality) splits none of them.

The slate is eight (map, stream) rows: the identity on the golden ratio, √2, √3 and [0; 8, 8, …], the eights; y ↦ y² on √2 and on the golden ratio; y ↦ 2y on the golden ratio and on √2. The row y ↦ y² on √2 is the wall row: its images close on the rational 2, the vertex of the tile (1, ∞), so no tile below (1, ∞) holds them. A row is read over the 120 steps 0 to 119, and loss counts steps 8 to 119.

The bottom lemma

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; learner.py::section_cover.

Let J be a bounded interval of positive length held by the root tile, I* the deepest tile holding J, and k* the largest index whose straddle at I* holds J, or 0. Then (I*, k*), the bottom of J, lies inside every member of the cover holding J.

Proof. Two tiles are nested or disjoint, so the tiles holding J form a chain, finite because J is bounded and has positive length, and I* exists. Let C = (I, k) hold J. S_1 of I's vertex lies inside I, so I holds J and I* lies inside I. If I = I*, then k ≤ k* and the member (I*, k*) lies inside C. If I* lies strictly inside I, then J's closure misses I's vertex v, since otherwise no tile below I would hold J; so, with I = (l, r), J lies inside one of C's two child tiles (l + kv, v) and (v, r + kv), that child holds J, and I* lies inside it and so inside C.

The members holding J are not a chain in general. S_1 of the root is (1/2, 2) and S_1 of the tile (0, 1) is (1/3, 2/3), and an image whose closure lies inside (1/2, 2/3) is held by both. On the slate, over the 120 steps, the holding set has two members neither inside the other at 826 of 959 row-steps at least, counting only crossings among the path tiles and their outermost and innermost straddles, and the bottom lies inside every holding member: the script checks each path tile with its outermost and innermost straddle, which bound the rest since straddles at one vertex nest, 193,362 distinct member-steps. The containment test can fail: the bottom with its index lowered by one, a strictly larger member, fails at the 219 row-steps whose bottom has index 1 or more.

Greedy patience dominates every sound reader

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; learner.py::section_readers, learner.py::section_tight.

Call a reader sound when its committed interval holds J_n at every step whose image is a bounded interval of positive length held by the root. At every such step, every sound reader's interval contains bottom(J_n); the sequence bottom(J_n) is itself sound and never undone; and every policy with both patiences 0 commits it, whatever its preferences. So greedy patience minimizes every loss that is monotone in the committed intervals over such steps, on every stream at once; the deficit loss is among them on every stream whose counted images are all bounded intervals of positive length held by the root.

Proof. A committed interval holds J_n, so by the bottom lemma bottom(J_n) lies inside it. J_(n+1) lies inside J_n, so bottom(J_n) holds J_(n+1) and contains bottom(J_(n+1)). At both patiences 0 every legal move holds J_n, the loop starts from its last commitment, which holds J_(n − 1) and so J_n, or from the root, which holds J_n, and it stops only where neither move is legal, which is at the bottom: at a member (I, k) above it, either I* lies strictly inside I and the bottom lemma's proof hands a child tile holding J_n, or I = I* and k < k*.

With no resource limit, then, the optimum is a function of the image alone, the same function on every stream: descent that finds it has learned nothing about the data. Every policy has a floor, the policy with its preferences and both patiences 0, which at every step whose image is a bounded interval of positive length held by the root commits inside it and, since distinct nested intervals differ in length when the outer is bounded, strictly shorter wherever the two differ and the policy's member is bounded; the bottom itself can be unbounded, as (1, ∞) is for the image (3/2, 5/2). Preferences are free at both patiences 0, where every move holds the one reference J_n; with two references they can matter: at patiences (7, 6), outside the policies' range, on the golden ratio at step 8 the tree reference is (3/2, 2) and the chain reference (3/2, 5/3); tree first commits the tile (1, ∞) and is stuck, chain first commits (1/2, 2). All 95,900 policy-steps of the 100 policies on the slate contain the bottom, and the four greedy policies commit it at every step.

The partition valley

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; learner.py::section_valley.

With tiles alone the holding members are the chain of I*, so the greedy tile reader commits I*(J_n) and a tile reader of patience p commits I*(J_(n − p)), which contains I*(J_n). The loss is monotone in patience on every stream, strictly where the compared losses are finite and the committed tile deepens, and refusing every tile is worst. On the seven rows off the wall the loss rises strictly along patience 0, 1, 2, 3 and is infinite at ∞.

The funnel with one blind corner

Tier: rule (verified over the 100 policies on the slate over the 120 steps; the minimum and the policies with no better neighbour again over 240 steps).
Verifier: learner.py::section_landscape.

Strict descent over neighbours reaches the greedy optimum from every policy outside the refusal corner, the four policies with both patiences ∞. The minimum set is the four greedy policies, tied exactly; 36 policies have infinite loss; and the only policies above the minimum with no strictly better neighbour are the refusal corner's four. First- and best- improvement descent from all 100 starts reach the minimum in 192 of 200 runs, the other eight being the corner started in itself.

The corner is blind because every neighbour's loss is infinite too. The tree-only reader is infinite on the wall row, where it stops at the unbounded tile (1, ∞) above the image 2; the chain-only reader, read at chain patience 0, never leaves the root on five rows, since a straddle at the root holds only images around 1 and every deeper straddle needs a tree move first, and every policy refusing tree moves is among the 36 infinite ones. So single moves out of the corner compare infinity with infinity. The corner commits the unbounded root at every step, so no finite loss separates it from its neighbours.

The layers of a holding set

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; rationed.py::section_layers.

Let J be an image that is a bounded interval of positive length held by the root, with path tiles T_0 … T_h at tree depths 0 … h and k_i* the largest index whose straddle at T_i holds J, or 0. The members holding J are the (T_i, k) with k ≤ k_i*, of rank i + k. If one holding member lies strictly inside another, its rank is higher. So the members of one rank, a layer, are pairwise incomparable, and the bottom, inside every other member, is alone in the top layer. Every rank R from 0 to the bottom's carries a member: T_R for R ≤ h, and (I*, R − h) above.

Proof. Let M′ = (T_j, k′) lie strictly inside M = (T_i, k). If j = i the straddles nest and k′ > k. If j < i, M lies inside a child tile of T_j, which misses T_j's vertex, while M′ is T_j or straddles its vertex, so M′ is not inside M; hence j > i, and the rank rises if k = 0. If k ≥ 1, write T_i = (l, r) with vertex v_i = l + r; J's closure misses v_i, since T_j lies below T_i, so J lies in a child tile of S_k(v_i), say (l + k v_i, v_i), and the path below T_i runs T_(i+m) = (l + (m − 1) v_i, v_i) for m ≤ k + 1. For j ≤ i + k the tile T_j reaches left of S_k's end l + k v_i, and a straddle at T_j holds its vertex l + (j − i) v_i, at or left of that end, in its interior; so M′ is not inside M. Hence j ≥ i + k + 1, and the rank of M′ exceeds i + k.

On the nine rows (the slate and the ninth row of Destination universality) over steps 0 to 20, with indices up to 40 and each tile's top two, 927,384 ordered pairs break none of this, and 152 of the 188 row-steps have a layer of two or more.

Under an income the rank is common

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; rationed.py::section_common_rank.

A rationed reader receives an income of B rank units a step. A move costs the rank it gains, and a chain move truncates to what is left, which stays sound because the straddles at one vertex nest. Unspent units go to a bank of cap W, overflow lost, and a drawdown coordinate caps a step's spending at B + δ, δ = ∞ drawing whatever is banked. Let the reader have both patiences 0 and δ = ∞, a greedy spend-all reader. Then from ρ_(−1) = b_(−1) = 0 its rank follows ρ_n = min(ρ*(J_n), ρ_(n−1) + B + b_(n−1)), ρ* the bottom's rank and 0 while no member holds the image, with the bank b_n = min(W, B + b_(n−1) − (ρ_n − ρ_(n−1))), whatever its route preferences. So the four such readers share one rank trace and one bank trace, and at every step their committed intervals are equal or incomparable.

Proof. Every legal move holds J_n. Above the bottom a move is legal, by the dominance proof, and affordable, a tree move costing 1 and a chain move truncating. So the loop stops exactly when the rank reaches ρ*(J_n) or the units run out, and the rank gained does not depend on the route. Two members of one rank holding J_n are equal or incomparable by the layers.

The recursion, run with no reader, reproduces the rank and bank traces at all 16 settings (B = 1, 2, 3, 4; W = 0, 2, 4, 8) on all nine rows, and the four readers' intervals differ at 11,904 pair-steps, every one incomparable.

On the eights row, patience with no bank lags for the whole run

Tier: rule (verified on (identity, eights) at B = 1, 2, 4, p ≤ 3, with no bank, W = 0, chains refused, at every counted step to n = 119; the loss at B = 1 also at chain patience 0 to 3).
Verifier: rationed.py::section_start_delay.

On (identity, eights) no member holds the first image (0, 1), whose closure meets 0, so the greedy reader first moves at step 1 and a reader of tree patience p, chains refused, at step p + 1. The demand there is about eight ranks a step, so both spend their whole income every step and the gap is B p ranks at every counted step, from step 8, where the run reads it. With no bank it does not close over the run: the catch-up that makes patience cheap without a limit was the unlimited loop. A bank lets the patient reader spend income it saved while idle, so with a bank the gap can shrink, and this rule does not read it. With tiles alone the holding members are a chain, so the patient tile reader's tile contains the greedy tile reader's. With chains allowed at chain patience 0 to 3, the loss at B = 1 still rises strictly with tree patience 0 to 3, 58.6 in ln-loss per unit of tree patience, at every fixed pair of preferences.

Destination universality

Tier: rule (verified at 16 settings on nine rows, two orders).
Verifier: rationed.py::section_universality, rationed.py::section_inversion.

A ninth row is read beside the slate, y ↦ 2y on the stream whose digits are the Fibonacci word over {1, 2}, bounded and never periodic. The policy space is the 100 policies crossed with the drawdowns 0, 1, 2 and 4 below W, and ∞, which stands for every δ ≥ W since δ ≥ W draws what ∞ draws: a sample of the drawdown axis, δ = 3, 5, 6 and 7 not run. Two losses are read: the deficit loss, and the lag loss, which sums the image lag n + 1 − τ(n), τ(n) counting the steps m ≤ n with |J_m| ≥ |C_n|, C_n the interval committed at step n: the steps the committed interval sits behind the stream. The sum of scale shortfalls would be no new loss, its image term being the same for every policy; the image lag quantizes the shortfall by the stream's own rate. The composite order compares lag losses and breaks ties by the deficit loss.

At every setting, under the deficit loss and under the composite order, the nine rows' argmin sets over that space have a common member, and a greedy spend-all policy is always among the common members, while no policy is common at all sixteen settings under either order. The per-row argmin sets differ at all 32 setting-orders, so this is a policy that is best on nine different rows, and a zero transfer gap from training on some rows and testing on others follows from it and adds nothing. The greedy spend-all policy chain first at tiles and tree first at straddles is common at 15 settings. At (B, W) = (4, 2) the common set is instead tree first at tiles, either preference at straddles, greedy, with δ = 1 or ∞, the same under both orders. There two greedy spend-all readers, U chain first at tiles and tree first at straddles and V tree first at tiles and chain first at straddles, tie on eight rows and part on (identity, eights), U at −14909.08 against V at −14920.56 in ln-loss, at 56 counted steps, every one a choice inside one layer. The first is step 8 at rank 34, where the straddle of index 3 has length 7/222732060 and the tile 1/39055634. The rank trace is fixed by the images and (B, W) alone, so a route's advantage is a comparison of two members at a rank the resources chose.

Read as forgetting (FORGET: Two witnesses buy the robust grade), a reader that takes one common member on every row is a state constant across the rows, a factoring, so it holds nothing of its row under any prior. That is a fact about the selection: the argmin sets themselves differ at every setting, so a reader ranking each row's set on its own may read the row. A state picked among co-optima is graded by the rule that picks it.

A density forecast is a length loss

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof.

Let the reader forecast the image point, f of the stream's point, at step n by the uniform density on its committed interval C_n, and let a score, read as a loss so that more is worse, read that forecast only through its density at the outcome, a lower density scoring worse and an unbounded interval worst. Then the score is a function of |C_n| that rises with it, whatever the stream, so the greedy reader is optimal under it on every stream. The log score of the next image is the instance: its sum is the logarithm of the deficit loss less a sum no policy moves.

Proof. The image point, f of the stream's point, lies in J_(n+1), inside J_n, which the sound reader's C_n holds (the root when no member does), so its density is 1/|C_n|; a loss rising with every committed length is monotone in the committed intervals, and greedy patience minimizes every such loss. The log score of J_(n+1) is ln|C_n| − ln|J_(n+1)|.

A forecast read from the image J_n instead is the same for every reader. So a score under which the stream chooses the reader must read the committed interval for something its length does not say, such as its position; a discrete decision, the side of a cut, is the instance read below.

The side loss makes the best reader depend on the stream

Tier: rule (verified on the nine rows over 120 steps, steps 8 to 119 counted).
Verifier: forecast.py::section_side, forecast.py::section_one_bit.

The cut at step n is the mediant of J_n's endpoints, interior to J_n, so which side of it the image point, f of the stream's point, falls on is decided only by later images, which read it off. The reader's forecast is the wider side of C_n at the cut, an unbounded side being wider and a tie forecasting left, and the side loss counts the counted steps it misses. Under the identity the cut divides the next digit 1 from the rest, so the forecast is of whether the stream's own next digit is 1.

The ninth row splits no class: the 100 policies still fall into 38 behaviour classes over all nine rows' counted steps. Over those classes the nine rows' argmin sets have no common member, under the side loss and with the deficit loss breaking its ties. The greedy class is out of the argmin on six rows. On the eight rows whose stream's point is a quadratic irrational some class never misses, a different set of classes on each, and the nine argmin sets are pairwise distinct, so the set of best readers is a state that reads its row (FORGET: The four grades). Scoring a single step is enough: at each of the 112 counted steps, the side forecast there alone leaves no common member, with or without the deficit loss beneath it.

The stream enters at a reversal

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; forecast.py::section_reversal.

With no resource limit, if a class scores below the greedy class on some row under any loss, the loss orders two traces against containment: the lower one's committed intervals contain the higher one's at every step whose image is a bounded interval of positive length held by the root. Call such a pair a reversal. Under the side loss, at every step where a class hits and the greedy class misses, the class commits a member strictly containing the greedy one's. So the stream enters the unlimited reader's optimum only at a reversal and never inside a layer.

Proof. Every sound reader's committed interval contains the bottom of J_n, which the greedy reader commits. A different forecast needs a different member, and a member containing the bottom other than the bottom contains it strictly. The bottom is alone in its layer, so no such pair lies inside one.

On the nine rows a class hits where the greedy class misses at 8,018 (row, step, class) sites, every one strictly containing, and the greedy class hits where some class misses at 6,792: the side loss is monotone in neither direction.

The two poles

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof.

On rows whose counted images are bounded intervals of positive length held by the root, a loss that never rises as the committed intervals shrink has the greedy class in every row's argmin, and one that never falls has the refuser in every row's argmin, the class of both patiences infinite, which commits the root at every step. Sort the distinct deficit-loss values over every class and row into positions, and let a band loss be 0 when a class's deficit loss on a row sits in an interval of positions and 1 otherwise, the same interval on every row. A band is clean when every row has a class in it and a class out of it. Then a clean band's nine argmin sets share a class exactly when the band contains some class's position hull, the positions from its least to its greatest over the rows.

Proof. The first pole is dominance. For the second, the root contains every member. For a clean band each row's argmin is its in-band set, so a common member is a class in the band on all nine rows, which for an interval is a class whose hull the band contains.

Breaking needs no stream and keeping needs no pole

Tier: rule (verified over all bands of the 117 positions on the nine rows).
Verifier: forecast.py::section_bands.

A band loss reads nothing but the committed lengths, and 146 of the 403 clean bands leave no common member, the first the positions 1 to 85: a reversal breaks the common optimum with no stream in it. The greedy class's hull is 0 to 85, so that band misses it at position 0 alone. The other way, take two positions a < b − 1 consecutive in one class's sorted positions and score 0 on the union of the bands from its least position to a and from b to its greatest. Of these (class, gap) choices, 213 give a clean union band, each keeping its own class common by construction, and in 201 neither pole is common: the first keeps, on every row, the three classes containing the policies that take the tree move first at a tile and the chain move first at a straddle, with tree patience 0 and chain patience 1, 2 or 3. A loss need not be monotone to keep a reader common to every row.

The collision grower

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_controls.

Let the substrate grow instead of the committed member. A labelled set is a finite set of integers with a label on each member; a substrate is a set P of primes from a pool, the first K primes, reading x as its residues mod each p in P. Two inputs with different labels must be separated, and P resolves them when some p in P tells them apart; an unresolved pair is a collision. P resolves (x, y) iff some p in P does not divide x − y, iff, the primes being distinct, their product does not. So a labelled set asks nothing but its difference set D, the |x − y| over pairs to be separated, a prime resolving d iff it does not divide d, and growth is a hitting set over D. The least-new grower adds, while a collision remains, the smallest pool prime not yet taken that resolves one, and stops when none remains or no prime resolves one.

The rung reading

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_reading, collision.py::section_least_non_divisor.

Write p_t# for the product of the first t pool primes, p_0# = 1, and D_t for the multiples of p_t# in D: the differences rung t of the tower leaves unresolved. The grower passes the pool once, upward, takes p_(t+1) exactly when D_(t+1) ≠ D_t, and stops at the first t with D_t empty or at the pool's end; once it has passed p_t, its collisions are exactly D_t. So the grown substrate is the set of least non-divisors ℓ(d), the least prime not dividing d, over the members of D, a member the whole pool divides being left a collision. Each difference names one prime, and the grower takes the primes named.

Proof. The collisions U are the members of D divisible by the product of P. A prime not taken is legal iff it fails to divide some member of U. When a legal prime is taken, every smaller prime not taken divides every member of U; U only shrinks, so such a prime stays illegal and the grower never turns back. After p_t is passed, every member of U is divisible by every prime passed, taken or not, so U lies in D_t, and D_t lies in U because the product of P divides p_t#. So p_(t+1) is legal iff some d in D_t escapes it, which is ℓ(d) = p_(t+1).

Both forms equal the grower on 20,000 random difference sets, 5,144 of them skipping a pool prime below their last take.

How fine, and what

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_depth, collision.py::section_skips, collision.py::section_witness.

The labelled set fixes how fine the substrate is by its depth, the index in the pool of its largest least non-divisor, and which primes it takes only by its skips: a pool prime below that one which no difference names. The primorial p_t# names p_(t+1), so a labelled set whose differences include p_t# for every t below the depth skips nothing and grows the first depth-many primes.

Proof. The rung reading.

The labelled set 0 … M − 1 with every label distinct has every difference below M, so it grows the first t primes for the least t with p_t# ≥ M: 3, 4, 5, 6 primes at M = 30, 210, 2310, 30030. On the multiples of 3, of 5 or of 7 below 630 every difference is a multiple of that prime, so none names it, and the substrate is [2, 5, 7, 11], [2, 3, 7, 11], [2, 3, 5, 11] in turn. Labelling 0 … 209 at random from eight classes, 24 labelled sets out of 24 have 1, 2, 6 and 30 among their differences, and every difference lies below 210, so all grow the first four primes: these labelled sets take no skip, and their depth is set by the interval's length.

The encoding bound

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_encoding, collision.py::section_compression, collision.py::section_binding.

If a squarefree m whose prime factors all lie in the pool divides no member of D, the grower stops by the time it passes m's largest prime factor. Under place value in base Q, a form with a one-symbol suffix is its stem's value times Q plus the suffix. Two forms with different suffixes differ by a number congruent mod Q to the suffix difference, which is nonzero and below Q in size. So when the suffix is the label, Q divides no difference, and for squarefree Q the substrate lies among the primes up to Q's largest factor.

Proof. Let p_t be m's largest prime factor; then p_t# is a multiple of m, so D_t is empty.

The bound is not containment in Q's factors: the pool below them is walked first. On the pool 2 … 11 the grower on a suffix-labelled set grows [2, 3, 5] at Q = 30, [2, 3, 5, 7] at Q = 35, and the whole pool at the prime Q = 29. What the grown set tracks is the encoding. Take the words of morphology: a paradigm is a table of forms, one per lexeme (a word's stem, here an integer) and cell (a slot such as a tense, the label being the cell), each entry a form. A paradigm whose forms are a lexeme plus a fixed offset per cell compresses the difference set, since one lexeme's forms differ only by offset differences, but it grows the same whole pool as a control whose forms are drawn at random (431 differences against 644): here compression moves no rung. Nor, over same-lexeme pairs, does joining lexeme and cell by addition, the form L + c, rather than by binding (BINDING), multiplication by a unit. With lexemes L units of a squarefree N that every pool prime divides, a bound form L · c mod N differs from its same-lexeme sibling by a number congruent to L times the cell difference, which has the cell difference's least non-divisor, so over same-lexeme pairs the sum and the binding grow one substrate; pairs across lexemes are not covered. When the cells are units of an even N their differences are even and 2 is never named: at N = 510510 twelve unit lexemes and five unit cells give 10 additive and 119 multiplicative same-lexeme differences, and both grow [5, 7]; 3 is never named because the five sampled cells all happen to be 2 mod 3, a feature of the sample.

The substrate parts by depth or inside a layer

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_layer, collision.py::section_sole_resolver.

If P lies inside P′, P′ resolves every pair P does, so every count of collisions is antitone in inclusion and no loss counting collisions orders two nested substrates against it, though a loss that also charges a substrate's size can. The grower takes one prime a step, so two labelled sets' growers first part either where one stops and the other takes a prime, nested substrates the loss orders with inclusion, or where both hold one set and take different primes, two incomparable members of one layer of the order by inclusion. So the data enters the substrate by its depth or inside a layer, and in a layer it chooses the member through a skip, where the reader's layer was the resources' choice and its member the policy's.

Proof. The first sentence is the definition of resolving; the second is the rung reading, the grower taking one prime at a time until it stops.

When p is the one pool prime not dividing a difference, its sole resolver, the difference fails every substrate lacking p, whatever else that substrate holds. On the pool 2 … 11, 770 = 2 · 5 · 7 · 11 grows [3], while the multiples of 3 below 300 grow [2, 5, 7, 11] and leave 770 a collision. The growers on the multiples of 3, 5 and 7 below 630 part at [2] and at [2, 3].

The grower's worst case

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; collision.py::section_worst, collision.py::section_reading.

When every member of D has a non-divisor in the pool, so that a resolving substrate exists, the grower's can exceed the least one by the factor |D|, and no more. The greedy that ranks primes by how many differences they resolve stays within the harmonic number H(|D|) of the least cover (Chvátal, "A greedy heuristic for the set-covering problem", Math. Oper. Res. 4 (1979) 233–235, which reduces at unit cost to Johnson's and Lovász's bound). The least-new grower ranks by size.

Proof. Each difference names one prime, so the grower takes at most |D|. On D = {p_0#, …, p_(T−1)#} it takes the first T primes, while the pool's T-th prime, the grower's own last pick, already divides none of them and resolves all alone, so the coverage greedy, which takes a prime resolving all of them first, stops at one. On {1, 2, 6, 30, 210} with the pool 2 … 13 the grower takes [2, 3, 5, 7, 11] and the coverage greedy [11].

Exact readout reads the subdominant

Tier: criterion.
Verifier: proof; readout.py::section_criterion, readout.py::section_subdominant, readout.py::section_line, readout.py::section_controls.

Let s be a symmetric grading of a finite world X into any totally ordered set, and give X a label. A reader handed a query q and a store of other members reads q's label off a most similar member; at a threshold t the store is admissible when that member has s ≥ t, and the readout is exact when every most similar member carries q's label. A label is readable at t when the readout is exact at every query and admissible store, and that holds iff the label is constant on the components of the graph G_t joining x and y when s(x, y) ≥ t. The components nest as t rises, so u(x, y), the largest t joining x and y, is the least grading above s with u(x, z) ≥ min(u(x, y), u(y, z)): the subdominant ultrametric of cluster analysis in its dual form, the one single linkage builds. Chierchia and Perret, "Ultrametric fitting by gradient descent", NeurIPS 2019, state it as the largest ultrametric below a dissimilarity and cite Gower and Ross, Appl. Stat. 18 (1969) 54–64, for its minimum-spanning-tree form. A label is readable at t iff it is constant on the balls of u at t, however the store breaks ties between equally similar members.

Proof. If the label is constant, a most similar member of an admissible store is joined to q at s ≥ t and shares its component. If not, it differs across an edge x ~ y, and the query x with the store {y} reads wrong. The components of G_t are the balls of u at t. A single edge is a path, so u ≥ s, and joining a path from x to y to one from y to z gives u(x, z) ≥ min(u(x, y), u(y, z)). A grading above s with the strong triangle is, along a path realizing u, at least its least edge, so it is at least u.

Nothing in the proof reads what the world is, only the order of s. On the line, s(x, y) = −|x − y|, the points 0, 1, 2 at t = −1 are an open triangle, 0 and 2 joined through 1 but not adjacent, and a label splitting 0 from 2 is unreadable: the query 1 ties them. So the line's distance, the size the tower deletes, reads no finer than its subdominant either, and a reading finer than a tree is bought by a different readout, never by a different world. The count of the primes 2, 3, 5 at which two residues agree opens the same triangle on 0, 6, 16 in Z/30. Brute readout over every query, store and label map in up to three labels matches the criterion at 88,938 cases on random, tower and line worlds.

The grown depth is the readable depth

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; readout.py::section_weld, readout.py::section_controls.

Write the agreement α(x, y) of two integers for the largest t with p_t# dividing x − y. Its balls are the residue classes mod p_t#, and congruences compose, so α keeps the strong triangle and is its own subdominant. When α(x, y) < K, the least non-divisor of x − y is p_(α(x, y) + 1). So on a labelled set whose separated pairs all agree below K, no separated difference divisible by p_K#, the least-new grower takes exactly the primes p_(α + 1) over the pairs to be separated: the levels at which a residue class carrying two labels splits them. Its depth, 1 + the largest such agreement, is the least threshold at which the label is readable by agreement: at the grown depth t, every store containing a member of q's class mod p_t# reads q's label exactly off its most-agreeing member.

Proof. The least non-divisor is the first prime past the leading run of divisors, which is the agreement's successor; the rest is the rung reading with the criterion above, the components of α at t being the classes mod p_t#.

What agreement cannot carry is the open triangle: the strong triangle forbids it for every assignment of integers, and under α none of the 27,000 triples of Z/30 realizes one, whereas the count of agreeing primes in the section above opens one on 0, 6, 16. On 600 labelled sets of seven integers below 2310, the grown depth equals the least readable threshold, at every depth from 0 to 5, and the grown set equals the successors of the separated pairs' agreements.
