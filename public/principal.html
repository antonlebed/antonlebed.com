Principal places

Which primes carry a principal place over a quadratic field, and how soon the first one arrives. Over an imaginary field a floor set by the units caps the reading at a quarter of the discriminant; over a real field there is no floor at all. Below that, the principal share runs short at small primes at both signs, and the whole of the shortfall is one term of the explicit formula — the prime powers a count of primes leaves out — except one narrow class over a real field, which is a race of odd characters read before its bias has developed. What is left after the term is the spacing: the principal places fall more evenly than chance, and that alone puts the first one early.

  • The split triple — what a totally split prime's three places can be in a cubic field, and the two arithmetics one class number turns out to hold
  • The degenerate regime — the subgroup condition behind a cubic field's degenerate regime, the conductor that sorts a field into it, and the shortfall one term of the explicit formula puts back
  • The generator ceiling — the principal shortfall graded by a class's order at degree 2 and degree 3, the ceiling at the generating class, its decay and its fracture, and the prime-power term that carries it
  • Observatory — the ramification of a local field read off the orbit of one unit: an exact digit channel, measured or designed

Three of the pages under this one carry the question above to degree 3 and class by class; the fourth reads a place from inside — the ramification of a local field off the orbit of one unit.

The question comes from growth. A greedy walk that extends a ring by the least admissible amount closes a characteristic — a rational prime p, the walk at it running over the places of the ring above p — when its ladder of moves runs out, and closing one characteristic closes the ring (The cascade boundary). A characteristic carries such a ladder only when it is rank-1: some place above it is unramified with residue field the prime field, so that the completion there is Qp itself. And the walk closes it cheaply only when that place is principal — the ideal it names is generated by one element — because the price of making a place's power principal runs over the powers of its ideal class, the ideal taken modulo the principal ones. Those classes form a finite group, the class group Cl(K), of order the class number h. So the arithmetic under the boundary question is: which of a field's rank-1 characteristics are principal, and how soon is the first.

Over a quadratic field of discriminant D — fundamental throughout, the discriminant of the field and not of an order — an odd prime is rank-1 exactly when D is a nonzero square modulo it; such a prime splits, into two places carrying inverse classes, so it is counted in one of them by a choice no statistic here can see, a class and its inverse sharing their order and their squareness. A prime whose ideal stays prime is inert; one dividing D is ramified. Write L1(K) for the least principal rank-1 characteristic over the ODD primes, as the walk runs them, 2 being settled by the budget inequality. The narrow class number h+ counts form classes up to proper equivalence: it is h over an imaginary field and 2h over a real field whose fundamental unit has norm +1, and most of the statistics below are graded by it. Three instruments recur. The share is the fraction of split primes below a cut that are principal, quoted over its nominal 1/h or 1/h+. The first-hit model draws each split prime independently at that density and integrates to a predicted L1. The index of dispersion of a field's count of principal split primes is its variance over its mean, 1 under independence and below 1 when the count sits closer to its mean than independent draws would.

The floor

The floor over an imaginary field theorem

Over an imaginary quadratic field, L1 ≥ |D|/4, and the bound is attained exactly when (|D|+1)/4 is an odd prime. A rank-1 place is principal exactly when its prime is represented by the principal form, which after completing the square reads 4p = u² + |D|y². Since y = 0 would make p a square, y ≠ 0 and 4p ≥ |D|, with 4p ≥ |D| + 1 at odd D by parity. At odd D the principal form takes the value (|D|+1)/4 at (0, 1), which is coprime to D, so a prime of that value is unramified and principal by that very representation; at even D the floor value |D|/4 divides D and ramifies, so no even discriminant attains it. D = −19995 sits on the floor at L1 = 4999. The floor knows nothing about the class group.

Scope. Elementary, every imaginary quadratic field, no computation in it; controlled at zero violations over the 6079 fundamental discriminants down to −20,000.

How far the per-place reading reaches rule

The floor makes the reading's coverage a finite set and names it: at a sweep bound P no field with |D| > 4P is closed by this route at all. At P = 1000 that is 856 of the 1217 fundamental discriminants down to −4000 and none of the 4862 beyond. The rival route — closing through a factor of at most 4 at the place rather than through principality — reaches at most 36 of that same 1217, its test being necessary and not sufficient, so the per-place reading is the wider by at least 23 times, and both stop. Inside |D| ≤ 1000 the reading looks near-total, 296 of 305 fields, and that is the range's number, the whole range sitting under the cap: the fraction of fields with L1 > 1000 runs 3.0, 35.8, 65.0, 100 and 100 per cent over the |D| bands cut at 1000, 2500, 5000, 10,000 and 20,000, a crossover near 4000 that the floor forces where the first-hit model at density 1/(2h) puts it near 10⁴. That model undershoots the measured mean L1 at every one of the 34 class numbers in range, by 2.9 to 8.6 times, and the density is not what fails far out — measured, it sits between 0.887 and 0.980 of 1/(2h) over 10⁵ primes. The start is part of it, there being nothing to hit below the floor; the rest survives where no floor exists and is the subject of the next section.

Scope. Rule, proved from the floor and verified to |D| ≤ 20,000. The multiple by which L1 exceeds |D|/4 is a function of neither variable alone — 4.3 to 1.0 across |D| at a fixed class number, 1.0 to 5.0 down the class number at a fixed |D| — and stays modest, row means 1.3 to 3.7, which is what makes the cap the right order of magnitude.

The floor is the unit rank property

A principal degree-1 place is generated by an element of norm p, its generators form one orbit under the units, and an imaginary quadratic field's unit group is finite: the norm form is definite, and its values off the rational line are bounded below by |D|/4. At positive unit rank — the number of independent units of infinite order — the generator moves through an INDEFINITE form, which has no such floor. By Dirichlet's unit theorem a number field has unit rank 0 exactly when it is Q or imaginary quadratic, which is exactly when the norm form is definite, so the rank and the signature are one condition and no field tells them apart.

Scope. Property, from the construction; the floor's restriction to imaginary quadratic fields is load-bearing and transfers to no field of positive unit rank.

Over a real field the floor is absent, not weakened rule

At D > 0 the completion of the square gives u² − Dy² = ±4p, which constrains no size, and the test is the classical one: p is principal exactly when the reduced form (±p, b, (b² − D)/(±4p)) lies in the principal CYCLE, both signs needed because ideal classes match indefinite form classes narrowly and the ordinary class group is the quotient the sign disjunction takes. D = 19996 carries L1 = 3 where the floor would demand 4999, and over the 6081 real fundamental discriminants to 20,000 coverage at the same sweep bound runs 100.0, 100.0, 99.9, 99.9 and 99.8 per cent over the five bands — flat where the imaginary side falls from 97 per cent to nothing. What capped the per-place reading was the unit rank.

Scope. Rule; one witness refutes a universal. It does not follow that the reading never caps: eight real fields in range are uncovered at this bound (3601, 7057, 9217, 11665, 12469, 13321, 14401, 19041), a measured residue. The closure step itself is certified over every characteristic-zero ring holding a rank-1 characteristic below the bound, not over imaginary quadratic fields alone.

The nominal is derived, so none of the deficit is the model's rule

A split prime's Frobenius — the element of a Galois group the prime determines up to conjugacy — lies in Gal(H/K) for H the Hilbert class field, the maximal unramified abelian extension of K, whose Galois group over K is the class group itself. Chebotarev's density theorem distributes that Frobenius uniformly over the group, and conjugation c ↦ −c is the pairing of the two places above the prime, so the density of principal split primes is 1/h, and 1/h+ by the same argument in the narrow Hilbert class field.

Scope. Proved. A cubic field's partially split primes, whose Frobenius is a transposition, need a different argument (The split triple).

The whole of the shortfall is the explicit formula's prime-power term rule

Chebotarev's explicit formula counts prime POWERS, weighted 1/k at the k-th power; a count of primes omits them and so runs short of the formula's main term in exactly the classes the powers land in — the prime-power term: one half for each prime below √x whose Frobenius SQUARES into the class (a split prime of class b onto 2b, an inert prime onto the identity), a third per cube, and the ramified ideals at one half each on the classes of order at most 2. Over one field the oscillatory remainder is as large as that shift; over a population the oscillations average out and the shift survives. Write the level of a class for its observed count of places over the nominal count. Put the weights back per field and per prime window and the imaginary table flattens whole: the generating classes from 1.0875 to 1.0011 ± 0.0033 below 1000 and from 1.0240 to 1.0000 ± 0.0011 below 10⁴, the trivial class from 0.52–0.79 to 0.96–1.02 below 1000 over h = 6 to 14, every cell within 0.03 of 1 at 10⁴. The real narrow side carries the term to three quarters or more — 1.1041 to 1.0190 ± 0.0025 below 1000, 1.0246 to 1.0008 at 10⁴ — and what it leaves is one class, the next block's.

So every grading of the last block is a face of one class function, the weight of squares, cubes and ramified ideals each class receives. The grading by the narrow class number is the inert primes' squares, all on the identity, h/2 of them per class-count: the trivial class is short by (h + |Cl[2]|)/2 · π(√x)/π(x), h the order of whichever group the places are sorted into and |Cl[2]| its number of classes of order at most 2. The grading by the number of genera is the split squares, which land on the doubled classes and on the identity from exactly the classes of order 2, plus the ramified ideals. The decay along the cut is the term's own, π(√x)/π(x) at leading order — a factor of 8 from cut 250 to 10⁴, where a constant and a 1/log law were both excluded. And the ceiling at the classes that generate the group is the classes that receive nothing: in a cyclic group of order 2bn the non-squares are the classes whose order carries the full 2-part, at h = 4, 8, 16 the generators alone, so the order ladder and the square ladder coincide there and part wherever h has an odd factor — at h = 6, 10, 12, 20 the odd-order classes are squares and the order ladder inverts exactly there.

Scope. Rule in range: the term is classical and the accounting is verified on the fundamental discriminants of both signs to 4000 (narrow classes on the real side, to 16,000 for the real ceiling) over the odd primes to 10⁴, with the arithmetic progressions to 10⁷ over 77 prime moduli as the control — the non-residue classes lead by 1.15 · π(√x)/π(x) at eight spreads and by 0.9 of one spread once the same weights go back. What is NOT this term is a cubic field's partially split places: a transposition is never a square in S3, and that population's ladder is a second object (The generator ceiling).

The bias behind the term is a published theorem. Aoki and Koyama (Chebyshev's Bias against Splitting and Principal Primes in Global Fields, J. Number Theory 245, 2023) read a principal split prime as one splitting completely in the Hilbert class field, so the deficit at small primes is a prime race against the identity class; under the Deep Riemann Hypothesis and the non-vanishing at ½ of the class-group L-functions they prove a bias against principal primes, weighted by 1/√N(p), of size ((|Cl/Cl²| − 1)/2) · log log x — the split-square term in log-density form, vanishing at odd h exactly as the split squares on Cl² do. What that coefficient does not carry is what an unweighted count at a finite cut shows first: the inert primes' squares, h/2 of them per class-count, linear in h at x ≪ |D|.

The real residual is one narrow class, an odd-character race before its bias has developed observation

Read as counts of primes per field, the corrected real side's order-2 shortfall is entirely the narrow class k0 of a principal ideal with a generator of NEGATIVE norm — present exactly when the fundamental unit has norm +1, of order 2 — which sits 2.10 ± 0.14 primes per field below its nontrivial siblings below 1000, pooled over h+ = 4 to 10 with every stratum between 1.6 and 2.5, and 1.5 ± 0.3 below at 10⁴, while the other order-2 classes read flat at +0.18 ± 0.16. At h+ = 2 the seat is an identity: D = D1D2 with both prime discriminants negative, and a split prime's place lies in k0 exactly when the Kronecker symbol (D1/p) is −1, at zero mismatches over 1133 fields. So the residual there is the race of two ODD quadratic characters — ones taking −1 at −1 — read at primes comparable to the discriminant, before the bias the prime squares drive has developed: over the fundamental d < 0 to 16,000 the square-corrected race at x = 1000 reads +2.8, +3.9, +8.4 and +6.6 primes by |d| band against the +5.5 the square term carries, full only where |d| ≪ x (−12.9 against −12.5 at |d| < 100 and x = 10⁴), and the class's shrinking by 10⁴, from −1.07 ± 0.07 to −0.84 ± 0.18 primes per field, is the bias catching up. Above h+ = 2 the characters −1 on k0 are odd in the same archimedean sense — complex conjugation is k0's image in the Galois group of the narrow class field over Q — and their trivial zeros alone put k0 one prime below its siblings, measured two.

Scope. Observation on the real sweep to 16,000, read as counts per field; the h+ = 2 identity is a property, exact over its 1133 fields. The classical instance controls the instrument: across 77 prime moduli the non-residue classes lead by 1.15 · π(√x)/π(x) at 8σ and by 0.9σ once the weights are put back.

Not the x-independent term; the imaginary cut ladder is the hard zero's repayment observation

Beyond the main term, the zeros and the prime powers, the explicit formula carries a constant of size ½ log|D| per nontrivial character — a fixed number of primes per class, a level falling as 1/π(x), the shape every residual here wears. And at D < 0 there is the hard zero: the principal form's minimum is 1, which is not prime, so no prime below |D|/4 is principal at all. As counts it fails: the real narrow cells are flat from cut 400 to 1000 (order 2 at −1.12, −1.19, −1.31 primes per field) and then SHRINK by 10⁴, to 0.67 ± 0.19 and 0.18 ± 0.53 of the cut-1000 value, where a constant would hold and a prime-power leftover would read 2.27, excluded at 4σ to 8σ per cell. The imaginary trivial class jumps from −0.83 ± 0.09 primes per field at cut 630 to −0.05 ± 0.09 at cut 1000: a field's trivial class is empty below |D|/4 while the main term is unchanged, so the deficit is repaid above |D|/4, and the fields with |D| in (2520, 4000] have their floor inside that band.

Scope. Observation; the imaginary jump is a reading of a pooled figure, and the reason no imaginary cell is read as a count below its discriminants.

The hard zero sets where the count starts, and a class of small order starts later observation

The hard zero has no indefinite twin, and it grades nothing where it holds. The least split prime a class holds, q(C), is the dial the minimum failed to be: the median q of the classes of order at most 2 exceeds their field's median over its other classes at 0.873 of real fields and 0.982 of imaginary ones, against a size-matched placebo at 0.492 and 0.465, and at MATCHED q the order-2 gap survives at 0.80 of its size real and 0.59 imaginary over coarse bands — the later start about a quarter of the real deficit and three fifths of the imaginary, the asymmetry the hard zero would give.

In the character basis the term sits on the quadratic characters, and no component beyond it is established observation

The split squares' weight is uniform on the doubled classes, and a subgroup's indicator transforms onto the characters trivial on it, which for the doubled classes are exactly the characters with χ² = 1 — the quadratic characters, which ARE the genus characters. The inert term sits on the identity and is flat on every character; the ramified term transforms onto the characters trivial on the classes of order at most 2. A spectrum concentrated at the quadratic characters and starved at the maximal-order ones is therefore the term's own transform, and that is what is measured: the quadratic share of the per-class deviation's energy beats its rotation band at all eight cyclic strata and the maximal-order share sits below it at all eight, the same on non-cyclic groups. Three readings looked for a component the term does not give and none stands: a per-field amplitude removed, the quadratic excess survives at three strata of eight; the reachability criterion for the 2-torsion term does not grade the residual; and a held-out amplitude decays straight through its gate to 0.95 of its null, the signature of a fit's own distortion thinning.

Scope. The support is a property; the readings are observations over 981 real and 1208 imaginary fields. The allowance for a quadratic share after symmetrizing is 1.5 to 1.8 times flat, most of the raw multiple.

The deficit's shape is scale-free; its level shallows with |D| and its reach grows, and nothing derives either observation

Where the deficit ENDS in p never moves with |D|: the prime at which a fixed-h+ row reaches 0.95 of nominal, regressed on log|D| across a 512-fold lever from 2·10³ to 10⁶, has slope −0.245 to +0.003 across h+ = 2 to 8, every value at or below zero. But the LEVEL at p ≤ 100 is graded by |D|, in the direction that shallows it: 0.880 to 0.940 at h+ = 2, 0.659 to 0.826 at 4, 0.342 to 0.627 at 8 across the lever, not levelled off by 10⁶. What is left is the spacing

Fed back as the model's own density, the share overshoots the undershoot, and the residual is flat in h observation

The surviving residual is the spacing, in sign and in size pattern

The first hit is the count's survival function — E[I] = Σn P(N(n) = 0) exactly, I the index of the first principal prime among the field's split primes — so no phase and nothing at the cap enters. The count is under-dispersed and its index has a scale: 1 at the first split prime whatever the joint law, then 0.943, 0.717, 0.612, 0.523 at n = 1 to 4 at h = 2, falling to 0.142 at the cap. What a scan pays observation

A consumer's unit is trials, and its statistic is the least principal split prime read NARROWLY — not L1, which tests wide principality and so is smaller (real |D| ≤ 4000: narrow median 17 against wide median 11). The scan's range splits the signs: the real median runs 19, 23, 23, 29, 29 across the 512-fold lever, so a real quadratic scan finishes at a prime of order 20 to 30 whatever the field and sits permanently inside the transient, while the imaginary median runs 929, 3541, 14,323, 48,311, tracking |D|/4 — at |D| ≥ 5·10⁵ no sampled field carries a principal split prime below 10⁵. In trials against the uniform model's exact mean of h+, the narrow overshoot is flat across the lever exactly where it is small — 1.07 to 1.16 at h+ = 2, 1.17 to 1.30 at 4 — and 1.89 falling to 1.31 at 8. A scan wanting a GENERATOR, the wide notion against 1/h, keeps a correction of about 1.13 to 1.32 at every h to |D| ~ 10⁶, reached by decay, three of the four strata clearing 1 by more than three standard errors.

Scope. Observation. One cell runs the other way at its own weight, h = 3 at 0.79 ± 0.08 in the top band on 51 fields, one stratum of twenty-five. At D < 0 a scan starts at the floor and pays about 23 per cent, the tail of a classical floor; separating the class group's deficit from it needs a cell both well clear of the floor and at large |D|, which the prime cap forbids. Three factors are quoted on this page and they are three quantities: 1.15–3.23 is the first-hit model at 1/(2h) at wide h; 1.07–1.89 is mean narrow trials against 1/h+; 0.79–3.23 is mean wide trials against 1/h, on a different population.

One degree up

The floor is a fact about a quadratic field's units, so nothing like it survives a lift to degree 3, where a totally split prime carries three places whose classes are constrained only by summing to zero: coverage is full there, the class group decides only the wait, and the even spacing lifts with it (The split triple). At class number 3 the triples a field realizes form one of exactly two subgroups, and which one is read off the field's discriminant (The degenerate regime). The ladder in the order, read as levels at both degrees, meets at one ceiling and then fractures along the square map on the Galois group (The generator ceiling).
