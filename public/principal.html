Principal places

Which primes carry a principal place over a quadratic field, and how soon the first one arrives. Over an imaginary field a floor set by the units puts the first such prime no lower than a quarter of the discriminant, capping the fields the per-place reading — closing a prime through a principal place above it — can reach at any bound; over a real field there is no floor at all. Where the floor does not bite, the principal share runs short at small primes at both signs, and the whole of the shortfall is one term of the explicit formula — the prime powers a count of primes leaves out — except one narrow class over a real field, a race of odd characters read before its bias has developed, and a residual count no mechanism derives. What is left after the term is the spacing: the principal places fall more evenly than chance, and that alone puts the first one early.

The question comes from growth. A greedy walk that extends a ring by the least admissible amount closes a characteristic — a rational prime p, the walk at it running over the places of the ring above p — when its ladder of moves runs out, and closing one characteristic closes the ring (The cascade boundary). A characteristic carries such a ladder only when it is rank-1: some place above it is unramified with residue field the prime field, so that the completion there is Qp itself. And the walk closes it cheaply only when that place is principal — the ideal it names is generated by one element — because the price of making a place's power principal runs over the powers of its ideal class, the ideal taken modulo the principal ones. Those classes form a finite group, the class group Cl(K), of order the class number h. So the arithmetic under the boundary question is: which of a field's rank-1 characteristics are principal, and how soon is the first.

Over a quadratic field of discriminant D — fundamental throughout, the discriminant of the field and not of an order — an odd prime is rank-1 exactly when D is a nonzero square modulo it; such a prime splits, into two places carrying inverse classes, so it is counted in one of them by a choice no statistic here can see, a class and its inverse sharing their order and their squareness. A prime whose ideal stays prime is inert; one dividing D is ramified. Write L1(K) for the least principal rank-1 characteristic over the ODD primes, as the walk runs them, 2 being settled by the budget inequality. The narrow class number h+ counts form classes up to proper equivalence: it is h over an imaginary field, and over a real field 2h when the fundamental unit has norm +1 and h when it has norm −1; it grades the share and the narrow scan below, where the first-hit model and the spacing are read at the wide h. Three instruments recur. The share is the fraction of split primes below a cut that are principal, quoted over its nominal 1/h or 1/h+. The first-hit model draws each split prime independently at that density and integrates to a predicted L1. The index of dispersion of a field's count of principal split primes is its variance over the variance independent draws at the same densities would have, 1 under independence and below 1 when the count sits closer to its mean than independent draws would.

The floor

The floor over an imaginary field theorem

Over an imaginary quadratic field, L1 ≥ |D|/4, sharpened at odd D to (|D|+1)/4, which is attained exactly when it is an odd prime. A rank-1 place is principal exactly when its prime is represented by the principal form, which after completing the square reads 4p = u² + |D|y². Since y = 0 would make p a square, y ≠ 0 and 4p ≥ |D|, with 4p ≥ |D| + 1 at odd D by parity. At odd D the principal form takes the value (|D|+1)/4 at (0, 1), which is coprime to D, so a prime of that value is unramified and principal by that very representation; at even D the floor value |D|/4 divides D and ramifies, so no even discriminant attains it. D = −19995 sits on the floor at L1 = 4999. The floor knows nothing about the class group.

Scope. Elementary, every imaginary quadratic field, no computation in it; controlled at zero violations over the 6079 fundamental discriminants down to −20,000.

How far the per-place reading reaches rule

The floor makes the reading's coverage a finite set and names it: at a sweep bound P no field with |D| > 4P is closed by this route at all. At P = 1000 that is 856 of the 1217 fundamental discriminants down to −4000 and none of the 4862 beyond. The rival route — closing through a class group every class of which holds an ideal of norm at most 4, rather than through principality at the place — reaches at most 36 of that same 1217, its test being necessary and not sufficient, so the per-place reading is the wider by at least 23 times, and both stop. Inside |D| ≤ 1000 the reading looks near-total, 296 of 305 fields, and that is the range's number, the whole range sitting under the cap: the fraction of fields with L1 > 1000 runs 3.0 per cent below 1000 and 100 beyond 5000, a crossover near 4000 that the floor forces where the first-hit model at density 1/(2h) puts it near 10⁴. That model undershoots the measured mean L1 at every one of the 34 class numbers that occur to |D| ≤ 1000, by 2.9 to 8.6 times, and the density is not what fails far out — measured, it sits within 12 per cent of 1/(2h) over 10⁵ primes. The start is part of it, there being nothing to hit below the floor; the rest survives where no floor exists.

Scope. Rule, proved from the floor and verified to |D| ≤ 20,000. The multiple by which L1 exceeds |D|/4 is a function of neither variable alone — 4.3 to 1.0 across |D| at a fixed class number, 1.0 to 5.0 down the class number at a fixed |D| — and stays modest, which is what makes the cap the right order of magnitude.

The floor is the unit rank property

A principal degree-1 place is generated by an element of norm p, its generators form one orbit under the units, and an imaginary quadratic field's unit group is finite: the norm form is definite, and its values off the rational line are bounded below by |D|/4. At positive unit rank — the number of independent units of infinite order — the generator moves through an INDEFINITE form, which has no such floor. By Dirichlet's unit theorem a number field has unit rank 0 exactly when it is Q or imaginary quadratic, so over a quadratic field the rank and the signature are one condition and no such field tells them apart. Definiteness is not that condition higher up: a totally imaginary field of degree 4 has a definite norm form and unit rank 1.

Scope. Property, from the construction; the floor's restriction to imaginary quadratic fields is load-bearing and transfers to no field of positive unit rank.

Over a real field the floor is absent, not weakened rule

At D > 0 the completion of the square gives u² − Dy² = ±4p, which constrains no size, and the test is the classical one: p is principal exactly when the reduced form (±p, b, (b² − D)/(±4p)) lies in the principal CYCLE, both signs needed because ideal classes match indefinite form classes narrowly and the ordinary class group is the quotient the sign disjunction takes. D = 19996 carries L1 = 3 where the floor would demand 4999, and over the 6081 real fundamental discriminants to 20,000 coverage at the same sweep bound runs 99.8 to 100.0 per cent over the five bands — flat where the imaginary side falls from 97 per cent to nothing. What capped the per-place reading was the unit rank.

Scope. Rule; one witness refutes a universal. It does not follow that the reading never caps: eight real fields in range are uncovered at this bound (3601, 7057, 9217, 11665, 12469, 13321, 14401, 19041), a measured residue. The closure step itself is certified over every characteristic-zero ring holding a rank-1 characteristic below the bound, not over imaginary quadratic fields alone.

What is left is the spacing

Fed back as the model's own density, the share overshoots the undershoot, and the residual is flat in h observation

Give the first-hit model each field's stratum's measured share per prime, with the field itself left out, and integrate it to a predicted L1: measured over predicted reads 1.000 at h = 1 and 0.714 to 0.924 over h = 2 to 8, where the constant density 1/(2h) ran 1.15 to 3.23. The model now arrives LATE. The h = 1 entry is an identity, every split prime being principal there, so the constant model's 1.15 was its form — a geometric draw over all odd primes standing in for the field's own split sequence — and never arithmetic. The residual is flat in h, slope −0.005 ± 0.014 against +0.320 ± 0.014 for the constant model, which was the whole evidence for a second mechanism graded by the class number.

Scope. Observation over the seven real strata beside the h = 1 identity. It is not the instrument: no field in these strata lacks a principal split prime below 1000, the prime cap raised to 10⁴ moves no ratio, and the pipeline regenerated under its own model 60 times reads 0.998 and 0.993 at h = 2 and 4, where the measurement gives 0.865 and 0.802.

The surviving residual is the spacing, in sign and in size pattern

The first hit is the count's survival function — E[I] = Σn Pr(none among the first n) exactly, I the index of the first principal prime among the field's split primes — so no phase and nothing at the prime cap enters. The count is under-dispersed and its index of dispersion has a scale: no regularity lives in one split prime, so the index there is 1 whatever the joint law, and the control run reads 0.943 within its noise, then 0.717, 0.612 and 0.523 over two, three and four of them at h = 2, falling to 0.142 at the prime cap. A two-moment binomial law carrying mean, variance and index to a zero-probability, exact at the independent end and at the regular end, fed that curve returns the measured mean L1 at every stratum — 1.017 ± 0.028 at h = 2, 0.957 ± 0.064 at h = 4, within one bootstrap spread of 1 at all seven — where the uncorrected ratios ran 0.714 to 0.924.

Scope. Pattern across the seven real strata, on an identity and a two-moment law. The law does not reproduce the survival pointwise: every one of 392 fields has hit by its eighth split prime, where independence keeps 2.5 per cent alive and the two-moment law 0.5 per cent, so the regularity is stronger in the tail than any binomial with that variance, the family is a floor on it and not its shape, and the shape of the count's law is open. The same derivation one degree up lands at the one cubic stratum whose early hit clears its band (The split triple).

What a scan pays observation

A consumer's unit is trials, and its statistic is the least principal split prime read NARROWLY — not L1, which tests wide principality and so is smaller (real |D| ≤ 4000: narrow median 17 against wide median 11). The scan's range splits the signs: the real median runs 19, 23, 23, 29, 29 across the 512-fold lever, so the median real quadratic scan finishes at a prime of order 20 to 30 whatever the field's size and sits permanently inside the transient, while the imaginary median runs 929, 3541, 14,323 and 48,311 over the lever's first four bands, tracking |D|/4 — at |D| ≥ 5·10⁵ no sampled field carries a principal split prime below 10⁵, which is the fifth. In trials against the h+ a uniform draw gives as its exact mean, the narrow overshoot is flat across the lever exactly where it is small — 1.07 to 1.16 at h+ = 2, 1.17 to 1.30 at 4 — and 1.89 falling to 1.31 at 8. A scan wanting a GENERATOR, the wide notion against 1/h, keeps a correction of about 1.13 to 1.32 at every h to |D| ~ 10⁶, reached by decay, three of the four strata clearing 1 by more than three standard errors.

Scope. Observation. One cell runs the other way at its own weight, h = 3 at 0.79 ± 0.08 in the top band on 51 fields, one cell of twenty-five. At D < 0 a scan starts at the floor and pays about 23 per cent, the tail of a classical floor; separating the class group's deficit from it needs a cell both well clear of the floor and at large |D|, which the prime cap forbids. Three of the factors quoted are three quantities, two of them ending at 3.23 by coincidence: 1.15–3.23 is the first-hit model at 1/(2h) at wide h; 1.07–1.89 is mean narrow trials against 1/h+; 0.79–3.23 is mean wide trials against 1/h on a different population, its 3.23 at the lever's small-field end.

One degree up

The floor is a fact about a quadratic field's units, so nothing like it survives a lift to degree 3, where a totally split prime carries three places whose classes sum to zero and are otherwise free at most fields: coverage is full there, the class group decides only the wait, and the even spacing lifts with it (The split triple). At class number 3 the triples a field realizes form one of exactly two subgroups, and which one is read off the field's discriminant (The degenerate regime). The ladder in the order, read at both degrees, meets at one ceiling and then fractures along the square map on the Galois group (The generator ceiling).

What moved

The accounting of the shortfall is now The shortfall accounted, and with it the anchors of its eight claims. What they establish: the nominal 1/h is Chebotarev's and not a guess, so the deficit is real; the whole of it is the explicit formula's prime-power term, a half per prime below √x squaring into the class, and putting that term back flattens the imaginary cells to within 0.03 of 1; what it leaves is one narrow class over a real field, an odd-character race read before its bias has developed. The formula's x-independent constant is excluded as the residual, and the imaginary cut ladder is the floor being repaid above |D|/4. Read per class the deficit is a ladder in the class order which pooling across fields destroys, and in the character basis the term's weight sits on the quadratic characters, with no component beyond them established. What nothing derives is the deficit's level and its reach: across a 512-fold lever in |D| the level shallows and the reach grows, and where the deficit ends never moves as √D would.
