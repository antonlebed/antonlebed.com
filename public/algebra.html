The division algebras

Cayley–Dickson doubling climbed over a residue window instead of over the reals: division is gone by the second step and at four of seven channels by the first, the classical walls are total from the quaternion floor up, and what survives the whole ladder is a multiplicative size the window can compute.

A rung of the tower is Z/N with N the product of the first k primes, read as one channel per prime, the residue mod p, a reduction at one finite place with the archimedean place deleted (The Object). Every channel is a field Fp. A function is channel-local when its value mod p depends only on the input mod p; on a squarefree rung those are exactly the polynomial functions, and integer size is not among them (the locality criterion). Figures below are computed on the seven-channel rung Z/510510, channels 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17.

Cayley–Dickson doubling builds an algebra of twice the dimension out of one carrying a conjugation. Floor n below means n doublings, dimension 2n, performed over one channel's field rather than over R. Over R the ladder runs R → C → H → O, all four division algebras: commutativity goes at floor 2 and associativity at floor 3, where what is left is alternative, any two elements generating an associative subalgebra, and division itself dies only at floor 4. Frobenius bounds the associative list at H, Hurwitz the normed list at O.

Over a finite field the classical walls arrive earlier and leave nothing: Wedderburn (a finite division ring is a field), Artin–Zorn (the same for the alternative case), and Chevalley–Warning, under which a form of degree below its variable count is isotropic, carrying a nonzero vector it sends to 0. An algebra whose norm form is isotropic splits: it degenerates to the non-division shape its dimension allows, a product of fields in dimension 2 and a full matrix algebra in dimension 4. A degree-2 form needs three variables before Chevalley–Warning says anything, so it reaches every floor from the quaternion one up and neither of the two below. Floor 0 is the channel's own field; floor 1, the entry floor, carries a two-variable form, the one place on the ladder where the answer can depend on the channel.

The floors

One channel-sensitive floor, then uniform collapse rule

The entry floor, one doubling adjoining a square root of −1, is the Gaussian splitting, and it is the only floor whose division verdict reads the channel. At p ≡ 1 (mod 4) the doubling splits into Fp × Fp: 5, 13, 17. At p ≡ 3 (mod 4) it is the field Fp², division intact: 3, 7, 11. At 2 it ramifies, x² + 1 = (x + 1)², and the doubling is a local ring, one maximal ideal and everything outside it a unit, carrying a nilpotent, so division is gone there too. Floor 2's verdict reads no channel: the quaternion doubling is M2(Fp) at every odd channel, by explicit isomorphism constructed per channel, while channel 2 degenerates in the doubling presentation to the commutative local ring F2[C2 × C2], division gone either way. The commutative shape is a fact about the presentation and not about the place; designed ramification separates the two. Three channels arrive at floor 2 still holding division and none carries it out, against the ladder over R, where division reaches floor 3. Across channels the floor lifts by construction, matrix rings commuting with products:

M2(Z/N)  ≅  ∏p∣NM2(Fp)\mathrm{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}/N) \;\cong\; \prod_{p \mid N} \mathrm{M}_2(\mathbf{F}_p)

Scope. Isomorphisms constructed and checked at every odd channel of Z/510510; channel 2's commutativity exhaustive over all 256 pairs. The splitting law itself is classical.

The composition norm is the channel-computable size rule

At floor 2 the norm is the determinant, a polynomial in the entries, hence channel-local by the criterion in its n-variable form: a multiplicative size living inside the channel-local class, where integer size is the information wall. It is the multiplicative half of size and no more, a field of characteristic p carrying no order. The norm's fibers are exactly flat over the units, p³ − p matrices at every nonzero value, and its zero set is the cone where the zero divisors live, p³ + p² − p elements at floor 2 and p⁷ + p⁴ − p³ at floor 3; invertibility over the whole rung is per-channel cone avoidance.

Scope. Fiber and cone counts exhaustive at every odd channel of Z/510510, the floor-3 cone by dynamic programming over the same channels.

What a split floor loses

Nilpotents return, and the meadow splits by the entry floor rule

A squarefree rung is a meadow: every element a has a pseudo-inverse a′ with a·a′·a = a and 0′ = 0, defined on the whole ring at once, which is what makes division total there (the wall-breaker). Doubling reinstates what squarefreeness banished. The floor-2 matrix ring M2(Fp) carries p² − 1 nonzero nilpotents per channel, elements with a zero power, the matrix unit E12 among them, now unavoidable over a field rather than a symptom of a repeated prime. Five candidate inverses follow them down, and each fails in a different place.

  • Powering, am+1 = a: dead for every m, since E12 has no nonzero power.
  • Any polynomial in A with scalar coefficients: dead, a commuting inner inverse at a nilpotent forcing 0 = A.
  • The adjugate map adj(A)·det(A)2p−3, adj the transposed cofactor matrix with adj(A)·A = det(A)·I: total, polynomial and exact on units, but it collapses the entire cone to 0.
  • Von Neumann regularity, some X with AXA = A: total but choiceful, no canonical X.
  • The Moore–Penrose inverse, the choice-free patch pinning a candidate G by four equations in A, G and the transpose: exists exactly where Pearl's rank criterion rank(AAT) = rank(ATA) = rank(A) holds, and is total at exactly the p ≡ 3 (mod 4) channels.

The failures are exactly the rank-1 matrices whose row or column space is isotropic, 4p(p − 1) of them at each p ≡ 1 (mod 4) channel. So the floor-1 norm x² + y² governs the floor-2 meadow: the congruence deciding whether the entry floor splits is the one deciding whether the floor above it has a canonical inverse. The coupling is 2 × 2-specific: at 3 × 3, x² + y² + z² is isotropic over every Fp by Chevalley–Warning, so a rank-1 witness on an isotropic vector breaks the criterion at every channel and totality fails everywhere.

Scope. Nilpotent counts and the adjugate and von Neumann candidates exhaustive at p ≤ 5, the scalar-polynomial one at p ≤ 3; Moore–Penrose existence exhaustive at p ≤ 5 with the failure-set equality checked per channel; the 3 × 3 failure exhaustive at p = 2, 3 and resting on Chevalley–Warning at the rest.

Napier narrows to the norm rule

Recoding a channel by a discrete logarithm turns × into +, the index transform ind (Number systems). At floor 2 there is no such coordinate: GL2(Fp) is nonabelian, and its commutator subgroup is exactly SL2 (Dieudonné), so every homomorphism to an abelian group factors through det, and ind ∘ det is the whole of Napier's move at floor 2: what is left of the logarithm is the channel-computable size. Channel 2 is exceptional in the opposite direction, GL2(F2) ≅ S3 having an abelianization det cannot see, F2* being trivial.

Scope. Commutator generation computed at every odd channel of Z/510510, the generated subgroup at full order p³ − p; the factoring is algebraic.

The criterion saturates within channels and holds across them rule

The locality criterion's proof never used commutativity, a matrix function being four functions of four scalar variables, so its identification of channel-local with compatible, a ≡ b (mod p) forcing f(a) ≡ f(b) (mod p), transfers verbatim to M2(Z/N) and the cross-channel wall keeps its teeth. Within one channel the polynomial class saturates: every function M2(Fp) → M2(Fp) is a generalized polynomial, coefficients permitted on both sides of the variable, since E1r·X·Ec1 extracts an entry into a corner where entries multiply like scalars and Lagrange does the rest; the CRT glue collapses to one line because the central idempotents are now coefficients.

Scope. Circuits built and verified pointwise at p = 2 and 3; the glue at M2(Z/6) on 150 random points.

Where the ladder ends

Composition outlives division by exactly two floors rule

Division dies at floor 2 at the p ≡ 3 (mod 4) channels, the last to hold it. Composition, N(xy) = N(x)N(y), outlives it by two: floor 3 is nonassociative but alternative and still composes, its cone exactly its zero divisors, and floor 4 loses both composition and alternativity. That is Hurwitz's dimensions 1, 2, 4, 8 verbatim; the ladder ends where it ends everywhere. Over R the two deaths coincide at floor 4, and the gap is what a channel opens. What the tower changes is not where the shadow stops but what it falls on: the surviving norm is channel-local, and the size it half-replaces sat at the deleted place.

Scope. Floor 3 by witness plus 3000-pair samples at p = 3, 5; the floor-4 failures by witness over F3. The dimension classification is classical.

The channels as a design knob

Floor 2's one oddity is channel 2, and it has a cheap explanation: ij = −ji collapses to commutativity when −1 = 1, so every naive order Z⟨1, i, j, k⟩ is commutative mod 2 whatever algebra it presents. It also has an expensive one: the degeneration is the Hamilton quaternions' classical ramification set {2, ∞} seen through residue windows with ∞ deleted. Only the second predicts anything, and what it predicts is a pattern of channels fixed before any of it is computed.

Designed ramification rule

A quaternion algebra (a, b | Q) is ramified at a place when it stays a division algebra over the completion there, the p-adic field at a finite place and R at the archimedean one, and split when it becomes a matrix algebra; Hilbert reciprocity makes the ramification set finite and of even size across all places, and every such set is realized. So which channels ramify is chosen, not found. Four algebras across the seven channels give 28 verdicts, all fixed before the run and all holding: the ramified channels are exactly the designed finite primes, {3, 5} for (5, 3), {7, 11} for (77, −1), {2} for (−1, −1), {3} for (−3, −1), and channel 2 splits in each of the three designs that leave it out. Separating design from presentation needs a maximal order, a full-rank lattice subring maximal among such, certified by its reduced discriminant equalling the product of the designed primes: it reduces to M2(Fp) at exactly the undesigned channels, channel 2 included whenever undesigned, and at each designed channel to a local algebra with a 2-dimensional radical and residue field Fp². A designed channel does not vanish, it fattens to the quadratic extension. And the visible parity comes out odd: (−3, −1) ramifies at {3, ∞}, so the set the channels see has size one. Evenness is conserved only across all places and the tower deleted one, so the finite half of a reciprocity constraint is free to be odd, a design rule whose partner term sits at the missing window.

Scope. Maximality certified by reduced discriminant; Hilbert symbols computed at every place and reciprocity checked; the ramified shape certified per designed channel.

Every floor above the entry one falls to a wall that needs a single field and nothing about the tower: Wedderburn, Artin–Zorn and Chevalley–Warning are one-field facts, the row of the provenance ladder the division-ladder floors sit on. What the tower supplies is the reading, which channel a wall bites at, and at floor 2 a set of them that can be chosen.

The deleted budget

Both shapes generalize, on separate tracks. Designed ramification is a global constraint losing a term: reciprocity still binds the places that remain, and the freedom bought is the design knob. The entry floor is a theorem left silent: Chevalley–Warning's hypothesis is unmet at two variables, and what survives below its threshold is whatever it never governed. Priced, the two tracks converge on one coin: what deleting a place buys at a global constraint is exactly the deleted term's own local group, the group that term ranged over, both its torsion and its rank.

The place track: the torsion factor rule

Reciprocity's term is a sign, so the budget is one parity bit, the odd visible sets above. The Brauer sum generalizes it: a quaternion algebra carries one invariant per place, ½ where it ramifies and 0 where it splits, reciprocity is the law that they sum to 0, and the real term's group is Br(R) = ½Z/Z, so the budget is one bit and 2-torsion's alone; for the odd-degree cyclic algebras the visible invariants still sum to zero exactly (Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether). The product formula ∏v |x|v = 1 over all places loses an archimedean term whose values range over everything, so the visible constraint is void: every finitely supported vector of exponents is realized. The control is F2(t) with its degree place deleted, non-archimedean and the same total freedom, so the law is deletion's, not archimedean-ness's.

Scope. The parity point is the rule above, the visible parity odd on 1444 squarefree pairs exactly on the a < 0, b < 0 quadrant; the Brauer point stands on Frobenius and the classical exact sequence; the product formula and its control verified on 200 constructed rationals in exact arithmetic.

The rank face rule

An S-unit is an element invertible once denominators are allowed at the places in a set S; Dirichlet's theorem gives the S-unit group rank |S| − 1, the archimedean places counted in S. Read as the budget: each deleted term buys the rank of its own value group, which is 1, always, while the one rank lost is the product formula's single relation. That is why Z's unit group is {±1}: its S is the one archimedean place alone. Which prime is deleted enters only through its splitting, the count of terms and never the name: over Q(√−5), deleting the places over p buys rank 1, 2, 1, 2 at p = 2, 3, 11, 29, as the prime ramifies, splits, stays inert, splits. What the rank cannot see, which place, returns one floor down: the valuation image sits at finite index in the integer lattice with one coordinate per deleted finite place, and that index is the order of the subgroup of the class group, the finite group of ideal classes, generated by the deleted places' classes. Over Q the class group is trivial and the budget is paid with no correction; the witness pair p = 2 against p = 11 over Q(√−5) has equal rank and index 2 against 1.

Scope. A rule at its measured scope, over Q and an imaginary quadratic field where the unit rank is 0: ranks and indices over enumerated S-units at |S| ≤ 7 over Q and eight S-sets over Q(√−5), exact arithmetic; the general rank is Dirichlet's theorem.

The regulator face rule

Map an S-unit to the logarithms of its absolute values at the places of S: the image is a full lattice in the hyperplane where the coordinates sum to zero, the sum being the product formula, and that lattice's covolume, the volume of one fundamental cell, is the regulator. Over a real quadratic field the S-unit covolume is the field's unit regulator times one factor log NP per deleted term, the covolume of that term's own local log-value group with the residue degree included (log 9, not log 3, at the inert place over 3 in Q(√2)), times an index, with no cross-term residual. Deletion buys volume multiplicatively, and the one correction, applied once and not per term, is the same class-group index: over Q(√10) the ramified place over 2 against a split place over 41, equal rank, covolume ratio 2 against 1.

Scope. A rule at its measured scope: covolumes and lattice indices over enumerated S-units at sixteen S-sets across Q(√2) and Q(√10), the ratio equal to the index at every set; the product form in general is the classical S-regulator formula.

The product-formula, rank and regulator faces are one theorem seen three ways, and the theorem is classical. The idele class group has one coordinate per place of the field, each ranging over that completion's nonzero elements, almost all of them local units, modulo the field's own elements placed diagonally; an idele's norm is the product of its coordinates' absolute values, and the product formula says the field's elements land in the norm-one part. The norm-one ideles modulo the field are compact (Fujisaki), and that one compactness is exactly class-group finiteness plus the unit theorem: the class group is the compact quotient's discrete image, the rank face its projection onto the sum-zero log hyperplane, the covolume its Haar measure, per term because Haar measure on a restricted product is a product measure. Reciprocity is a character of the ideles trivial on the field, and the Brauer sum reads the same quotient's degree-2 cohomology. Five faces, one object at two levels, and one which-place correction because it is one object being read.

The ramification face refuses the per-term reading rule

Delete not a place but a layer of one: at a chosen set of finite places, cut the local units to a congruence depth instead of removing anything. The places stay, ramification there is newly visible, and what is bought is a ray class group, ideal classes counted with a congruence condition at a modulus m, since every quotient of the idele class group by an open subgroup is one. Its order is the class number times the count of residues invertible at m, divided by one correction, the image of the global units among those residues. The local factor is per term outright, residues factoring place by place; the correction is not. The unit image is one joint subgroup on at most unit-rank + 1 generators, and its failure to factor into per-place projections is exactly the excess of the true ray class number over the per-term prediction: over Q(√2), excess 2 at one two-place modulus against 1 at another, so the entanglement is the modulus's property, and largest at conjugate pairs, the two places over one prime, where the fundamental unit's norm ties one residue to the other's negated inverse, 16 against 256 at the places over 17. Over Q(√−5), where the units are ±1, the excess at s odd unramified places is 2s−1, unbounded in the place count. So both deletions price the same way, per-term local factors times one global corrector, and the two finitenesses the compactness bundles are the budget's two correctors: the class group when a place goes whole, the unit image when only a layer goes. Neither factors place by place.

Scope. A rule at its measured scope: unit images and ray class numbers by exact subgroup closure at two- and three-place moduli over Q(√2) and Q(√−5), all 21 place-pairs over Q(√2) scanned; that every open-subgroup quotient is a ray class group is classical.

The threshold track: the square ladder rule

Below an unmet uniformity hypothesis, what survives is local data deeper than a residue window. Every residue at every channel is a sum of two squares, so the classical laws over Z are channel-invisible: two squares needs the parity of the exponent of each prime ≡ 3 (mod 4), a valuation, depth below the window; three squares the 2-adic condition n ≠ 4a(8b + 7), strip the 4s and read mod 8, depth then precision; four squares nothing. Witnessed by pairs congruent modulo 510510 with opposite verdicts, 3 and 2552553 for two squares, 7 and 510517 for three. Wedderburn and Artin–Zorn have no sub-threshold object, every channel being finite, and the Hurwitz boundary is uniform, so the entry floor above was not luck: both tracks price an import's failure in the local coordinates the deletion removed or the window coarsened.

Scope. Exhaustive to 20000, the pair verdicts by direct search.
