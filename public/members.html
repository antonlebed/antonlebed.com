The gated members

The periodic digit patterns a gated stride carries onto the lattice, and what they are made of.

An Ostrowski window writes the integers in the digits of an irrational α = [0; a1, a2, …]: with convergents pk/qk, n = Σ bk qk greedily, legality capping bk at the partial quotient ak+1 (b0 at a1 − 1) and forcing a zero below any digit at its cap. A string is read through its star Σ bk θk, θk = qkα − pk the convergent remainder, and a shift by stride r sends every digit bk to position k + r, its image the shifted sum Σ bk θk+r. The designed family is the one-class window [0; (1, …, 1, a)∞], one large quotient a per period of length P, the position carrying it the class slot and a itself the class cap, every other slot capped at 1; a cell is a (window, stride) pair, written (P, a, r), and a column is a cell with its cap left free, (P, ·, r). On the lattice L = Z + Zα the unit η = −θP−1 acts by θk+P = ηθk, and the cuts, the circle points with two codings, are −sα for s ≥ 1, so −θj is the cut with s = qj. The parity law (Digit shifts) reads a stride's verdict off r mod P alone: an even residue reads at bounded lookahead and an odd residue gates, image points of ever-agreeing inputs straddling a cut.

A periodic pattern is a legal digit pattern repeating with the period or a multiple of it, and its one-period shifted sum Ψ gives it the image limit λ = Ψ/(1 − η). The pattern is a member of its cell when λ is a lattice point, Ψ ≡ 0 mod (1 − η)L, and λ is then its value; a value m − sα with s ≥ 1 is a cut, and the derivation of the gated half lands its patterns on the cuts −θj (the gated half). The deficit of a pattern at a depth is what its remaining tail must still sum to for the image to land, and the members are the cycles of the finite graph the deficits walk on, the graph on which a stride's verdict is decided (the bounded half). Two words for a pattern's shape: a comb is a periodic pattern written down rather than searched, and a tooth is one nonzero digit of it.

The member monoid

The member monoid rule

At the odd residues, where the parity law gates, nonzero periodic legal patterns landing the lattice exist — each a member, their deficit orbits the cycles of the bounded half's graph. A member's value is linear in its digits, so a legal digit-wise sum of members is a member, and every scanned member is such a sum over its cell's indecomposables, one to five generators per cell, every one of three shapes. The comb: t(−θr) at even P, t(−θr+1) at odd, the gated derivation's cuts, scaled to the class cap only where every supported position is a class slot. The pure class tooth, all of its digits on one large-quotient slot, whose height-h word is a member exactly when h0 divides h — h0 the order of θP−1+r in L/(1 − η)L, a divisor of det(I − H) for the period matrix H — so the pure tooth generates exactly where h0 ≤ a. And tooth-plus-unit words — a class tooth of some height, the digit at the slot, with unit digits beside it — carrying deeper cuts as the cap rises, each such cut a rung and a column's rungs its ladder. The cycle set equals the scaled comb orbits except where a tooth-plus-unit word joins at a = 4: 42 of 48 cells at P ≤ 8, 10 of 12 at P = 9.

What fixes a rung's height, and which rungs a column carries, is the rung law. A generator's value is not always a single cut: from cap 9 the (7, ·, 3) column carries 2(−θ5) − θ2, no multiple of any cut. The double-period members are decided by a concatenation criterion: member words u, v concatenate to a period-2P member iff λu + Tu ≡ λv + Tv mod (1 + η)L, T the cyclic wrap's r-truncation term, every period-2P member's blocks being members themselves — so the branching is concatenation freedom, not sum headroom, and exactly four cells fuse.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope: the chart at P ≤ 9, a ≤ 4, every odd residue, periods one and two; the monoid at P ≤ 8 to cap 8 and at P = 9 to cap 13; the columns at P ≤ 8 to cap 13. Component tiers: closure under legal sums a property; the tooth criterion a criterion, with h0 | det(I − H) a property checked at every cell; the ladder's cuts and heights a rule at that scope, the landed-cut reading of a generator's value a rule only to cap 8 and an observation past it; the concatenation criterion derived and machine-confirmed at all 95 legal pairs over 48 cells with zero mismatches. Missing for a theorem: that no member at one cap only exists, which the rung law admits only below cap about 8 and the census finds nowhere to P = 11.

The rung law and the existence criterion theorem

In the basis (θP−2, θP−1) the large quotient a enters θj only for j ≥ P, as a times the θP−2-coefficient, so a word's whole dependence on a is its class height h and its near units, the unit digits before the class slot, which wrap past the period; the units at or after the stride are deep and a-free. For a value λ write N(λ) for the negated α-coefficient of λ in L, so a cut −θj with j ≤ P − 1 has N = Fj+1. The rung law. A word that is a member at two consecutive caps a, a + 1 (a ≥ 2) has hFr + s = N(λ) exactly, s the sum of Fj+1 over its near slots j: the member identity solves to Δ · det(I − Ha) = −C with Δ the law's defect and C a cap-free signed Fibonacci sum smaller than aFP−1 + FP+1 + FP + 2FP−1, and two consecutive determinants both dividing C leave only C = 0. So the value is cap-free and the word is a member at every cap it is legal at; a rung landing t(−θr+m) sits at height ⌊tFr+m+1/Fr⌋ with its near units the Zeckendorf digits of the remainder — the greedy expansion over nonconsecutive Fibonacci numbers — and first fits one cap above its height exactly when the slot before the class slot is occupied. A member at one cap only would need |C| at least the determinant, possible only below cap (3FP + 2)/FP−2, about 8.

The existence criterion. The identity is linear in a, so it holds at two caps iff it holds at the golden window a = 1, where θj = (−Fj, Fj+1), and its a-coefficient vanishes, which is the law. The Z-linear map Φ(u + wα) = w − u/φ sends θj to φj, and the golden identity reads the word as a cyclic golden numeral, hφP−1+r + Σnear φP+j + Σdeep φj = Λ(φP − 1) with Λ = Φ(−λ), a cut −θj having Λ = φj. With the law it becomes Σdeep φj = kφP−1 − Λ for an integer k, one of at most two in [Λφ1−P, Λφ1−P + φ). Expansions over nonconsecutive powers of φ are unique, so the deep set is forced, the support of that expansion, and the word exists iff the support lies in [r + 1, P − 1], the height and near set forced the same way from Λφ − k, with one wrap rule: a deep unit at slot P − 1 forbids a near unit at slot 0. For a cut t(−θr+m) the condition is that kφn − t, n = P − 1 − r − m, expands within [1 − m, n]; at t = k = 1, φn − 1 expands to {1, 3, …, n − 1} at even n and {−1, 2, 4, …, n − 1} at odd. Read the other way, the member set at (P, r) is a table over (h, near set, k) that the cap truncates, finite off the half-period (the finite table), the generators the rows no two rows sum to: the members of a designed window are Fibonacci combinatorics in (P, r), with no arithmetic in the window consulted.

The r = 3 column: at P = 8 and 9 the rungs −θ5, −θ6, 2(−θ5), −θ7 at heights 4, 6, 8, 10, first fitting caps 4, 7, 8, 11; at P = 10 and 11 the first two alone, since the height-8 rung needs 2φ4 − 2 = φ5 + φ−1 inside P = 10's four deep slots and 2φ5 − 2 = φ6 + φ + φ−1 inside P = 11's five, and the top digit overflows both. At P = 11 the table's thirteen generators legal to cap 13 over the five odd strides, written before the enumeration, are exactly the enumeration's.

Scope. The law and its persistence a theorem for every word that is a member at two consecutive caps, a ≥ 2, P ≥ 3, the census's worst |C| at 0.247 of the divisibility floor its control; the criterion a theorem for such words, and that no other member exists a rule at P ≤ 11 and caps ≤ 13, every odd stride: 490 member readings to P = 10 and 120 at P = 11 against as many table rows, zero mismatches either way. Whether the table's fitting rows are regular in the height's Zeckendorf digits is open.

The table over every cap

The finite table theorem

With the slot set S fixed — a word's unit slots, near and deep together — the golden identity reads hφr−1 + ΣS φj = k(φP−1 − φ−1), and in the basis (1, φ) of Z[φ] that is two integer equations in the two unknowns (h, k), with columns (Fr−2, Fr−1) and (FP−2 + 1, FP−1 − 1). The matrix is singular exactly where φP − 1 is a rational multiple of φr, which the conjugate reduces to Fr = FP−r: the half-period stride 2r = P and the one coincidence (P, r) = (3, 1). Off those cells every slot set fixes at most one (h, k), and the slot sets — no two cyclically adjacent, the class slot excluded and the slot above it free only at height 0 — number fewer than the Lucas number LP, so a column holds at most one word per admissible slot set among the words that are members at two caps and is finite over every cap: the census to cap 13 read the bottom of a table, not the foot of a ladder. Once P ≥ 2r + 4 the column has exactly Fr+1 rows, one per Zeckendorf-legal near set (3, 8 and 21 at r = 3, 5, 7), and the tables swell only beside the half-period, where the matrix is nearly singular: 23 rows to height 61 at (11, 5), 154 to height 426 at (19, 9). At 2r = P, φP − 1 = φrLr, so the identity reads ΣS φj = cφr−1 with c = kLr − h, and every fitting slot set carries the whole progression h = kLr − c: the column is a finite set of residues mod Lr, each infinitely populated — heights ≡ 0, 1, 4, 6, 8 mod 11 at (10, 5), c = 0 the pure tooth and c = 3 the height-8 word; 34 residues mod 76 at (18, 9). In neither regime is membership a function of the height's Zeckendorf digits below a fixed index: at (10, 5) two heights agreeing below index 8 differ, and a finite nonempty set is no union of digit cylinders, the sets fixed by finitely many low digits. A column's member language over every cap is a lookup or a residue test, and no automaton is owed.

Scope. Theorem: the singular locus, the finiteness off it and the half-period's progressions, at every P ≥ 3 and every odd r, for the words that are members at two caps; a member at one cap only, none known, would sit below cap 8 over the same finite slot sets. The counts are prints at P = 4..20, the complete table cut at height 13 equal to the census's table row for row at all 28 cells with P ≤ 11; the Fr+1 row count a pattern at P ≤ 20, its derivation open.
