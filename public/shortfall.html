The shortfall accounted

The principal share of split primes runs short at small primes and climbs toward its nominal. The whole of that shortfall is one term of the explicit formula — the prime powers a count of primes leaves out — with the formula's own x-independent constant excluded as a rival, one class over a real field left over as a race of odd characters read before its bias has developed, and a residual count no mechanism derives. This page is the accounting: the nominal derived, so that the deficit is not an artifact of it; the term identified and put back; what it leaves and what it is not; the deficit read per class and per character; and what nothing derives: a deficit that shallows as the discriminant grows while its reach grows with it.

The setting, stated so this page reads alone. Over a quadratic field K of discriminant D — fundamental throughout: the discriminant of the field itself, never that of a subring of finite index — an odd prime splits when D is a nonzero square modulo it, and then carries two places whose ideal classes, the ideal taken modulo the principal ones, are inverse: it is counted in one of them by a choice no statistic here can see, a class and its inverse sharing their order — the least power of a class that is trivial — and their squareness. A prime whose ideal stays prime is inert; one dividing D is ramified. The classes form the class group Cl(K), of order the class number h, and a place is principal — its ideal generated by one element — exactly when its class is the trivial one. The narrow class number h+ counts form classes up to proper equivalence: it is h over an imaginary field, and over a real field 2h when the fundamental unit has norm +1 and h when it has norm −1, and most of this page's statistics are graded by it. The page opens on the share, the fraction of split primes below a cut that are principal, quoted over its nominal 1/h or 1/h+, the deficit being what it falls short of that nominal by; past the term, three of the blocks below read a COUNT of primes per field instead, a share pooled across fields being the one thing that cannot be read. The correction is this page's business throughout: the missing term put back.

The phenomenon being accounted for is on the landing: the share is short at small primes at both signs and climbs toward nominal as the cut rises, graded by the narrow class number — 0.868 to 0.990 across the prime bins at h+ = 2 and 0.332 to 0.964 at h+ = 8 on the real side (Principal places). Over an imaginary field a floor cuts across all of it, no prime below |D|/4 being principal at all (the floor).

The term

The nominal is derived, so the deficit is not a wrong nominal rule

A split prime's Frobenius — the element of a Galois group the prime determines up to conjugacy — lies in Gal(H/K) for H the Hilbert class field, the maximal unramified abelian extension of K, whose Galois group over K is the class group itself. Chebotarev's density theorem distributes that Frobenius uniformly over the group, and conjugation c ↦ −c is the pairing of the two places above the prime, so the density of principal split primes is 1/h, and 1/h+ by the same argument transposed to the narrow Hilbert class field.

Scope. Proved. A cubic field's partially split primes, whose Frobenius is a transposition, need a different argument (The split triple).

The shortfall is the explicit formula's prime-power term rule

Chebotarev's explicit formula counts prime POWERS, weighted 1/k at the k-th power; a count of primes omits them and so runs short of the formula's main term in exactly the classes the powers land in — the prime-power term: one half for each prime below √x whose Frobenius SQUARES into the class (a split prime of class b onto 2b, an inert prime onto the identity), a third per cube, and the ramified ideals at one half each on the classes of order at most 2. Over one field the oscillatory remainder is as large as that shift; over a population the oscillations average out and the shift survives. Write the level of a class, or of a stratum of them, for its observed count of places over the nominal count — the ratio the share is quoted at, read per class. Put the weights back per field and per prime window and the imaginary cells flatten whole: the generating classes from 1.0875 to 1.0011 ± 0.0033 below 1000, the trivial class from 0.52–0.79 to 0.96–1.02 below 1000 over h = 6 to 14, every cell within 0.03 of 1 at 10⁴. The real narrow side carries the term to three quarters or more below 1000 — and what it leaves is one class (the real residual).

So every grading of the share BY THE CLASS GROUP is a face of one class function, the weight of squares, cubes and ramified ideals each class receives; what the field's SIZE grades is a separate question and open (what nothing derives). The grading by the narrow class number is the inert primes' squares, all on the identity, h/2 of them per class-count; with the split squares the classes of order at most 2 send to the identity as well, the trivial class is short by (h + |Cl[2]|)/2 · π(√x)/π(x), h the order of whichever group the places are sorted into, |Cl[2]| its number of classes of order at most 2, and the nominal counted over SPLIT primes. The grading by the number of primes dividing D is those split squares, which land on the doubled classes and on the identity from exactly the classes of order at most 2, plus the ramified ideals. The decay along the cut is the term's: the excess falls by a factor of 8 from cut 250 to 10⁴, where π(√x)/π(x) itself falls by 5.6, a 1/log law by 1.7 and a constant by 1, and putting the term back flattens every cut to within its error. And the ceiling at the classes that generate the group is the classes that receive nothing: in a cyclic group the non-squares are the classes whose order carries the full 2-part, at h = 4, 8, 16 the generators alone, so the order ladder and the square ladder coincide there and part wherever h has an odd factor — at h = 6, 10, 12, 20 the odd-order classes are squares and the order ladder inverts exactly there.

Scope. Rule in range: the term is classical and the accounting is verified on the fundamental discriminants of both signs to 4000 (narrow classes on the real side, to 16,000 for the real ceiling) over the odd primes to 10⁴, with the arithmetic progressions to 10⁷ over 77 prime moduli as the control — the non-residue classes lead by 1.15 · π(√x)/π(x) at 8σ and by 0.9σ once the same weights go back. What is NOT this term is a cubic field's partially split places: a transposition is never a square in S3, and that population's ladder is a second object (The generator ceiling).

verifier: explore_ceiling_squares.py

The bias behind the term is a published theorem: a principal split prime is one splitting completely in the Hilbert class field, so the deficit at small primes is a prime race against the identity class, and Aoki and Koyama (Chebyshev's Bias against Splitting and Principal Primes in Global Fields, J. Number Theory 245, 2023) prove, under the Deep Riemann Hypothesis and the non-vanishing at ½ of the class-group L-functions, a bias against principal primes weighted by 1/√N(p) of size ((|Cl/Cl²| − 1)/2) · log log x — the split-square term in log-density form, vanishing at odd h exactly as the split squares on Cl² do. What that weighted coefficient does not carry is the inert primes' squares, linear in h at x ≪ |D|, which an unweighted count at a finite cut shows first.

What it leaves, and what it is not

The real residual is one narrow class, an odd-character race before its bias has developed observation

Read as counts of primes per field, the corrected real side's order-2 shortfall is entirely the narrow class k0 of a principal ideal with a generator of NEGATIVE norm — present exactly when the fundamental unit has norm +1, of order 2 — which sits 2.10 ± 0.14 primes per field below its nontrivial siblings below 1000, pooled over h+ = 4 to 10, and 1.5 ± 0.3 below at 10⁴, while the other order-2 classes read flat at +0.18 ± 0.16. At the h+ = 2 fields that carry k0 the seat is an identity: D = D1D2 with both prime discriminants negative, and a split prime's place lies in k0 exactly when the Kronecker symbol (D1/p) is −1, at zero mismatches over 1133 fields. So the residual there is the race of two ODD quadratic characters — χD1 and χD2, equal at a split prime and each taking −1 at −1 — read at primes comparable to the discriminant, before the bias the prime squares drive has developed: over an imaginary population instead, the fundamental discriminants d to 16,000, that race averages +1.2 primes at x = 1000 against the −5.5 the square term carries and −3.9 at 10⁴ against −12.5, reaching the term only where |d| ≪ x (−12.9 against −12.5 at |d| < 100 and x = 10⁴), a fifth of its size at |d| ~ x and reversed to a residue lead between x and 4x; and the class's shrinking by 10⁴, from −1.07 ± 0.07 to −0.84 ± 0.18 primes per field, is the bias catching up. Above h+ = 2 the characters −1 on k0 are odd in the same archimedean sense — complex conjugation is k0's image in the Galois group of the narrow class field over Q — and their trivial zeros alone put k0 one prime below its siblings, measured two.

Scope. Observation on the real sweep to 16,000, read as counts per field; the h+ = 2 identity is a property, exact over its 1133 fields. The instrument is the one the arithmetic progressions control under the prime-power term.

Not the x-independent term; the imaginary cut ladder is the hard zero's repayment observation

Beyond the main term, the zeros and the prime powers, the explicit formula carries a constant of size ½ log|D| per nontrivial character — a fixed number of primes per class, a level falling as 1/π(x), the shape every residual here wears. And at D < 0 there is the hard zero: the principal form's values below |D|/4 are the squares, none of them prime, so no prime below |D|/4 is principal at all. As counts it fails: the real narrow cells are flat from cut 400 through 630 to 1000 (order 2 at −1.12, −1.19, −1.31 primes per field, the trivial class at +0.38, +0.37, +0.45) and then SHRINK by 10⁴: of the three decidable ratios to the cut-1000 value, 0.01 ± 0.45 and 0.18 ± 0.53 sit below the half-to-double band a constant would hold in and 0.67 ± 0.19 inside it, while a prime-power leftover would read 2.27, excluded at 4σ to 8σ per cell. The imaginary trivial class jumps from −0.83 ± 0.09 primes per field at cut 630 to −0.05 ± 0.09 at cut 1000: a field's trivial class is empty below |D|/4 while the main term is unchanged, so the deficit is repaid above |D|/4, and the fields with |D| in (2520, 4000] have their floor inside that band.

Scope. Observation; the imaginary jump is a reading of a pooled figure, and the reason no imaginary cell is read as a count below its discriminants.

The hard zero sets where the count starts, and a class of small order starts later observation

The hard zero has no indefinite twin, and it grades nothing where it holds, the share non-monotone across the bands of the class's own minimum (0.985, 1.060, 1.022 imaginary). The least split prime a class holds, q(C), is the dial the minimum failed to be: the median q of the classes of order at most 2 exceeds their field's median over its other classes at 0.873 of real fields and 0.982 of imaginary ones, against a size-matched placebo at 0.492 and 0.465, and at MATCHED q the order-2 gap survives at 0.75 of its size real and 0.38 imaginary by the fine bands — the later start about a quarter of the real deficit and three fifths of the imaginary, the asymmetry the hard zero would give.

Scope. The zero is the floor over an imaginary field, verified at zero violations; the later start is an observation. Whether the later start is the prime-power term read at a first arrival is not computed.

Read per class and per character

Read per class it is a ladder in the order, and it must be read within a field observation

A class's order is shared by a class and its inverse, so the ambiguity in the sorting does not reach it. The trivial class holds its field's smallest bottom-bin level at 0.746 of real fields with h+ > 1 and 0.542 of imaginary ones. Paired within a field against that field's classes of order above 2, order 2 runs −0.151 real and −0.216 imaginary, order 3 −0.113 and −0.056, order 4 −0.096 and −0.013, order 5 or more +0.112 and +0.040: small order short, large order long, the trivial class the extreme. Pooled across fields the column must not be read: a field's levels average to 1, so a short trivial class inflates its other classes, and which column receives the surplus is the field's composition — the pooled order-2 column reads 1.059 real against 0.889 imaginary from composition alone. Priced absolutely against 1/h+, which is what removes the within-field normalization the paired reading needs, the table has two gradients: at fixed order the level falls with h+ on the imaginary side in a STEP at 16 (order 2 between 1.011 and 1.041 through h+ = 12, then 0.966 to 0.814), the trivial class the one order falling monotonically (0.833 to 0.334 over h+ = 4 to 20), while the classes that GENERATE the group — order equal to h+, existing only where the narrow group is cyclic — read between 1.079 and 1.105 across ten strata, a spread of 0.026 with no trend.

Scope. Observation, per class on the same discriminants over the odd primes below 1000; a field with fewer than twenty split primes carries no share. The paired figures are comparative, a class against the other classes of the same field. The observed spread beats every rotation of the order labels within each field at 12 of 13 strata, so the ladder is structure, and grouping the raw cells by squareness fits better than by order, which is the prime-power term's own reading of it.

In the character basis the term's split squares sit on the quadratic characters, and no component beyond them is established observation

The split squares' weight is uniform on the doubled classes, and a subgroup's indicator transforms onto the characters trivial on it, which for the doubled classes are exactly the characters with χ² = 1 — the quadratic characters, which ARE the genus characters, the ones sorting the classes by the quadratic characters of the discriminant's prime factors. The inert term sits on the identity and is flat on every character; the ramified term transforms onto the characters trivial on the classes of order at most 2. A spectrum concentrated at the quadratic characters and starved at the maximal-order ones is therefore what the term predicts, and that is what is measured: the quadratic share of the per-class deviation's energy beats its rotation band at all eight cyclic strata and the maximal-order share sits below it at all eight, the same on non-cyclic groups. Three readings looked for a component the term does not give and none stands: a per-field amplitude removed, the quadratic excess survives at only three strata of eight; the reachability criterion for the 2-torsion term — which characters can receive it — does not grade the residual; and a held-out amplitude decays straight through every complement size it is gated on, to 0.95 of its null, the signature of a fit's own distortion thinning.

Scope. The support is a property; the readings are observations over 981 real and 1208 imaginary fields. The allowance for a quadratic share after symmetrizing is 1.5 to 1.8 times flat, most of the raw multiple.

What nothing derives

The deficit's shape is scale-free; its level shallows with |D| and its reach grows, and nothing derives either observation

Where the deficit ENDS in p never moves with |D| as √D would, and where it moves at all it moves earlier: the prime at which a fixed-h+ row reaches 0.95 of nominal, regressed on log|D| across a 512-fold lever from 2·10³ to 1.024·10⁶, has slope −0.245 to +0.003 across the five real strata from h+ = 2 to 8, three below zero and two within 0.003 of it against the +0.5 a √D transient predicts. That kills the one form-geometric mechanism available — a reduced indefinite form has |a| < √D, predicting a transient ending at √D graded by the length of the principal cycle — and the regulator, the logarithm of the fundamental unit, is not a second dial, ranking the fields with that length at Spearman 0.94 to 1.00 within a band. But the LEVEL at p ≤ 100 is graded by |D|, in the direction that shallows it: 0.880 to 0.940 at h+ = 2 and 0.342 to 0.627 at 8 across the lever, not levelled off by 10⁶. Run through the correction, the trivial class's residual COUNT rises with log|D| at every h+: at cut 100 by 0.107 primes per unit, which is the constant's own allowance of 0.109, and at cut 1000 by 0.14 to 0.35 where it allows 0.072 — the grading growing with the cut where the allowance falls, so what matches at the first cut is not what stands at the second; read in windows of the norm the count above p = 100 runs from −0.04 ± 0.21 primes per field at the bottom of the lever to +1.28 ± 0.14 at its top at h+ = 8; and √D is not its scale. What stands is a count that reaches further out as the field grows while the point where the deficit ENDS never moves right, densest at the smallest primes and nowhere zero below 1000, not derived.

Scope. Observation, both signs over five |D| bands against the odd p ≤ 10⁵ with h+ fixed in every load-bearing row. Which term of which formula sets the growing reach is open; a population where the regulator and h+ move independently, which h+R+ = √D·L(1, χ) forbids over quadratic fields, is what a mechanism would need. At D < 0 the level is not measurable: above the floor the share is ordered by CLEARANCE, the decade's lower edge over |D|/4, the floor's own tail. A cubic field's count, by contrast, closes inside its range (The generator ceiling).
