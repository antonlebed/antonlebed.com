The Object

The definition, the deleted place, the first fourteen rungs and what is born along them.

  • Classical structure — the geometry of one rung: curvature, shells, Betti numbers, knots
  • The frequency response — one real number per element, and what a plateau adds
  • The seed-flower — the primes a sub-ring names, the gate on naming, and the map that iterates it

The identity

A channel is a residue window: reduction at one finite place, the residue mod one prime. The rung Z/pk# — pk# the product of the first k primes — reads the first k windows simultaneously: every element is a unique k-tuple of residues, arithmetic decomposes window by window, no window sees another's content:

Z/pk#  ≅  F2×F3×F5×⋯×Fpk\mathbf{Z}/p_k\# \;\cong\; \mathbf{F}_2 \times \mathbf{F}_3 \times \mathbf{F}_5 \times \cdots \times \mathbf{F}_{p_k}

Each rung is nested in the next, Z/6 ⊂ Z/30 ⊂ Z/210 ⊂ ⋯, and each is a finite section of one ambient object, the product of all prime fields. The CRT encode/decode and per-channel arithmetic the verifier scripts import is one small shared library, crt.py.

The limit object rule

The inverse limit of the tower along its rungs is the full product of the prime fields, one free residue per prime:

lim←⁡kZ/pk#  =  ∏pFp\varprojlim_k \mathbf{Z}/p_k\# \;=\; \prod_p \mathbf{F}_p

Every exponent stays 1, so no Zp ever forms: the limit is not the profinite completion Ẑ = ∏p Zp. The two differ by exactly the radical, ∏p Fp = Ẑ/J(Ẑ): squarefree is strip-the-radical, place by place.

The integers embed in the limit injectively, but the certificate is the deleted place: a nonzero integer n reads 0 in only finitely many windows because p > |n| forces p ∤ n, a size bound. The image is dense and never surjective — a tuple prescribing zeros at every prime ≡ 3 mod 4 outruns every integer — and under Haar measure almost every element has infinitely many zero channels, so the integers sit in a null set. Of the blueprint, the all-field channels, the meadow and the idempotents survive the limit; λ (below) and the code die, since no fixed exponent works at every prime and no height exists to cut at.

Scope. The transition maps are channel-forgetting projections (checked exhaustively for k ≤ 5, sampled at k = 6, 7); the identification of the limit, the radical, the embedding and the density are algebraic; the null-set statement is Borel–Cantelli under the Mertens rate, its ratio 0.9997 at 10⁴ channels.

The deleted place

The construction removes exactly one window: the archimedean place. Size, sign, comparison, carries, overflow — none is a channel quantity, and no reading of a proper subset of windows recovers one: to every strict channel subset, the size of an element is essentially uniform. Measuring this deletion and its echoes — which functions can be read through which windows, and at what price — is its own section (Walls).

The tower split rule

At rung k ≥ 4 take the first k−3 primes as data and the last 3 as parity: an element below the data modulus pk−3# is determined by its data residues and its parity residues are forced. The code has minimum distance exactly 4 at every such rung — any k−3 channels reconstruct the element, any single error corrects — meeting the Singleton bound: two codewords agreeing on a channel set S differ by a multiple of ∏S, and a difference below the data modulus supports at most k−4 channels, the pair (0, p1⋯pk−4) attaining it. The rate is (k−3)/k, passing 1/2 at k = 7.

The guarantee is a height cut: "below the data modulus" is a bound on the integers, the deleted place re-imported by choice. Redundancy is a property of the integers under a bound, never of the product ring — in the limit there is no height and no code — and ring arithmetic does not preserve the data range, so any use of the code along a computation re-derives parity per step.

Scope. The distance and the weight matrix are proved for every squarefree rung with k ≥ 4, and swept exhaustively k = 4..10.

verifiers: explore_genesis_ladder.py; the code's construction and self-test, crt.py.

The sieve identity property

Along the primorial trajectory the tower is the algebraic form of the sieve of Eratosthenes — identity, not analogy: sieving by p is reading the window at p and discarding the zero fiber. The survivors of the k-prime sieve on {1, …, pk#} are exactly the units of Z/pk#; each sieve step is the CRT projection onto one channel; the 2k inclusion–exclusion terms are the 2k idempotents; the survivor count is φ(pk#), the inclusion–exclusion formula itself; and any k−3 prime sieves reconstruct the full sieve result, which is the tower split read on the sieve.

Scope. A term-by-term dictionary — units, idempotents, φ, λ, the error-correcting split — with each computable row verified in the script.

verifier: explore_sieve_connection.py

Read the units as the sieve's survivors and one question becomes arithmetic about the channels: step off a non-survivor by ±g for a ring prime g — does the step land on a survivor?

The generator-step escape rule

Let n be a non-unit, S the set of ring primes dividing it — the channels where n reads 0 — and take the coupling class of n to be every element with exactly that null set. For a signed generator step ±g: if g ∈ S no member of the class escapes at all, since g divides both the element and the step. If g ∉ S, exactly (g−1)·∏(p−2) of the class's ∏(p−1) members land on a unit, both products running over the primes outside S and the first also skipping g — an escape probability

∏p∉S,  p≠gp−2p−1.\prod_{p \notin S,\; p \neq g} \frac{p-2}{p-1}.

The factor at p = 2 forces a parity dichotomy: an odd non-unit escapes only via ±2, every odd step landing even, while an even one escapes via any odd g ∉ S and never via ±2. Every nonzero class has positive escape probability and the class {0} alone has none. The law is class-level, not per-element: individual stuck non-units exist, 122 of them in Z/2310.

Run the same product with no channel dead at all — a survivor stepping by 2 rather than a non-survivor escaping — and it is a known density: ∏3≤p≤pk(p−2)/(p−1) is exactly the conditional twin-survivor density of the k-prime sieve, the chance that n + 2 survives given that n does — 22,275/92,160 = 0.2417 at k = 7 — with Mertens asymptotic 2C2e−γ/ln pk for C2 the twin prime constant (ratio 0.9999 by k = 10⁵). The twin-survivor density is a built-in channel statistic.

Scope. Proved for all k within a class — CRT runs the non-null residues over every nonzero tuple exactly once — and verified exhaustively for k ≤ 7.

verifier: explore_unit_escape.py

The genesis ladder

Reading the rungs as a chronology: which capabilities exist at rung k, and for how long. The provenance ladder pins each obstruction down to its minimal carrier; the genesis ladder is its mirror — each capability pinned up to its birth rung, starting from Z/1, the rung without distinction.

The fate rule rule

Channels only accumulate along the trajectory, so a capability's temporal fate is its quantifier shape over channels. An ∃-shaped capability (some channel carries the defining property — in general, any condition inherited by channel supersets) is monotone: born at the rung of the least qualifying prime, immortal after. A ∀-shaped one is anti-monotone: dead at the rung of the least failing prime, forever — 2 and 3 are permanent vetoes. A conjunction of the two is a window, born and mortal. Walls are ∃-shaped over substructure, so walls never heal.

The founding steps: k = 1 is the first distinction and the trajectory's only field rung, where Boolean logic is the ring; k = 2 brings composition and its price in the same rung — zero divisors, idempotents beyond {0, 1}, the hiding of sign; k = 3 the first grade strictly between "some channel" and "every channel", the first λ with a proper divisor, and an element of order 4 born while the unit group stops being cyclic. The i specimen is the deletion in one row: √−1 as a constant — the archimedean form of i — dies at rung 2 under 3's veto, while i as rotation, an order-4 element, is born at rung 3 and immortal. The tower keeps the turn and discards the square root. The chart's one window is the Fano plane, born at rung 5 and dead at rung 6 when a Z/3 leaks into the character ladder.

Scope. The fate rule is proved; accumulation is the only ingredient, so a designed tower chooses its own vetoes (skip 3 and √−1 lives on every rung whose odd primes are ≡ 1 mod 4). Every chart row's trajectory is computed per rung and verified monotone in its predicted direction.

verifier: explore_genesis_ladder.py

The birthday formula rule

An element of multiplicative order m exists at rung k iff m | λ(k), so its birthday is set by least primes in arithmetic progressions: writing L(q) for the least prime ≡ 1 (mod q) — Linnik's function — the birthday of order m is the largest rung(L(qa)) over the prime powers qa exactly dividing m. Dirichlet guarantees every order is born, Linnik prices the wait, and the wait is rough: order 23 arrives at rung 15 while order 19 waits until rung 43, and m ≤ 200 already needs the prime 3547, rung 497.

Scope. Proved; swept exact for m ≤ 200.

verifier: explore_genesis_ladder.py

The saturation observation

Every mortal row of the chart is dead by rung 6 and every qualitative birth has happened by rung 7, where the tower split's rate crosses 1/2 — its last such step. Past it, births are parametric and Linnik-priced (an order at a time), trajectory events (a transparent prime) or capacity. The emergence staircase has a top step, and it is Z/510510.

Scope. The chart's rows, computed k ≤ 12; a statement about the capabilities charted, not about every capability.

Lambda and transparency

The universal period of the power maps at rung k is the Carmichael exponent λ(k) = lcm(p1−1, …, pk−1): every unit satisfies xλ = 1 and every non-unit converges under powering to the idempotent on its support. Along the tower it runs 1, 2, 4, 12, 60, 60, 240, 720, 7920, 55440, … — and the repeats are the interesting part.

The transparency criterion property

Adding pk jumps λ iff pk − 1 introduces a prime-power factor not already present in λ. Otherwise pk is transparent: capacity grows (φ, idempotents) but no new orbit period appears. The criterion: pk is transparent at rung k iff (pk − 1) | λ(k−1). A plateau is a maximal run of two or more rungs sharing one λ — a jump rung followed by consecutive transparent primes: k = 10..14 (29, then 31, 37, 41, 43, at λ = 55,440) is the first.

Scope. All k, by construction. Computed landscape and plateaus k = 1..50; the first transparent prime is 13 at k = 6, where 12 divides λ(5) = 60.

verifier: explore_lambda_tower.py

The tower thus alternates between two growth modes: complexity rungs, where λ jumps and new orbit periods appear, and capacity rungs, where a transparent prime adds space on the same dynamical skeleton. In the limit the balance tips entirely to capacity — the transparency density approaches 1, a theorem whose proof and rate live in the density theorem.

The primes that appear as factors of λ enter in an order of their own, governed by Linnik's function L(q) — the same function that prices the element birthdays above, since an order-q element and the prime q in λ arrive at the very same rung. It inverts the natural order: 11 enters before 7 (L(11) = 23 < L(7) = 29), 23 before 13 (47 < 53), and 17 — a ring prime already at rung 7, where it contributes 2⁴ — becomes a factor of λ in its own right only at rung 27, via p = 103.

The Linnik ordering rule

No rung's pk − 1 ever contributes two never-before-seen prime factors to λ: each rung introduces at most one new prime, so the introductions are injective and the entry order is well defined. Verified exhaustively through k = 3,001,134 — every prime p ≤ 5·10⁷. The margin is not thin: in every one of the 414,894 introductions the new prime is the largest factor of p − 1, and the near misses — a co-factor of p − 1 that entered outside the first 10% of the rungs below k — die out at k = 43. A collision needs a prime p ≡ 1 (mod 2q1q2) for two new primes at once, each having dodged all its earlier chances to enter.

Scope. Exhaustive to p ≤ 5·10⁷; a proof for all k is open, so always injective stays a conjecture — a Cramér–Dirichlet model makes a collision past the checked horizon vanishingly unlikely, and a heuristic is not a proof. The serial ledger below certifies injectivity itself much further, for every p ≤ 10¹⁶.

verifier: explore_linnik_injectivity.py

The ordering rules out two never-before-seen primes arriving at one rung. The full ledger law is stronger: counting prime powers — so that a rung raising an exponent already present (2² at p = 5, 2⁴ at p = 17, 3² at p = 19) counts as a birth too — every jump of λ still delivers exactly one.

The serial ledger rule

Every jump of λ introduces exactly one new prime power, never two — at k ≤ 2000 the 468 jumps split into 438 that bring a new prime and 30 that only raise the exponent of one already present, none both. What the law is: with L(m) the least prime ≡ 1 (mod m), adding the prime p raises the exponent of q in λ iff p = L(qj) for some j ≥ 1 — iff p is a record, the first prime a progression 1 mod qj ever sees. The record sets {L(qj) : j ≥ 1} are classical objects — in base 2 the record is 2j + 1 exactly when that number is prime, the Fermat primes — and the law claims nothing about any one of them: it says they are pairwise disjoint across bases. A violation would be a single prime standing as the least in two prime-power progressions with distinct bases; both moduli then divide p − 1, and being coprime so does their product, so the smaller is ≤ √(p − 1): every violating prime up to 10¹⁶ is the record of some modulus below 10⁸. Scanning them all, then testing each record prime against every prime-power divisor of its p − 1, decides every prime up to 10¹⁶ and finds no violation: 5,762,848 record primes, zero collisions. The closest call anywhere is 7 = L(3) against L(2) = 3.

Scope. The record characterization is proved (a criterion); disjointness — hence the law — is verified for every prime p ≤ 10¹⁶. Beyond that it is a conjecture with a convergent price: a rival modulus m at a record prime p requires every prime below p to have missed a progression of density 1/φ(m), chance about exp(−π(p)/φ(m)), summable over the records with its mass at the small primes already enumerated. So the law should hold for every p while sitting past the same wall as the ordering above: a proof needs least-prime control at every modulus and scale at once.

Designed towers

The primorial path is one trajectory through a lattice: any squarefree set of primes is a tower ring with the full blueprint — fields, total division, idempotents, an MDS code where the split is sized. The sieve identity is primorial-specific; the blueprint is not. The Walls section carries the charted specimens, including the internet-checksum ring Z/(216−1) and its machine-word family.

The seed-flower

A sub-ring on m of the tower primes, N their product, carries the integer −χ = N(m−1) − Σ N/pi, whose prime factors are routinely tower primes the sub-ring does not contain — {3, 5} gives 7 — and one congruence governs every such naming: an absent prime s divides −χ exactly when the members' inverses mod s sum to m − 1, so naming reads residues and nothing else. The long-run supply of new namings is a Hardy–Littlewood sum over the factorizations of pk + 1; the fraction of sub-rings whose −χ is itself prime decays with no floor, at a rate derived within a conditioned model; a missed prime is a subset-sum accident at the rate a random model predicts; and iterated, the naming map is fixed-point-free and drains almost every orbit to the empty set: The seed-flower.
