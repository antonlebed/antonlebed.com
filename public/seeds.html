Menu seeds

Which menus can take part in a collision at all. A collision needs a menu whose polynomial factors through a factor with a negative coefficient — a seed — and which menus those are has a closed-form answer at two and three members, a ceiling on a seed's core that holds at every size — half the size — and a split in the negative factor itself, between the kind that vanishes at roots of unity and the kind that never does: along a line the commoner kind from degree 7 on, while four members are closed off every line by a theorem and five first carry the free kind beside a rooted one.

A menu is a finite set of integers at least 2 — in the grown worlds of the amnesia certificate, the moves a state admits, each move multiplying the state by one member. Read at a temperature β a menu becomes the sum of m−β over its members, and writing one variable xp for p−β at each prime p makes that sum a polynomial in the primes' variables with every coefficient 0 or 1 — a 0/1 polynomial, one term per member, the term's exponent vector being the member's factorization. Dividing out its content, the largest single-term factor, leaves the menu's core, which is what every statement here is about: multiplying every member by one fixed integer changes the content and not the core. Where every factor of a core has nonnegative coefficients each factor is itself a 0/1 polynomial and the factorization stays among the menus; where one factor irreducible over the integers carries a NEGATIVE coefficient it stands for no menu, and the factors beside it can pair around it two ways. A menu whose core has such a factor is a seed, and only a pair of menus with a seed on one side can collide — weigh routes identically at every temperature through two different factorizations, the subject of Menu collisions. So the seeds are what every collision sweep is really walking, and the question here is which menus they are and what shape a seed's core can have.

The shape is read off the exponent vectors. Their convex hull is the core's hull, its dimension the core's dimension — 1 when the vectors are collinear, which makes the core a polynomial in a single variable after a change of monomial variables. A product's hull is the Minkowski sum of its factors' hulls (Ostrowski), and a face of a hull — the terms extreme in some direction — carries the product of the factors' faces in that direction. The smallest seeds all run on one negative factor, the integers' own: 1 + x³ = (1 + x)(1 − x + x²), which is why {2, 16} is a seed and (1 + x³)(1 + x + x²) = (1 + x)(1 + x² + x⁴), the six-term identity, is the smallest collision that is a genuine failure of factorization. A box is a bound on menu elements, which nothing in the mathematics names.

Which menus are seeds

At a prime size, a seed is a reducible core property

A 0/1 polynomial's value at 1 is its number of terms; if it is a product of two nonnegative polynomials each factor's value is at least its own term count, and the product's terms all lie among the pairwise products of the factors', so term counts MULTIPLY and both factors are themselves 0/1 — the term-count law, a lemma of the classification of factorization in this semiring by number of terms (van de Woestijne 2011), used here one menu at a time. When the number of members is PRIME no nonnegative factorization of the core survives the content division, and being a seed is exactly being REDUCIBLE over the integers — the semiring question turned classical. At four members the same law says what the NON-seeds look like: every reducible non-seed is a product of two 0/1 binomials, the only split four admits.

Scope. The law and the prime-size identity are elementary and proved; the four-member shape is checked at every one of the 31,465 menus of {2..32}.

Two members close in the elements rule

Write g for gcd(m, n) and d for the largest integer with m/g and n/g both perfect d-th powers: {m, n} is a seed exactly when d has an odd prime factor. The core is Ud + Vd for coprime monomials U, V, and a sum of d-th powers factors over the integers exactly on that condition. Twelve menus of {2..64} pass, eleven at d = 3 and one at d = 5, {2, 64}.

Scope. Proved, and checked against factorization at all 1,953 two-member menus of {2..64} with no disagreement.

verifier: explore_seed_shape.py

Three members close in the exponent vectors rule

A three-member menu is a seed exactly when its reduced exponent vectors are COLLINEAR — a non-collinear 0/1 trinomial is irreducible outright, the trinomial lemma — and the collinear exponents {0, a, b} meet all three residues modulo 3, with {a, b} not {1, 2}. The middle clause is divisibility by 1 + x + x², the polynomial of the primitive cube roots of unity; the last removes that factor itself, which is irreducible. That a cyclotomic factor — one whose roots are roots of unity — is the ONLY route into a reducible 0/1 trinomial is classical; what is proved here is the reduction to it. Five menus of {2..64} pass, carrying three trinomials: 1 + x² + x⁴ at three of them, 1 + x + x⁵ and 1 + x⁴ + x⁵ at one each.

Scope. Proved, its middle clause resting on the classical trinomial classification, cited rather than re-derived; checked against factorization at all 39,711 three-member menus of {2..64} with no disagreement.

verifier: explore_seed_shape.py

Four members keep neither mechanism observation

44 seeds among the 31,465 four-member menus of {2..32}, and exactly 1 of the 44 is collinear — {2, 4, 16, 32} — while none carries 1 + u + u² in any monomial u; each carries a binomial 1 + u instead, which the cofactor theorem proves is forced, and 41 of the 44 have exactly two integer-irreducible factors. The collinear seeds are what the collision sweeps keep returning to: {2, 8, 32} is the unique collinear three-member seed of {2..32} as {2, 4, 16, 32} is the unique four-member one, and their product is the one place either box holds where a collinear seed meets a collinear seed — the graded instance of the ten-term rule.

Seeds are generated, not filtered property

Each closed criterion is a parametric family rather than a list: {c·u, c·v} with u, v coprime d-th powers and d carrying an odd prime factor at two members; {c, c·Qa, c·Qb} with {0, a, b} meeting all three residues modulo 3 and not {0, 1, 2} at three. So the seed side of any sweep at those sizes needs no box, and only the PARTNER side is sampled by element size. Six members is composite and pays the census: 55 seeds among the 100,947 six-member menus of {2..24}, 203 among the 736,281 of {2..32}.

Scope. The families are the two criteria read as generators; the six-member counts are exhaustive over their boxes.

How high a seed's dimension goes

A seed's core has dimension at most half its size theorem

Every 0/1 polynomial with n terms that factors into two non-monomial factors has hull dimension at most ⌊n/2⌋, so a seed of n members has core dimension at most ⌊n/2⌋. Three pieces. THE TRINOMIAL LEMMA: a 0/1 trinomial whose exponents are not collinear is irreducible over the complex numbers — a monomial change of variables writes it Xm(1 + Xa) + Xm′Yc, Eisenstein at a root of 1 + Xa; indecomposability of the hull alone would not do, a simplex being the Minkowski sum of two of its own homothets, and at n = 2 a segment is indecomposable while 1 + x³ factors. THE POLYTOPE HALF, the literature's: a d-polytope with fewer than 2d vertices is indecomposable, and a decomposable one with 2d or fewer is a prism over a simplex (Kallay 1979; Przesławski and Yost 2016). THE CLEAN-TRIANGLE LEMMA: a d-polytope carrying fewer than 2d marked points, its vertices among them, has a triangular 2-face marked only at its vertices — a simplex's C(d+1, 3) triangles outnumber the (d−2)(d−1) its at most d − 2 extra points can spoil, and any other polytope has a facet missing two marked points, induction ending at a polygon marked at three points or fewer. So with n < 2d terms the hull is indecomposable, a factorization's hulls are homothets of it, and the clean triangle's face polynomial factors a non-collinear 0/1 trinomial. The intermediate bounds n − 2 (n ≥ 3, the hull a simplex) and n − 3 (n ≥ 5) are the same argument with the triangle found by hand. The bound is tight at every n ≥ 4: (1 − x + x²)(A + Σ yiBi) with each (1 − x + x²)A and (1 − x + x²)Bi one of 1 + x³, 1 + x² + x⁴ — {2, 16, 6, 24, 96} at size 5 and dimension 2, {2, 16, 6, 48, 10, 80, 14, 112} at size 8 and dimension 4 — which is exactly what a NONNEGATIVE split gives by the term-count law, ab terms at dimension at most a + b − 2 ≤ ab/2, a prism over a simplex at equality: cancellation never beats the prism.

Scope. Proved, resting on the cited polytope theorem for its indecomposability step; the attaining family is a construction. Checked at 0 reducible classes above ⌊n/2⌋ over every menu of sizes 5, 6 and 9 in {2..24} and 7, 8 and 10 in {2..20}, 31,521 classes at dimension 5 of size 9 alone.

verifiers: explore_seed_rank_law.py, explore_seed_rank_nine.py

What the ceiling places observation

Dimension at most 2 at four and five members, so the seed side of every three-against-four collision is capped at every bound and size 5 holds no dimension-3 seed by law; dimension 3 is first reached at six members, where 6 = 2·3 is where the term-count law reaches it — (1 + x³)(1 + y + z), the menu {2, 6, 10, 16, 48, 80} — and the census then finds dimension 3 the MAJORITY of six-member seeds, 46 of 55 in {2..24} and 177 of 203 in {2..32}. A negative factor of dimension 2 exists, the full-dimension witness's seed core being (x₀ + 1)·(x₀²x₁ + x₀² − x₀x₁ + x₁² + x₁), where the earlier several-variable negatives were one-variable images; and at eight members in {2..20} every negative factor of the five dimension-4 seeds is multivariate of one shape, x₂³ − x₂² + x₂ + L with L a sum of single terms in the other primes' variables.

Scope. The placements follow from the theorem; the counts and the shapes are observations over the boxes named. Toy scale.

The negative factor's torsion

Every negative factor written out above is 1 − x + x², which vanishes at a primitive sixth root of unity. Call a factor torsion-rooted when it vanishes at some tuple of roots of unity — a torsion point of the torus — and torsion-free when it vanishes at none; call a seed rooted or free by its negative factor, and mixed where its core carries one of each. Along a line, where the core is a polynomial in one variable, torsion-rooted means carrying a cyclotomic factor. The split is decidable: a factor sees a torsion point only through the lattice its exponent differences span, so it is rewritten in a basis of that lattice, and Mann's bound on minimal vanishing sums of roots of unity — in such a sum of r terms, normalized to contain 1, every root has order dividing the product of the primes up to r — makes one finite search over that lattice's torsion decisive.

Along a line, the free kind is generic rule

The least size of a free seed is 3: a two-member core is 1 plus a monomial, every factor of which is cyclotomic, and 1 + x⁴ + x⁵ = (x² + x + 1)(x³ − x + 1) — the plastic number's polynomial in −x beside the cube roots' — is the menu {2, 32, 64}, one of the five three-member seeds of {2..64}. Ljunggren and Tverberg, a reducible 0/1 trinomial's non-cyclotomic part being irreducible or 1, make that the trinomial rule: a three-member seed is free exactly when that part is negative, 98 of the 435 reducible trinomials to degree 30. The least DEGREE of a free seed is 5 — none at 4 or below, four at 5 — five terms first reach one at degree 7, 1 + x + x³ + x⁴ + x⁷ = (1 + x + x² + x³ + x⁴)(x³ − x² + 1), and FREE IS THE GENERIC KIND: from degree 7 on the free seeds outnumber the rooted at every degree, 7,966 against 767 at degree 16. The shape throughout is a cyclotomic cofactor beside a torsion-free negative factor; a census of 0/1 polynomials reducible with no cyclotomic factor at all, whose floor is degree 12 at seven terms, counts something else.

Scope. The size floor proved; the degree floor and the generic count exhaustive over every 0/1 polynomial with constant term 1 to degree 16, and the trinomial rule to degree 30 with the cited theorem re-verified on every reducible trinomial there.

In the plane, two free seeds among thirty-seven property

The swept boxes hold 37 seeds — 28 of six members in {2..24}, 9 of eight in {2..20}, none of five, seven or nine there — 35 rooted and TWO free: {2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 24}, core (1 + x)·[x(1 + x²) + y(1 − x + x²)], and its mirror {2, 3, 6, 12, 16, 24}, both of dimension 2. Both are free by hand: on the unit circle the bracket's two coefficients have moduli |2cos θ| and |2cos θ − 1|, a zero with |y| = 1 needs them equal, and they agree only at cos θ = ¼, where 2cos θ = ½ is no algebraic integer — so the factor meets the unit torus at a non-torsion point and meets no torsion point. Every seed in these boxes has the shape (1 + x)(A + L) with (1 + x)A and (1 + x)L both 0/1 — an even term count, which is why the odd sizes are empty there and not in general — and the split is whether L can vanish where A does. Of the 35 rooted, 14 have a zero on a coordinate axis of the lattice and 21 only off the axes, and 20 contain a whole torsion line.

Scope. The two witnesses are free by the proof given and by the complete search; the counts are observations over the five boxes named, every seed decided at order 6. Toy scale.

Four members are closed off every line: the cofactor theorem theorem

Every four-term 0/1 polynomial whose core is reducible over the integers, in any number of variables, is divisible by a binomial 1 + u — and every negative factor of a non-collinear four-member seed vanishes at a torsion point. A four-term core has a torsion zero only where its monomials split into two antipodal pairs, a vanishing sum of four roots of unity having no other shape by Mann's bound, so the question is read off the exponent vectors alone. Dimension 3 is irreducible outright, a facet trinomial being irreducible by the trinomial lemma. Along a line Mills's theorem — the non-cyclotomic part of xn ± xm ± xp ± 1 is irreducible but for four forms, none all-plus — gives a reducible core a cyclotomic factor, and the pairing at its root makes it a 1 + xd. In the plane a factorization specialized along y → xK for every large K hands one factor infinitely many torsion points (Hajós bounding a root's multiplicity below the term count), the Ihara–Serre–Tate theorem puts a torsion coset — the locus w = ζ of one monomial and one root of unity — on it, and a pairing holding at a coset's torsion points forces the shape (1 + w)(m₁qs(w) + m₂qt(w)) with qn the alternating sum 1 − w + ⋯ + wn−1, s and t odd. Off a line the ratio m₂/m₁ is independent of w; the bracket's non-cyclotomic factor is then irreducible by Capelli's criterion, read over the rational functions of w's line and carried to the integers by Gauss's lemma, and it vanishes at the character sending w to 1 and the ratio to −1. Along a line that character can be gone, and the same shape is free at degree 5, 1 + x³ + x⁴ + x⁵ = (1 + x)(1 − x + x² + x⁴). So a free seed of the plane has at least five members: three are collinear and four are closed by the theorem.

Scope. Proved in every number of variables, resting on Mills, Hajós, Ihara–Serre–Tate and Capelli as cited, with Mann's bound supplying the pairing. The 43 non-collinear four-member seeds of {2..32} are the census the theorem was read from — 41 with bracket m₁ + m₂(1 − u + u²), two with core a binomial times 1 + u³ — and the box checks beside them are checks of a proved statement: every four-point configuration of [0, 6]² up to translation (1,921 reducible, each with a binomial factor), [0, 2]³ at dimension 3 (none reducible), the line to degree 60 (14,825 seeds, each with a binomial factor).

Five members: a free factor beside a rooted one theorem

A five-member core can carry a torsion-free negative factor, and everywhere the plane has been searched a rooted factor stands beside it. The menu {2, 6, 16, 96, 1536} has core 1 + x³ + y(1 + x⁴ + x⁸) = (1 − x + x²)·[(1 + x) + y(1 + x + x²)(x⁴ − x² + 1)], the bracket linear in y with coprime coefficients and so irreducible, and torsion-free by hand: on the unit circle, with t = 2 + 2cos θ, the two coefficients' moduli agree only where t = (t − 1)²(t² − 4t + 1)², a sextic irreducible over the integers with two non-real roots, so no root of it is totally real while 2 + 2cos(2πk/n) always is; the factor meets the unit torus at the sextic's real roots and meets no torsion point. Beside it stands 1 − x + x², rooted: the seed is mixed. And the mixed shape is FORCED at five members, THE ROW LEMMA: 5 is prime, so any factorization is a seed with values ±1 and ±5 at the all-ones point; a core with a cyclotomic factor has, in coordinates with w = xd, rows of sizes 2 and 3 in x each vanishing at a primitive n-th root, so 6 | n, the rows' common factor G is a product of cyclotomics of such orders — value 1 at the all-ones point, hence carrying a negative coefficient, a rooted negative factor — and the core is G·(A′ + ykB′) with the bracket irreducible of value 5, by Capelli past k = 1 since A′ and B′ take the coprime values 2 and 3. So the rooted factor sits on the value-1 side and any free one on the value-5 side, and a five-member seed with EVERY negative factor free carries no cyclotomic factor at all — the shape a line first reaches at degree 12 with seven terms.

Scope. The exhibit is a proof, the sextic's irreducibility and root count computed exactly and the same factor read free by the complete torsion test; the row lemma is proved at five members on Mann's bound and Capelli's criterion. The box [0, 8]² holds 130 seeds among 25,621,596 five-point subsets: 0 free outright, 33 mixed — every one the identity above in some direction — and 97 rooted, complete there. Open: whether a size-5 seed is ever free outright — by the row lemma such a core has no cyclotomic factor at all. On the line such a seed would answer, in the negative, the question Filaseta and Solan left open (Math. Scand. 84, 1999): they proved that a 0/1 pentanomial, five terms, with its reciprocal irreducible factors removed — a polynomial is reciprocal when it equals its own coefficient reversal xnf(1/x) — is 1 or irreducible, and asked whether "reciprocal" can read "cyclotomic", which asks more than the absence of a free size-5 seed, since it also forbids a cyclotomic factor beside two non-cyclotomic ones. The census reads yes to degree 70: the non-cyclotomic part of every 0/1 pentanomial there is 1 or irreducible (rule, exhaustive), and every proper non-cyclotomic reciprocal factor seen sits inside a reciprocal pentanomial beside a cyclotomic one.
