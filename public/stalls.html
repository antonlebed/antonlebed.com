Stalls

Where an exact descent stops short of the bottom, including where no measurement tie sits anywhere near it: which stall species exist, what cures each, what a stall is made of, and why every one of them is a fact about the size of a single step.

The thing being descended is a commitment policy over a cover of intervals (descent in reader space), and what follows states what it needs of one. A stream is the continued-fraction expansion of a real number, arriving digit by digit, and a map is a function applied to it. After n digits the value is pinned to a cylinder, and what the reader is certain of about the output is the image interval, that cylinder's picture under the map; these nest and shrink as digits arrive. A row is one (map, stream) pair.

What a reader commits to are cells drawn from one fixed family, the cover: the tree cells, the Stern–Brocot cells, each the set of positive reals whose expansion opens with a given run of digits, and the straddle chains standing at their vertices, a cell's vertex being the mediant of its two endpoints, the fraction whose numerator and denominator are their sums. Both kinds are graded by rank, which steps by one along every child relation (the redundant cover). A commitment is a ratchet, never undone and permanently sound, since a cell containing the current image contains every later one. What a policy chooses is the route past a vertex — the tree child or the chain — and how long to wait before committing: four coordinates, a route preference at each of the two cell kinds and a patience for each. A kind at patience p commits a candidate only once it has contained the image for p+1 consecutive steps; patience 0 is greedy, committing on sight, and patience ∞ refuses that kind outright. A kind's reference is the image interval its patience makes a candidate contain, the one from p steps back, so a policy with equal patience on both kinds reads one reference at both, and a patience of 0 is pinned to the present image, doubly pinned when both are.

Two exact rulers price a policy over a counted window, a fixed span of input steps, the same for every policy compared, its length the horizon. The deficit at a step is the scale the reader is short — writing a cell's scale for ln(1/length), the image interval's scale less the committed cell's — and the clock ruler is that same lag counted in whole steps. Both compare by cross-multiplying big integers, so no float enters any decision. Descent is the matching finite-graph notion: a move is taken only when it strictly lowers the loss, the descent fixing one ruler and running against it alone, and a stall is a policy with no strictly better neighbour. Descent runs on the behavioural quotient — policies identified when their committed-cell traces over the window agree, since two such policies cannot be ordered by any loss reading that window — and its classes are what descent moves between, a class's members being its policies and its standing its position in the landscape's loss order, counted from the bottom. Resources, where in force, enter as settings rather than as coordinates: a rank budget B of cover-rank units per input step and a bank of capacity W holding what a step does not spend, against which the policy gains a fifth coordinate, its drawdown, for how much of the bank a single step may draw. A setting with neither in force is unresourced, and there the four coordinates are all of it. A landscape is one loss surface, a row and a setting fixed; the composite order on one reads the clock loss first and the deficit as its tiebreak.

The move set is the unit step: neighbouring policies differ by one notch in one coordinate, or by both route preferences flipped together. A stall is a fact about that move set before it is a fact about a landscape, and every stall named below is a reader who may change patience only one notch at a time; what a wider step does to them is the move-set law.

The spine theorem theorem

The spine is the policies whose two kinds share one reference: equal patience on both, unresourced, every patience shorter than the counted window's start. On the spine, lexicographic deficit descent on the behavioural quotient stalls nowhere off the bottom, which is greedy: the cover cells containing an interval have an inclusion minimum, that minimum is monotone in its interval, so committed cells nest pointwise along the patience diagonal and the deficit descends strictly across distinct classes, the refuser last. The patience bound is load-bearing — a patience past the window start leaves infinite counted cells, and a finite-part tie becomes designable. Off the spine the statement "exact learning cannot stall except by ties" is false: a measurement tie is a pair of neighbouring policies whose exact losses read equal, and the burst trap under a budget and the horizon-cut stall with none are both genuine hills with no measurement tie anywhere in their neighbourhoods, so neither ties nor resources are what stalling is made of.

Scope. Proved for this cover and move set, at any nested strictly shrinking stream, any horizon, and every finite patience below the counted-window start. The two off-spine counterexamples are the census's own specimens.

The decision trade and its assembly rule

What an unresourced off-spine stall must satisfy is a condition on route decisions (the decision lemma), and a decision alone, with no conserved budget behind it, can buy a late gain with an early loss. Across three designed batteries, unresourced, 10,174 pairs of policies one move apart carry a first differing counted step and a total deficit pointing opposite ways — the side worse where the traces part ends strictly ahead — and 4,114 of them hold equal patience on both ends, so the two runs read identical references at every step and the whole divergence is the two route bits. The mechanism: the early winner's own next subdivision point sits inside the shrinking references, so its counted lengths freeze while the other side keeps refining. The cleanest witness pays 3.244 in the scale's log units at its divergence step, collects 6.415 at each of the next three while the early winner freezes, and the traces re-merge 15.9995 apart; nothing is conserved anywhere in it, the only coupling between steps being the ratchet's position. A budget manufactures not the trade but its assembly into a local minimum, and no budget is needed for the assembly either: the horizon-cut specimens are unresourced, and the stall-free censuses before them — 348 distinct unresourced worlds with zero stalls and zero adjacent value ties — were a property of their batteries, generic streams, long counts and slow maps. A stall is an assembled trade held at the cut: the counted window ends inside the trade, the late win stands, and it beats whatever neighbours remain.

Scope. The trade's existence a rule, an exact-arithmetic witness re-derived by hand; its abundance and the desert observations over the three batteries and the 348 worlds. Toy scale.

The slow-map refusal observation

Of the battery's three legs — generic streams, long counts, slow maps — the slow map is the one an adversary without a budget cannot buy back; the burst trap already buys the doubling map back with one. Widen the digit range to 256 and take horizons 9 to 20: the identity and doubling maps refuse across about 2,800 distinct censused landscapes and 70,000 adversarial search evaluations, under the same detector that reproduces every squaring-map specimen. A large digit buys one step of shrink under any map, but only the squaring map compounds it along the window, so the best slow-map stall margin — the signed gap, in the scale's log units, by which a class's best neighbour fails to improve on it, positive exactly at a stall — walks to zero like the reciprocal of the digit cap at the shortest horizons and never crosses, each added pair of counted steps costing about 0.7 more.

Scope. An adversarial search at the stated digit caps and horizons, so an observation and not a law about slow maps. Toy scale.

The one-cell wall and the domination law theorem

Behind the walk to zero stands a wall. Take any class holding a one-move neighbour whose committed cells sit nested-or-equal at every counted step — the domination condition. What nesting buys is a margin, and the margin is at least one cover refinement: such a class keeps a neighbour better by at least the price of one step down the cover, from a cell to a child of it, so the best margin an adversary can forge against it is at most −ln(1 + 1/R), R the binding cell's lopsidedness, the ratio of its endpoint denominators, the other counted steps cancelling or absent. The two frontier records of the refusal meet the bound with equality: at doubling with digit cap 64, R ≈ 128.5 against the cap's bound of 132, the binding edge one mediant step and the other counted step's cells coinciding; at identity with cap 256, R ≈ 256 against 258, a single counted step by construction. The reciprocal walk is the wall's own asymptotic. The domination condition is the hypothesis, and it is measured: the identity map holds it at every one of 9,401 classes; the doubling map's alignment shift dents it at 632, of which 540 keep a neighbour merely shorter step for step and 92 lose only on aggregate, the witnesses in burst, head-period and spike worlds; and the squaring map breaks it at every stall specimen, each neighbour conceding somewhere inside the window.

Scope. The wall proved for this cover, conditional on domination. Domination is exhaustive at scope under the identity map — every off-bottom finite-loss class of the identity battery at horizons 9, 10, 12 and 16, 9,401 classes, zero exceptions — and measured, not derived, under the other two. Toy scale.

The move-set law rule

A stall is "no neighbour stands lower" and an escape radius is "fewest edges to a better class", so both are facts about the edge set before they are facts about a landscape, and the edge set here moves patience one notch at a time on the axis 0, 1, 2, 3, ∞. Widen that one parameter and the stalls go: at a two-notch step nine of the ten stop stalling, each gaining a strictly better neighbour; at three the count is zero over all 601 off-bottom classes of the ten landscapes; a full clique adds nothing past distance 3. Stalls are monotone under adding edges, and only 6 of the burst battery's 448 landscapes hold one under the unit step, so none holds one at three notches either. Every dissolution is a single downward patience step with everything else fixed, none reaching refusal. The mechanism is the floor theorem read at step size: all ten sit with one patience at 0, so the floor — the cell the doubly pinned policy commits, the inclusion minimum of the cover cells containing the current image — is one patience jump away and strictly better in deficit, and the run supplies what the theorem does not: the standing in the composite order falls too, at all 13 stall members, and the floor's class stands strictly lower at 1,328 of 1,328 members of the classes whose floor is a distinct class. What makes a unit-step reader stop is that standing is non-monotone in patience: the classes between it and the floor stand worse than its own. Patience is not the whole alphabet — 30 of the 601 classes have no improving patience jump at any distance and are cured only by a route flip or a drawdown move — so what the widening dissolves is the stalls, never the dependence on the other coordinates. The last stall to fall, the burst trap on the stream of ones with one spike at digit 6, differs from its control — the trap on the stream spiking at digit 12, identical in every recorded coordinate — at one cell: the same five standings are reached along each patience axis, and the stream flips which of the adjacent pair 8 and 9 the specimen holds. Why an adjacent inversion should also cost a member-realisability failure is open.

Scope. Exhaustive over the ten landscapes' 601 off-bottom classes and 1,362 members, resourced and unresourced, the clique result derived from the monotonicity; the corpus's two move-set definitions coincide at eight of the ten specimens and agree at the other two, and the preference diagonal moves no stall and no radius anywhere. Toy scale.

The escape radius observation

A stall's escape radius is the length of the shortest improving move sequence out of it, and the answer depends on who counts. Through the cure graph — whose edge between two classes exists as soon as some policy in the first carries the move — all ten finite-loss stalls have radius exactly 2. Counted as one reader's own moves, nine of the ten agree and the tenth needs 3: at the burst trap on the stream of ones with one spike at digit 6, under doubling at horizon 120 and budget 2, the one intermediate class supporting a two-edge walk is entered and left at disjoint member sets. The quotient's free re-selection of member does not shorten it — one re-selection plus two moves is three, and three moves reach a better class directly. Off the stalls that freeness is a discount, and a small one: over the 601 off-bottom classes the per-member count spreads at 8, all large classes near the bottom at class radius 1, where re-selection is worth up to two moves, always to the unlucky member and never to the best one. Start selection is load-bearing at no stall. An escape is not a refinement: at the three unresourced horizon-cut stalls, 16 of the 40 (stall member, escape member) pairs hold, at some counted step, a cell not inside the one the stall committed there, so no reader that only refines takes them.

Scope. Measured over the ten finite-loss stalls under each reading of the count, at the unit patience step; the discount over the 601 classes of their landscapes. Toy scale.

verifiers: explore_charged_radius.py, explore_four_prints.py

Every stall is pinned rule

Two-move lookahead is a proved cure wherever an off-bottom finite-loss class holds a policy with both patiences finite and positive whose counted window still refines (the shift telescope). No stall in the census holds one: every one of the ten has a patience pinned to the current image — the three squaring-map stalls at the chain, the ruler disagreement and all six burst traps at the tree — and no species straddles the split. The theorem is therefore about the classes that do not stall, and nothing forces that: a shiftable policy escapes at distance two while stalling is a statement about distance one. Outside that frame is not outside a cure. The floor theorem reaches every member of every off-bottom finite-loss class short of the floor itself, the ten stalls included; what is open is the radius, never the existence of the cure, and what the cure is — the doubly pinned floor at a policy's own route preferences, and what freshening one reference cures short of it — is nesting's.

Scope. Exhaustive over the ten finite-loss stalls at the unit patience step. Toy scale.

Nesting

What decides whether a lowered patience improves anything at all is whether the two readers' committed cells nest. Where no resource cap is in force, a greedy reader commits the floor of the current image — the inclusion minimum of the cover cells containing it, a closed form that reads the image and runs no policy — and that floor sits inside every cell any policy commits, which is why a strictly better class exists from every class off the bottom at finite loss, with no map fence and no patience hypothesis on it. Which single moves nest is measured, and the answer is narrow: patience-down moves and nothing else. One corner is proved — a run preferring the chain at both kinds, under the identity map — and that proof's hypothesis is two inequalities on the indices of the references a run reads at the one step where it can leave a chain for the cell the chain sits in. Off the spine, nesting dies only where the commit loop had a route preference to consult, though not at every such place: nesting.
