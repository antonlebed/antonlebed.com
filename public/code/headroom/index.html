headroom.py

Checks: HEADROOM: The headroom property; HEADROOM: The ledger theorem; HEADROOM: The premium in closed form theorem; HEADROOM: Silence lands in a blind class theorem; rule; HEADROOM: The closure theorem; HEADROOM: The resolution criterion criterion; HEADROOM: The non-divisor bound theorem; observation; HEADROOM: No blindness is permanent theorem; observation; HEADROOM: The watcher's guaranteed blindness theorem; HEADROOM: The blind-density theorem theorem; HEADROOM: The least resolver is unbounded theorem.

Cited on: HEADROOM.

To run it: save headroom.py, with growth.py beside it; standard library only.

"""headroom.py -- what a probe learns of a grown state by offering it
moves: the headroom xi(M) = W(lambda(M))/M, what a move spends of it and
what it opens, the closure underneath, what it costs to tell apart two
states the reading merges, and the density of the probe's blind classes.

QUESTION. growth.py grows Z/M by the cheapest move a demand admits. The
demands that read lambda (the transparency demand and its complement)
answer one bit per offered move m: does lambda(Mm) equal lambda(M)?
What does that family of answers see of a state, how does the thing it
sees move under a move, and how coarse is it: which states does it
merge, what does it cost to split them, and how many are merged?

THE OBJECT. lambda is the Carmichael function and W(L) the wall, the
largest N with lambda(N) | L (growth.py, where the wall is Adams'
number). The HEADROOM of a state M is xi(M) = W(lambda(M))/M, an
integer since lambda(M) | lambda(M). A move m sends M to Mm; it is
TRANSPARENT when lambda(Mm) = lambda(M). The PROBE is the family of
lambda-reading demands; its READING of M is xi(M). Two distinct states
with one reading are a BLIND PAIR, and the states sharing a reading,
when two or more do, a BLIND CLASS. Along a move, the WALL GAIN is
Gamma(M, m) = W(lambda(Mm))/W(lambda(M)), the BURN is gcd(m, xi(M)),
the UNPAID PART is u(M, m) = m/gcd(m, xi(M)), and the PREMIUM is
G(M, m) = Gamma(M, m)/u(M, m). A move m RESOLVES a blind pair
(M1, M2) when xi(M1 m) != xi(M2 m); R is the set of resolvers;
kappa(xi) is the least integer >= 2 not dividing xi.

THE ARGUMENT (written before the engine).
  (1) THE HEADROOM. {M : lambda(M) | L} is closed under lcm, so every
      member divides W(L). lambda(M) | lambda(Mm) always; so m is
      transparent iff Mm | W(lambda(M)) iff m | xi(M). A lambda-reading
      demand's whole response to M is the indicator of the divisors of
      xi(M): the reading is one number. By the wall's closed form, at
      an odd prime l with (l - 1) | lambda (a DOOR of lambda),
      v_l(xi) = v_l(lambda) + 1 - v_l(M); at a non-door odd l, v_l(xi)
      = v_l(M) = 0 (l | M forces (l - 1) | lambda); at 2, v_2(xi) =
      v_2(lambda) + 2 - v_2(M) at even lambda and 1 - v_2(M) at odd.
      Neither the reading nor the support determines the other: 855 and
      2565 share the support {3, 5, 19} and read 161616 and 53872; 10
      and 11 read 24 on the supports {2, 5} and {11}.
  (2) THE LEDGER. xi(Mm) = xi(M) Gamma/m. Mm divides its own wall
      Gamma W(lambda(M)) = Gamma xi(M) M, so m | Gamma xi; prime by
      prime, v_l(u) = max(0, v_l(m) - v_l(xi)) <= v_l(Gamma). So G is
      an integer and
          xi(Mm) = (xi(M)/gcd(m, xi(M))) G(M, m):
      the burn is truncated subtraction in the exponents, and a move
      never destroys more headroom than it spends. SPLITTING: Gamma
      telescopes, Gamma(M, ab) = Gamma(M, a) Gamma(Ma, b), so
          G(M, a) G(Ma, b) = G(M, ab) k,  k = u(M, ab)/(u(M, a) u(Ma, b))
      inverted, and k is an integer: with x = v_l(xi(M)), a_l, b_l the
      exponents and g_l = v_l(G(M, a)), v_l(xi(Ma)) = x - min(a_l, x) +
      g_l, and min(a_l + b_l, x) <= min(a_l, x) + min(b_l, x -
      min(a_l, x) + g_l) in both cases a_l >= x and a_l < x. Equality
      holds iff at every l | b, g_l = 0 or b_l <= x - a_l. So a split
      move never undercounts the premium, and of the four parts only
      the wall gain is a function of the endpoints.
  (3) THE PREMIUM IN CLOSED FORM. lambda(Mm) = lcm(lambda(M),
      lambda(l^(c_l + e_l)) : l | m), c_l = v_l(M), e_l = v_l(m), and
      c_l = v_l(W(lambda(M))) - v_l(xi(M)), whose first term is a
      function of lambda(M). So G(M, m) is a function of lambda(M), m
      and the exponents v_l(xi(M)) at the primes l of m, and of
      nothing else; at a move prime to M every c_l is 0 and G is a
      function of lambda(M) and m. The exponents are needed: lambda(3) =
      lambda(8) = 2, and m = 3 has premium 7 at 3 (xi = 8) and 1 at 8
      (xi = 3). The primes of a move interact: at lambda = 1, 5 opens
      the doors 3, 5, 7 opens 3, 7, and 35 opens 3, 5, 7 and 13, since
      13 - 1 = 12 takes its 4 from lambda(5) and its 3 from lambda(7).
      On a prime q not dividing M the wall gain's odd part is the
      COHORT (odd p newly doors of lambda' = lambda(Mq), each at
      v_p(lambda') + 1) times the BUMPS at the old odd doors, each at
      v_p(lambda') - v_p(lambda). From the state 1 the cohort of q is
      {p odd prime : (p - 1) | (q - 1)}.
  (4) SILENCE. A prime q not dividing M with (q - 1) not dividing
      lambda = lambda(M) is SILENT at M when G(M, q) = 1: the reading
      does not move while lambda does. Then u = q and W(L') = q W(lambda),
      L' = lcm(lambda, q - 1). Every door of L' other than q is a door
      of lambda at the same exponent, and v_2(L') = v_2(lambda); so
      every door p != q of q - 1 has (p - 1) | g = gcd(q - 1, lambda)
      and v_p(q - 1) <= v_p(lambda), whence v_p(q - 1) = v_p(g), and
      v_2(q - 1) = v_2(g).
      Hence W(q - 1) = q W(g): every prime silent at M lies in the
      blind class xi = W(g). At lambda = 2 silence is exactly membership
      of the class xi = 24. At odd lambda nothing is silent: W's 2-part
      is 2 and a prime q > 2 sends it to at least 8. Conversely every
      prime q = 1 (mod lambda) with xi(q) = W(lambda), q not dividing
      M, is silent at M. Off that class lie the silent q with lambda
      not dividing q - 1: since lambda(W(lambda)) = lambda at lambda =
      lambda(M) ((5), idempotence) and lambda(W(g)) | g, W(g) !=
      W(lambda) at every proper divisor g, so they read another class.
  (5) THE CLOSURE. M -> M* = W(lambda(M)) is inflationary (M | M*),
      monotone (M | M' gives lambda(M) | lambda(M'), and W is monotone
      under divisibility) and idempotent (lambda(M*) = lambda(M): one
      way by M | M*, the other by the definition of W). The fixed
      points are the TOMBSTONES, xi = 1, and every W(L) is one. A
      tombstone above 2 is divisible by 24 (lambda even, so 2^3 and the
      door 3); 2 is the only tombstone with odd lambda. The tombstones
      are one infinite blind class: W(2^k) >= 2^(k+2).
  (6) BLINDNESS AND ITS CURE. A blind pair has different lambdas, since
      M = W(lambda)/xi. A divisor d of xi keeps the pair blind (both
      read xi/d). RESOLUTION CRITERION: d resolves iff the wall gains
      differ, since xi(M_i d) = xi Gamma_i/d. So every resolver is a
      non-divisor of xi, and the least one is at least kappa(xi), a
      prime power (a least non-divisor p^a r, r > 1 prime to p, has
      both parts dividing xi, hence their product). NO PERMANENT
      BLINDNESS: a prime q = 1 (mod lcm(lambda_1, lambda_2)) not
      dividing M_1 M_2 (Dirichlet) sends both lambdas to q - 1; the
      walls agree and the readings W(q - 1)/(M_i q) differ. The next
      reading is no function of the reading and the move: 2 and 24
      read 1, and 5 sends them to 24 and 2. On multipliers d prime to
      M_1 M_2, Gamma_i depends on d only through lambda(d), so R is a
      union of lambda-fibres there. A WATCHER sees the readings at
      M_i d for the divisors d of a move it is handed; the moves that
      keep a pair blind at every such reading are those whose divisors
      all miss R, a divisor-closed family containing every divisor of
      xi, so a pair survives at least tau(xi) - 1 moves with
      certainty. A move blind at its end but split inside (9 and 513,
      reading 56, under 57 = 3 19, split at 3) is a resolver whose
      multiple is not one: R is not an up-set.
  (7) THE BLIND-DENSITY THEOREM. For every even lambda, the primes
      q = 1 (mod lambda) with xi(q) = W(lambda) have a positive
      relative density among the primes. Within that progression the
      conditions read v_p(q - 1) <= v_p(lambda) at every door p of
      lambda and (p - 1) not dividing q - 1 at every odd prime p off
      them, p != q: all DECREASING events in the valuation vector
      (v_l(q - 1))_l. Under the prime measure the coordinates are
      independent (Dirichlet: the density of the residue class 1 mod
      D is prod 1/phi(l^a) over l^a || D), each a chain, so Harris's
      inequality gives P(E and F) >= P(E) P(F) for decreasing E, F.
      Write delta_T for the relative density, inside the progression,
      of the conditions at the doors and at the p <= T, a residue
      condition, and eps(T) for the upper density among all primes of
      the q with (p - 1) | (q - 1) for some prime p > T, p != q; inside
      the progression that event has relative upper density at most
      phi(lambda) eps(T). The class share lies in [delta_T -
      phi(lambda) eps(T), delta_T], and delta_T' >= delta_T (1 -
      phi(lambda) eps(T)) for T' > T. So if eps(T) -> 0 the class has
      density lim delta_T > 0. eps(T) << (log T)^(-c), uniformly in
      x, is Theorem 3 of Luca, Pizarro-Madariaga and Pomerance, "On
      the counting function of irregular primes", Indag. Math. 26
      (2015) 147-161, its two parameters at 1 and -1, and the
      lambda = 2 case of
      the theorem is Theorem 3 of Pomerance and Wagstaff, "The
      denominators of the Bernoulli numbers" (Acta Arith., doi
      10.4064/aa210601-16-11; arXiv:2105.13252) for the denominator
      6q, both read
      in full;
      theirs never caps a valuation, so the class at a general lambda
      is their set cut by a residue condition. The record's own
      derivation of the tail, redundant with the 2015 theorem, is
      Erdos and Wagstaff's Theorem 2 carried to the shifted primes:
      their three classes
      for q - 1 = (p - 1) n, against loglog p, with every count by
      Pollack's Theorem 1.1 ("Nonnegative multiplicative functions on
      sifted sets, and the square roots of -1 modulo shifted primes",
      Glasgow Math. J. 62 (2020) 187-199; a Shiu bound over a sifted
      set) applied
      to z^Omega on {n : dn + 1 prime}. The Chernoff exponents are
      d1 = 1/3 - (2/3) log(3/2) = 0.0630 for the "few factors" classes
      and eta = (7/6) log(7/6) - 1/6 = 0.0132 for the third, whose
      union over t_i = exp(i^(2/eta)) costs a square root, so eps(T)
      << (log T)^(-eta/2). The proof needs only d1 > 0.
      THE TAIL, step by step. q - 1 = (p - 1) n with p > T prime,
      p != q, n >= 2; L = loglog p; Omega_p(n) counts the prime
      factors of n below p with multiplicity. Every q counted by
      eps(T) falls in one of three classes. (1) Omega(p - 1) < (2/3)
      L. (2) Omega_p(n) < (2/3) L. (3) both at least (2/3) L, whence
      Omega_p(q - 1) >= (4/3) L, since every prime of p - 1 is below
      p. The tool is Pollack's Theorem 1.1: f multiplicative with
      f(l^v) <= A^v and f(n) <<_e n^e, E_l a union of at most k
      nonzero classes mod l for each prime l <= x, S the integers in
      no E_l; then the sum of f over S in (x - y, x] is << (y/log x)
      exp(sum_(l <= x) (f(l) - nu(l))/l) for x^a < y <= x. For the
      set {n : dn + 1 prime} take E_l = {-1/d} at l not dividing d:
      exp(-sum nu(l)/l) is the Brun-Titchmarsh 1/log x, and the l | d
      return at most d/phi(d). The Chernoff step takes f =
      z^Omega_p, z < 1 for "few factors", z > 1 for "many".
       (1) z = 2/3 on {n <= y : n + 1 prime}: #{p <= y : Omega(p - 1)
           < (2/3) loglog y} << y/(log y)^(1 + d1). Each such p
           carries pi(x; p - 1, 1) << x/(phi(p - 1) log(x/p)) primes
           q. Summed over p <= sqrt(x) by parts this is << pi(x)
           (loglog T)/(log T)^d1; over p > sqrt(x), dyadically in
           x/p, it is << pi(x) (loglog x)^2/(log x)^d1. Both vanish.
       (2) p <= sqrt(x), d = p - 1, X = x/d: z = 2/3 on {n <= X :
           dn + 1 prime} gives << (d/phi(d)) (X/log X) (log p)^(-d1)
           class-(2) primes, and the sum over p > T of 1/(phi(p - 1)
           (log p)^d1) tends to 0. For p > sqrt(x), n < sqrt(x) < p,
           so Omega(n) < (2/3) loglog x; the same tool with f = 1 on
           the two conditions p prime and n(p - 1) + 1 prime bounds
           each n's count by << (n/phi(n)) x/(n log^2 x), and the sum
           of 1/phi(n) over n <= sqrt(x) with Omega(n) < (2/3) loglog
           x is << (log x)^(1 - d1): total << pi(x)/(log x)^d1.
       (3) z = 7/6 on {n <= x : n + 1 prime} gives #{q <= x :
           Omega_t(q - 1) >= (7/6) loglog t} << pi(x) (log t)^(-eta),
           uniformly in t <= x. With t_i = exp(i^(2/eta)), a q with
           Omega_p(q - 1) >= (4/3) loglog p for some p in
           [t_(i-1), t_i) has Omega_(t_i)(q - 1) >= (7/6) loglog t_i
           once i is large, and sum over t_i > T of (log t_i)^(-eta)
           = sum i^(-2) is << (log T)^(-eta/2).
      This is Erdos and Wagstaff's proof of their Theorem 2 (Lemmas
      1 to 4) with each integer count replaced by its shifted-prime
      count. The dyadic sum in (1) costs a second loglog x, which
      changes nothing.
      COROLLARIES. The silent set at every even lambda has positive
      density ((4), converse). Every reading W(lambda), lambda even,
      hosts infinitely many blind pairs, so the least resolver is
      unbounded: along L_n = lcm(1, ..., n) every prime r <= n + 1 is
      a door (2 by the wall's 2-part) at an exponent with
      r^(v_r(W) + 1) > n + 1, so kappa(W(L_n)) > n + 1.
      The class xi = 24 is a Bernoulli class: by von Staudt-Clausen
      the denominator of B_(q-1) is the product of the p with
      (p - 1) | (q - 1), so for q >= 11 the class is exactly the primes
      with denom(B_(q-1)) = 6q; 5 and 7 have that denominator and read
      48 and 72.

A KILL is any identity violation, which kills the claim it tests; a
D2 gap above 2 10^-3, which kills the measure the density proof runs
on; a D3 inequality failing, which kills the positivity step. A
control out of its band voids the verdicts that lean on it. An
earlier run's figure not reproduced with the identities green is
a definition to read, not a kill.

SPF_N = 10 ** 7


def spf_sieve(n):
    """Smallest prime factor below n + 1, 0 at the primes: the primes up
    to sqrt(n) stamped from the largest down, so the least one wins."""
    r = int(n ** 0.5) + 1
    small = bytearray([1]) * (r + 1)
    for i in range(2, int(r ** 0.5) + 1):
        if small[i]:
            small[i * i::i] = bytearray(len(small[i * i::i]))
    s = array("i", [0]) * (n + 1)
    for p in range(r, 1, -1):
        if small[p] and p * p <= n:
            k = len(range(p * p, n + 1, p))
            s[p * p::p] = array("i", [p]) * k
    return s


SPF = None


def fac(n):
    """Factor n by the sieve below SPF_N, by trial division above."""
    if SPF is None or n > SPF_N:
        return factor_big(n)
    f = {}
    while n > 1:
        p = SPF[n] or n
        f[p] = f.get(p, 0) + 1
        n //= p
    return f


def factor_big(n):
    f = {}
    p = 2
    while p * p <= n:
        while n % p == 0:
            f[p] = f.get(p, 0) + 1
            n //= p
        p += 1 if p == 2 else 2
    if n > 1:
        f[n] = f.get(n, 0) + 1
    return f


def is_prime(n):
    if n < 2:
        return False
    if SPF is not None and n <= SPF_N:
        return SPF[n] == 0
    return growth.is_prime_mr(n)


def divisors_f(f):
    ds = [1]
    for p, e in f.items():
        ds = [d * p ** k for d in ds for k in range(e + 1)]
    return ds


@lru_cache(maxsize=None)
def lam_n(M):
    return value(lam(fac(M))) if M > 1 else 1


def wall_f(lf):
    """W(L) from L's factorization, doors found among L's divisors."""
    L = value(lf)
    if L % 2:
        return 2
    w = 2 ** (lf[2] + 2)
    for d in divisors_f(lf):
        p = d + 1
        if p > 2 and is_prime(p):
            w *= p ** (lf.get(p, 0) + 1)
    return w


@lru_cache(maxsize=None)
def W(L):
    return wall_f(fac(L)) if L > 1 else 2


@lru_cache(maxsize=None)
def xi(M):
    return W(lam_n(M)) // M


def gain(M, m):
    return W(lam_n(M * m)) // W(lam_n(M))


def unpaid(M, m):
    return m // gcd(m, xi(M))


def premium(M, m):
    g, u = gain(M, m), unpaid(M, m)
    assert g % u == 0
    return g // u


def vp(n, p):
    k = 0
    while n % p == 0:
        n //= p
        k += 1
    return k


def kappa(x):
    k = 2
    while x % k == 0:
        k += 1
    return k


def is_prime_power(n):
    return len(factor_big(n)) == 1


def unit_exponent(M):
    units = [a for a in range(1, M) if gcd(a, M) == 1] if M > 1 else [0]
    e = 1
    for a in units:
        o, x = 1, a % M
        while x != 1 % M:
            x = x * a % M
            o += 1
        e = e * o // gcd(e, o)
    return e

def premium_closed(L, x, m):
    """G from lambda(M) = L, m and v_l(xi) at the primes l of m alone."""
    lf = fac(L) if L > 1 else {}
    WL = W(L)
    newf = dict(lf)
    u = 1
    for l, e in fac(m).items():
        c = vp(WL, l) - vp(x, l)
        for p, a in (growth.lam_pp(l, c + e)).items():
            if a > newf.get(p, 0):
                newf[p] = a
        u *= l ** (e - min(e, vp(x, l)))
    return wall_f(newf) // (WL * u) if newf else 2 // (WL * u)

def silent(L, q):
    """q silent at a state of lambda L (q not dividing it): the door of
    q shut and the wall grows by exactly q."""
    if L % (q - 1) == 0:
        return False
    merged = growth.merge_max(fac(L) if L > 1 else {}, fac(q - 1))
    return wall_f(merged) == q * W(L)

def door_primes(n, cap):
    """The primes p <= cap, p != n + 1, with (p - 1) | n."""
    return [d + 1 for d in divisors_f(fac(n))
            if 2 <= d + 1 <= cap and d + 1 != n + 1 and is_prime(d + 1)]


def measure_p(l, a):
    """P(v_l(q - 1) >= a) over the prime measure."""
    if a == 0:
        return Fraction(1)
    return Fraction(1, (l - 1) * l ** (a - 1))


def delta_exact(ps):
    """The exact density of primes q with (p - 1) not dividing q - 1 for
    every p in ps, by enumerating the valuation vectors at the primes
    of the p - 1, truncated at the largest exponent any p - 1 needs."""
    need = [fac(p - 1) for p in ps]
    ls = sorted({l for f in need for l in f})
    top = {l: max(f.get(l, 0) for f in need) for l in ls}
    vecs = [({}, Fraction(1))]
    for l in ls:
        out = []
        for v, pr in vecs:
            for a in range(top[l] + 1):
                if a == top[l]:
                    pa = measure_p(l, a)
                else:
                    pa = measure_p(l, a) - measure_p(l, a + 1)
                if l == 2 and a == 0:
                    continue
                nv = dict(v)
                nv[l] = a
                out.append((nv, pr * pa))
        vecs = out
    tot = Fraction(0)
    for v, pr in vecs:
        if not any(all(v.get(l, 0) >= e for l, e in f.items())
                   for f in need):
            tot += pr
    return tot


def section_d(primes):
    section("D  THE DENSITY")
    B = growth.bernoulli(40)
    bern = all(B[2 * k].denominator == value(
        {p: 1 for p in [d + 1 for d in divisors_f(fac(2 * k))]

        bad += c != (vsc == 6 * q)

          "product of q - 1 is 6q, 11 <= q < 10^6; B_2..B_40 exact; 137 "
          "reads 480 at denominator 30q",
          bern and bad == 0 and e57 == (48, 72)
