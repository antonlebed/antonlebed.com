TOWER — the primorial rungs

The object: the rungs Z/p_k#, each the integers read through the first k primes and nothing else. This page states what a rung is and what it is missing, the first fourteen rungs, the transparency criterion that says when a rung adds a new element order, the fate rule that decides which properties the rungs gain or lose as channels accumulate, the birthday formula for element orders, the residue code every rung from k = 4 carries, the spectra of its Hamming graph and of its unitary graph, the escape law for a step from a non-unit, how λ, the unit group's exponent, grows (almost every rung leaves it alone, and below 10^16 each rung that moves it raises one prime power), and what the limit of the rungs keeps. The library is crt.py.

The rung

Tier: property.
Verifier: crt.py::self_test.

Write N = p_k# = p_1 p_2 ⋯ p_k for the product of the first k primes. The rung R_k = Z/p_k# is read through k channels, the reduction at each of its primes, and by the Chinese Remainder Theorem (the CRT) that reading is a ring isomorphism:

Z/p_k#  ≅  Z/p_1 × Z/p_2 × ⋯ × Z/p_k
Z/2  <  Z/6  <  Z/30  <  Z/210  <  Z/2310  <  Z/30030  <  Z/510510  <  ⋯

An element is a k-tuple of residues, and addition, multiplication and powering act channel by channel, no channel seeing another's content. At every rung, by construction: every channel is a prime field; the idempotents are exactly the 2^k elements that are 1 on a set of channels and 0 on the rest; the unit group is the product of the cyclic groups C_{p_i − 1}, of order φ(N) with φ Euler's totient, and of exponent λ(k) = lcm(p_1 − 1, …, p_k − 1); and powering by λ sends every element to the idempotent of its support, the set of channels where it is nonzero, so units go to 1 and every non-unit to the channels it lives on.

A rung reads the first k finite places and is missing everything else: the archimedean place, where size, sign and ordering live, every prime larger than p_k, and every higher level Z/p² at the primes it keeps. The rungs nest as blocks of channels, R_k's channels being the first k of R_{k+1}'s, which is all the display's < says; the ring map that keeps 1 runs the other way, reduction from Z/30 onto Z/6. The limit of the tower keeps every finite place: the product of F_p over all primes p.

Size, sign and the ordering of the integers are not channel quantities. Every claim in this page is a statement about channels, and about which primes they are, except two: the residue code's, whose guarantee is a bound on size, and the limit's embedding of the integers, which size certifies.

The transparency criterion

Tier: criterion.
Verifier: proof; tower_rungs.py::section_c.

Rung k ≥ 2 is transparent when λ(k) = λ(k − 1), and that holds iff (p_k − 1) divides λ(k − 1). Proof: λ(k) = lcm(λ(k − 1), p_k − 1). The element orders a rung realizes are exactly the divisors of λ (the birthday formula), so a transparent rung adds capacity — φ(N) multiplies by p_k − 1, the idempotents double — and no new order. At every other rung λ jumps, by λ(k)/λ(k − 1). The first transparent rung is k = 6, where p_6 = 13; through k = 14 the transparent rungs are 6, 11, 12, 13 and 14, the last four holding λ at the 55,440 that k = 10 set.

The fate rule

Tier: rule (proved; each row's fate read at its rungs, by brute force for k ≤ 6 and by the row's criterion to k = 12, the code's by its sieve to k = 10).
Verifier: tower_rungs.py::section_f.

Channels only accumulate: R_{k+1} has R_k's channels and one more. Call a property of a set of channels ∃-shaped when it passes to every larger set (some channel, or some subset of channels, carries it) and ∀-shaped when it passes to every smaller nonempty set. An ∃-shaped property is then born at the first rung that has it and holds at every later rung; a ∀-shaped one dies at the first rung that fails it and fails at every later rung, so the small primes are permanent vetoes. Where a property is born or dies is its fate. A conjunction of one of each is born at most once and, once dead, stays dead.

Every row of the chart but the code's is computed per rung by brute force over the ring's elements for k ≤ 6, where it is monotone in its direction, and by the row's criterion to k = 12, monotone as written; the code's row is read by a sieve over differences to k = 10, and is monotone too. A planted row that is neither is flagged by the same check. The chart's last row is the residue code with three parity channels (The residue code's distance), whose distance argument holds on any set of four or more primes, data on the smallest, so it is ∃-shaped and born at 4.

property                        shape            fate
field                           ∀                dies at 2
a square root of −1             ∀                dies at 2
cyclic unit group               ∀                dies at 3
zero divisors                   ∃                born at 2
idempotents beyond {0, 1}       ∃                born at 2
an element of order 4           ∃                born at 3
an element of order 3           ∃                born at 4
the distance-4 residue code     ∃                born at 4

A square root of −1 needs every odd channel to be 1 mod 4, so the prime 3 vetoes it at k = 2 for good. In a tower built the same way from 2, 5, 13, 17 and 29 instead, the rings Z/2, Z/10, Z/130 and so on, it exists at every stage, so the veto is the prime and not the rule. An element of order 4 is born at k = 3 and never dies, and none squares to −1: the order-4 elements of Z/30 are 7, 13, 17 and 23. The tower keeps an element of order 4 and discards the square root of −1.

The birthday formula

Tier: theorem; observation (the birthdays for m ≤ 200).
Verifier: proof; tower_rungs.py::section_b.

An element of multiplicative order m exists at rung k iff m divides λ(k), since the element orders of a finite abelian group are the divisors of its exponent (checked element by element for k ≤ 6). So for m ≥ 2 the first rung with one is

birthday(m)  =  max over prime powers q^a exactly dividing m of
                the rung of the least prime ≡ 1 (mod q^a)

where the rung of the prime p_k is R_k, rungs compared by k. Every order is eventually born, by Dirichlet's theorem, and the wait is the least prime in an arithmetic progression, which Linnik's theorem bounds by a fixed power of the modulus. The wait is rough: order 23 arrives at rung 15 and order 19 at rung 43, and the latest birthday for m ≤ 200 is order 197's, at the prime 3547, rung 497.

The residue code's distance

Tier: theorem.
Verifier: proof; tower_rungs.py::section_d; crt.py::code_correct; crt.py::code_syndrome.

On rung k with r parity channels, the codewords are the integers 0 ≤ n < p_{k−r}#, read through all k channels: the redundant residue number system with the smallest primes as data. Its minimum distance is exactly r + 1 at every k > r. Two codewords that agree on a channel set S differ by a multiple of the product of S, and that difference is below p_{k−r}#, which no product of k − r of the rung's primes is below; so they agree on at most k − r − 1 channels. The pair 0 and p_{k−r−1}# agrees on exactly that many. With the data on the largest primes instead the distance falls to 1 exactly when p_r# < p_k: k − 1 channels then have a product below the data range, and the pair 0 and p_{k−1}# agrees on them. At r = 3 it is 2 for k = 4..10, where 30 > p_k while 30 < p_{k−1}p_k lets 0 and p_{k−2}# agree on k − 2 channels, and 1 from k = 11 on; the sieve that counts, for every difference, the channels it vanishes on finds 2 at k = 5..8, the argument carrying k = 4, 9 and 10, and 1 with r = 1 at k = 3..7 and with r = 2 at k = 4..8.

With r = 3 the distance is 4 from k = 4 on, the data range Z/2 at k = 4: any one wrong channel is corrected while any two are detected, a decoder that corrects nothing detects any three, and any k − 3 channels reconstruct. What k = 7 adds is only the rate, the fraction (k − 3)/k of channels that carry data, passing 1/2.

At r ≥ 2 the syndrome tells a data error from a parity error and names the wrong parity channel; at r = 1 it only detects. Read the integer off the data channels and check it against each parity channel. A wrong data channel p_i moves that integer by (p_{k−r}#/p_i)·t with 0 < |t| < p_i, and every parity prime is coprime to the product and larger than p_i, so every one of the r checks fails; a wrong parity channel fails only its own. With the data on the largest primes instead, some data errors pass a check: with M the data product, setting the data channel p_i of the codeword 0 to the residue of (M/p_i)·p_j, for a parity prime p_j < p_i, makes the data read (M/p_i)·p_j, below M and 0 at p_j.

The guarantee is a bound on size: an integer is a codeword because it is small, which re-imports the archimedean place the rung is missing. It is the one property of a rung on this page whose statement needs the size of its elements, and ring arithmetic does not keep integers small, so the sum or product of two codewords need not be one.

The Hamming spectrum

Tier: rule (proved for every k ≥ 4; the base cases k = 4, 5 computed).
Verifier: tower_rungs.py::section_h.

The rung's Hamming graph joins two residues that differ in exactly one channel, the metric the residue code's distance counts in. At every k ≥ 4 it has exactly T_k − 5 distinct eigenvalues, where T_k is the sum of the first k primes.

The graph is the Cartesian product of the complete graphs K_p, one per channel. K_p has eigenvalue p − 1 on the constant vector and −1 on the p − 1 dimensions summing to zero, and a Cartesian product's eigenvalues are sums with one term per factor. So each set S of channels gives the eigenvalue Δ − σ(S), where Δ = Σ (p − 1) is the degree and σ(S) the sum of the primes in S. Each S contributes the product of p − 1 over S to its eigenvalue's multiplicity, and sets with the same sum share one eigenvalue. The distinct eigenvalues are the distinct subset sums of the first k primes.

Those sums fill [0, T_k] but for 1, 4, 6 and their reflections T_k − 6, T_k − 4, T_k − 1. The set W_k of sums is closed under v ↦ T_k − v, by taking complements. Adding the next prime p_{k+1} gives W_k ∪ (W_k + p_{k+1}), which covers [7, T_{k+1} − 7] as soon as W_k covers [7, T_k − 7] and p_{k+1} ≤ T_k − 13; below 7 nothing changes, since p_{k+1} ≥ 13, and the top follows by the reflection. That step inequality holds at k = 5 (13 ≤ 15), and if it holds at k then T_{k+1} − 2p_{k+1} = T_k − p_{k+1} ≥ 13, so Bertrand's postulate, p_{k+2} < 2p_{k+1}, carries it to k + 1. What keeps the spectrum full is the primes' size: each new prime is small against the sum of the ones before it.

At k = 7 there are 53 distinct eigenvalues, the least −7 with multiplicity φ(510510) = 92160. At k = 3 the missing set is {1, 4, 6, 9}, the same union with 4 and 6 reflecting onto each other, so only the count needs k ≥ 4. The script checks an eigenvector for each set S against the adjacency read off the residue tuples at k = 3 and 4, and there the whole spectrum with its multiplicities by counting closed walks, the missing sums at every k = 3..60, and the step inequality to k = 1999, after a control set, {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 97}, whose last prime breaks the inequality and leaves 74 sums between 7 and 118 (its total 125, less 7) missing.

The unitary spectrum

Tier: theorem (the eigenvalues, the criterion for a shared one, the recursion and the doubling rung); observation (the doubling rung's converse, to k = 24; the ratio, at k = 2..20 and 24).
Verifier: proof; tower_rungs.py::section_u.

The rung's unitary graph joins two residues whose difference is a unit, so two residues that differ in every channel: the tensor product of the complete graphs K_p, where the Hamming graph was their Cartesian product. Its eigenvalues are therefore products with one factor per channel, p − 1 or −1, where the Hamming graph's were sums of the same factors. Each set S of channels gives the eigenvalue

e(S)  =  (−1)^|S| · Π (p − 1)      over the channels p off S,

with multiplicity the product of p − 1 over S. On the additive character of an m with m ≡ 0 exactly at the channels off S, it is the Ramanujan sum c_N(m), which with g = gcd(m, N) equals μ(N/g)φ(g), μ the Möbius function and N squarefree; Klotz and Sander read the unitary graph's eigenvalues as these sums (Electron. J. Combin. 14 (2007) #R45, eqs. (8) and (9)).

Two sets share an eigenvalue iff their sizes have one parity and the products of p − 1 off them agree. The channel 2 contributes 1, so moving it across S flips the sign and keeps |e(S)|: the spectrum is ±U_k, with U_k the totients φ(d) of the squarefree d built from the odd primes to p_k, and the rung has 2|U_k| distinct eigenvalues against 2^k sets. The next prime p_k gives U_k = U_{k−1} ∪ (p_k − 1)U_{k−1}, so

|U_k|  =  2|U_{k−1}| − |U_{k−1} ∩ (p_k − 1)U_{k−1}|,

and each value in that intersection is a totient collision, a value φ(a) = (p_k − 1)φ(b) with a and b squarefree and built from the older odd primes. None occurs before 13, where φ(21) = 12. Where the Hamming spectrum is kept full by the primes' size, this one is thinned by the factoring of p_k − 1.

The doubling rung. If p_k − 1 has a prime factor ℓ dividing p_i − 1 for no i < k, that is, one that λ of the rung below lacks, then ℓ divides every element of (p_k − 1)U_{k−1} and none of U_{k−1}: the rung has no collision and |U_k| doubles exactly (λ is the exponent of the unit group, the lcm of the p_i − 1). To k = 24 the rungs without a collision are p_k = 3, 5, 7, 11, 23, 29, 47, 53, 59 and 83, and every one but 5 brings a new prime into λ. At 5 no prime is new, yet 4 · 1 and 4 · 2 miss U = {1, 2}: there the power of 2 in 4 blocks the collision, not a new prime. The ratio |U_k|/2^(k−1), distinct values against sign-paired sets, falls from 1 at k = 5 to 0.81 at k = 6, 0.10 at k = 20 and 0.039 at k = 24.

The script checks an eigenvector for each set S against the adjacency read off gcd(n − n′, N) = 1 at k = 3 and 4, and there the multiplicities by counting closed walks, the Ramanujan sums summed in floats over the units of Z/2310 at every m and of Z/30030 at the divisors m of 30030 (worst error 5.6 × 10⁻¹¹), the number of distinct values e(S) over all 2^k sets against 2|U_k| to k = 16, and |U_k| by a full listing of subset products to k = 20. Its control, "distinct gcd, distinct eigenvalue", fails first at k = 6.

The escape law

Tier: theorem; observation (the count at k = 5 and the ratio's readings).
Verifier: proof; tower_rungs.py::section_e.

A non-unit n of the rung has a null set S, the channels where n ≡ 0, and its null class is every residue with that null set. A generator step sends n to n + s or n − s, with s one of the rung's primes. If s is in S no member of the class reaches a unit, since s divides n ± s. If s is off S, exactly

(s − 1) · Π (p − 2)      over the channels p off S, p ≠ s

members of the class land on a unit, out of Π (p − 1) over the channels off S; this count is the escape law. Proof: by CRT the class's residues off S run over every tuple of nonzero values once. A channel in S reads n + s ≡ s, never 0. The channel s reads n + s ≡ n, never 0 since s is off S, and leaves s − 1 values free. Every other channel off S must avoid −s, which p − 2 of its p − 1 nonzero values do. The step −s is the image of the step s under n ↦ −n.

So the chance of escape is a product with one factor, (p − 2)/(p − 1), per channel p off S other than s; s and the channels of S contribute 1. The factor at 2 is 0 whenever 2 is off S and s is odd: an odd non-unit escapes only by ±2, and an even one never by ±2. The class {0} is the one class with no escape at all: any other class misses some channel, and if it misses 2 the step 2 lifts it, while if it holds 2 an odd s it misses does, every factor p − 2 being then at least 1. The law is about classes and not single residues: at k = 5, 122 nonzero non-units have no unit among their ten neighbours, n = 119 among them.

The proof never uses that S is nonempty, so the count holds as well at S empty, the class of units, and with s = 2 the chance of escape, one factor (p − 2)/(p − 1) per odd channel, is A(k), the chance that z + 2 is a unit given that z is: 22275/92160 = 0.2417 at k = 7, 22275 being the number of residues z with z and z + 2 both units. Since (p − 2)/(p − 1) = (1 − 1/(p − 1)²)(1 − 1/p), A(k) is a partial product of the twin prime constant C₂, the product of 1 − 1/(p − 1)² over every odd prime, 0.6602, times the product of 1 − 1/p over the odd primes to p_k, which Mertens' theorem puts at 2e^(−γ)/ln p_k, γ being Euler's constant. So A(k)·ln p_k tends to 2C₂e^(−γ), and their ratio A(k)·ln p_k/(2C₂e^(−γ)) reads 0.9544 at k = 10 and 0.9993 at k = 2000. The script counts every non-unit class and signed step at k = 3..7 from a table of units, never from the formula, and compares each count with it, after a control formula with p − 1 in place of p − 2, which is wrong at 1428 of them; it counts the class of units at the step 2 at k = 7.

The density theorem

Tier: theorem; observation (the in-range table and L's readings).
Verifier: proof; tower_growth.py::section_w.

Almost every rung is transparent. With x = p_k and N_nt(k) the number of rungs among 2, …, k that move λ,

N_nt(k) / k  ≪  loglog x / log x  →  0.

The proof has two steps, and neither needs more than Chebyshev-strength prime counting and an upper-bound sieve.

λ grows slowly. Write L(x) = log λ(k) = log lcm{p − 1 : p ≤ x}, the sum of a_q log q over the primes q, with q^{a_q} the largest power of q dividing some p − 1. Exponents above 1 need q² < x and cost O(√x). The primes q ≤ x/log x cost at most θ(x/log x) ≪ x/log x, θ being Chebyshev's sum of log q over the primes q up to its argument. A prime q > x/log x that divides p − 1 < x is the largest prime factor of p − 1, with cofactor m < log x, so the top range costs at most log x times the number of pairs (q, mq + 1), both prime, with m < log x; the prime-pair upper-bound sieve counts those as ≪ x·loglog x/(log x)², the loglog coming from the sum of 1/φ(m) over m < log x. So L(x) ≪ x·loglog x/log x. The step needs both primalities: with either one alone the top range is bounded no better than the trivial O(x), and the bound fails.

Jumps are written once. A rung that moves λ raises the exponent of some prime q, an event at q. Exponents only climb and q^a < x, so q hosts at most ⌊log x/log q⌋ events, and an event at q > y adds at least log y to L. For every y,

N_nt(k)  ≤  Σ_{q ≤ y} ⌊log x / log q⌋  +  L(x) / log y,

and y = x/(log x)³ makes the first term O(x/(log x)³) and the second ≪ x·loglog x/(log x)², which is the theorem.

The cyclicity ratio. The same two bounds pin a second ratio, α(k) = log λ(k)/log φ(p_k#) for k ≥ 2: how far the unit group is from cyclic, on a log scale, with α = 1 exactly when it is cyclic. A rung adds at most log(p − 1) to L, since the new λ divides the old one times p − 1, so α ≤ (N_nt(k)/k)(1 + o(1)) by the prime number theorem, so α vanishes with the share of rungs that move λ, and

N_nt(k)/k − α(k)  =  O(loglog x / log x).

Both ratios vanish, each O(loglog x/log x), so the bound on their difference says no more than that: the unit group's exponent is a vanishing power of its order, and the share of rungs that move it vanishes too. In range the gap closes faster than either ratio. log λ is the ledger of the jumps, the sum over the rungs that move λ of the log of what each one adds.

In range, over the first 664,579 rungs (every prime below 10^7):

 k        p_k        transparent   N_nt/k   α        gap
 50       229        0.469         0.520    0.296    0.224
 2000     17389      0.766         0.234    0.183    0.051
 10^4     104729     0.801         0.199    0.165    0.033
 664579   9999991    0.851         0.149    0.132    0.017

The transparent share is taken over the k − 1 rungs 2, …, k and N_nt over k, so the two columns sum to 1 only as k grows: at k = 50, 23 of 49 rungs are transparent and 26 move λ.

L(x)·log x/(x·loglog x) reads 0.774, 0.779, 0.766 and 0.764 at x = 10^4, 10^5, 10^6 and 10^7, so in range L sits near 0.77·x·loglog x/log x, the shape of the upper bound. A matching lower bound is open; a printed one is L(x) ≫ x^0.6265 (Nath and Jha, arXiv:2106.06782, Thm 1.1 at degree 1), whose upper side is the trivial O(x); the upper bound is not in that paper. The primes q ≤ x/log x contribute at most x/log x, so a matching lower bound must come from the top range: it needs ≫ x·loglog x/(log x)² prime pairs (q, mq + 1) with m < log x, a lower bound for the very count the sieve caps from above, and of twin-prime type. The convergence runs at log speed, and at x = 10^7 about 15% of rungs still move λ.

The serial ledger

Tier: rule (verified p < 10^16; the record criterion proved).
Verifier: tower_growth.py::section_s.

Below 10^16, every rung that moves λ raises the exponent of exactly one prime q, to v_q(p_k − 1), the exponent of q in p_k − 1. Up to x = 10^7 that is read directly off the scan of the rungs; beyond it, off a criterion.

The record criterion. Let f(m) be the least prime ≡ 1 (mod m). For q dividing p − 1, the prime p raises q iff h = v_q(p − 1) exceeds v_q(p′ − 1) for every prime p′ < p, iff no prime below p is ≡ 1 (mod q^h), iff p = f(q^h); and p = f(q^j) for any j ≥ 1 already forces p = f(q^h). So p raises q iff p lies in the record set {f(q^j) : j ≥ 1}, and the ledger's jumps are serial, no rung raising two primes at once, iff the record sets of distinct primes are disjoint. A prime in two of them, a double birth, has witnesses m₁ and m₂ with m₁m₂ | p − 1, so the smaller is at most √(p − 1). Taking every prime power m ≤ 10^8, setting p = f(m) and testing p against the other primes of p − 1 is therefore complete below 10^16. The search, run at m ≤ 10^8 (the script's argument; its default 10^6 is complete below 10^12), finds 5,762,848 record primes and no double birth. For a record prime p = f(m) and each other prime r dividing p − 1, the margin p/f(r^u), u = v_r(p − 1), is at least 1 and equals 1 exactly at a double birth. The smallest margin is 7/3, at p = 7 = f(3) against f(2) = 3, and from the twentieth smallest on every margin is above 34. A planted control deletes 5 and 7 from the primes, leaving 13 the least prime ≡ 1 (mod 3) and the least ≡ 1 (mod 4), and the search reports it.

Two readings of the same law, each holding as far as it does. The birthday formula's maximum, over the prime powers q^a exactly dividing an order, of the rung of f(q^a) is never a tie between two primes while the maximizing f(q^a) is below 10^16, since a tie is a double birth. And no rung brings two new primes into λ: the prime q enters at the rung of f(q), so distinct primes enter at distinct rungs, every one below 10^16.

Beyond 10^16 the law is open: a search cannot exclude a double birth at every scale, and no proof that the record sets of distinct primes are disjoint is known.

The limit

Tier: property.
Verifier: proof.

Reduction from R_{k+1} to R_k forgets the last channel and moves nothing else, so a coherent sequence of residues is a free choice of one residue at every prime, and the limit of the rungs is

lim R_k  =  Π_p F_p  =  Ẑ / J(Ẑ),

the product of every prime field. No exponent rises above 1, so no p-adic integers form: the limit is the profinite completion Ẑ = Π_p Z_p with its Jacobson radical J(Ẑ) = Π_p pZ_p stripped.

The integers sit inside by size. Z maps into the limit injectively, but no finite set S of channels certifies it, since the product of S is a nonzero integer reading 0 on all of S. Size certifies it: a prime larger than |n| cannot divide n, so a nonzero integer is nonzero on all but finitely many channels. Over F₂[x] the same proof runs on degree, since an irreducible cannot divide a nonzero polynomial of lower degree, and degree is the place at infinity of F₂(x), which is not archimedean. So this certificate comes from a place the channels do not read; over Q, whose places are the primes and one archimedean place, that place is size.

The image is dense, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem on any finite set of channels, and small. A nonzero integer has finitely many zero channels, while the tuple that is 0 at every p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and 1 elsewhere has infinitely many and so is no integer. Under the product of the uniform measures a random element is 0 at p with probability 1/p, independently, and Σ 1/p diverges, so by Borel–Cantelli almost every element has infinitely many zero channels: the integers lie in a set of measure zero, and a finite zero set is a necessary mark of a nonzero integer, never a sufficient one. A restricted product taken with respect to subrings containing 1 is all of Π_p F_p, since F_p has no proper subring containing 1; the finite adeles differ, their restriction Z_p ⊂ Q_p being such a subring, which gives a ring strictly between ⊕_p Q_p and Π_p Q_p. Restricting to {0} at every p gives ⊕_p F_p, an ideal and not a subring containing 1, meeting Z at 0 alone.

What the rungs keep and lose. What holds channel by channel survives: every coordinate is a field, the idempotents are the 0/1 tuples, now one for every set of primes, and the pseudo-inverse a′ (r ↦ r⁻¹ on each channel, 0 ↦ 0) satisfies a·a′·a = a. What needs a bound dies. No exponent E gives a^{E+1} = a on the limit, since a generator of F_p* has order p − 1 > E once p > E + 1: λ(k) ≥ p_k − 1 is unbounded, and an identity written with λ(k) fails at the first later rung that moves λ. The residue code dies with size, its codewords being the small integers. And some channel-wise maps stop being polynomials. On a rung every channel-wise map is one, interpolated on each channel and glued by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, but a function on F_p has exactly one polynomial of degree below p, which for the pseudo-inverse is x^{p−2} at every p ≥ 3; a polynomial of degree D with coefficients in the limit would give one of degree at most D at every p > D + 2. So the limit keeps the channel-wise operations, and the pseudo-inverse keeps no polynomial formula, only the limit of the rungs' x^{λ(k)−1}.

Open fronts

Three edges are open, and two are questions about the distribution of primes. The serial ledger past 10^16 needs a proof that the record sets of distinct primes are disjoint; the density theorem's matching lower bound needs a lower bound for prime pairs (q, mq + 1), of twin-prime type, and the constant of L·log x/(x·loglog x) with it. Either would be settled only by a proof idea, an Elliott–Halberstam-level advance or an elementary lower-bound argument; a bigger scan can only refute the first, by finding a double birth.

The third is the unitary spectrum's: whether a rung with no collision past k = 3 must bring a new prime into λ (true to k = 24, unproved), and how fast the share |U_k|/2^(k−1) falls, a count of the coincidences among totients of squarefree numbers that no scan settles.
