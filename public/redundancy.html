Redundancy

What an overlapping alphabet buys a most-significant-first reader, and what it costs, in exact form.

A window reads a number through a nested grid of cells refining toward a point, the first t digits naming the depth-t cell, and it reads a map at lookahead c when the output's depth-t cell is a function of the input's depth-(t + c) cell; the least such c is the map's delay, a map with a finite delay is local, and one with none is gated (Windows). Where the writing is unique the cells of one depth partition, and at the leading end — the most significant digits first, the end a floating-point number keeps — a map is local only under an alignment condition, a divisibility tie between the map and the base, and ×m is gated at every Ostrowski window. A redundant alphabet writes a number several ways: a positional digit set with more digits than its base, {−a−..a+} in radix b with slack ρ = a− + a+ + 1 − b at least 1, or an Ostrowski output written past legality's caps. Its cells of one depth overlap, and the overlap is what the reader spends: an input cell's image need no longer sit inside one output cell, only inside one of two that share a zone, so the reader may commit a digit before the unseen digits have settled which of the two the value belongs to. That is what dissolves the alignment condition — every rational slope, addition itself, every smooth map of streams goes local — and the pages below price what it buys, one map class each.

  • The widened output — ×m read at bounded lookahead once the reader may write past legality, the floors torn regardless, and the two-digit floor that finishing on an integer costs
  • Redundant arithmetic — what a redundant digit set makes local, and at what delay
  • Polynomial maps — the many-stream theorem, every smooth map of any number of streams at the margin in its box width rate, and the piecewise theorem's three clauses
  • Chained units — the chained delay, a multiplier feeding an adder paying the sum of the two floors whatever its digits, a multiplier feeding a multiplier one below it at one system, a pole's lead propagating by depth, and the comparator delay, max, min, the clamp and the rectifier at lookahead 0 and a comparator free inside any unit

The margin, and the law read against it

Every law here is read against one size-only heuristic. An operation injects a spread, the width of what its unseen digits can still move the output by; with c digits of lookahead an overlapping cover swallows whole any spread up to bcρ, its Lebesgue number, and the Lebesgue margin is the least c at which that reaches the spread injected — a count that reads the slack and nothing of the digit set's shape or the map's. The exact delay sits at the margin or one digit off it, and which is the whole of the story: the reader at an overlapping cover has no strategy, since it dies exactly when some image strictly contains an overlap zone, the intersection of two consecutive output cells, a condition on the input alone (the stream correction). Where the image endpoints a map's finite prefixes can reach are dense, the margin is exact, and for every smooth map that is not affine on the window it is the margin law: the delay is the least L with bLρ ≥ Λ(a− + a+), Λ the sup over the window of the gradient's L¹ norm, a theorem over the contiguous digit sets and a rule off them (the many-stream theorem). Where the reachable endpoints form a lattice — scaling by a rational slope, addition — the law is the lattice against the margin's excess and the delay departs from the count by at most one digit either way, with the cell where it does named (Redundant arithmetic). Composed units pay at most the sum of their floors, the escape below it bought from the producer's slack (Chained units), and at an Ostrowski window, where the redundancy is bought on the output side alone, the same overlap buys ×m a bounded reader while the reader that must finish on an integer pays a floor of its own (The widened output).
