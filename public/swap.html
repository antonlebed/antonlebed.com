The swap

The optimum that hands one atom the empty set is not always the only optimum. Which of those abandonments some optimum undoes, and what it trades to do so: a divisibility at one partner, a subset sum over several, and at the lightest masses a trade that takes more of a partner than it gives back. Behind the trades sits one identity, and in front of them a certificate whose room decides none of it.

A cell is a finite set of weighted atoms, each a value of a predictor's input carrying a mass w, the masses summing to 1, and each holding an exact posterior over a shared list of labels, all rationals fixed by hand. A rule takes some number of labels at each atom, best first; it costs the atom's mass per label and covers that mass times the posterior of what it takes, and its coverage summed over the cell must reach a bar T. A pair is an atom with one of its labels, and a rule is the set of pairs it takes. An optimum is a cheapest rule meeting the bar, computed exactly, and a rule's overshoot is the coverage it delivers past the bar. The operative level t* is the largest posterior value at which taking every pair at or above it still reaches the bar, and the certificate is the rule taking every pair strictly above it plus the cheapest subset of the pairs sitting exactly at it that closes the coverage still owed, its overshoot D and its room D/t*. At equal masses the certificate is an optimum; it abandons, takes no label at, every atom whose best label falls under the level and serves, takes at least one label at, every atom with a label above it, an atom whose best label sits exactly at the level going either way, and that split is the abandonment condition (Prediction sets, where the level, the certificate and the condition's refutation off equal masses stand). Its below half says an atom lying wholly under the level is abandoned; its above half says an atom peaking above the level is served. A weight vector is clean at a bar when every optimum of every cell obeys both halves, forced-clean when no cell has an above-level atom abandoned by every optimum or a below-level atom served by every optimum, and clean above when no cell has the first, the above half of forced-clean; an atom whose best label sits exactly at the level is read by neither half. The cells swept are these: every weight vector of three atoms on a fortieth grid, 741 of them, crossed with a fixed first family of five menus of five three-label posterior rows, one row chosen per atom, 625 cells per vector, at bars 3/4, 7/10, 3/5 and 1/2, and a second family of five other menus at 7/10; and at four atoms, on the first family at 3/4 and 7/10, 3,125 cells per vector over the 119 vectors whose parts in fortieths share a divisor of 4 or more, with 200 gcd-1 and 200 gcd-2 vectors added as controls at those two bars, entering only the counts that name them. One arity, the number of atoms, and bar on one family is an arm, seven in all, and every count is on the first family unless the second is named. The fifths and quarters are the weight vectors whose parts, in fortieths, share a divisor of 8 or 10, the coarse vectors. The other atoms of an abandoned atom's cell are its partners, and a cell with more than one optimum is tied.

The clean family has no shape observation

At a bar of 7/10, 87 vectors of 741 are clean and 105 forced-clean, and none of the eight non-empty clean and forced-clean sets over the four non-empty three-atom arms, the first family at 3/4, 7/10 and 3/5 and the second at 7/10, is a coordinate box or fits a spread or ratio bound; the vectors that break the box shape have an odd part each, (7, 16, 17) and (9, 10, 21), which a twentieths grid cannot write. The equal-mass control, off the grid, takes no forced failure at any bar, the proved property holding exactly, while it is not clean at 3/5 and 1/2, the gap being agreement among TIED optima and not uniqueness: (11, 11, 18) carries 297 cells with several optima and takes no failure, (2, 3, 35) has a unique optimum in all 625 cells and fails 619, both at 7/10. The forced-clean set collapses with the bar, 183, 105, 18, 0 at 3/4, 7/10, 3/5 and 1/2, while the below-level population grows 26.7-fold, 5,898 atoms to 157,623; at 1/2 no vector survives all five menus though 21, 9, 12, 12 and 6 survive them one at a time, a statement about resolution.

Scope. Observation, exhaustive over the fortieths grid at three atoms crossed with five menus, exact integer arithmetic, every optimum enumerated. Toy scale.

The certificate's room decides none of it observation

The room is a subset-sum overshoot in which the level fixes only a position, so it sawtooths in the bar, grid means 0.166, 0.157, 0.178, 0.170 as the bar falls, rather than running with it; and the exactness test D/t* < g, which when it holds makes the certificate an optimum, g the least positive integer combination of the weights, clears no vector on any bar, firing at 17 to 23% of cells, the best vectors, (1/4, 1/4, 1/2) and its permutations, missing 41 to 70 of 625. What the gcd of the weights buys is a sub-description: the test's per-cell rate stratifies monotonically by the gcd of the parts in fortieths, 15.3% at gcd 1 to 91.5% at gcd 10 at 3/4, every vector of gcd 8 or 10 is forced-clean at 3/4 and 7/10 and every gcd-10 one at 3/5, while strata 2, 4 and 5 track the gcd-1 bulk in forced-clean share and no non-empty forced-clean set is a union of strata, 135 of the 552 gcd-1 vectors being forced-clean at 3/4. Nor is it tie multiplicity: (7, 16, 17) is forced-clean at zero tied cells and (5, 5, 30) fails with 350 of its 625 cells tied, at 3/4. Every stratum is empty by 1/2, the gcd-8 one already at 3/5 where the gcd-1 and gcd-2 strata are not; gcd sorts the survivors at 3/4 and 7/10, the top strata whole, and does not cause the death.

Scope. Observation over the same grid and menus, the test and the room read at every cell. Toy scale.

The level-excess identity property

A pair at atom r with label y costs wr and covers wrp(y|r); at the level that coverage is worth wrp/t* of cost, the rest being wr(t* − p)/t*, and the pair's level-excess wr|t* − p| is t* times its size, zero at the level. For any rule R that drops the set X of above-level pairs and buys the set Y of below-level ones,

t∗ cost(R)  =  cov(R)  −  EA  +  e(X)  +  f(Y),t^{*}\,\mathrm{cost}(R) \;=\; \mathrm{cov}(R) \;-\; E_{A} \;+\; e(X) \;+\; f(Y),

EA the level-excess of every pair above the level, e and f that of the pairs dropped and bought. The certificate drops and buys nothing, so with DR the rule's own overshoot a rule beats the certificate exactly when e + f + DR falls under D. Two things read off it. An overshoot under the least excess of a pair off the level makes the certificate an optimum, a second sufficient condition needing no lattice, and it clears no vector either, firing at 12 to 25% of cells: no vector's certificate is optimal at every cell at three atoms at any bar, so the forced-clean set is rescued by optima that are NOT the certificate and still respect the split. The certificate-optimal share per cell climbs with the gcd stratum, 56.6% at gcd 1 to 94.6% at gcd 10 at 3/4, the lattice test's firing rate grading the same way under it. And it names a third way to beat the certificate, beside buying a pair below the level and dropping an above pair whose coverage fits inside D: dropping an above pair whose whole coverage exceeds D and re-filling from the pairs at the level. Of the 189,126 three-atom cells at 7/10 where the certificate loses, 75,786 have every optimum buying below, and at every one of the 3,846 where neither of the first two ways is open some optimum takes that third way.

Scope. Property for the identity, a reduced-cost identity at the dual price t*, algebra at any cell; checked in exact integer arithmetic at the certificate and at every optimum of every cell of the sweep, an integer scorer reproducing the forced counts of all five three-atom arms exactly. The second test is derived from the identity; its firing rates are observations. Toy scale.

The below half is vacuous at the coarse vectors at 3/4 and 7/10 on the first family by a mass bound property

An atom wholly under the level contributes nothing there, so the others reach the bar alone and its mass is at most 1 − T/smax, smax the largest sum a menu row carries at or above the level, the bound being 4/40 at 3/4, 7/40 at 7/10, 11/40 at 3/5 and 16/40 at 1/2 on the first family and 8/40 at 7/10 on the second, each attained. A fifths or quarters vector has no atom that light, so on the first family it carries no below-level atom at 3/4 or 7/10 at either arity and gains them at 3/5, where the fifths go 0 of 6 forced-clean; on the second family the ceiling is a fifth exactly and the fifths carry 36 below-level atoms, none served by every optimum. At 3/4 and 7/10 on the first family, then, a coarse vector's below half is vacuous and its content is the above half: at 3/4 and 7/10, at three atoms and at four, no above-level atom at gcd 8 or 10 is abandoned by every optimum, though some optima abandon one; at four atoms, forced-clean at 3/4 and 7/10, 4 of 4 gcd-8 vectors against 6 of 80 of gcd 4 at each, and 9 and 2 of 200 gcd-1 controls.

Scope. Property, from the level's definition, tight at all seven arms, the four-atom arms sharing the first family's ceilings at 3/4 and 7/10 and attaining them through the added controls; the counts observations on the sweep. Toy scale.

The above half is a swap property

A rule that abandons an atom covers the bar from the others alone, so an atom any feasible rule abandons weighs at most 1 − T, the feasibility window, the mirror of the mass bound and weaker than it. The fifths and quarters sit inside it, and what the window does at 3/4 and 7/10 is confine their abandonment to the LIGHTEST atom, whose partners are then within a slack σ = 1 − w − T of full coverage and hold their low labels. The swap: an optimum abandoning atom r that holds a lowest taken pair on a partner at least s·wr heavy, of coverage at most wr times the sum of r's top s posteriors plus the optimum's own overshoot, trades that pair for r's top s labels at no extra cost and still covers the bar, so some optimum serves r. At every one of the nine coarse vectors at 3/4 and 7/10, and the five at four atoms, every abandoning optimum is rescued by one swap; the swap reaches a vector when it rescues every abandoning optimum there, as it does vacuously where no optimum abandons, and it reaches 162 of the 183 forced-clean vectors at 3/4 and all 105 at 7/10, and at four atoms 23 of 29 and 5 of 11. The window alone decides only the top of the grid: a vector whose lightest mass exceeds 1 − T, 11/40 or more at 3/4 and 13/40 or more at 7/10, has no atom any feasible rule abandons, while the sweep finds every vector clean above from lightest mass 9/40 at 3/4 and 10/40 at 7/10. The variable is the lightest mass: clean above is zero through 5/40 at 3/4 and 6/40 at 7/10, then climbs until every vector is clean above, and the gcd returns at one edge as divisibility, the six vectors clean above at lightest mass 8/40 at 7/10 being exactly the fifths, whose partner masses are multiples of the lightest so that labels of the light atom trade evenly for one pair of any partner, two for a 16/40 pair and three for a 24/40 one, the gcd-1 vectors there going 0 of 24; at four atoms the edge is absent, 10 of 10. At 3/5 the fifths fail at the light atom, whose slack has doubled, and never at the 16/40 atom the window admits; the quarters fail only at 1/2.

Scope. Property for the window and the swap, algebra at any cell; the count exhaustive at every cell of the coarse vectors with each rescue re-scored; the grading by lightest mass an observation on the sweep. Toy scale.

The set-valued swap closes the rest, and the zero-slack row is proved at positive posteriors rule

The swap's mass condition is an EQUALITY at an optimum, wr' = s·wr at the partner r', the traded rule costing no more and an optimum costing least. Drop the lowest di labels on each of a set of partners and add r's top s: an optimum exactly when Σ widi = s·wr and the dropped coverage is at most wr·tops(r) + DR, tops(r) the sum of r's top s posteriors, and such an exchange exists exactly when some optimum serving r is DOMINATED, partner by partner, by the abandoning one. At every forced-clean vector at both arities and both bars every optimum abandoning an above-level atom has one, 183, 105, 29 and 11 exactly. The 21 vectors at 3/4 the one-pair swap misses all sit at zero slack, lightest mass 10/40, rescued at s = 3 by each partner dropping one label, 12 + 18 = 30, or a 15/40 partner dropping two; the permutations of (8, 8, 12, 12) by one 12/40 partner dropping two, 24 = 3·8. The full-partner exchange: the lowest d of k posteriors average at most 1/k, so any drop-set over fully served partners conserving mass at s = k rescues with no coverage condition at all. At zero slack the partners leave nothing untaken, so with every posterior positive each is full, and at 3/4 with three atoms their masses sum to three times the lightest, the label count times it: every vector of lightest mass 10/40 is clean above at 3/4, 30 of 30, the one-pair swap reaching 9.

Scope. The exchange and the full-partner property are proved, algebra at any cell, and the zero-slack row at 3/4 with three atoms and three labels is proved wherever every posterior is positive; the closing count is exhaustive at every cell of every forced-clean vector at both arities and both bars, every rescue re-scored in exact integer arithmetic. Toy scale.

The gain exchange is the whole rescue, and at three atoms the obstruction is mostly arithmetic property

A serving optimum is the abandoning one with its lowest taken labels dropped on some partners, the next labels ADDED on others, and r's top s labels: an optimum exactly when the dropped mass minus the gained mass equals s·wr and the dropped coverage is at most the gained coverage plus wr·tops(r) plus DR, the set-valued swap being the case with nothing gained. At three atoms a gain case is one dropper and one gainer, the dropper heavier wherever each moves one label: masses (1, 18, 21), the abandoning optimum at sizes (0, 1, 3) and the serving one at (3, 2, 2), 21 = 3 + 18. On the four arms, three atoms at 3/4 and at 7/10 and four atoms at 3/4 and at 7/10, 2,604, 2,142, 4,536 and 6,120 abandoning optima, none in a forced-clean vector, have a serving optimum and none dominated, every one at lightest mass 6/40 or under; a vector failing above at 7/40 to 9/40 fails without a gain case. A dominated rescue needs a depth-capped drop-set over the served partners of mass exactly s·wr, and where the masses admit none the rescue is a gain case before any coverage is read: 85% and 89% of the gain cases at three atoms at 3/4 and at 7/10, at (1, 18, 21) no 18d + 21d' being 1, 2 or 3; the rest sit at lightest mass 4/40 and 5/40 alone with a mass-conserving drop-set that loses too much coverage. At four atoms, where the parts share a divisor and a partner of mass 4, 8 or 12 (5, 10 or 15 where the divisor is 5 or 10) always exists, the arithmetic kind is 0 at 3/4 and 2% at 7/10, the 2% being that partner abandoned too. So the dominated exchange is the shape of the vectors clean above and not the grid's.

Scope. Property for the decomposition, algebra at any cell, its equality and coverage condition asserted at every serving optimum of every abandoning optimum on the sweep, the four-atom half restricted to the 119 vectors whose parts share a divisor of 4 or more; the counts and shares are observations. Open: the closed form for the grading in the slack above zero, where a partial partner's dropped label sits above its row's mean and the coverage condition returns, and the collapse by 1/2, which leaves no stratum. Toy scale.
