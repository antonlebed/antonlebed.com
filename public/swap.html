The swap

The optimum that hands one atom the empty set is not always the only optimum. Which of those abandonments some optimum undoes, and what it trades to do so: a divisibility at one partner, a subset sum over several, and below a band of the lightest mass a trade that takes more of a partner than it gives back. Behind the trades sits one identity, and in front of them a certificate whose room decides none of it.

A cell is a finite set of weighted atoms, each a value of a predictor's input carrying a mass w, the masses summing to 1, and each holding an exact posterior over a shared list of labels, all rationals fixed by hand. A rule takes some number of labels at each atom, best first; it costs the atom's mass per label and covers that mass times the posterior of what it takes, and its coverage summed over the cell must reach a bar T. A pair is an atom with a label taken there. An optimum is a cheapest rule meeting the bar, computed exactly, and a rule's overshoot is the coverage it delivers past the bar. The operative level t* is the largest posterior value at which taking every pair at or above it still reaches the bar, and the certificate is the rule taking every pair strictly above it plus the cheapest subset of the pairs sitting exactly at it that closes the coverage still owed, its overshoot D and its room D/t*. At equal masses the certificate is an optimum and abandons, takes no label at, exactly the atoms whose best label falls under the level, which is the abandonment condition (Prediction sets, where the level, the certificate and the condition's refutation off equal masses stand). Its below half says an atom lying wholly under the level is abandoned by every optimum; its above half says an atom peaking above the level is served by some. A weight vector is clean at a bar when every optimum of every cell abandons exactly the below-level atoms, and forced-clean when no atom is abandoned by every optimum against the condition. The cells are the ones the condition's failures were counted on: every weight vector of three or four atoms on a fortieth grid, 741 and 519 of them, crossed with a fixed first family of five three-label posterior rows, 625 and 3,125 cells per vector, at bars 3/4, 7/10, 3/5 and 1/2, and a second family at 7/10.

The clean family has no shape observation

At a bar of 7/10 the two readings give 87 and 105 clean vectors of 741, and none of the eight non-empty survivor sets over four bars and both readings is a coordinate box or fits a spread or ratio bound; the vectors that break the box shape have odd numerators, (7, 16, 17) and (9, 10, 21), which a twentieths grid cannot write. Equal mass takes no forced failure at any bar, the proved property holding exactly, while failing the strong reading at 3/5 and 1/2, the gap being agreement among TIED optima and not uniqueness: (11, 11, 18) carries 297 cells with several optima and takes no failure, (2, 3, 35) has a unique optimum in all 625 cells and fails 619. The forced-clean set collapses with the bar, 183, 105, 18, 0 at 3/4, 7/10, 3/5 and 1/2, while the below-level population grows 26.7-fold, 5,898 atoms to 157,623; at 1/2 no vector survives all five menus though 21, 9, 12, 12 and 6 survive them one at a time, a statement about resolution.

Scope. Observation, exhaustive over the fortieths grid at three atoms crossed with five menus, exact integer arithmetic, every optimum enumerated. Toy scale.

The certificate's room decides none of it observation

The room is a subset-sum overshoot in which the level fixes only a position, so it sawtooths in the bar, grid means 0.166, 0.157, 0.178, 0.170 as the bar falls, rather than running with it; and the exactness test D/t* < g, g the least positive integer combination of the weights, clears no vector on any bar, firing at 17 to 23% of cells with the best vector, (1/4, 1/4, 1/2), missing 41 to 70 of 625. What the gcd of the weights buys is a sub-description: the test's per-cell rate stratifies monotonically by gcd, 15.3% at gcd 1 to 91.5% at gcd 10 at 3/4, every vector of gcd 8 or 10 is clean at 3/4 and 7/10 and every gcd-10 one at 3/5, while strata 2, 4 and 5 track the gcd-1 bulk and no non-empty clean set is a union of strata, 135 of the 552 gcd-1 vectors being clean at 3/4. Nor is it tie multiplicity: (7, 16, 17) is clean at zero tied cells and (5, 5, 30) fails at 350 of 625. Every stratum collapses at 1/2; gcd orders the survival and does not cause the death.

Scope. Observation over the same grid and menus, the test and the room read at every cell. Toy scale.

The level-excess identity property

A pair at atom r with label y costs wr and covers wrp(y|r); at the level that coverage is worth wrp/t* of cost, and the pair's level-excess wr|t* − p| is the rest, zero at the level. For any rule R that drops the set X of above-level pairs and buys the set Y of below-level ones,

t∗ cost(R)  =  cov(R)  −  EA  +  e(X)  +  f(Y),t^{*}\,\mathrm{cost}(R) \;=\; \mathrm{cov}(R) \;-\; E_{A} \;+\; e(X) \;+\; f(Y),

EA the level-excess of every pair above the level, e and f that of the pairs dropped and bought. The certificate drops and buys nothing, so with DR the rule's own overshoot a rule beats the certificate exactly when e + f + DR falls under D. Two things read off it. An overshoot under the least single-pair excess makes the certificate an optimum, a second sufficient condition needing no lattice, and it clears no vector either, firing at 12 to 25% of cells: no vector's certificate is optimal at every cell at three atoms at any bar, so the clean set is rescued by optima that are NOT the certificate and still respect the split. The certificate-optimal share per cell climbs with the gcd stratum, 56.6% at gcd 1 to 94.6% at gcd 10 at 3/4, the lattice test's firing rate grading the same way under it. And the identity's third shape is real: an optimum dropping an above pair whose whole coverage exceeds D and re-filling from the tied block, at 3,846 of 3,846 such cells at 7/10.

Scope. Property for the identity, complementary slackness at the dual price t*, algebra at any cell; checked in exact integer arithmetic at the certificate and at every optimum of every cell of the sweep, an integer scorer reproducing all five forced counts exactly. The second test is a rule from the identity; its firing rates are observations. Toy scale.

The below half is vacuous by a mass bound property

An atom wholly under the level contributes nothing there, so the others reach the bar alone and its mass is at most 1 − T/smax, smax the largest sum a menu row carries at or above the level: 4/40 at 3/4, 7/40 at 7/10, 11/40 at 3/5 and 16/40 at 1/2 on the first family and 8/40 at 7/10 on the second, each attained. A fifths or quarters vector has no atom that light, so it carries no below-level atom at 3/4 or 7/10 at either arity and gains them at 3/5, where the fifths go 0 of 6; on the second family the ceiling is a fifth exactly and the fifths carry 36 at-risk atoms, none forced. What is coarse-and-clean is the ABOVE half alone: no optimum abandons an above-level atom at gcd 8 or 10 at 3/4 and 7/10, at three atoms and at four, 4 of 4 fifths against 6 of 80 tenths and 9 and 2 of 200 gcd-1 controls.

Scope. Property, from the level's definition, tight at all seven arms; the counts observations on the sweep. Toy scale.

The above half is a swap property

A rule that abandons an atom covers the bar from the others alone, so an atom any feasible rule abandons weighs at most 1 − T, the feasibility window, the mirror of the mass bound and weaker than it. The fifths and quarters sit inside it, and what the window does is confine their abandonment to the LIGHTEST atom, whose partners are then within a slack σ = 1 − w − T of full coverage and hold their low labels. The swap: an optimum abandoning atom r that holds a pair on a partner at least s·wr heavy, of coverage at most wr times the sum of r's top s posteriors plus the optimum's own overshoot, trades that pair for r's top s labels at no extra cost and still covers the bar, so some optimum serves r. At every one of the nine coarse vectors at 3/4 and 7/10, and the five at four atoms, every abandoning optimum is rescued by one swap; the swap reaches 162 of the 183 forced-clean vectors at 3/4 and all 105 at 7/10, and at four atoms 23 of 29 and 5 of 11. The window alone decides the top of the grid, every vector of lightest mass 9/40 or more being clean above at 3/4 and 10/40 or more at 7/10. The variable is the lightest mass: above-clean is zero through 5/40 at 3/4 and 6/40 at 7/10, then climbs to the window's floor, and the gcd returns at one edge as divisibility, the six clean vectors of lightest mass 8/40 at 7/10 being exactly the fifths, whose partner masses are multiples of the lightest so that two labels of the light atom trade evenly for one pair of a 16/40 atom, the gcd-1 vectors there going 0 of 24; at four atoms the edge is absent, 10 of 10. At 3/5 the fifths fail at the light atom, whose slack has doubled, and never at the 16/40 atom the window admits; the quarters fail only at 1/2.

Scope. Property for the window and the swap, algebra at any cell; the count exhaustive at every cell of the coarse vectors with each rescue re-scored; the grading by lightest mass an observation on the sweep. Toy scale.

The set-valued swap closes the rest, and the zero-slack row is a theorem rule

The mass condition is an EQUALITY at an optimum, wr' = s·wr, the traded rule costing no more and an optimum costing least. Drop the lowest di labels on each of a set of partners and add r's top s: an optimum exactly when Σ widi = s·wr and the dropped coverage is at most wr·tops(r) + DR, and such an exchange exists exactly when some optimum serving r is DOMINATED, partner by partner, by the abandoning one. At every forced-clean vector at both arities and both bars every abandoning optimum has one, 183, 105, 29 and 11 exactly. The 21 vectors at 3/4 the one-pair swap misses all sit at zero slack, lightest mass 10/40, rescued at s = 3 by each partner dropping one label, 12 + 18 = 30, or a 15/40 partner dropping two; the permutations of (8, 8, 12, 12) by one 12/40 partner dropping two, 24 = 3·8. The full-partner exchange: the lowest d of k posteriors average at most 1/k, so any drop-set over fully served partners conserving mass at s = k rescues with no coverage condition at all. At zero slack the partners leave nothing untaken, so with every posterior positive each is full, and at 3/4 with three atoms their masses sum to three times the lightest: every vector of lightest mass 10/40 is clean above at 3/4, 30 of 30, the one-pair swap reaching 9.

Scope. The exchange and the full-partner property are proved, algebra at any cell, and the zero-slack row at 3/4 with three atoms is a theorem wherever every posterior is positive; the closing count is a rule, exhaustive at every cell of every forced-clean vector at both arities and both bars, every rescue re-scored in exact integer arithmetic. Toy scale.

The gain exchange is the whole rescue, and below the band the obstruction is arithmetic property

A serving optimum is the abandoning one with prefixes dropped on some partners, prefixes ADDED on others, and r's top s labels: an optimum exactly when the dropped mass minus the gained mass equals s·wr and the dropped coverage is at most the gained coverage plus wr·tops(r) plus DR, the set-valued swap being the case with nothing gained. At three atoms it is one dropper and one gainer, the dropper heavier: masses (1, 18, 21), the abandoning optimum at sizes (0, 1, 3) and the serving one at (3, 2, 2), 21 = 3 + 18. Every abandoning optimum below the clean band, 2,142 to 6,120 per arm at lightest mass 6/40 or under, has a serving optimum and none dominated. A dominated rescue needs a depth-capped drop-set over the served partners of mass exactly s·wr, and where the masses admit none the rescue is a gain case before any coverage is read: 85% and 89% of the gain cases at three atoms, at (1, 18, 21) no 18d + 21d' being 1, 2 or 3; the rest sit at lightest mass 4/40 and 5/40 alone with a mass-conserving drop-set that loses too much coverage. At four atoms, where the parts share a divisor and a partner of mass 4, 8 or 12 always exists, the arithmetic kind is 0 and 2%, the 2% being that partner abandoned too. So the dominated exchange is the clean band's shape and not the grid's.

Scope. Property for the decomposition, algebra at any cell, its equality and coverage condition asserted at every serving optimum of every abandoning optimum on the sweep, the four-atom half restricted to the 119 vectors whose parts share a divisor of 4 or more; the shares are observations. Open: the closed form for the grading in the slack above zero, where a partial partner's dropped label sits above its row's mean and the coverage condition returns, and the collapse at 1/2, which takes every stratum at once. Toy scale.
