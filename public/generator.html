The generator ceiling

One statistic read at two degrees. Over a quadratic field the principal shortfall, graded by the order of the class, is a ladder with the generating classes fixed at the top, nearly all of it the explicit formula's prime-power term; a cubic field's partially split primes carry the same shape. Whether the ceiling at the generators is one number across degrees, what the prime-power term does and does not pay on each population, and what the cubic population carries instead — a fixed count of primes seated at the field's least places, read by rank and blind to norm, whose cell is the class group modulo squares — with the open front what puts a non-square first.

A place of a number field is principal when the ideal it names is generated by a single element; its ideal class is that ideal modulo the principal ones, the classes forming the finite class group, of order the class number h, the principal places lying in its trivial class. The order of a class is the least power of it that is trivial, and a class of order h generates the group, which exists exactly when the group is cyclic. A degree-1 place has the prime field as residue field. A level is an observed count of primes whose degree-1 place lies in a class, over its Chebotarev nominal, so that a stratum's order cells — a stratum being the fields of one class number — are weighted-mean 1 by construction. The generator cell is the level at the classes of order h, and the ceiling its value. A cut is the prime bound a level is read to, a band a window of primes. Over a quadratic field the two places over a split prime carry inverse classes and the sorting runs over the narrow class group, of order h+, which is h over an imaginary field and 2h over a real field whose fundamental unit has norm +1. Over a cubic field a prime carrying all three degree-1 places is totally split and one carrying a single degree-1 place beside a place of degree 2 is partially split; how a prime splits is recorded by its Frobenius, a conjugacy class in the Galois group of the field's closure, the symmetric group S3 at a non-cyclic cubic field, the identity at a totally split prime and a transposition at a partial one. The partial fiber is the population of degree-1 places over partially split primes, the split fiber the places over totally split primes. A place's rank is its position in its own field's norm order among the odd partial places, 1 at the least. The prime-power term is the quadratic page's (Principal places): a count of primes with Frobenius in a class is short of the explicit formula's main term by ½ per prime below √x whose Frobenius squares into the class, ⅓ per cube, and the ramified ideals at ½ on the classes of order at most 2. A cubic field is complex with one real embedding and totally real with three, and every cubic field here is complex unless the signature is named.

One ceiling, and where it fractures

The three cubic strata entitled to vote fit one constant inside the ten quadratic strata's spread observation

Over p < 1000, degree 2's bottom-bin cut, the partial fiber's generator cells read 1.086 ± 0.019, 1.153 ± 0.047, 1.070 ± 0.056 at h = 4, 6, 8 and fit one constant at 1.0935 (χ² = 1.95 on 2), against degree 2's ten generator cells spanning 1.079 to 1.105 about a mean of 1.0932 — one number landing inside the other's spread across two degrees, two splitting types, a narrow class group against an ordinary one, and two populations sharing no field. The agreement is of SHAPES: the degree-3 constant's own uncertainty is about 0.017 and the four-figure coincidence is luck.

Scope. Observation. The error bars carry the dispersion measured on that cell about each stratum's own mean (scale 0.734, stricter than independence), one scale assumed common across the three strata.

Four of the seven cubic strata cannot vote, which is arithmetic rule

A stratum's order cells are indexed by the divisors of h and weighted-mean 1, so at a PRIME h there are two cells and L(h) = (h − L(1))/(h − 1): the generator level is the trivial level divided by h − 1 and carries no content of its own. Degree 2's two gradients are alternatives there — a flat ceiling forces the trivial deficit to grow like (c − 1)(h − 1), a flat trivial level forces the ceiling to decay to 1. The cost of missing it is a flipped verdict: the generator column across all seven strata rejects a constant at χ² = 35.62 on 6, and the same numbers with the four forced cells dropped fit one at 1.95 on 2. What disqualifies a cell is its row having ONE free number, already spent in the trivial column.

Scope. Rule, proved; the derivation checked to 10⁻¹⁵. Every class group in the cubic population is cyclic, so the cell there rests on the whole population where at degree 2 it rests on the cyclic subset. The under-dispersion of the principal count reaches the generator cell on the 26 composite-h fields where that is a second measurement (index 0.506 over p < 1000, the 2 per cent point of a chi-square on 25), not at the 201 prime-h fields where the two cells' residuals are exact negatives.

Scope. Observation, both over field sets frozen at the smallest cut; the cross-degree curve on the 24 fields of h = 4 and 6 to |d| ≤ 6000 against 224 imaginary quadratic fields over five strata, each cut's error bar measured at that cut.

The fracture line is the square map on the Galois group: a population carries the prime-square term iff its Frobenius class is a square rule

The term put back per field and window removes the quadratic surplus exactly — the imaginary top band 1.0426 → 1.0001 ± 0.0097, the real 1.0613 → 1.0151 ± 0.0066, the tail 1.0240 → 1.0000 ± 0.0011 at 10⁴. A transposition is never a square in S3, so the partial fiber carries no prime-square term: its levels move by at most 0.0017 under the cube term and its top band 0.9780 ± 0.0123 stands. The cubic SPLIT fiber, Frobenius the identity, carries the term as the formula says — on 1274 fields its ladder 1.115, 1.086, 1.063, 1.048 corrects to 1.045, 1.022, 1.010, 1.004 ± 0.005, its top band 1.019 ± 0.014 raw to 0.991 ± 0.013. Four cells of a form-by-rank design say what does NOT carry it: real quadratic fields — unit rank 1, the number of independent units of infinite order, an indefinite form with no hard zero — keep the top-band surplus at full size, 1.0613 ± 0.0069; the totally real cubic fourth cell (a streaming census of 28,072 fields to discriminant 530,000, 406 usable at the 0.010 bar) sits on 1 in every band, top 1.0167 ± 0.0100, combined [630, 10⁴) 1.0042 ± 0.0025, −3.77σ and −3.69σ against the two quadratic populations there. Not the form, not the signature, not the unit rank; the two quadratic signs' ceilings agree at the tail (1.0246 ± 0.0010 against 1.0240 ± 0.0012 at 10⁴), parting only mid-ladder.

Scope. Rule in range, the term classical and its accounting verified on the populations named; the cubic populations omit the prime 2 and the ramified primes, printed as an unallocated bound, zero in every band above 300. Whether the two formless cubic cells share one asymptote is open (+2.4σ apart, opposite sides of 1, the complex side with no wide-band read).

It is seated at the field's least places, read by rank and not by norm, and the cell it lives on is Cl/2Cl observation

On the 185 cyclic fields of the box to |d| ≤ 24,000 at h = 4, 6, 8, the least partial place lies in a generating class 0.283 ± 0.032 above the uniform share φ(h)/h, the excess decaying over rank (0.204 ± 0.017 pooled over ranks 1–3, 0.108 ± 0.022 over 4–6, 0.076 ± 0.018 over 7–10) and the windows closing inside the range (+0.67 ± 0.05 primes over [0, 20), −0.03 ± 0.19 over [500, 1000)). The S4 reading states what the cell is. A field of 2-rank 1 — one class of order 2 — has one unramified quadratic extension, whose Galois closure over Q is that of an S4 quartic field F with cubic resolvent K; a partial prime's degree-1 place lies in 2Cl exactly when its Frobenius is a transposition (p of type (1, 1, 2) in F) and outside 2Cl when it is a 4-cycle (p inert in F), and a split prime carries one or three places in 2Cl as its Frobenius is a double transposition or the identity. In that vocabulary the seat is one statement at every even h: THE LEAST PARTIAL PLACE IS A NON-SQUARE. It reads the same on two disjoint populations, 686 fields to |d| = 24,000 and 840 over 24,000 < |d| ≤ 48,000: rank 1 +0.338 ± 0.014 and +0.300 ± 0.014 over one half; h = 2 +0.341 ± 0.017 and +0.307 ± 0.017, where it says the least partial place is non-principal 84 and 80 times in 100; h = 4 +0.322 ± 0.038 and +0.322 ± 0.035; h = 6 +0.392 ± 0.039 and +0.295 ± 0.037, where the class of order 2 joins (the generator cell alone +0.251 ± 0.062); still +0.08 to +0.10 at rank 10. On the two-way table of prime and rank the share is a function of RANK and not of the prime — about 0.85 and 0.80 at rank 1 at every p from 3 to 13, 0.75 and 0.67 at rank 2, 0.62 and 0.60 from rank 4 on, flat across p from 11 to 250 (rank-1 slope on p −0.005 ± 0.007 and −0.003 ± 0.005) — so conditioning on the splitting of every prime below the least place leaves the seat standing, and a seat at the NORMS dies. The cubic count is CLOSED, seated in its field's own norm order and complete by p = 500, where the quadratic real residual is OPEN, its window moving outward with |D| (Principal places); both are early, only one finishes.

Scope. Observation; the dictionary between the class labels and the factoring in the quartic field is a rule, verified at 101,592 primes across 616 fields without exception, the quartic built from the order alone — a generator of an order-2 ideal's square, twisted through the unit and sign classes, the one class real-positive and a square mod 4 — which certifies the 2-part of every class label independently of the relation lattice. The two S4 classes are of equal size, neither a square nor a power of anything outside its own class, so no prime-power term separates them.

Both seats are graded by the discriminant's smallest prime factors: the whole of the quadratic bias, and a third of the cubic one observation

At degree 2 the seat is read over the 11,833 imaginary quadratic fields with a cyclic class group of even order to |D| ≤ 100,000. Each class is one reduced form ax2 + bxy + cy2, its leading coefficient a the least norm the class attains; the least odd split prime lands in a non-square class 0.640 ± 0.004 of the time against a reduction null of 0.580, the non-square share among the classes whose least norm the prime clears. Every class counted once and binned by a, the non-square share is multiplicative in a and no function of its size: 0.636 at a = 2 and 0.640 at a = 3 against nulls near 0.507, 0.000 over the 9,019 classes with a = 4 — the square of the norm-2 class — 0.444 over 7 ≤ a ≤ 10, and within 0.02 of the null from a = 11 up. With 2-rank 1 the discriminant is a product of two prime discriminants, one of them positive, +q, and the genus character is the Kronecker symbol (+q/·) read at a class's norm, trivial exactly on the squares: the class of a split prime p is a non-square exactly when that character at p is −1, at all 18,398 classes with a = 2 over odd D and a = 3 over the D prime to 3 that carry a +q factor. The bias is where the smallest prime factors of D sit in that character's residue classes — the character at 3 is −1 for a +q factor 5 mod 12 and +1 for one 1 mod 12, 5 against 13 — so over the fields whose least prime factor of |D| exceeds 7 the a = 3 share falls from 0.824 to 0.493 and the a = 2 share from 0.781 to 0.604. The +0.060 residual is a finite-scale artifact of the discriminant's smallest factors and not a seat, its slower fall at a = 2 the open number.

What puts the 4-cycles first there — a bias between two classes of S4 of equal size that no prime-power term separates, graded by rank and blind to norm — and why 2 ramified all but forces one, is the front this page leaves open, with the totally real signature unread.
