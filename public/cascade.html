The cascade boundary

Whether a growth law's lock has to exist at all: the reduction turning that question into a ladder of primes, the dichotomy on greedy walks it shares with the lock, the walks that close it at every characteristic yet swept, the residual those walks never empty, and the shape a proof closing it wholesale would have to have.

A growth law is a structural demand plus a greedy move: extend the state by the least admissible m ≥ 2. The demand here is dynamics-greed, which asks that λ(N), the exponent of the unit group of the state N, the least L with aL = 1 for every unit a, keep growing. A prime's window is its slot in the state, OPENED by seating the prime and DEEPENED by raising its exponent; the door at a prime is the least move there that raises λ against the whole state at once. A law locks when it collapses within a bounded number of moves onto one prime's column forever, no rival door falling afterwards. Over Z it does (the lock-prime law), and over every number field censused so far (Growth over other fields), where a prime becomes a place, a prime ideal with a norm, and a state is an ideal (the ideal world) or an element generating one (the element world). A rank-1 place is unramified of residue degree 1, so its completion is Qp exactly; its prime p is a rank-1 characteristic of the ring.

Every census finds a lock at the end of each seed's walk. What no census settles is whether a lock must exist. Call that open half the cascade boundary. Its shape is sharp: a trajectory escapes only by keeping move costs from ever settling, and to do that it must find at every stage a fresh place of norm m·pv+1 + 1, a carrier, with v the exponent of p in λ and m a small multiplier, priced below the virgin door at p, the cost of opening p from a state that has never seated it. Lock existence in characteristic zero is exactly the ruling out of that infinite carrier ladder.

The reduction

The escape's conjunction rule

The demand on an escaping trajectory is a conjunction over characteristics, never a disjunction it may satisfy at one of its choosing. An escape's move costs must diverge: a ring with finitely many places under any norm has finitely many moves under any ceiling, so a stretch of moves staying under one would pick some place forever at bounded cost, and such a place absorbs the tail, a lock. Every rank-1 characteristic's door is a λ-growing move available whatever the state has done, at cost pv+2, so each of them, alone and at every stage, caps what the escape can be paying, and every one of those exponents must run away at once. The rises cannot run through a place over p itself, which would seat it at a constant price, so they run through places over other characteristics, where only the norm minus one can carry a factor of p: a carrier's norm is one more than a multiple of pv+1, and comparing it against the door confines the multiplier to m ≤ p − 1.

Scope. Proved for the ideal world of any characteristic-zero ring, and every step carries into the element world: the finiteness argument needs only finitely many ideals under a ceiling, a power of the recurring bundle is the always-available move at a fixed price, and the door, charged on a generator, is padded by an ideal in the inverse class, so the multiplier's range widens by the factor τ that padding costs, 1 exactly at a principal place.

That derivation used nothing about rings, and stripped of them it is one statement about greedy walks, of which the conjunction and the lock-prime law are the two branches.

The standing-move dichotomy rule

A standing move is a recipe, state ↦ a move admissible whatever the state has done, never a fixed element: over Z the door at a fixed q is 5 at N = 71 and 25 at N = 355. Give a greedy walk a family of standing moves, each priced through a nondecreasing function of one coordinate of the state, an integer read off it, and the walk pays at most the least of those prices at every step. Under norm-finiteness, either some coordinate stays bounded along the walk, and then a move of bounded cost recurs forever and the walk locks, or every coordinate runs away at once. A member priced constant in the state locks the walk outright; escape is a conjunction over the whole family. Over Z dynamics-greed's cheapest standing price goes constant and the walk locks; in a characteristic-zero ring the door's price grows with the state and an escape must outrun every index at once. Nothing but whether the standing price is constant or grows separates the two arguments.

Scope. Proved for any greedy walk under the three hypotheses: standing recipes, coordinate pricing, norm-finiteness. A family whose coordinates never repeat reads nothing: the greedy move itself, priced by N, meets every hypothesis on all eleven locked depth walks censused and reads each as "the coordinate diverges", so the content lies in choosing coordinates that are projections of the state, bounded along a lock. What the dichotomy does not recover is WHICH prime a locking walk locks.

The excess prices the door rule

Over Z the door menu is a standing family, one recipe per prime, standing because λ(qd) carries an unbounded power of q. Its coordinate at q is the excess xq = vq(λ(N)) − vq(N), the exponent of q in λ less the depth of q's own column. The door at an odd q dividing N is exactly qx+2, and qx+2 bounds the door at every state, every strict case an opening at cost q where q − 1 carries a factor λ lacks. At odd q the excess never falls below −1, since qe dividing N makes λ(qe), which carries qe−1, divide λ(N). The lock-prime law's recurrence invariant, the lock prime's λ-exponent sitting one below its column depth, IS x = −1: the coordinate at its floor. So Z's lock is the cap pinned at the bottom of the coordinate that prices it, and the budget inequality bounds the same coordinate's rise from above, one per move at odd p. At q = 2 the floor and the bound both shift by one, the bound failing at the virgin state alone.

Scope. The floor is proved for every odd prime; the door bound is censused over twelve seeded walks, 402 exact and 1326 strict over 1728 odd-prime/state pairs, the floor reached at 113, and along seed 71's locked tail x17 = −1 out to N = 59,981,858,965. Over Z the excess is defined out of the door formula, so that half is a re-description; the content is that a differently priced world charges the same quantity.

All three fates sit in the schema rule

The branch is decided by whether some pricing coordinate stays bounded. Depth locks with its excess pinned at the floor. Mortality, λ frozen and the walk halting at the seed's wall, the largest modulus whose λ divides the seed's, is the bounded branch with the coordinate driven to its bottom: from every live state the jump straight to the wall is a standing move, priced by the wall's ratio to the state alone, which descends 48, 24, 12, 6, 3, 1 off seed 5. Breadth, demanding that the extension split, which picks the least prime not dividing N, is the growing branch: independence CONSUMES each move on use, every move of cost at most 30 permanently inadmissible by step 10 from seed 1, so no standing recipe of bounded price exists and the walk cannot lock. One recipe per reduced residue class mod m, the least unused prime in the class, priced by how many primes of the class the walk has picked, is a standing family whose every price grows, whose cap equals the greedy cost at every step but the one that picks a prime dividing m, and whose every coordinate diverges: the escape's conjunction with real conjuncts, Dirichlet's theorem read per class being what makes each recipe standing.

Scope. The three placements are proved. The class family is a rule in range, m ∈ {3, 4, 5, 8}, eleven seeds, twelve steps, every class coordinate repeating and ending above its start, Dirichlet's theorem checked only inside a sieve to 200,000; the wall jump is measured at 13 states off three seeds.

Closing the boundary

The budget inequality rule

A door cheap enough to afford is too cheap to buy the exponent that would keep the ladder climbing. Write V = vp(λ); a rung is a value of it. V rises only at an opening, an opening is paid at its face value N(Q), which the ladder premise holds below the virgin door, and pvp(X−1) divides X − 1, so vp(N(Q) − 1) < logp N(Q) < logp of the door. At odd p the virgin door is pV+2, so V rises by at most ONE per move, the multiplier's cap is pinned at p forever, and the excess that alone could widen it is unbuyable. At p = 2 the unit group's extra Z/2 makes the door 2V+3 and the rise at most TWO, exactly one unit of excess, and banking it needs a place of norm 2V+2 + 1 with multiplier 1. The prime powers of the form 2k + 1 are the Fermat primes together with 9 (a prime forces k a power of 2, and Mihailescu leaves 9 the only proper power), so the whole excess supply is the five known Fermat primes and 9, and the open finiteness question is the Fermat primes' own. The inequality reads a door's size and never its shape, so no residue-degree or lifting-the-exponent route escapes it. The carrier condition is on a NORM, so the supply is prime powers, not primes: the p = 2 rung at V = 1 is carried by 9, an inert place over 3 in a quadratic field, a rung a census over primes certifies dead. Over that widened supply the ladder at the pinned cap dies anyway, every candidate printed with its factorization: p = 2 climbs V = 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 through the norms 9, 17, 97, 193, 257 and misses everything under the door 2048 (513 = 3³·19, 1025 = 5²·41, 1537 = 29·53); p = 3 dies at V = 2 and p = 5 at V = 3. The death is terminal: a stuck V is a stuck door, every later move sits under one fixed ceiling, and a place picked infinitely often at bounded cost absorbs the tail. So a characteristic-zero trajectory with a rank-1 place over 2, 3 or 5 LOCKS.

Scope. The inequality and the three deaths are proved, the door values transferring because a rank-1 completion is Qp with Zp's unit filtration verbatim; checked against 5,656 greedy moves × 46 unseated primes and a brute scan of the 26,152 prime powers below 300,000. The excess here is a valuation gap and not the ghost count the lock-prime law bounds; the ghost mechanism was the proposal this inequality refutes.

The characteristic sweep rule

At the pinned cap the affordable carriers at rung V are exactly the p − 1 numbers m·pV+1 + 1, 1 ≤ m ≤ p − 1, so each characteristic is a direct O(p)-per-rung walk. All 167 odd primes below 1000 reach a death rung, one where every candidate is a certified non-prime-power, and the rung is erratic: D(19) = D(23) = 1 against D(719) = 62, and D(17) = 2 beside D(29) = 8. The odd multipliers contribute nothing, since for odd p they make the carrier even, so only a power of 2 could qualify and none does; the effective supply is the ⌊(p−1)/2⌋ even multipliers. Under the conjunction the sweep buys a statement about rings: closing one of a ring's rank-1 characteristics closes the ring, so every characteristic-zero ring with a rank-1 characteristic below 1000 has its boundary closed, 2 by the inequality above.

Scope. Verified p ≤ 1000 at a rung ceiling of 120. The test is primality plus exact integer roots and never factorization, which is load-bearing: a test that factors reports not-a-prime-power when it merely fails to certify, and at candidates of 183 digits that manufactures false deaths. Open: the rings whose rank-1 characteristics all exceed 1000.

The loosened ladder rule

Charging the door on a generator widens the multiplier's range by the factor τ, and the ladder is delayed by the widening, not saved. Re-walked with the multiplier allowed up to τ·p, all 167 odd characteristics below 1000 still die at τ = 2, all 124 below 700 at τ = 3 and all 94 below 500 at τ = 4; the death rung deepens by about the factor, 1.97, 2.82 and 3.61 times on average, each over its own range, and always arrives, 62 at p = 719 becoming 116 at p = 859. The parity half stays empty: one odd-multiplier carrier is accepted across all four walks, 64 = 7·3² + 1 at p = 3, the power of 2 the parity argument demands. The factor is exact for a quadratic field: ideal classes are the reduced binary quadratic forms of the discriminant and a class's least norm is its form's leading coefficient, so τK is the largest leading coefficient, at Q(√−23) exactly 2 from the forms (1,1,6) and (2,±1,3), against a Minkowski bound of 3.05. That 2 is not the element census's 4 a move against an ideal 2, which is a lock price (the element price) and agrees by coincidence.

Scope. The bounds fall as τ rises because the walk costs about τ³; no walk at any bound has produced a survivor. What a wider τ does not buy is fields: over the 305 fundamental discriminants down to −1000 the factor averages 9.58, only 11.8% of fields have one of 4 or less, and it sits at 0.705 of its Minkowski bound on average, so the factor genuinely grows. Read per place instead: the padding at a characteristic runs over the powers of that place's own class, so the factor is 1 whenever the place is PRINCIPAL, and there the unwidened walk closes the element dynamics outright. Which characteristics of a field are principal is Places.

The residual

A ring is closed the moment one of its rank-1 characteristics is, so the survivors are the rings whose rank-1 characteristics all exceed 1000. Write L(K) for a field's least rank-1 characteristic. A prime fails to be rank-1 in a quadratic field in two ways: it is ramified, dividing the discriminant, or inert, the field's one place above it of degree 2. Pushing L up means buying every prime below it out of contention one way or the other, and the two prices differ: ramification is paid in discriminant, inertness in a congruence. Everything below carries to the least PRINCIPAL rank-1 characteristic, the quantity the element world is closed by, since it is in particular a rank-1 one.

The inhabited residual theorem

The residual has members at every bound. Let d = 2·3·5·⋯·P and K = Q(√d). Since d ≡ 2 mod 4 the discriminant is 4d, so every prime up to P divides it, ramifies, and is not rank-1: L(K) > P. Raising the sweep's bound raises the bar and never clears it, so a close covering every ring at once is a theorem's job and not a longer computation's. A density argument cannot see the construction: a degree-n field's rank-1 characteristics carry density at least 1/n among the primes, a point stabilizer having index n, which proves L finite for every field and says nothing about where it sits, a density being a statement about the tail that survives deleting any finite set of primes. The ramified primes are such a set, and they are exactly what L is at the mercy of.

Scope. Proved for every P; the rig controls it at every odd P ≤ 97. Degree 2 throughout. It closes no characteristic and frees no ring: the survivors are inhabited, not shown to escape.

The target is density zero rule

A uniform close is formally weaker than a death rung at every characteristic. A ring closes as soon as one of its rank-1 characteristics does, and those carry positive density in every field, so a set of undying primes of density ZERO could not contain all the rank-1 characteristics of any field: density zero closes every characteristic-zero ring, and "every odd p has a death rung" is strictly stronger than the job. The weakening is a fact about the statement and not about the work: no argument built on small-prime divisibility separates the two targets (the shape of a death rung).

Scope. Proved by hand from the conjunction and the density bound; not run.

The entry price rule

Retail, the residual is cheap. Sweeping every fundamental discriminant to 108 gives 15 champions, the least |D| attaining each record L, reaching L = 127 at D = 46,305,413, where log|D| = 17.65 sits beside π(<127)·ln 2 = 20.79 against the primorial witness's 107.07 over the same primes: the existence proof pays 6.07 times the exponent for the same guarantee, and the gap is a logarithm, a witness bought by ramification costing about P in the exponent where one bought by inertness costs P/ln P. The champion law log|D| ≈ ln 2·π(L) describes the measured family and bounds nothing: inverted it is implicit, and the explicit expansion in |D| alone reads 71 where the top champion has 127. What makes the retail case cheap is decidability: L(K) is computed, a walk up the primes testing one residue condition each, and the ladder is then run at that single characteristic. Exactly two champions buy in with no ramified prime below L, D = −67 and D = −163: that is Rabinowitsch's condition, their polynomials Euler's prime-generating x² + x + 17 and x² + x + 41, reached by a rig carrying no class field theory and therefore a control on the sweep.

Scope. Verified over every fundamental discriminant to 108, degree 2. For an arbitrary field what is available is conditional, effective Chebotarev under GRH giving L ≪ (log|disc|)². A prime fails to be rank-1 with probability (p+1)/2p, the inert (p−1)/2p plus 1/p from ramification, so ramification is a bonus term on inertness, worth ∏(1 + 1/p) over the range, and none of the champions past L = 41 is ramification-free; the Euler pair is not evidence that the unramified route closes at 41.

The shape of a death rung theorem

THE EXPONENT IDENTITY: a carrier qa at rung V has a equal to the multiplicative order of q mod pV+1, the least e with qe ≡ 1 there, exactly: otherwise the order itself already clears the modulus and the carrier, at least its square, clears the door. Hence a divides p − 1, q < p, and a ≥ V + 2, confining the proper prime powers to an enumerable set, and across every rung of all 167 ladders exactly one inhabits it, 35 = 2·11² + 1 at p = 11, which saturates the divisibility that produced the confinement, (35−1)/2 = 121 = 11². A death is therefore a finite check plus one question: is there a PRIME ≡ 1 mod pV+1 below pV+2. AND THE RUNG IS INVISIBLE TO EVERY SMALL-PRIME INSTRUMENT: a prime r ≠ p divides m·pV+1 + 1 for exactly one class of m mod r at every rung alike, so the multipliers left after sifting by every prime below z number about (p−1)∏(1−1/r), with no V in it, while the carriers thin like 1/V. Covering congruences act through exactly that sifted set, and averaging inherits the defect, a bound that does not fall with the rung bounding a sum over R rungs by R times its first value. So no argument built on small-prime divisibility separates the density-zero target from the universal one, and what suffices for both is the expected-order bound on the least prime ≡ 1 mod pV+1.

Scope. The identity and the invisibility are proved for every odd p, every rung and every sifting bound. Measured p ≤ 1000: pooled over the 144 primes whose death rung is at least 10, the carrier density across rungs 1 to 10 falls by 0.245 against the heuristic 0.25, while the sifted count moves by 1.025 and holds the level 0.1219 Mertens' theorem gives, the absolute agreement being what says the sifted count is the Mertens count. Sifting by every prime below 104 empties no death rung at any swept p ≥ 100; a covering that closed a rung would need its moduli to multiply past p − 1.

So the boundary is closed retail and open wholesale: a computation that has terminated on every input tried, and no theorem covering every ring at once. The analytic route stays short for a plain reason: the carrier window is linear in the modulus, where the least prime in an arithmetic progression is placed only below a power of the modulus, Linnik's exponent unconditionally and about the square up to logarithms under the generalized Riemann hypothesis. The currency the boundary finally pays in is the Fermat primes, which the corpus reaches a second time by an unrelated road: they are also where the phoenix criterion lands the cheapest escape from death.

Places

Which characteristics a walk closes cheaply is decided by whether their places are principal. Over an imaginary quadratic field the least principal rank-1 characteristic has a floor, none sitting below |D|/4, so at any sweep bound that reading closes finitely many fields, though at least 23 times as many as the widened walk's factor-4 route; the floor is made of the unit rank and vanishes over a real field, where the same reading runs at nearly full coverage. Principal places also fall more evenly than chance, at degree 2 and, once one cubic class number is separated into the two arithmetics a totally split prime's triple of classes allows, at degree 3: Places.
