The Tower

The primorial tower is the sequence of rings built from the first k primes. Each rung adds one prime, one independent channel, one sieve step. This page shows what you get at each rung and how the structure scales.

Two growth modes

Adding a prime either changes lambda or doesn't. This creates two modes:

Complexity — a non-transparent prime introduces a new prime-power factor to lambda. New dynamical structure: new orbit periods, new order levels. Lambda jumps. Examples: 11 (jump ×5), 17 (jump ×4), 23 (jump ×11).

Capacity — a transparent prime adds space without new complexity. Phi grows, idempotents double, but lambda stays the same. The dynamical skeleton is unchanged. Example: 13, whose φ(13) = 12 already divides lambda = 60.

Transparency criterion: prime p is transparent at rung k iff (p−1) divides λ(k−1).

Lambda plateaus occur when consecutive primes are all transparent. k = 10–14 is a 5-rung plateau at λ = 55,440. By k = 2000, 77% of primes are transparent, the longest plateau is 24 consecutive primes (k = 1849–1872), and the non-transparent fraction decays as approximately k−0.17.

ECC rate scales

At every rung k ≥ 7, designate the last 3 channels as parity and the rest as data. Any (k−3) of k channels reconstruct the original. The code is MDS (distance 4) at every rung — optimal.

Rate approaches 1 as the tower grows. No blind spots at any verified rung (k = 7–10): parity primes are larger than data primes, so no data idempotent can vanish mod a parity prime.

The k = 7 rung

Most interactive demos on this site use k = 7: the ring Z/510,510. Seven channels give MDS ECC at rate 4/7, the ring is small enough for exhaustive computation (510,510 elements, 92,160 units), and the 4+3 split is the first with distance 4.

Z/510,510 ≅ Z/2 × Z/3 × Z/5 × Z/7 × Z/11 × Z/13 × Z/17

The Cunningham construction

The first 7 primes are uniquely determined by a single map. Start with seed 2 and apply c(n) = 2n+1. Two chains emerge, each terminating at a composite:

From 1: 1 → 3 → 7 → 15 (stop: 15 = 3×5)
From 2: 2 → 5 → 11 → 23 → 47 → 95 (stop: 95 = 5×19)

Five primes ≤ 17: {2, 3, 5, 7, 11}. Two more close the set: 13 = 2²+3² and 17 = 2+3+5+7. Why seed 2 is forced: any prime > 2 is odd, so 2n+1 is even and ≥ 4 — composite. The only even prime is 2.
