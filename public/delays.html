The library and the roster The delay engines

The delay engines

The engines that read the delay of an on-line unit: the many-stream engine, the piecewise clause tables, and the lookahead table that prints a unit's delay from its rate and its range.

An on-line unit reads its operands most significant digit first, through a signed digit set, and emits its result the same way; what it costs is how many input digits it must see past each output digit. Each script below is served verbatim at /files/, is the full record of the claim it verifies, prints its own checks in one Python 3 process, needs no package outside the standard library, and finds a sibling it imports beside itself; its run record states its own peak and wall time. What is proved lists every claim with its script.

The many-stream delay

A map of digit streams, each read through a signed digit set from its most significant digit, is local at lookahead c when its output's first t digits are a function of the inputs' first t + c. Over the digit set {−a−..a+} at radix b a stream's values fill [−M−, M+], M± = a±/(b − 1) (Mm and Mp below), and the window is the product of those intervals over the streams. Every C² map of any number of streams that is not affine on the window is local at exactly the margin, the least lookahead the law below grants, a law in its box width rate Λ, the sup over the window of the gradient's L¹ norm Σ|∂if|, ρ = a− + a+ + 1 − b the slack of the digit set (its digits beyond the radix), at least 1, and o the lead its range sets (the output's first digit o positions above the input's), the least L = c + o with bLρ ≥ Λ(a− + a+) (the many-stream theorem, proved; the polynomial maps, the square root, the reciprocal and the divider its specimens). The engine in explore_manystream_delay.py reads that delay and its certificate, the prefix at which a reader one lookahead short dies, for a map of d streams you hand it. A map is a class with three attributes, name, d and e, an integer direction along which the Hessian is nonzero, and ten methods, exact where a verdict rests on them: image(us, n), the integer ends and common denominator (lo, hi, S) of the image over the box of the n-digit prefixes us; approx(xs), a float; window_range(Mm, Mp), the range's ends as fractions, for the lead; law_ok(b, rho, wsum, L), the margin law at L in exact arithmetic; lam(), Λ; grad_iv(box) and hess_e_iv(box, e), interval bounds on the partials and on eTHe over a float box; grad(xs) and hess_e(xs, e), the same at a point, for the spot check; and peak(Mm, Mp), the corner where Λ sits. The five classes in the file — the product, the multiply–add, x² + y, x² + xy and the divider x/(s + y) at a pole distance P, the least value of its denominator over the window, which fixes the shift s = M− + P — are templates. With least_lead and law_L reading the lead and the least L the law admits, written L*, ReaderD(b, am, ap, c, map, o, lam_law) is the game — a reader at lookahead c and lead o against an adversary choosing the streams — lam_law, optional, the law's threshold on the box width rate at the L under test: scan(budget, region), with region true, walks the excess region, the prefix boxes whose width rate exceeds that threshold, pruned by interval bounds on partial tuples, and returns the least output depth at which some box's image strictly contains an overlap zone, the interval two consecutive output cells share, with the input depth, the prefixes read and the count at that depth, or None first when the budget runs out; certificate_depth(budget, confined), with confined true, plays the exhaustive game with the adversary's moves confined to the region, returning its kill round, the output depth at which the reader dies, and the input depth searched. Cube finds the ball where every partial is one-signed and the Hessian is bounded away from zero, LineRegion the proof's constant N₀ along the lattice line through it, and one_pair(b, am, ap, map, label, o), the label and the lead optional, prints the whole certificate line, its last field the proof's discrepancy check, the discrepancy D_N against its bound. The run at radices 2..5 takes 43 s and 51 MB; the calls below took under a second.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_manystream_delay import Division, SquareCross, ReaderD, least_lead, law_L, one_pair
>>> f = Division(2, 1, 1, Fraction(2))     # x/(s + y) at radix 2 over {-1..1}, P = 2
>>> o, L = least_lead(2, 1, 1, f), law_L(2, 1, 1, f)
>>> o, L, L - o                            # lead, L*, lookahead c*
(-1, 1, 2)
>>> W = 2.0; lam_law = (W - 1) * 2 ** (L - 1) / W   # W the window width (a- + a+)/(b - 1); the threshold at L* - 1
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o - 1, f, o, lam_law).scan(400_000, region=True)
(3, 4, 83, 5)                             # dead at c* - 1: output depth 3, input depth 4
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o, f, o).scan(150_000, region=False)
(None, 7, 86309, 65025)                   # alive at c* through input depth 7
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o - 1, f, o, lam_law).certificate_depth(150_000, confined=True)
(3, 4)                                    # the confined tree agrees: round 3, searched to 4
>>> r = one_pair(3, 2, 2, SquareCross(3, 2, 2))
  (3,2,2) : o=1 L*=2 c*=1 | L*-1: g=1.667 cube r=0.125 e=(1, 0) kappa=2 Delta=0.25 N_0=6.62e+04 [H=64] kill t=2 n=2 (region 1; line holds 1, before 0); tree 2 [3] | L*: scan clean n<=4, tree survives [2] | P-D n=10 N=14761: D_N=0.004 vs B=0.96; |S_h|/vdC=0.00,0.00,0.00 (bound below N at 3 of 3)

The piecewise delay

A continuous map of one stream that is C² on each of finitely many pieces, its kinks rational and its affine pieces' slopes and offsets rational, has its delay decided by three finite clauses: a curved piece's sup of |f′| against the law's threshold; an affine piece's image ends, whose phase runs through a lattice that cycles in the depth, checked over one period of that cycle; and the prefixes whose boxes straddle a kink, those containing it, at the depths below that period (the piecewise theorem, proved). Two engines read it. In explore_piecewise_delay.py, the piecewise-affine case, two_piece(s_left, s_right, kappa, d_left) builds a continuous two-piece map, the right offset forced by the kink; Table(b, am, ap, f) is the clause table at one digit system, verdict(L) which pieces and kinks kill at L, with the cycle's depths (where every affine piece holds a full period, where the check ends, and the depth the cycle returns to) and floor(Lstar) the least L with every clause empty; engine_floor(b, am, ap, f, o, Lstar) plays the game at the lead o, the region scan and the confined tree, and returns the certified floor with the verdict at each L read, a kill as its output depth, input depth, prefix and whether the prefix straddles. In explore_piecewise_smooth.py the pieces are polynomials of degree at most 3, monotone on each: PolyPieces(coeffs, kinks, Mm, Mp, name) takes one coefficient list per piece, low to high, and the kinks in order; TableS adds the curved clause, its depths the first two and a fourth field listing straddlers that killed with no adjacent piece's clause firing (empty, as the theorem says), marking a piece with the string tag 'a' when its sup decides or 'b' when its lattice does and a kink by the depth at which it first fires; specimen_below(Mm, Mp) builds, at the window [−Mm, Mp], the file's specimen that reads below the margin, a curved piece beside the identity, and engine_floor is the game. The runs at radices 2..5 take 338 s and 213 s; the calls below took under four seconds.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_piecewise_delay import two_piece, Table, engine_floor, law_L
>>> from explore_onestream_delay import least_lead
>>> f = two_piece(Fraction(1, 2), 1, 0, 0)        # x/2 on x <= 0, x on x >= 0
>>> law_L(5, 2, 3, f), least_lead(5, 2, 3, f)      # L* and the lead at radix 5 over {-2..3}
(1, 0)
>>> Table(5, 2, 3, f).verdict(0)                  # at L = 0 the slope-1/2 piece kills, the kink is empty
({0: True, 1: False}, {0: False}, (2, 2, 2))
>>> engine_floor(5, 2, 3, f, 0, 1)[0]              # the game agrees: floor 1, equal to the margin's L*
1
>>> from explore_piecewise_smooth import specimen_below, TableS, engine_floor as engine_floor_s
>>> g = specimen_below(Fraction(1, 2), 1)         # x/5 + x^2/20 on x <= 0, x on x >= 0, at radix 3 over {-1..2}
>>> g.sup, TableS(3, 1, 2, g).verdict(0)          # the sup per piece; every clause empty at L = 0
([Fraction(1, 5), Fraction(1, 1)], ({0: None, 1: None}, {0: None}, (2, 2), []))
>>> TableS(3, 1, 2, g).verdict(-1)                # at L = -1 the sup, the identity's lattice and the kink all fire
({0: 'a', 1: 'b'}, {0: 0}, (2, 2), [])
>>> engine_floor_s(3, 1, 2, g, 0, 1)[0]            # the game's floor 0, one below the margin
0

The lookahead table

The law above makes a unit's delay a function of its box width rate Λ and its range, whose ends set the lead. So the delays derived unit by unit for hardware — the multiplier's, the square root's, the divider's, each from its own selection rule — have their floors in one table, and explore_lookahead_table.py prints it. table(b, am, ap, P) takes the radix, the digit set {−a−..a+} and, for the units with a pole, the pole distance P, the least value the radicand or the denominator takes over the window, and returns for each of six units — the product, the multiply–add, x² + y, the reciprocal 1/(s + x), the root √(s + x) and the divider x/(s + y) — the lead o, the law's L*, the lookahead c* = L* − o, and the value of the lead law: at a unit whose range's top sets its lead (a positive range, or a multiplied stream's), c* sits within one digit of logb(ΛM+(a− + a+)/(Gρ)), M+ = a+/(b − 1) a stream's top and G the range's, so the map enters only through its relative gradient Λ/G, and a unit runs ahead without bound (c* → −∞) exactly where that ratio falls to 0 along its family — the reciprocal and the root as P grows, the divider never (a property, both halves closed forms; explore_lead_law.py, explore_divider_lookahead.py). The floor is the many-stream theorem's, proved at every contiguous digit set of slack at least 1; Unit(name, lam, lo, hi, sq) is the template for a unit of your own, its rate and its range's ends as exact rationals, with sq true for a root, whose rate and ends are passed squared, and unit_row(b, am, ap, unit) returns its row.

Three columns stand beside the table. chain_rows(b, am, ap) is the chain column, one unit's digit stream fed to a second: for the multiplier feeding an adder, a product consumer (xy)z and a sum-fed consumer z(x + y) it returns the naive delay, the sum of the two units' floors, and the floor the escape law admits, marking a chain that reads below the sum; at slack 1 the mark is the threshold-miss law, a gain exactly where the consumer's box width rate Λ₂ exceeds its threshold at L = 1, which is 1 at slack 1, by at most a budget of order 1/b that the producer's floor leaves; with M̂ = max(M−, M+), the producer's top M̂² fed a product at lead 0 and 2M̂/b fed a sum at lead 1, Λ₂ = M̂ + M̂² and M̂(1 + 2/b), so the threshold is met at M̂ = 1/φ and at b/(b + 2), and fed a product the law reads 0 < Λ₂ − 1 ≤ (M̂/b)(1 − 2M̂/b); a consumer whose range does not fit the cell is absent from the rows (a property, the gain cells a rule to radix 40; explore_threshold_miss.py). beta_row(B, f) is the Pisot column, the law read in a base β that is not an integer — a Pisot unit, the streams and the output over {−a..a} — and certify_beta(B, f) its level walk at L* − 1, which carries one number per box and no game and returns the round at which a reader dies whose digit is forced at every box, the one output digit whose cell holds the box's image, the boxes walked and the round reached, that round the game certificate's at 22 of 23 decided pairs and one earlier at the plastic base's x² + y; gap_row(b, D, f) is the gap-set column, the law over a digit set with a gap, written bLz ≥ Λw with w the digit span over b − 1 and z the least zone two sibling cells share (ρ/(b − 1) at a contiguous set, the law above), and certify_gap(b, D, f, L) the confined tree that decides the delay below it, returning the kill round, the rounds completed and the nodes. Both carry the many-stream law to those numerations, where the delay is a rule at the pairs certified and the law's sufficiency a property. The run at radices 2..5, which checks every closed form against the engines' own loops at 243 pairs, takes 1 s and 74 MB; the calls below took under half a second.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_lookahead_table import table, chain_rows, beta_base, beta_row, certify_beta, gap_row, certify_gap
>>> t = table(4, 2, 2, Fraction(1, 3))         # radix 4 over {-2..2}, the pole units at P = 1/3
>>> t["x y"], t["x/(s+y)"]                      # (lead, L*, lookahead, the lead law's value)
((0, 2, 2, 1.5), (1, 3, 2, 1.7924812503605783))
>>> table(4, 2, 2, 32)["1/(s+x)"]               # the reciprocal far from its pole emits two digits before it reads
(-2, -4, -2, -1.7924812503605783)
>>> chain_rows(4, 2, 2)                         # naive, floor, gain, the threshold-miss law's verdict
{'(x y) + z': (4, 4, False, None), '(x y) z': (4, 3, True, True), 'z (x + y)': (3, 3, False, False)}
>>> import explore_beta_delay as bd
>>> beta = beta_base("golden")
>>> beta_row(beta, bd.BProduct(beta)), certify_beta(beta, bd.BProduct(beta))
((4, 1, 3), (5, 1976, 5))                       # L* = 4 at lead 1; the walk at L = 3 kills at round 5, 1976 boxes
>>> import explore_manystream_delay as ms
>>> f = ms.Product(3, 3, 2)
>>> gap_row(3, (-3, -1, 0, 1, 2), f), certify_gap(3, (-3, -1, 0, 1, 2), f, 2)
((3, 1, Fraction(1, 2)), (4, 3, 36539))         # L* = 3, lead 1, least zone 1/2; the tree at L = 2 kills at round 4
