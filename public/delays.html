The library and the roster The delay engines

The delay engines

The engines that read the delay of an on-line unit: the many-stream engine, the piecewise clause tables, and the lookahead table that prints a unit's delay from two numbers of its map.

An on-line unit reads its operands most significant digit first, through a signed digit set, and emits its result the same way; what it costs is how many input digits it must see past each output digit. Each script below is served verbatim at /files/, is the full record of the claim it verifies, prints its own checks in one Python 3 process, needs no package outside the standard library, and finds a sibling it imports beside itself; its run record states its own peak and wall time. Each entry says what to run, what to pass in and what it printed here; the pages that state the result and its tier are linked from each entry, and What is proved lists every claim with its script.

The many-stream delay

A map of digit streams, each read through a signed digit set from its most significant digit, is local at lookahead c when its output's first t digits are a function of the inputs' first t + c, and every C² map of any number of streams that is not affine on the window is local at exactly the margin, the least lookahead the law below grants, in its box width rate: with Λ the sup over the window of the gradient's L¹ norm Σ|∂if|, ρ the slack of the digit set {−a−..a+} (its digits beyond the radix) and o the lead its range sets (the output's first digit o positions above the input's), the least L = c + o with bLρ ≥ Λ(a− + a+) (the many-stream theorem, proved; the polynomial maps, the square root, the reciprocal and the divider its specimens). The engine in explore_manystream_delay.py reads that delay and its certificate, the prefix at which a reader one lookahead short dies, for a map of d streams you hand it. A map is a class with three attributes, name, d and e, an integer direction along which the Hessian is nonzero, and ten methods, exact where a verdict rests on them: image(us, n), the integer ends and common denominator (lo, hi, S) of the image over the box of the n-digit prefixes us; approx(xs), a float; window_range(Mm, Mp), the range's ends as fractions, for the lead; law_ok(b, rho, wsum, L), the margin law at L in exact arithmetic; lam(), Λ; grad_iv(box) and hess_e_iv(box, e), interval bounds on the partials and on eTHe over a float box; grad(xs) and hess_e(xs, e), the same at a point, for the spot check; and peak(Mm, Mp), the corner where Λ sits. The five classes in the file — the product, the multiply–add, x² + y, x² + xy and the divider x/(s + y) at a pole distance, the least value of its denominator over the window — are templates. With least_lead and law_L reading the lead and the least L the law admits, written L*, ReaderD(b, am, ap, c, map, o, lam_law) is the game — a reader at lookahead c and lead o against an adversary choosing the streams — lam_law, optional, the law's threshold on the box width rate at the L under test: scan(budget, region=True) walks the excess region, the prefix boxes whose width rate exceeds that threshold, pruned by interval bounds on partial tuples, and returns the least output depth at which some box's image strictly contains an overlap zone, the interval two consecutive output cells share, with the input depth, the prefixes read and the count at that depth, or None first when the budget runs out; certificate_depth(budget, confined=True) plays the exhaustive game with the adversary's moves confined to the region, returning its round and the depth searched. Cube finds the ball where every partial is one-signed and the Hessian is bounded away from zero, LineRegion the proof's constant N₀ along the lattice line through it, and one_pair(b, am, ap, map, label), the label optional, prints the whole certificate line, its last field the proof's discrepancy check, DN against its bound B. The run at radices 2..5 takes 40 s and 52 MB; the calls below took under a second.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_manystream_delay import Division, SquareCross, ReaderD, least_lead, law_L, one_pair
>>> f = Division(2, 1, 1, Fraction(2))     # x/(s + y) at radix 2 over {-1..1}, P = 2
>>> o, L = least_lead(2, 1, 1, f), law_L(2, 1, 1, f)
>>> o, L, L - o                            # lead, L*, lookahead c*
(-1, 1, 2)
>>> W = 2.0; lam_law = (W - 1) * 2 ** (L - 1) / W   # W the window width (a- + a+)/(b - 1); the threshold at L* - 1
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o - 1, f, o, lam_law).scan(400_000, region=True)
(3, 4, 83, 5)                             # dead at c* - 1: output depth 3, input depth 4
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o, f, o).scan(150_000, region=False)
(None, 7, 86309, 65025)                   # alive at c* through input depth 7
>>> ReaderD(2, 1, 1, L - o - 1, f, o, lam_law).certificate_depth(150_000, confined=True)
(3, 4)                                    # the confined tree agrees: round 3, searched to 4
>>> r = one_pair(3, 2, 2, SquareCross(3, 2, 2))
  (3,2,2) : o=1 L*=2 c*=1 | L*-1: g=1.667 cube r=0.125 e=(1, 0) kappa=2 Delta=0.25 N_0=6.62e+04 [H=64] kill t=2 n=2 (region 1; line holds 1, before 0); tree 2 [3] | L*: scan clean n<=4, tree survives [2] | P-D n=10 N=14761: D_N=0.004 vs B=0.96; |S_h|/vdC=0.00,0.00,0.00 (bound below N at 3 of 3)

The piecewise delay

A map of one stream that is C² on each of finitely many pieces, its affine data rational, has its delay decided by three finite clauses: a curved piece's sup of |f′| against the law's threshold, an affine piece's lattice over one period, and a kink's straddlers at the depths below that period (the piecewise theorem, proved). Two engines read it. In explore_piecewise_delay.py, the piecewise-affine case, two_piece(s_left, s_right, kappa, d_left) builds a continuous two-piece map, the right offset forced by the kink; Table(b, am, ap, f) is the clause table at one digit system, verdict(L) which pieces and kinks kill at L with the lattice cycle's depths and floor(Lstar) the least L with every clause empty; engine_floor(b, am, ap, f, o, Lstar) plays the game at the lead o, the region scan and the confined tree, and returns the certified floor with the verdict at each L read, a kill as its output depth, input depth, prefix and whether the prefix straddles. In explore_piecewise_smooth.py the pieces are polynomials of degree at most 3, monotone on each: PolyPieces(coeffs, kinks, Mm, Mp, name) takes one coefficient list per piece, low to high, and the kinks in order; TableS adds the curved clause, marking a piece with the string tag 'a' when its sup decides or 'b' when its lattice does and a kink by the depth at which it first fires; specimen_below builds the file's specimen that reads below the margin, a curved piece beside the identity, and engine_floor is the game. The runs at radices 2..5 take 338 s and 213 s; the calls below took under four seconds.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_piecewise_delay import two_piece, Table, engine_floor, law_L
>>> from explore_onestream_delay import least_lead
>>> f = two_piece(Fraction(1, 2), 1, 0, 0)        # x/2 on x <= 0, x on x >= 0
>>> law_L(5, 2, 3, f), least_lead(5, 2, 3, f)      # L* and the lead at radix 5 over {-2..3}
(1, 0)
>>> Table(5, 2, 3, f).verdict(0)                  # at L = 0 the slope-1/2 piece kills, the kink is empty
({0: True, 1: False}, {0: False}, (2, 2, 2))
>>> engine_floor(5, 2, 3, f, 0, 1)[0]              # the game agrees: floor 1, equal to the margin's L*
1
>>> from explore_piecewise_smooth import specimen_below, TableS, engine_floor as engine_floor_s
>>> g = specimen_below(Fraction(1, 2), 1)         # x/5 + x^2/20 on x <= 0, x on x >= 0, at radix 3 over {-1..2}
>>> g.sup, TableS(3, 1, 2, g).verdict(0)          # the sup per piece; every clause empty at L = 0
([Fraction(1, 5), Fraction(1, 1)], ({0: None, 1: None}, {0: None}, (2, 2), []))
>>> TableS(3, 1, 2, g).verdict(-1)                # at L = -1 the sup, the identity's lattice and the kink all fire
({0: 'a', 1: 'b'}, {0: 0}, (2, 2), [])
>>> engine_floor_s(3, 1, 2, g, 0, 1)[0]            # the game's floor 0, one below the margin
0

The lookahead table

The law above makes a unit's delay a function of two numbers of its map, its box width rate Λ and the ends of its range, which set the lead. So the delays the hardware literature derives unit by unit — the multiplier's, the square root's, the divider's, each from its own selection rule — are one table, and explore_lookahead_table.py prints it. table(b, am, ap, P) takes the radix, the digit set {−a−..a+} and, for the units with a pole, the pole distance P, the least value the radicand or the denominator takes over the window, and returns for each of six units — the product, the multiply–add, x² + y, the reciprocal 1/(s + x), the root √(s + x) and the divider x/(s + y) — the lead o, the law's L*, the lookahead c* = L* − o, and the value of the lead law: c* sits within one digit of logb(ΛM+(a− + a+)/(Gρ)), M+ = a+/(b − 1) the window's top and G the range's, so the map enters only through its relative gradient Λ/G, and a unit emits before it reads (c* < 0) exactly where that ratio falls along its family — the reciprocal and the root without bound as P grows, the divider never (a property, both halves closed forms; explore_lead_law.py, explore_divider_lookahead.py). The floor is the many-stream theorem's, proved at every contiguous digit set; Unit(name, lam, lo, hi) is the template for a unit of your own, its rate and its range's ends as exact rationals.

Three columns stand beside the table. chain_rows(b, am, ap) is the chain column, one unit's digit stream fed to a second: for the multiplier feeding an adder, a product consumer (xy)z and a sum-fed consumer z(x + y) it prints the naive delay, the sum of the two units' floors, and the floor the escape law admits, marking a chain that reads below the sum; at slack 1 the mark is the threshold-miss law, a gain exactly where the consumer's own threshold is missed by at most the producer's budget (the product-fed root 1/φ, the sum-fed b/(b + 2); a property, the gain cells a rule to radix 40; explore_threshold_miss.py). beta_row(B, f) is the Pisot column, the law read in a base β that is not an integer — a Pisot unit, the streams and the output over {−a..a} — and certify_beta(B, f) its certifier, the level walk at L* − 1, which carries one number per box and no game and returns the round at which a reader with its digit forced at every box dies, the boxes walked and the round reached; gap_row(b, D, f) is the gap-set column, the law over a digit set D with a gap, the slack replaced by the least overlap of the set's cells, and certify_gap(b, D, f, L) the confined tree that decides the delay below it, returning the kill round, the rounds completed and the nodes. Both are the many-stream theorem's clauses for those numerations, where the delay is a rule at the pairs certified and the law's sufficiency a property. The run at radices 2..5, which checks every closed form against the engines' own loops at 243 pairs, takes 1 s and 74 MB; the calls below took under half a second.

>>> from fractions import Fraction
>>> from explore_lookahead_table import table, chain_rows, beta_base, beta_row, certify_beta, gap_row, certify_gap
>>> t = table(4, 2, 2, Fraction(1, 3))         # radix 4 over {-2..2}, the pole units at P = 1/3
>>> t["x y"], t["x/(s+y)"]                      # (lead, L*, lookahead, the lead law's value)
((0, 2, 2, 1.5), (1, 3, 2, 1.7924812503605783))
>>> table(4, 2, 2, 32)["1/(s+x)"]               # the reciprocal at a deep pole emits two digits before it reads
(-2, -4, -2, -1.7924812503605783)
>>> chain_rows(4, 2, 2)                         # naive, floor, gain, the threshold-miss law's verdict
{'(x y) + z': (4, 4, False, None), '(x y) z': (4, 3, True, True), 'z (x + y)': (3, 3, False, False)}
>>> import explore_beta_delay as bd
>>> B = beta_base("golden")
>>> beta_row(B, bd.BProduct(B)), certify_beta(B, bd.BProduct(B))
((4, 1, 3), (5, 1976, 5))                       # L* = 4 at lead 1; the walk at L = 3 kills at round 5, 1976 boxes
>>> import explore_manystream_delay as ms
>>> f = ms.Product(3, 3, 2)
>>> gap_row(3, (-3, -1, 0, 1, 2), f), certify_gap(3, (-3, -1, 0, 1, 2), f, 2)
((3, 1, Fraction(1, 2)), (4, 3, 36539))         # L* = 3, lead 1, least zone 1/2; the tree at L = 2 kills at round 4
