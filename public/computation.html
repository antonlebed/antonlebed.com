Computation

The growing tower read as a machine: the depth face decidable, one deleted move short of universal; the element face universal with nothing imported; and the deletion paid back in algebra.

  • The static dividend — what the deletions pay back with no run: the predicate perimeter, the ruler ladder, the reversible core and the canonical forms
  • The recovery chart — what buys universality back, priced in re-imports
  • The delay line — the growing machine's normal form, and the supply's own arithmetic as the channel the capped side leaves open

The machine is the growth apparatus of the Growth section: the tower Z/pk#, the integers modulo the product of the first k primes, growing one prime at a time. Its state is a modulus N read as a depth vector: each prime p is a window, and window p carries the depth vp(N), the exponent of p in N. A push by m multiplies N by m; every growth move and every intervention is a push. The presence test "p ∤ N" is the read that admits p as a new window. The size of an integer is its archimedean place, the one place the construction deletes (The Object). A zero-test is a branch on a counter being zero, consulted as often as the program likes; a borrow is a move that lowers a counter while a test reads the value it descends through. The tower has two faces: the depth face, moduli and their monotone growth, is this page's machine; the element face, residues inside a fixed or growing modulus, owns subtraction natively and is the recovery chart's. Universality throughout means Minsky's: two counters with increment, decrement and an exact zero-test compute everything computable (Minsky 1967), and every decidability entry names the result that owns it.

The machine and the second door

The growth machine: increment and zero-test with no decrement rule

Every move multiplies N, so the depth vector rises component-wise and nothing lowers a depth. INC(p) is a push by p; the presence test is an exact zero-test on depth(p); DEC is absent. A counter machine whose counters only rise is decided on the finite quotient Q × {0, +}k of control states and zero-patterns: each zero-test flips once, the zero-pattern changes at most k times, and a depth-first search over the quotient settles programs whose forward simulation never ends. Restore the decrement and the same quotient goes unsound: a machine that halts exactly when its counter starts even sends c0 = 2 to HALT and c0 = 3 to a loop from the same (control, zero-pattern) node, the discarded magnitude deciding halting. The Turing threshold sits exactly at the decrement.

Scope. The no-decrement law is verified exhaustively over 18 states × 19 moves with the search for a lowering move empty; the presence test is verified exact with no exception; the quotient decider settles five programs, two of them non-terminating naively. That a decrement beside a zero-test is universal is Minsky's theorem, cited.

The depth system is well-structured, which is the standard reason rule

The move monoid is commutative, so composition order is invisible to the state, and the presence bit is write-once. Pushes are translations of the depth vector and monotone threshold tests respect the product order on vectors, so the system is a well-structured transition system over Dickson's well-quasi-order, and termination is decidable by the general theorem (Finkel–Schnoebelen 2001). What the tower adds is the location: universality needs destructive memory reuse, and at the depths every such move is the archimedean borrow.

Scope. Commutativity checked on 128 move triples and write-once presence on 21 tests; the decidability is the cited theorem applied to a verified structure.

What growth computes it computes witness-readably and state-deniably observation

A monotone endpoint under-determines its route: the primorial pk# is reached by k! orderings of its k pushes, all collapsing to one state (6, 24, 120, 720 and 5040 routes at k = 3 to 7). The state certifies the move multiset and never the order: computation one can watch but not audit from the result. What this witness gap costs against Landauer's floor is priced on the static dividend.

Scope. Route counts computed at k = 3 to 7; the collapse is the commutativity above.

The no-decrement law is about depths, and depths are not the only counters a state carries. Write λ(N) for the period of N, the exponent of its unit group (the Carmichael function), and at an odd prime l dividing N define the slack δl = vl(odd part of λ(N)) − vl(N) + 1. The slack is the exponent of l in the headroom V(N) = W(λ(N))/N, where W(L) is the largest modulus whose period divides L; the headroom is the largest multiplier that leaves the period fixed (the headroom ledger).

The slack is a counter with truncated subtraction, and its zero-test is fused to the decrement theorem

A push of le sends δl to max(0, δl − e): the primitive that lossy counter machines are built from. The counters read together are the odd part of the exponent vector of the headroom, so the machine holds a proper part of the one number the probe page reads. The test is fused to the decrement: for l already seated, λ(N·l) ≠ λ(N) exactly when δl = 0 — a bare repeat push of a seated prime is a decrement that perturbs nothing, and whether it moved the period is the zero-test.

Scope. The lossy law is proved and checked on 750 (state, prime, exponent) triples with 0 exceptions; the fused test is proved for seated primes and checked on 47 pairs with 0 disagreements. On a first push λ also moves for another reason, so the scope is seated primes.

A Minsky machine runs on pushes alone rule

INC on the slack counter at l imports a fresh prime q with vl(q − 1) exactly one above the current depth and q ≢ 1 modulo every sibling counter prime — coprime congruences, so Dirichlet supplies such a q at any number of counters and the instruction set carries no open-problem dependency. DEC is the bare repeat push and the zero-test is the period read above. Three programs — halts-iff-even, a transfer and a round trip — run step for step against a reference interpreter at 2, 3, 4 and 5 counters, 70 increments perturbing no sibling. The fingerprint the dial below names as a borrow's does occur here: over two counter primes the depth tests flip 0 times against their bound of 2 while the slack test flips twice per cycle, 24 over 12 cycles, so the finite quotient that decides the depth machine does not decide this one. A composite push runs the pair on several counters at once, and its two halves behave oppositely: the decrements are independent per counter while whether the push admits a new prime is read off the whole of λ, so a composite push can admit a prime no single-prime factor admits.

Scope. Universality is Minsky's theorem applied to a verified instruction set, three programs at probe scale, never a claim over programs. The variant disturbing no odd prime at all wants a Proth prime, whose existence at every depth is an open question. The composite push's closed form is proved and checked on 23,084 moves. Imports above about 3.3·1024 are strong probable primes.

A growth move is governed by a demand law, the admissibility condition a state imposes on what may be multiplied into it. A machine asks a law for two independent things: a gate, which moves may be made, and a probe, one bit about an offered move. The bit at stake is the guard: whether the slack at a counter prime has fallen to zero.

Reading and hosting are independent axes, and the demand laws fill a square rule

On reading: the support-blind laws — independence (the move is coprime to the state), new-idempotents (the move widens the prime support), semisimplicity (the state stays squarefree) — are functions of prime support and squarefreeness alone, so they cannot see the guard: N = 855 and 2565 share support {3, 5, 19} and squarefreeness with slacks 1 and 0, and no probe of the three separates them. The dynamics law, admissible means raises λ, answers the guard exactly by the fused test; new-orders is the same predicate (the unit orders a state realizes are the divisors of λ), and transparency is its complement, which costs no reading power since a one-bit probe separates the same states as its negation. The sighted family's whole reading of a state is the headroom, one number, and the two readings are incomparable: N = 10 and 11 share headroom 24 with different supports, so the sighted probe agrees on every move there is. On hosting, independently: independence and semisimplicity host no decrement (a seated counter falls only under a move sharing its prime, never a coprime one), transparency hosts no increment and has a finite reachable set besides (its closed form), and new-idempotents hosts both.

The padded decrement: hosting is the price of autonomy rule

Under the dynamics law the bare decrement, a transparent push, is inadmissible. But a move is multiplication by any m. Pad it: m = l·q with q a fresh prime, q ≢ 1 modulo every counter prime and (q − 1) ∤ λ(N). Then δl falls by exactly one, no sibling moves, λ rises in both branches of the guard, and the machine spends its probe on the bare push it never makes. Where universality switches on then depends on what the machine may do. Hand-driven — moves injected from outside regardless of admissibility, the law consulted only as a probe — it is exactly sightedness, the whole bottom row: transparency drives all three programs while making 104 moves it rejects itself. Law-autonomous, every move made admissible, it is sighted and hosting, one corner. A move admissible in both branches carries no bit and a move carrying the bit is inadmissible in one, so a machine wanting both uses two moves, which is what the padded decrement does.

Scope. Three programs run law-autonomously with 0 inadmissible moves, 18 padded decrements at 2, 3 and 4 counters each crossing both branches; Minsky universality cited.

verifier: explore_demand_reading.py

Sub-universal under the blind laws, universal under every sighted one, autonomously only under dynamics rule

The obstruction was never "there is no decrement" but that reading the decrement is not in every probe repertoire, and one law pays for it as a side effect of what it means by admissible. The price is a second door — a door being a read the construction deleted, re-imported as a primitive — and not the borrow: the slack compares a valuation of λ against a valuation of N, depth read across two windows at once, so the read-import law — every universality purchase on the depth face is a read the construction deleted — survives and gains its sharpest instance.

Scope. The verdict is the two blocks above read together; the universality claims are Minsky's theorem applied to a verified instruction set, never an exhaustive claim over programs. The second door's machine layer is the comparison threshold and its predicate layer the ruler ladder.

The fates as decision problems

A fate question asks: from seed s under law L, does some policy in a class reach fate F? The three fates of the Growth page are mortality, every run stopping; breadth, every prime seated at exponent 1; depth, one prime's exponent running away.

A fate question's decidability does not come from an outer clock rule

A fate question is decided by a bound on the step at which the fate is settled together with a bounded witness search in every object it reduces to, since a boundedly deep, finitely branching tree can be exhausted. That implication is the whole of what a clock — a computable step after which the verdict cannot change — is worth. Read loosely, its converse is unfalsifiable, T = 0 covering every verdict that is a function of the seed; read so that the clock bounds a search whose answer varies, it is false at every row filed decidable: mortality, breadth under greedy independence and depth under greedy dynamics take one verdict each at 30 of 30 seeds, and the outer column reproduces "already filed decidable" at 7 of 7 rows. The bounds in those arguments are real and live one granularity down, at questions whose answers vary — the least prime ≡ 1 mod B takes 122 values over B = 2 to 200 — and at uniformity, which is policy-independence and not a bound. Undecidable needs a universal embedding, which a hosting law supplies; open is the absence of both.

Scope. Rule at 30 seeds per row; the clocks and the frozen sort refuted by their own printed kill-shapes.

verifiers: explore_clock_vacuity.py, explore_fate_clock.py

Mortality's closed form, and the one bound that does decide rule

Under the transparency law the states reachable from a seed s are exactly the multiples of s dividing W(λ(s)): a transparent move leaves λ fixed, so the wall W(λ) is the same at every state of the run and the moves out of a state N are the divisors of its headroom. The set has one terminus, the wall itself, the only state with headroom 1; every maximal run ends there whatever chooses the moves; and the longest run has exactly Ω(V(s)) moves, Ω counting prime factors with multiplicity. Non-hosting and policy-independence are one finiteness seen twice. One granularity down the answer varies and a bound reads it: under greedy dynamics the first non-ghost pick locks the trajectory, pre-lock picks being ghost openings that visit only odd prime factors of λ(seed), so the lock prime is found within ωodd(λ(seed)) + 1 menu evaluations — seed 20231 wanders through 5, 7 and 17 to lock 19.

Scope. The closed form is proved from the divisor identity for transparent moves and exhausted at ten seeds from 2 to 36, 57 reachable states in all, one terminus and a longest chain of Ω(V(s)) at 10 of 10. The wander bound is proved and verified at 2,000 seeds, 8 distinct lock primes appearing at 400 seeds.

verifiers: explore_clock_vacuity.py, explore_fate_clock.py, explore_lock_prime.py

Mortality holds under every policy, breadth and depth are greedy, and a free policy can hold none of the three rule

Under independence coprimality fixes a prime's depth at the move that seats it, so depth is unreachable by every policy — yet the policy avoiding one prime seats 300 others at depth 1 and holds none of the three fates. Under dynamics greed locks the 3-column while a padded policy seats every prime below 100 in 18 steps. Fate is decidable by policy, not by demand, and the fate with no policy freedom is exactly the one whose law cannot host the machine. The three fates are the extremes of the law's fate image (the limit), of which the square above reads two coordinates — hosting predicts whether an infinite exponent is reachable, reading whether a run may stop widening — and no more.

Scope. Rule on the policies run: the free policies exhibited at the seeds and step counts printed, the greedy fates as filed on the Growth page.

The knife edge

The archimedean dial: universality needs a zero-test and a borrow, and the depth face is one borrow short rule

Neither ingredient alone suffices. A zero-test without a borrow is the growth machine, decidable on the capped-depth quotient; a borrow without a zero-test is a vector addition system, counters that rise and fall with no branch on zero, whose reachability is decidable (Mayr 1981, Kosaraju 1982, Leroux–Schmitz 2019). Neutralize a Minsky machine's zero-branch and c0 = 2, 3 share a fate; restore it and the halts-iff-even witness splits them. The fingerprint is the down-flip, a zero-test going from nonzero to zero along a run, and its scope is exactly "a depth": multiply-only runs show zero down-flips of any depth, while the slack above down-flips unboundedly with no borrow anywhere, so the down-flip diagnoses a borrow only among counters that are depths. The dial is graded, top down: a plain decrement with no zero-branch keeps every question decidable; a forgetful borrow — reset, clearing an unbounded window — already loses reachability and boundedness while keeping termination and coverability, whether a state at least as large as some target is reachable (Dufourd–Finkel–Schnoebelen 1998); a remembering borrow beside the native zero-test is Minsky. Behind the deletion is carry-freedom, verified directly: CRT addition is componentwise, (a + b) mod pi = (a mod pi + b mod pi) mod pi with no inter-window coupling, while positional addition couples digits through a propagating carry. The deleted archimedean place is that coupling, and the borrow is it read as an operation.

Scope. Proved for pushes with monotone threshold tests and cited for the rest; the down-flip invariant, the two-ingredient decomposition and the carry-freedom identity (Z/2310, 5 pairs; 9 carries over 5 positional pairs) run-verified. The finite places being exactly the non-archimedean ones (Ostrowski) makes the tower the ultrametric part of arithmetic, an observation; ultrametric character alone does not buy the decidability, since the second door — depth compared across two windows — never touches the archimedean place, so the decidability rests on both constructive deletions.

The reset corner: the forgetful borrow is value-blind, and INC + RESET + zero-test is finite-state rule

Reset zeroes a counter unconditionally, reading nothing on the descent, so the map (control, counter values) ↦ (control, signs) is a bisimulation and the machine is bisimilar to a finite automaton on Q × {0, +}k; halting is finite-graph reachability, sound and complete even on reset oscillators whose simulation never ends. The decrement breaks the quotient (c0 = 2 halts, 3 loops from the shared node); with bounded-threshold tests v ≥ t ≤ T the invariant is min(v, T). The forgetful borrow is a cliff down to finite-state, so the tower may wipe a window freely: safe destruction is total. Reset is inert alone — reset nets are undecidable for reachability only jointly with a decrement (Dufourd–Finkel–Schnoebelen 1998), and the plain decrement's reachability is decidable (Mayr, Kosaraju, Leroux) — so the climb needs a value-reading down-move present, which reset amplifies and never supplies. The danger is the reading, not the destruction.

Scope. Proved and verified: 8 sign-classes, a five-program decider, the invariant checked at c = 0 to 199.

What the depth face collects in exchange is the other half of the theory of computation. Half of the payoff is algebra and stands on both faces — canonical forms, decidable equivalence, a reversible core, and the exact perimeter of what the windows can say: the static dividend. The other half is runtime — decidable halting, decidable games against an outside hand — and is a property of interfaces: it holds where a boundary's reads flip at most once and the machine asking cannot count how long it waited (the read surface).

The static dividend

Every native predicate is a union of residue classes modulo a squarefree modulus, a class sitting strictly inside Presburger arithmetic by the tower's own two deletions; undecidability over ⟨N, +⟩ is quantized, opening only when two independent magnitude rulers are compared, and the first modulus whose Pascal triangle reads two such rulers is the tower's k = 2 rung; the native arithmetic is a reversible core with erasure channel-local and exactly priced; and three canonical forms make function equivalence decidable where Rice forbids it: The static dividend.

The recovery chart and what is under it

The knife edge run backwards — what buys universality back, priced in re-imports, with the depth face's door still selling possibility and the element face's, the bare class there being universal already, selling only per-step exactness — is The recovery chart. One level under its supply law, where the supply grows too slowly to carry a counter and the window pool turns out to hold nothing but the machine's own operation stream played back, is The delay line.
