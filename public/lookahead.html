The limit of the lookahead

How far past its depth a reader of a digit shift must see, read as an object: a column of numbers with an exact limit at every periodic window, and what plays the period at a window that has none.

An Ostrowski window writes n = Σ bk qk over the denominators qk of an irrational α's continued-fraction convergents, the partial quotients ak of α = [0; a1, a2, …] setting the digit caps: legality caps b0 at a1 − 1 and bk at ak+1, and forces a zero below any digit sitting at its cap, which makes the writing unique. The golden window is [0; 1, 1, 1, …], at α = 1/φ. The depth-t cell of n is the set of inputs agreeing with it on their t low-order digits, and the agreement depth of two inputs is the number of low-order digits they share (the family is on Quadratic windows). A shift by stride r moves every digit up r positions and renormalizes where the moved string breaks legality; a window reads it at lookahead c when the output cell at depth t is a function of the input cell at depth t + c. The least lookahead that works at depth t, less t, is the excess lookahead c(t), and a shift's verdict is one of three: lookahead 0 at every depth, bounded — some finite lookahead serves every depth — or gated, no finite lookahead serving every depth. A large quotient is a partial quotient above 1, and the designed family [0; (1, …, 1, a)∞] carries one per period of length P; there the verdict is the parity law's — a stride ≡ 0 mod P reads at lookahead 0, an even nonzero residue reads bounded, an odd residue gates (Digit shifts, which owns the law and its mechanism). Under a shift a large digit may land on a position whose own cap is smaller and overflow it: that is a drop, the position it left its source and the position it lands on its drop site; a site whose own cap is large enough to hold the digit is an absorber. A witness pair for a gate is two inputs agreeing deeper than their images, and its margin is that agreement depth less the position at which the images part. A (window, stride) pair is a cell of the verdict table, and a window's limit map is its row of limit verdicts. Here the lookahead itself is the object.

The limit column

The limit column exists, and a finite automaton decides it theorem

A reading of a shift over the inputs below a range N gives one excess lookahead per depth, the excess column. It can only rise as N grows, so it has a limit c∞(t) at every depth, finite or not — and at a purely periodic window that limit is computable. Put θk = qkα − pk, the signed error of the k-th convergent, so that a string's star Σ bkθk is its value on the circle; with the quotients periodic of length P the recurrence gives θk+P = ηθk for a unit η of ℤ + ℤα with |η| < 1. A shift's image is the legal string of the same value, and two strings share a value exactly when their stars differ by an integer; so reading input and image from the bottom digit up, the running discrepancy divided by θk lies in one fixed lattice for each residue of k mod P and is bounded in both embeddings on any run that can still end in agreement. The discrepancy is the carry, and it takes finitely many values at each residue: the shift relation is a finite synchronous automaton, by an elementary argument. Drive two copies of it on one common input; the pairs whose images have already parted form a sequence of sets under a fixed map, hence eventually periodic, so the limit column is eventually periodic in t or infinite from some depth on, and which — with the preperiod, the period and the values — is read off the automaton cell by cell.

Nothing in that used that the map was a shift. Any linear relation between bounded-digit strings has the same bounded carry, so ×m, n + 1, the deletion of one digit and ⌊n/m⌋ — its remainder guessed and checked by whether the automaton accepts the pair — are decided by the same automaton. At a quadratic α the graph of addition itself is recognized by a finite automaton (Hieronymi and Terry, 2018; the golden case is Frougny's normalization theorem, the base 1/η being a Pisot unit), so the arithmetic maps have regular graphs there; what the column reads is the delay a bottom-up sequential function needs, which a regular graph leaves open — ×m's graph is regular and ×m is gated. A finite range reads a lower reading: cN(t) ≤ c∞(t) at every depth, so whatever lookahead a finite table shows a depth to need, the limit needs too, while a bounded reading is open to the next range and no finite table by itself decides that a column is infinite.

Scope. Theorem at every purely periodic window and every stride; the values at each cell exact. Controls: 5000 of 5000 pairs accepted at three windows and three strides, exactly one image accepted at each of the first 300 inputs there, the period strides of the designed family printing the zero column, the finite column at N = 30000 at or below the limit at every depth of every cell, and the bound the automaton's carries are kept within tripled without a verdict moving. A window with no period has no automaton.

The half-period stride gates at every unequal pair rule

Give the period two different large quotients. The graded window [0; (1, 1, A, 1, 1, B)∞] has its large quotients three apart while its quotient sequence repeats at six, and its whole limit map is the parity law read against 3, with one departure. Strides 1, 4, 7 gate at every pair; strides 2, 5, 8 are bounded at every pair; stride 6 is the zero column; and the half-period stride 3 — the one shift carrying each large quotient onto the other, a large digit dropping onto a site that is itself an absorber — gates at every one of the 201 pairs with A ≠ B and reads zero at the 9 with A = B, where the window is the designed family and 3 is its period. The verdict vector of a pair, its eight verdicts over strides 1..8, is one of four, and the diagonal's is the designed family's own.

The values A and B carry no law. The criterion "bounded at stride 3 exactly when B < A ≤ 2B + 1 and A·B ≥ 30" reads bounded exactly the cells whose first witness pair lies beyond the range read: at (8, 4) the integers 1638 and 364170 agree on ten digits while their images differ at position 3, so c(4) ≥ 7 at any range holding both, and the automaton's pair states cycle with period 6, each turn of that cycle handing over a deeper witness pair — agreeing on sixteen digits, then twenty-two — at (8, 4) and at each of the other five cells that criterion calls bounded. What survives is that the verdict is not a function of the large quotients' positions alone, since A = B and A ≠ B share every position and read differently. Nor is the parity of the gap between a drop's source and its site the general form: at the cubic ∛2 − 1 every stride carries both gap parities among its drops, and the stride with the fewest odd gaps is a gated one.

Scope. Rule, exact at the 210 pairs B = 3..12, A = 4..24 at every stride 1..8, none off the map above, each cell's limit column read from the automaton.

Reading a finite table

The run-length rule reads one table, and both its halves are readings rule

A verdict can be read off a single finite column. Read the column at the data's depth — each cell to the depth its inputs reach, rather than to one fixed depth shared by every cell — and cut it into maximal runs of nonzero entries; its peak is its largest entry. At a window of period P the run-length rule reads a run of length P or more as gated — a local obstruction recurs with the period, and consecutive copies of one would touch — a peak reaching a fixed ceiling as saturated, meaning gated, a table shallower than P + 1 as refused, no verdict, and runs shorter than P as bounded. Seen from the limit, the bounded half is a lower reading, open to the next range, and the gated half is refuted: touching copies merge. At two cells of the two-class family — windows whose period carries two large quotients, here period 5 with large quotients 5 or 4 and 2, at stride 4 — the limit column is the sawtooth 5 4 3 2 1 recurring with the period and returning to 0 at no depth, bounded at lookahead 5, which the rule reads gated at N = 300000.

No choice of length repairs either half. A window's data fix two recurrence lengths — the sequence period, at which the quotients repeat, and the largeness period, at which the large quotients recur — and they part at the graded window, 6 against 3. The limit column carries the sequence period exactly: 6 at every finite nonzero cell of the graded window, P at every cell of the designed and two-class families. But reading runs against 6 calls (8, 4) at stride 3 bounded where the limit gates it, and reading against 3 would gate the 175 stride-2 pairs whose limit column is the finite 0 0 0 3 2 1 recurring at six. So at a periodic window the rule is a first reading and the automaton the verdict. Where there is no period only the saturation clause can fire: at the ceiling the next block sets it catches the cubic's strides 1, 3, 5 and e − 2's 1, 4, 7, reads the dropless strides at 0, and returns no verdict elsewhere.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope: a first reading at the periodic windows, the saturation clause alone at the two aperiodic ones. Two facts it established stand exact in the limit: the stride-2 region B = 3..12 over A = 4..24 is bounded throughout, its limit columns 18 zero, 17 with a single 1 and 175 with the run 3 2 1, preperiod at most 3; and reading at the data's depth rather than at a shared depth of 10 moves no cell of that map while reproducing the designed family's parity law at 0 misses of 54 strides against 4 at the shared depth. On the two-class family's 84 cells over P = 4..7 the limit map is exact, and against it the criterion predicting a verdict from the large quotients' positions alone fails at 18 cells, and this rule at 16 at one range.

The saturation ceiling is calibration pattern

At a window with no period the saturation clause is the only positive verdict the rule has, and its ceiling is a bare constant. No property of a finite column replaces it: the column of the cubic's stride 5 read to depth 11 is the single run 5 4 3 2 1, and the column of (8, 4) at the half-period stride read to the same depth is the same single run; the second is the first run of a gated column, so a gated column truncated at its first run is indistinguishable from a bounded one, and a finite table cannot tell them apart. The constant is instead calibrated on the bounded cells, and their ceiling is a limit: over every finite limit column of the graded window at strides 2, 5, 8 and of the designed family the largest run and peak are 4 and 4, attained at three cells of stride 5, and the two-class family adds two finite cells at peak 5, the sawtooth above, whose peak is its period. The constant is set one past that ceiling, at 6. The split it must reproduce at the two windows with no period has its gated strides at peaks 12, 9, 7 (∛2 − 1, strides 1, 3, 5) and 12, 10, 7 (e − 2, strides 1, 4, 7) at N = 300000 with every bounded stride at 3 or below, so 7 would issue the same split: two values of margin. Every finite column of the designed family's shape at P = 8, 9, 10 tops at 2, under the ceiling.

Scope. Pattern at the aperiodic windows, and it stays pattern: they have no automaton, and the constant does inductive work there that nothing derives. The cells it is calibrated on are exact — the limit columns of the graded window's 210 pairs at three strides, of the designed family at its even nonzero residues, and of the two-class family's 84 cells.

What plays the period

Nothing at the source of a drop decides an aperiodic stride rule

A periodic window has one drop per stride and repeats it forever, so a criterion on a drop's two quotients — the dropping one and its site's — is what its readings are made of; a window with no period carries a whole multiset of quotient pairs per stride, and any such criterion reads a verdict per drop. At e − 2 the quotient pattern 1, 1, 2n puts a cap of 1 under every drop, so the six strides that carry drops there, 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, carry one identical multiset — the even quotients dropping onto 1 — from the same sources j = 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, differing only in where the drop lands, and they read gated, bounded, gated, bounded, gated, bounded. No function of a drop's source can be the reading, and the refutation is a miss rather than a silence: any one verdict per multiset is wrong at one stride of each of the three pairs. A drop's source has two data, its position and its value: the graded window kills the position, A = B and A ≠ B sharing every position and reading differently, and this kills the value. What is left is the site. There e − 2's verdicts do live: its large quotients sit on one residue class mod 3, which is what makes it the P = 3 pattern the parity law already calls, while the cubic's sit on no class and the same reading is dead there.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope, the two windows with no period, every stride read at three ranges to N = 300000. Three forms of source criterion are tested — a roof, the dropping quotient held to at most one more than twice its site's, under each quantifier over the drops, and the product of the two quotients against a threshold — each missing three of six strides at e − 2; at the cubic the roof misses 2 of 7 strides under the existential quantifier and 4 of 7 under the universal. That no statistic over the pairs can do better is the identical-multiset argument, not a further measurement.

The prefix closures call every one-class window and refuse the cubic rule

Close a window's first m quotients into the purely periodic [0; (a1 … am)∞] — the prefix repeated forever — its m-th prefix closure; the automaton above decides it exactly, so a window with no period gets a ladder of exact verdicts, one per m. The candidate law for such a window is composite: a stride along which the quotient sequence is non-decreasing carries no drop and is the shift with no renormalization, at lookahead 0; every other stride takes the eventual verdict of its closure ladder where that ladder stabilizes. On the designed family it reduces to the parity law by construction. At e − 2 it is the whole table: the verdict vector is gated at {1, 4, 7} against bounded at {2, 5, 8} at every closure from m = 12 through 21 — the seam where the repeated prefix meets its next copy manufactures drops at the dropless strides 3 and 6, which gate the closure only at small m, where that seam is a large share of the period; the peaks read the same split; and the gated half is certified — witness pairs extracted from deep closures verify by integer arithmetic on the true window, the parting position fixed at 1, 3, 6 per stride while the input agreement climbs past 120 digits.

The call is the class's rather than e − 2's. Call a window one-class when its large quotients sit on one residue class mod P. Three designed aperiodic one-class windows sharing nothing with e − 2 but the class — quotients 1, 1, ck with ck the primes (unbounded), the primes adjacently transposed, and 2 plus the Thue–Morse bit (bounded) — read the same split, gated at r ≡ 1 mod 3 against not gated at r ≡ 2 mod 3, with certified witness ladders landing at exactly the strides ≡ 1 mod 3 at all three (agreement climbing to 170 digits) and no positive-margin pair anywhere else. Three things could ride the class, and they separate: the split and the certificates ride it alone, at all three windows alike; stabilization of the closure verdict rests on unbounded large quotients — at the Thue–Morse window the deep verdict vectors cycle with the repeated word while the peaks still separate the split; and a stride ≡ 0 mod 3 follows its own drops, lookahead 0 where it has none, gated in every stable closure with no certificate transferring where it has real ones, as at the transposed window's stride 3. At the cubic every observable refuses: the ladder never stabilizes, the peaks grow at every stride alike where the one-class windows' peaks stay within a fixed band, and no extracted witness transfers, margins topping at +1 against the class windows' ladders climbing to +169. The composite law is the aperiodic form of the parity law wherever the large quotients are one-class; the cubic's split remains a finite reading at the deepest table read, depth 12.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope: closures to m = 21 (24 at the prime window, 30 at the cubic), every stride to 8, every cell decided exactly. The certified ladders are verified instances — explicit integer pairs checked by exact arithmetic in the window's own digits, counted only where the images part strictly below the agreement. That stabilization needs unbounded large quotients rests on one bounded window and is an observation. Controls: the golden and designed windows reproduce the parity law at every cell, and the finite column at range 30000 sits at or below the limit column at every checked cell.
