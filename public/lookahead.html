The limit of the lookahead

How far past its depth a reader of a digit shift must see, read as an object: a column of numbers with an exact limit at every periodic window, and what plays the period at a window that has none.

An Ostrowski window writes n = Σ bk qk over the denominators qk of an irrational α's continued-fraction convergents, the partial quotients ak of α = [0; a1, a2, …] setting the digit caps: legality caps b0 at a1 − 1 and bk at ak+1, and forces a zero below any digit sitting at its cap, which makes the writing unique. The depth-t cell of n is the set of inputs agreeing with it on their t low-order digits, and the agreement depth of two inputs is the number of low-order digits they share (the family is on Quadratic windows). A shift by stride r moves every digit up r positions and renormalizes where the moved string breaks legality; a window reads it at lookahead c when the output cell at depth t is a function of the input cell at depth t + c. The least lookahead that works at depth t, less t, is the excess lookahead c(t), and a shift's verdict is one of three: lookahead 0 at every depth, bounded, or gated — no one lookahead serving every depth. A large quotient is a partial quotient above 1, and the designed family [0; (1, …, 1, a)∞] carries one per period of length P; there the verdict is the parity law's — a stride ≡ 0 mod P reads at lookahead 0, an even nonzero residue reads bounded, an odd residue gates (Digit shifts, which owns the law and its mechanism). Here the lookahead itself is the object.

The limit column

The limit column exists, and a finite automaton decides it theorem

A reading of a shift over the inputs below a range N gives one excess lookahead per depth, the excess column. It can only rise as N grows, so it has a limit c∞(t) at every depth, finite or not — and at a purely periodic window that limit is computable. Put θk = qkα − pk, the signed error of the k-th convergent, so that a string's star Σ bkθk is its value on the circle; with the quotients periodic of length P the recurrence gives θk+P = ηθk for a unit η of ℤ + ℤα with |η| < 1. A shift's image is the legal string of the same value, and two strings share a value exactly when their stars differ by an integer; so reading input and image from the bottom digit up, the running discrepancy divided by θk lies in one fixed lattice per phase and is bounded in both embeddings on any run that can still end in agreement. Finitely many carries per phase: the shift relation is a finite synchronous automaton, with nothing imported. Drive two copies of it on one common input; the pairs whose images have already parted form a sequence of sets under a fixed map, hence eventually periodic, so the limit column is eventually periodic in t or infinite from some depth on, and which — with the preperiod, the period and the values — is read off the automaton cell by cell.

Nothing in that used that the map was a shift. Any linear relation between bounded-digit strings has the same bounded carry, so ×m, n + 1, the digit drop and, its remainder guessed and verified by acceptance, ⌊n/m⌋ are decided by the same automaton. At a quadratic α the graph of addition itself is recognized by a finite automaton (Hieronymi and Terry, 2018; the golden case is Frougny's normalization theorem, the base 1/η being a Pisot unit), so the arithmetic maps have regular graphs there; what the column reads is the delay a bottom-up sequential function needs, which a regular graph leaves open — ×m's graph is regular and ×m is gated. A finite range reads a lower reading: cN(t) ≤ c∞(t) at every depth, so a gated verdict is final and a bounded one is open to the next range, and no finite table by itself decides that a column is infinite.

Scope. Theorem at every purely periodic window and every stride; the values at each cell exact. Controls: 5000 of 5000 pairs accepted at three windows and three strides, exactly one image accepted at each of the first 300 inputs there, the period strides of the designed family printing the zero column, the finite column at N = 30000 at or below the limit at every depth of every cell, and the automaton's pruning box tripled without a verdict moving. A window with no period has no automaton.

The half-period stride gates at every unequal pair rule

Give the period two different large quotients. The graded window [0; (1, 1, A, 1, 1, B)∞] has its large quotients three apart while its quotient sequence repeats at six, and its whole limit map is the parity law read against 3, with one departure. Strides 1, 4, 7 gate at every pair; strides 2, 5, 8 are finite at every pair; stride 6 is the zero column; and the half-period stride 3 — the one shift carrying each large quotient onto the other, a large digit dropping onto a position that is itself an absorber — gates at every one of the 201 pairs with A ≠ B and reads zero at the 9 with A = B, where the window is the designed family and 3 is its period. Every verdict vector over strides 1..8 is one of four, and the diagonal's is the designed family's own.

There is no value law. A criterion once read at stride 3 — the pair bounded exactly when B < A ≤ 2B + 1 and A·B ≥ 30 — was a fact about how deep the first witness sits against the range read: at (8, 4) the integers 1638 and 364170 agree on ten digits while their images differ at position 3, so c(4) ≥ 7 at any range holding both, and one turn of the automaton's six-cycle later the witnesses agree on sixteen digits, then twenty-two, at all six bounded cells that criterion exhibited. What survives is that the verdict is not a function of the large quotients' positions alone, since A = B and A ≠ B share every position and read differently. Nor is the parity of the gap between large quotients the general form: at the cubic ∛2 − 1 every stride carries both gap parities among its drop sites, and the stride with the fewest odd gaps is a gated one.

Scope. Rule, exact at the 210 pairs B = 3..12, A = 4..24 at every stride 1..8, none off the map above, each cell's limit column read from the automaton. The retired criterion's six bounded cells are recorded as the finite reading they were.

Reading a finite table

The run-length rule reads one table, and both its halves are readings rule

A verdict can be read off a single finite column. At a window of period P, cut the excess column, read at the data's own depth, into maximal runs of nonzero entries. The run-length rule reads a run of length P or more as gated — a local obstruction recurs with the period, and consecutive copies of one would touch — a peak at the lookahead ceiling as saturated, a table shallower than P + 1 as refused, and runs shorter than P as bounded. Seen from the limit, the bounded half is a lower reading, open to the next range, and the gated half is refuted: touching copies merge. At two cells of the two-class family — windows whose period carries two large quotients, here period 5 with large quotients 5 or 4 and 2, at stride 4 — the limit column is the sawtooth 5 4 3 2 1 recurring with the period and returning to 0 at no depth, bounded at lookahead 5, which the rule reads gated at N = 300000.

No choice of length repairs either half. A window's data fix two recurrence lengths — the sequence period, at which the quotients repeat, and the largeness period, at which the large quotients recur — and they part at the graded window, 6 against 3. The limit column carries the sequence period exactly: 6 at every finite nonzero cell of the graded window, P at every cell of the designed and two-class families. But reading runs against 6 calls (8, 4) at stride 3 bounded where the limit gates it, and reading against 3 would gate the 175 stride-2 pairs whose limit column is the finite 0 0 0 3 2 1 recurring at six. So at a periodic window the rule is a first reading and the automaton the verdict; the rule keeps its home where there is no period, at ∛2 − 1 and e − 2, where it refuses 7 of 8 and 4 of 8 strides and catches only what saturates.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope, periodic windows. Two facts it established stand exact in the limit: the stride-2 region B = 3..12 over A = 4..24 is bounded throughout, its limit columns 18 zero, 17 with a single 1 and 175 with the run 3 2 1, preperiod at most 3; and reading at the data's depth rather than a shared cap moves no cell of that map while reproducing the designed family's parity law at 0 misses of 54 strides against 4 at the cap. On the two-class family's 84 cells over P = 4..7 the limit map is exact, and three instruments give three counts against it — the criterion predicting a verdict from the large quotients' positions fails at 18 cells in the limit, the rule at 16 at one range — only the limit's being a fact about the family.

The saturation ceiling is calibration pattern

A column whose peak reaches a fixed ceiling is read as saturated, and at a window with no period that clause is the only one the rule has. No property of a finite column replaces it: one column read as gated at the cubic — the single run 5 4 3 2 1 at depth 11 — has an exact twin at (8, 4) of the graded window at the half-period stride, and that twin is a gated column at its first bump, so the two are two gated columns at the same truncation, which is exactly what one table cannot tell from a bounded one. The constant is instead calibrated on the bounded corpus's ceiling, and that ceiling is a limit: over every finite limit column of the graded window at strides 2, 5, 8 and of the designed family the largest run and peak are 4 and 4, attained at three cells of stride 5, and the two-class family adds two finite cells at peak 5, the sawtooth above, whose peak is its period. The honest constant is one past the widest corpus read, 6. The split it must reproduce at the two windows with no period has its gated strides at peaks 12, 9, 7 (∛2 − 1, strides 1, 3, 5) and 12, 10, 7 (e − 2, strides 1, 4, 7) at N = 300000 with every bounded stride at 3 or below, so 6 or 7 issues the same split — two values of margin. The ceiling rose by one when one more periodic family was read, and did not rise again when that family's shape was read at P = 8, 9, 10, where every finite column tops at 2.

Scope. Pattern at the aperiodic windows, and it stays pattern: they have no automaton, and the constant does inductive work there that nothing derives. The corpus it is calibrated on is exact — the limit columns of the graded window's 210 pairs at three strides, of the designed family at its even nonzero residues, and of the two-class family's 84 cells.

What plays the period

Nothing at the overflowing end of a drop decides an aperiodic stride rule

A drop is a large quotient overflowing, under the shift, onto a landing site r positions up. A periodic window has one drop per stride and repeats it forever, so a criterion on the drop's two quotients is what its readings are made of; a window with no period carries a whole multiset of quotient pairs per stride, and any such criterion reads a verdict per site. At e − 2 the quotient pattern 1, 1, 2n puts a cap of 1 under every overflow, so all six of its scored strides carry one identical multiset — the even quotients dropping onto 1 — at the same drop positions j = 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, differing only in where the drop lands, and they read gated, bounded, gated, bounded, gated, bounded. No function of a drop's own source can be the reading — not a roof under any quantifier, not a product under any threshold — and the refutation is a miss rather than a silence, one output per pair being wrong at one member of each. With the graded window this empties both ends of a drop's source: that window kills the positions, this one the values. What is left is the absorbing end, where e − 2's verdicts do live: its large quotients sit on one residue class mod 3, which is what makes it the P = 3 pattern the parity law already calls, while the cubic's sit on no class and the same reading is dead there.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope, the two windows with no period, every scored stride read at three ranges to N = 300000. Three forms are scored — a roof, the dropping quotient held to at most one more than twice its landing site's, under each quantifier, and the product of the two quotients at its threshold — each missing three of six strides at e − 2; at the cubic the roof misses 2 of 7 strides existentially and 4 of 7 universally. That no statistic over the pairs can do better is the identical-multiset argument, not a further measurement.

The prefix closures call every one-class window and refuse the cubic rule

Close a window's first m quotients into the purely periodic [0; (a1 … am)∞], its m-th prefix closure; the automaton above decides it exactly, so a window with no period gets a ladder of exact verdicts, one per m. The candidate law for such a window is composite: a stride whose drop sites carry no drop at all is the bare coordinate map, at lookahead 0; every other stride takes the eventual verdict of its closure ladder where that ladder stabilizes. On the designed family it reduces to the parity law by construction. At e − 2 it is the whole table: the verdict vector over strides 1..8 is gated {1, 4, 7} against bounded {2, 5, 8} at every closure from m = 12 through 21, the drops the tiling manufactures at the dropless strides 3 and 6 gating only while they dominate the period; the peaks read the same split; and the gated half is certified — witness pairs extracted from deep closures verify by integer arithmetic on the true window, the parting position fixed at 1, 3, 6 per stride while the input agreement climbs past 120 digits.

The call is the class's rather than e − 2's. Call a window one-class when its large quotients sit on one residue class mod P. Three designed aperiodic one-class windows sharing nothing with e − 2 but the class — quotients 1, 1, ck with ck the primes, the primes adjacently transposed, and 2 plus the Thue–Morse bit — read the same split, gated at r ≡ 1 mod 3 against not gated at r ≡ 2 mod 3, with certified integer ladders landing at exactly the strides ≡ 1 mod 3 at all three (agreement climbing to 170 digits) and no positive-margin pair anywhere else. The legs separate: the split and the certificates ride the class alone, unbounded, transposed and bounded large quotients alike; stabilization of the closure verdict rests on unbounded large quotients — the bounded window's deep vectors cycle with the tiled word while its peaks still separate the split; and a stride ≡ 0 mod 3 follows its own drop census, lookahead 0 where dropless, gated closure-side with no certificate transferring where truly dropped, as at the transposed window's stride 3. At the cubic every observable refuses: the ladder never stabilizes, the peaks grow at every stride alike where the one-class windows hold a band, and no extracted witness transfers, margins topping at +1 against the class windows' ladders. The composite law is the aperiodic form of the parity law wherever the largeness structure is one-class; the cubic's split remains a finite reading at its depth ceiling of 12.

Scope. Rule at scanned scope: closures to m = 21 (24 at the prime window, 30 at the cubic), every stride to 8, every cell decided exactly. The certified ladders are verified instances — explicit integer pairs checked by greedy arithmetic on the defining quotients, counted only where the images part strictly below the agreement. That stabilization needs unbounded large quotients rests on one bounded window and is an observation. Controls: the golden and designed windows reproduce the parity law at every cell, and the finite column at range 30000 sits at or below the limit column at every checked cell.
