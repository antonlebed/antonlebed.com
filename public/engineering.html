Engineering

The primorial tower as a compute substrate. CRT decomposition turns one giant problem into k tiny ones. Results below are at k = 7 (Z/510,510) and k = 8 (Z/9,699,690). Some paths worked. Some didn't. Both documented.

The Idea

Instead of one monolithic output head, use 7 independent CRT output heads (one per channel). A shared backbone projects into per-channel heads. Block-diagonal gradients. Built-in ensemble. Free error detection.

391M× Jacobian savings (Z/510,510)
8,802× Forward pass (Z/510,510)
4/7 ECC rate (free)
25M CRT-GPT params (vs 117M)

What's Proved

Architecture Property

Shared backbone + CRT output = unique optimal point. Maximum ECC reliability AND maximum efficiency. Neither alone is optimal. 7.5× fewer output params, 212× backprop speedup, 2.68× ECC reliability vs monolithic softmax.

Block-Diagonal Jacobian

CRT Jacobian is block-diagonal: O(Σqi²) vs O(N²). At Z/510,510: N²/Σqi² = 391 million ×. The gradient decomposes exactly like the number does.

Loop Property — Forward Pass

CRT forward: N/Σpi speedup. At Z/510,510: 510510/58 = 8,802×. CRT mixing is 50× faster than cyclic mixing.

Block-Diagonal FLOP Property

Matmul: (Σqi)³ / Σqi³ = 22× at Z/510,510. Params: 4.6× fewer. Per NN layer: 3.4×. 12-layer CRT-GPT: ~25M params vs ~117M GPT-1.

What's Measured

GPU benchmarks. Real hardware, real timing, N = 1,000,000 elements.

Theoretical 22× on Z/510,510. GPU measurements on the fat ring Z/214,414,200: 3.6–11.4× (theoretical 16× for that ring). The gap is memory bandwidth and kernel launch overhead — the algebra is exact, the hardware has friction.

Parity Syndrome — The Real Payoff

Free Error Detection

A CRT-LM with 7 output heads on Z/510,510. 4 data heads reconstruct the token; 3 parity heads are algebraically predictable from data. The syndrome = count of parity-head disagreements = a free “this token contradicts itself” signal.

122/122 Errors caught (98% accuracy)

100% error detection from 85% accuracy onward on 2,361 characters of natural English text. On simpler corpora, Z/510,510 converges to 100% accuracy (the fat ring plateaued at 95%). Every channel a prime field — no zero-divisor complications. No held-out set, no extra compute. No standard transformer has this — it's a consequence of decomposition.

Scaling the Ring

How do you scale CRT capacity without breaking the fields? Add more primes. The primorial ladder extends Z/510,510 (7 primes, 210 data slots) to Z/9,699,690 (8 primes, 2,310 data slots) by appending prime 19. Every channel stays a prime field. ECC rate improves from 4/7 to 5/8. No blind spots.

What Didn't Work

These were tried and conclusively failed.

× 3²=9 attention heads — multi-head averaging dilutes signal. Single full-coupling works.
× Eigenvalue sum as embedding — scalar collapses to noise. Per-channel residues ARE the embedding.
× ECC as emergent — parity channels not independently predictable. Must be a training objective.
× Hierarchy-ordered annealing — simultaneous converges 2.69× faster than sequential curriculum.
× AdaBoost for CRT models — bijectivity rotates ALL outputs; no local correction possible.
× Deeper per-channel architecture — width beats depth within each channel.

Conclusions: CRT decomposition is a structural technique, not a silver bullet. At char-level vocab, CRT costs more params than monolithic (58 residues vs 28 classes). At V=1,000, CRT has 17× fewer output params — but monolithic still converges faster (all 7 per-channel predictions must agree, compounding errors multiplicatively). The value is the syndrome payoff: free error detection that no monolithic architecture has.
